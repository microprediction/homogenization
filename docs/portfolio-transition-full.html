Faster switching removes two nonzero portfolio optima

A symmetric two-state market admits a complete constant-fraction optimization theorem, not just a fast-switching expansion. A Robin–Dirichlet resolvent proves that the exact objective has either one optimum at zero or exactly two nonzero optima, with a unique switching-speed threshold when a transition exists.

The model and classification

Let $Y_t\in\{-1,1\}$ flip at rate $m>0$ in each direction and start in its stationary law. The stock has drift $r+aY_t$, common volatility $\sigma>0$, and Brownian noise independent of $Y$, with constant interest rate $r$ and $a>0$.

Choose a single deterministic, unrestricted fraction $w\in\mathbb R$ for horizon $T>0$, with relative risk aversion $0<\gamma<1$. Put $I_T=\int_0^T Y_t\,dt$, $M_m(\theta)=\mathbb E e^{\theta I_T}$ and

$$C_m(w)=r-\tfrac12\gamma\sigma^2w^2 +\frac{\log M_m((1-\gamma)aw)}{(1-\gamma)T},$$ $$v_m=\operatorname{Var}I_T=\frac Tm-\frac{1-e^{-2mT}}{2m^2},\qquad b_m=\frac{(1-\gamma)a^2v_m}{T}-\gamma\sigma^2.$$
$$\begin{array}{ll} b_m\leq0:& w=0\text{ is the unique global maximizer};\\ b_m>0:& \text{exactly two global maximizers }\pm w_*(m),\quad w_*(m)>0. \end{array}$$

In the second case zero is a strict local minimum, and $C_m$ increases up to $w_*(m)$ and decreases afterwards on the positive half-line. If $b_m<0$ the objective is strongly concave with modulus $-b_m$; at equality it still has a unique maximum, but its curvature at zero vanishes.

A transition exists exactly when $(1-\gamma)a^2T>\gamma\sigma^2$. In that case there is a unique critical speed $m_c>0$, determined by $b_{m_c}=0$: two optima occur for $m<m_c$, and only zero for $m\geq m_c$. The positive optimum decreases strictly with speed before vanishing at the threshold.

If the displayed strict inequality fails, zero is uniquely optimal at every positive speed. Indeed $v_m/T=2T^{-1}\int_0^T(T-t)e^{-2mt}dt$ decreases strictly from $T$ to zero as $m$ increases.

The decreasing response is a spectral trace

The two-by-two Feynman–Kac system gives, with $d=\sqrt{m^2+\theta^2}$,

$$M_m(\theta)=e^{-mT}\left\{\cosh(dT)+\frac m d\sinh(dT)\right\}.$$

Define the continuous even response $L_m(\theta)=(\log M_m)'(\theta)/\theta$, with $L_m(0)=v_m$. On $[0,T]$ consider the positive self-adjoint operator

$$\mathcal A_m=-\frac{d^2}{dt^2}+m^2,\qquad u'(0)=mu(0),\quad u(T)=0.$$

Its quadratic form is $\int_0^T(|u'|^2+m^2|u|^2)dt+m|u(0)|^2$. The eigenvalues are $\lambda_j=m^2+\omega_j^2>0$, where

$$\omega_j\cos(\omega_jT)+m\sin(\omega_jT)=0,\qquad \frac{(j+1/2)\pi}{T}<\omega_j<\frac{(j+1)\pi}{T},\quad j=0,1,\ldots.$$
$$L_m(\theta)=2\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal A_m+\theta^2)^{-1} =2\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac1{\lambda_j+\theta^2}.$$

This series is strictly decreasing in $\theta>0$ and tends to zero. The root bounds make the series and its derivative locally uniformly convergent; dominated convergence also proves the limit at infinity.

For a direct proof of the trace identity put $u_\theta(t)=\cosh(dt)+(m/d)\sinh(dt)$ and $F(\theta^2)=u_\theta(T)$. The Green kernel of $\mathcal A_m+\theta^2$ is

$$G_\theta(s,t)=\frac{u_\theta(\min\{s,t\})\sinh(d(T-\max\{s,t\}))}{dF(\theta^2)}.$$

Differentiating $u_\theta''=(m^2+\theta^2)u_\theta$ with respect to $\theta^2$, with fixed initial data, and solving the inhomogeneous equation gives

$$\frac{dF}{d(\theta^2)}=\int_0^T u_\theta(t)\frac{\sinh(d(T-t))}{d}\,dt, \qquad \frac{d\log F}{d(\theta^2)}=\int_0^T G_\theta(t,t)dt.$$

The diagonal integral is the resolvent trace, proving the series without an assumption about complex zeros of the telegraph transform. This is a regular separated-boundary instance of the classical characteristic-function trace identity; Fucci, Piorkowski and Stanfill (2025), Theorem 3.4, equation (3.35), treats a broader Sturm–Liouville setting.

Why it determines every optimum

$$\frac{C_m'(w)}{w}=-\gamma\sigma^2 +\frac{(1-\gamma)a^2}{T}L_m((1-\gamma)aw),\qquad w>0.$$

The right-hand side decreases strictly from $b_m$ to $-\gamma\sigma^2$. Thus it has exactly one positive root when $b_m>0$ and none otherwise, giving the derivative signs above. Evenness and the bound $C_m(w)\leq r+a|w|-\gamma\sigma^2w^2/2$ complete the global classification.

Moreover $(\log M_m)''(\theta)=L_m(\theta)+\theta L_m'(\theta)\leq L_m(0)=v_m$. Therefore $C_m''(w)\leq b_m$, proving the stated strong-concavity modulus when $b_m<0$.

The operator forms share the domain $\{u\in H^1(0,T):u(T)=0\}$. Increasing $m$ strictly increases the form by a positive multiple of $\|u\|_2^2$ plus a nonnegative boundary term, so its positive resolvent trace $L_m(\theta)/2$ decreases strictly at each fixed $\theta$.

The unique positive root of $L_m((1-\gamma)aw)=\gamma\sigma^2T/((1-\gamma)a^2)$ consequently decreases with speed. Continuity and strict decrease of the response imply that this root tends to zero as $m\uparrow m_c$.

Certificate and scope

For $\gamma=1/2$, $a=2$, $\sigma=T=1$, the critical speed is $m_c=3.4149776722$. At speed one the only global maximizers are $\pm3.6411106114$ and their certainty-equivalent rate is $1.3216481724$ when $r=0$; at speed four the unique optimum is zero.

The certificate checks 75 transform/response cases using independent matrix derivatives, Green-kernel quadrature and 512-term spectral sums, plus 72 portfolio classifications, 19 with two optima. Its final line is literal PASS.

The spectral sum is not silently treated as exact: for $N\geq1$ the omitted response satisfies

$$0<2\sum_{j=N}^\infty\frac1{\lambda_j+\theta^2} \leq\frac{2T^2}{\pi^2(N-1/2)}.$$

The numerical checks keep this analytic tail bound separate from scaled floating-point roundoff and quadrature error estimates. The displayed critical speed and optimizer values are numerical root evaluations, not interval enclosures.

This is a stationary, symmetric two-state theorem with common volatility and opposite excess returns. It does not extend the classification to arbitrary priors, unequal switching rates, switched volatility, constrained fractions or adaptive trading; the extreme numerical example is not a market calibration.
