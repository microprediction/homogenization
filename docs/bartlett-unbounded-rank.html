One cycle, arbitrarily many finite-window skew modes

A sharp state-count construction for directed Bartlett forms, with an explicit small-horizon bound.

Research note, 20 October 2026. These are population forms for fixed features of a stationary finite chain, not ranks obtained by integrating different maturity loadings.

Construction and result

For each integer $r\ge1$, take states $a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1},b_0,\ldots,b_{r-1},c$. Give the directed triangle $a_0\to b_0\to c\to a_0$ unit rates and attach unit-rate reversible paths $a_i\leftrightarrow a_{i+1}$ and $b_i\leftrightarrow b_{i+1}$, for $0\le i

The generator $Q_r$ is irreducible and balanced, with uniform stationary distribution on $n=2r+1$ states and $\|Q_r\|_\infty\le4$. Its underlying undirected graph has just one cycle, and $\operatorname{rank}(\operatorname{skew}Q_r)=2$. Write $\operatorname{skew}D=(D-D^\top)/2$ and define

$$M_T=\int_0^T(1-t/T)e^{tQ_r}\,dt,\qquad B_{T,F}=n^{-1}F^\top M_TF,\qquad K_F=n^{-1}F^\top(-Q_r|_{{\bf1}^\perp})^{-1}F.$$

Here $F$ is any full-column-rank centered basis, with $2r$ columns; the inverse in $K_F$ acts on the centered space. Put $C_r=(1/[2(i+j+3)!])_{i,j=0}^{r-1}$ and $\mu_r=\|C_r^{-1}\|_\infty$.

Thus one cycle supports arbitrarily many finite-window skew-mode pairs, while only one pair survives in the infinite-time form. The state count is sharp: a centered form of skew rank $2r$ needs at least $2r+1$ states. For any prescribed horizon $T_0>0$, using generator $(\tau_r/T_0)Q_r$ gives the same rank conclusion at $T_0$.

Proof: isolate a cross block

Let $A=\{a_i\}$ and $D=\{b_i\}$, and let $X(T)=(\operatorname{skew}M_T)_{A,D}$. The $A,A$ and $D,D$ blocks of $M_T$ are symmetric for every $T$. Indeed, all connections of either path to its complement pass through its root, so its block of $(zI-Q_r)^{-1}$ is the inverse of a symmetric path matrix minus $h(z)e_0e_0^\top$ for a scalar $h(z)$.

This Schur-complement argument is valid for sufficiently large real $z$. Expanding the resolvent at infinity shows that every corresponding diagonal block of $Q_r^k$, hence of $M_T$, is symmetric. Consequently the principal skew block on $A\cup D$ is

$$\begin{pmatrix}0&X(T)\\-X(T)^\top&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

The shortest directed walk from $a_i$ to $b_j$ has length $d=i+j+1$ and is unique. The reverse walk needs one extra step through $c$, so $(Q_r^k)_{a_i b_j}-(Q_r^k)_{b_j a_i}=0$ for $k $$M_T=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{T^{k+1}Q_r^k}{(k+2)!},\qquad X_{ij}(T)=T^{i+j+2}\left\{\frac1{2(i+j+3)!}+O(T)\right\}.$$

Set $E_T=\operatorname{diag}(T,T^2,\ldots,T^r)$ and $W(T)=E_T^{-1}X(T)E_T^{-1}$. It extends analytically to $W(0)=C_r$, whose determinant is

$$\det C_r=(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}2^{-r}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{j!}{(r+j+2)!}\ne0.$$

To verify the identity, multiply row $i$ of $(1/(i+j+3)!)_{ij}$ by $(i+r+2)!$. Its columns are monic polynomials in $i$ of degrees $r-1,r-2,\ldots,0$; reverse them and apply the polynomial Vandermonde identity. This is a direct specialization of Krattenthaler (1999), Proposition 1, not a new determinant identity.

Proof: a horizon, not just an asymptotic claim

The entrywise skew coefficient of $Q_r^k$ is bounded by $4^k$. For $0 $$|W_{ij}(T)-(C_r)_{ij}|\le T\frac{4^{d+1}}{(d+3)!}e^{4T}\le\frac23Te^{4T}<54T.$$

The first inequality follows by bounding the later factorial ratios by $1/\ell!$. The second uses the decreasing sequence $4^{d+1}/(d+3)!$, and the last uses $e<3$. Thus $\|C_r^{-1}(W(T)-C_r)\|_\infty\le54r\mu_r T\le1/2$ for $T\le\tau_r$, so the Neumann series proves invertibility throughout that interval.

The principal skew block is invertible, while balance implies $(\operatorname{skew}M_T){\bf1}=0$. Its rank is therefore exactly $2r$, with kernel $\operatorname{span}\{{\bf1}\}$, and restriction to any complete centered basis preserves this rank.

On an orthonormal centered basis put $L=-Q_r$. The identity $\operatorname{skew}(L^{-1})=-L^{-\top}(\operatorname{skew}L)L^{-1}$ preserves the triangle's rank two, proving the long-run assertion. Finally $M_{T_0}(sQ_r)=s^{-1}M_{sT_0}(Q_r)$ proves the clock-rescaled assertion.

Certificates and scope

The rational certificate checks the determinant and the interval bound for $r=1,\ldots,6$, giving respectively $\mu_r=12,1200,206640,58181760,24685214400,14799656551680$. It also certifies full skew rank at $T=1$ for these six unit-rate chains by a degree-40 Taylor polynomial and an exact inverse-tail bound, independently checked against block exponentials and, in the first three cases, quadrature.

For example, $\tau_1=1/1296$ and $\tau_6=1/9590177445488640$. The additional $T=1$ certificates do not assert full rank at all horizons for the unit-rate family. The all-dimensions result comes from the proof, not extrapolation from these finite tests.

No lower bound on skew-mode strength or statistical detectability is claimed; the guaranteed interval becomes very small. Both symmetric forms retain full centered rank, and the occupation covariance is $2T\operatorname{sym}B_{T,F}$, not the directed form itself. This result neither counts additional graph cycles nor resolves infinite-time repeated maximal sector modes.

Finite-window symmetric positivity follows because a nonzero centered feature has a nonzero time integral on a positive-probability no-jump event. Long-run symmetric positivity follows from $\operatorname{sym}(L^{-1})=L^{-\top}(\operatorname{sym}L)L^{-1}$ and the connected graph's positive centered Dirichlet form.
