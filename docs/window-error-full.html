A computable error radius for ordered-window recovery

Given deterministic error budgets on two ordered window-covariance matrices, a positive symmetric part and a small residual certify an explicit generator error radius. This is not a sampling-confidence statement or a test that the data come from a Markov generator.

Exact model and noisy data

Let $Q$ be a finite irreducible row generator with known stationary law $\pi$, state labels and time unit. Choose a known complete centered basis $U$ with $U^TD_\pi U=I$, $\pi U=0$, and put $L=-U^TD_\pi QU$.

For a stationary start and fixed $T>0$, let $X_j=\int_{jT}^{(j+1)T}U^Te_{Y_t}dt$. The ordered matrices $J_0=\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_1)$, $J_1=\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_2)$ obey

$$E=J_0^{-1}J_1=e^{-TL},\qquad J_0=L^{-2}(I-E)^2,\qquad (I-E)^2J_0^{-1}=L^2.$$

These identities follow by integrating $e^{-(u-s)L}$ over the two windows. Since $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{spec}(L)>0$, the principal square root of $L^2$ is $L$, without a diagonalizability assumption. Earlier-window features index rows and later-window features index columns; do not symmetrize.

Suppose supplied matrices satisfy known bounds $\|\widehat J_i-J_i\|_F\le\eta_i$, $i=0,1$, and $\widehat J_0$ is invertible. Compute

$$\widehat A=\widehat J_0^{-1},\quad \widehat E=\widehat A\widehat J_1,\quad \widehat F=I-\widehat E,\quad \widehat Z=\widehat F^2\widehat A.$$

Choose any real candidate $R$ for the centered $L$, not necessarily an exact square root. Require a certified lower bound $(R+R^T)/2\succeq\mu I$ with $\mu>0$, and an upper bound $\zeta\ge\|R^2-\widehat Z\|_F$.

The certificate

The following quantities use only the supplied matrices, their error budgets and the candidate:

$$k=\|\widehat A\|_2,\quad q=k\eta_0<1,\quad a=\frac{k}{1-q},\quad b=ak\eta_0,$$ $$d=a(\eta_1+\eta_0\|\widehat E\|_2),\quad f=\|\widehat F\|_2,\qquad \Delta=a(2fd+d^2)+f^2b+\zeta.$$

If $\Delta<\mu^2$, put

$$r=\frac{\Delta}{\mu+\sqrt{\mu^2-\Delta}},\qquad \overline Q=-URU^TD_\pi,$$ $$\boxed{\|R-L\|_F= \|D_\pi^{1/2}(\overline Q-Q)D_\pi^{-1/2}\|_F\le r.}$$

Consequently each rate obeys $|\overline Q_{ij}-Q_{ij}|\le r\sqrt{\pi_j/\pi_i}$. Failure of either sufficient inequality is inconclusive, not a proof of instability or model failure.

Proof of error propagation

Write $A=J_0^{-1}$ and $F=I-E$. The Neumann inverse bound gives $\|A\|_2\le a$ and $\|A-\widehat A\|_F\le b$. The exact identity

$$E-\widehat E=A\bigl[(J_1-\widehat J_1)-(J_0-\widehat J_0)\widehat E\bigr]$$

gives $\|E-\widehat E\|_F\le d$, hence $\|F^2-\widehat F^2\|_F\le2fd+d^2$. Expanding $F^2A-\widehat F^2\widehat A$ proves $\|L^2-\widehat Z\|_F\le a(2fd+d^2)+f^2b$ and thus $\|L^2-R^2\|_F\le\Delta$.

Proof of the root radius

The Sylvester operator $\mathcal S(X)=RX+XR$ has inverse $\mathcal S^{-1}(Y)=\int_0^\infty e^{-tR}Ye^{-tR}dt$ with Frobenius norm at most $1/(2\mu)$. For $D=L^2-R^2$, solve

$$X=\mathcal S^{-1}(D-X^2).$$

On the Frobenius ball of radius $r=\mu-\sqrt{\mu^2-\Delta}$, this map preserves the ball and has contraction factor at most $r/\mu<1$. Its fixed point satisfies $(R+X)^2=L^2$ and $(R+X+(R+X)^T)/2\succeq(\mu-r)I\succ0$. Uniqueness of the principal square root forces $R+X=L$, proving the bound.

The weighted lifting identity follows from the orthonormal columns of $D_\pi^{1/2}U$. The root-stage radius is sharp already for candidate $R=1$, true root $L=1/2$, and $\Delta=3/4$, giving $r=1/2$. This does not assert sharpness of the preceding covariance-error propagation bound.

A rational end-to-end benchmark

For the symmetric two-state chain with $L=1$ and $T=\log2$, the exact scalar data are $J_0=1/4$, $J_1=1/8$. Supply $\widehat J_0=1/4$, $\widehat J_1=31/250$, so $\eta_0=0$, $\eta_1=1/1000$, and choose $R=126/125$.

Then $\zeta=0$, $\Delta=253/15625$, and the guaranteed radius lies strictly between $0.008064$ and $0.008065$. The actual weighted generator error is $1/125=0.008$. Rational quadratic inequalities verify the interval, independently of floating-point square roots.

Classical tools and precise scope

Accretive square-root perturbation bounds via Sylvester equations are classical; see Schmitt (1992) and Higham (2008), Chapter 6. The proof here gives an explicit residual-aware specialization to the two ordered covariance inputs.

For every fixed irreducible chain, $(L+L^T)/2\succ0$ by the stationary Dirichlet form. Thus the test succeeds for sufficiently accurate inputs and candidates, with a locally linear deterministic error bound even at reversible generators. No uniform constant over generators or window lengths is claimed.

The certificate checks the scalar benchmark exactly and tests nonuniform, sparse, reversible and defective chains numerically. These floating-point cross-checks are not interval proofs: rigorous numerical use requires bounds on all computed norms, inverses and residuals, including arithmetic error.

The theorem assumes an underlying generator exists and that the input error budgets are valid; it does not supply those budgets from data. The candidate $\overline Q$ need not have nonnegative off-diagonal entries, so its generator constraints require a separate check. No stationary-law uncertainty, hidden-state identification, finite-sample confidence, or maturity-integrated rank conclusion is covered.
