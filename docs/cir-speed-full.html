Faster switching need not reduce CIR variance

Exact finite- and long-maturity speed classifications when the mean level and vol-of-vol switch.

Setup and result

Let $Y$ have row generator $mQ_0$, where $Q_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-\alpha&\alpha\\ \beta&-\beta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $\alpha,\beta,m>0$, and invariant law $\pi=(\beta,\alpha)/(\alpha+\beta)$.

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta_{Y_t}-v_t)\,dt+\sqrt{a_{Y_t}v_t}\,dB_t,\qquad V_T=\int_0^T v_t\,dt.$$

Here $\kappa>0$ is common to both states, $a_i=\xi_i^2>0$, $\theta_i>0$, and $B$ is independent of $Y$. Assume $2\kappa\theta_i\ge a_i$ and start in the joint invariant law at each $m$, with finite second moment; the statewise Feller condition keeps positive paths away from zero.

Write $\bar\theta=\pi\theta$, $\bar a=\pi a$, $s_\theta^2=\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\theta)$, $c_{\theta a}=\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(\theta,a)$, $r=m(\alpha+\beta)$, and $A=\kappa s_\theta^2+c_{\theta a}/2$.

$$\mathbb E v=\bar\theta,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(v)=\frac{\bar a\bar\theta}{2\kappa}+\frac{A}{\kappa+r},$$ $$\Gamma_2(r):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Var}(V_T)}{T} =\frac{\bar a\bar\theta}{\kappa^2}+\frac{2s_\theta^2}{r} +\frac{c_{\theta a}}{\kappa(\kappa+r)}.$$

If $s_\theta^2=0$, then $c_{\theta a}=0$ and both quantities are constant in $m$, even if $a$ switches. For $s_\theta^2>0$, the classification is complete:

ConditionStationary $\operatorname{Var}(v)$Long-run rate $\Gamma_2$
$A>0$Strictly decreasingStrictly decreasing
$A=0$ConstantStrictly decreasing
$A<0$Strictly increasingExactly one minimum

In the last case put $q=\sqrt{2\kappa s_\theta^2/(-c_{\theta a})}\in(0,1)$. The minimum is attained at

$$r_*=\frac{\kappa q}{1-q},\qquad m_*=\frac{\kappa q}{(\alpha+\beta)(1-q)}.$$

Proof

Every centered two-state feature is an eigenfunction of $mQ_0$ with eigenvalue $-r$. Stationarity applied to $v$, $v(\theta_Y-\bar\theta)$ and $v(a_Y-\bar a)$ therefore gives

$$\mathbb E v=\bar\theta,\qquad u_r:=\operatorname{Cov}(v,\theta_Y) =\frac{\kappa s_\theta^2}{\kappa+r},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(v,a_Y)=\frac{\kappa c_{\theta a}}{\kappa+r}.$$

Applying the generator to $v^2$ gives $\operatorname{Var}(v)=u_r+\mathbb E[a_Yv]/(2\kappa)$, proving the first boxed variance formula. The conditional mean solves a scalar forced linear equation:

$$\mathbb E[v_t\mid v_0,Y_0=i] =\bar\theta+e^{-\kappa t}(v_0-\bar\theta) +\kappa(\theta_i-\bar\theta)h_{\kappa,r}(t),$$ $$h_{\kappa,r}(t)=\frac{e^{-rt}-e^{-\kappa t}}{\kappa-r}\quad(r\ne\kappa), \qquad h_{\kappa,\kappa}(t)=t e^{-\kappa t}.$$

Consequently the stationary covariance and its integrated rate are

$$C_v(t)=e^{-\kappa t}\operatorname{Var}(v)+\kappa u_r h_{\kappa,r}(t),\qquad \Gamma_2=2\int_0^\infty C_v(t)\,dt =\frac{2\operatorname{Var}(v)}{\kappa}+\frac{2u_r}{r}.$$

Both terms in $C_v$ are nonnegative and exponentially integrable, including at resonance. The identity $\operatorname{Var}(V_T)=2\int_0^T(T-t)C_v(t)\,dt$ proves existence of the rate and the second boxed formula.

$$\Gamma_2'(r)=-\frac{2s_\theta^2}{r^2} -\frac{c_{\theta a}}{\kappa(\kappa+r)^2}.$$

If $c_{\theta a}\ge0$ this derivative is negative. Otherwise put $q=\sqrt{2\kappa s_\theta^2/(-c_{\theta a})}$; its sign is the sign of $(r/(\kappa+r))^2-q^2$, which crosses zero exactly once when $q<1$ and never when $q\ge1$; this proves the classification.

A strictly positive U-shaped benchmark

Take $\alpha=\beta=\kappa=1$, $\theta=(0.03,0.04)$ and $a=(0.05,0.001)$. The Feller margins $2\kappa\theta-a=(0.01,0.079)$ are strictly positive, and

$$s_\theta^2=0.000025,\qquad c_{\theta a}=-0.0001225,\qquad A=-0.00003625,$$ $$\operatorname{Var}(v)=0.00044625-\frac{0.00003625}{1+2m},\qquad \Gamma_2=0.0008925+\frac{0.00005}{2m}-\frac{0.0001225}{1+2m}.$$

The minimum occurs at $m_*=0.8845681325$, with rate $0.0008765248$, below the averaged limit $0.0008925$. The rate first falls and then rises, while stationary variance increases throughout. Faster switching can raise diffusion noise because it changes the stationary dependence between $a_Y$ and $v$; negative autocovariance is not the mechanism.

Finite maturity: when does the minimum appear?

For every $T>0$, let $\mathcal V_T(r)=\operatorname{Var}(V_T)$ in the same joint invariant law. If $s_\theta^2>0$ and $A\ge0$, this variance strictly decreases with switching speed at every maturity; if $s_\theta^2=0$, it is constant in speed.

If $A<0$, define $D=-A/(\kappa^2s_\theta^2)>0$ and

$$F_\kappa(t)=2\int_0^t(t-u)e^{-\kappa u}\,du =\frac{2(\kappa t-1+e^{-\kappa t})}{\kappa^2}.$$

There is exactly one critical maturity $T_c>0$, determined by

$$\frac{T_c^3}{3F_\kappa(T_c)}=D.$$

For $0<T\le T_c$, $\mathcal V_T$ is strictly increasing for every $r>0$. For $T>T_c$, it first decreases and then increases, with exactly one minimum $r_T$. The minimizing speed $m_T=r_T/(\alpha+\beta)$ increases strictly with $T$, from zero as $T\downarrow T_c$ to the long-run minimizer $m_*$ as $T\to\infty$, always staying below $m_*$.

Positive-kernel proof

$$H_T(r)=\int_0^T F_\kappa(T-s)e^{-rs}\,ds,\qquad J_T(r)=\int_0^T F_\kappa(T-s)\{1+(\kappa+r)s\}e^{-rs}\,ds.$$

Integrating the exact covariance above and differentiating gives

$$\mathcal V_T(r)=\frac{\bar a\bar\theta}{2\kappa}F_\kappa(T) +\frac{AF_\kappa(T)+\kappa^2s_\theta^2 H_T(r)}{\kappa+r},\qquad \mathcal V_T'(r)=-\frac{AF_\kappa(T)+\kappa^2s_\theta^2J_T(r)}{(\kappa+r)^2}.$$ $$J_T'(r)=-(\kappa+r)\int_0^T s^2F_\kappa(T-s)e^{-rs}\,ds<0,\qquad J_T(0)=T^3/3,\qquad J_T(r)\longrightarrow0\quad(r\to\infty).$$

These identities prove the speed classification and the unique-root equation $L_T(r_T)=D$, where $L_T=J_T/F_\kappa(T)$. The critical-maturity ratio is strictly increasing from zero to infinity, since

$$\frac{T^3}{3F_\kappa(T)} =\frac{T}{6\int_0^1(1-u)e^{-\kappa Tu}\,du}.$$

To compare maturities, observe that $F_\kappa$ is strictly log-concave:

$$F_\kappa F_\kappa''-(F_\kappa')^2 =\frac4{\kappa^2}\{(1+\kappa t)e^{-\kappa t}-1\}<0\qquad(t>0).$$

Thus $F_\kappa(T-s)/F_\kappa(T)$ strictly increases with $T>s$. It follows that $L_T(r)$ strictly increases with $T$ for each $r\ge0$, while it strictly decreases with $r$. These facts prove strict increase of $r_T$ and its zero limit at $T_c$.

For $r>0$, dominated convergence and the same kernel give the explicit enclosure

$$0<\frac{\kappa+2r}{r^2}-L_T(r) \le\frac{2(2\kappa+3r)}{\kappa F_\kappa(T)r^3}.$$

Indeed, extend the normalized kernel by zero for $s>T$ and use $0\le1-F_\kappa((T-s)_+)/F_\kappa(T)\le2s/\{\kappa F_\kappa(T)\}$, which follows from $0\le F_\kappa'\le2/\kappa$. Integrating against $\{1+(\kappa+r)s\}e^{-rs}$ proves the bound.

The limiting profile has its unique level-$D$ root at $r_*$, proving $r_T\uparrow r_*$. The enclosure is uniform on every $r\ge r_0>0$, not through $r=0$.

Since the magnitude of the limiting profile's derivative decreases with $r$, the mean value theorem gives, for $T>T_c$, the explicit minimizer bound

$$0<r_*-r_T\le \frac{r_*^3(2\kappa+3r_T)} {\kappa F_\kappa(T)(\kappa+r_*)r_T^3}.$$

Divide by $\alpha+\beta$ to bound $m_*-m_T$. This yields $O(T^{-1})$ uniformly on $T\ge T_0>T_c$; the displayed upper bound diverges as $T\downarrow T_c$.

In the benchmark above, $D=1.45$ and $T_c\approx2.3018935934$. At $T=3,4,8,16,64$, the minimizing speeds are respectively $0.3663632166$, $0.5742283564$, $0.7759352488$, $0.8378987478$, $0.8739573031$.

Scope and certificate

For $M_T=\int_0^T\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t$ with $W$ independent of $(B,Y,v_0)$, $\kappa_4(M_T)=3\operatorname{Var}(V_T)$, so its fourth-cumulant rate has the same classification. The finite-maturity variance classification transfers to its fourth cumulant as well. This is not a claim about risk-neutral log returns, leverage or option-price ordering.

All speed comparisons here use the joint invariant law at each speed, not a fixed initial variance. No classification with switching $\kappa$ or more states is asserted.

The proof specializes the existing stationary moment and Poisson formulas, rather than claiming a new moment method; finite polynomial semigroups are standard in Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012). The certificate compares the formulas with generic moment/Poisson solves and finite-horizon covariance quadrature with a degree-two matrix exponential, including $r=\kappa$.

The maturity certificate independently differentiates that matrix exponential and the invariant moments, checking the full speed derivative rather than treating the starting law as speed-independent.
