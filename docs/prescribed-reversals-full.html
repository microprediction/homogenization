Prescribing every maturity reversal

The real-spectrum bound is sharp for every state count, inside positive switched-CIR models.

For every $n\ge3$ and every ordered set of $n-3$ positive maturities, there is an $n$-regime positive switched-CIR model whose share-weighted log-return mean gap vanishes exactly at those maturities. Every positive zero is simple; the switching speed and all model parameters remain fixed as maturity varies.

This strengthens the real-spectrum upper bound by attaining it at arbitrary nodes. It concerns zeros of one scalar observable in maturity, not call-price crossings in strike or identification of full return laws.

Exact observable and finite rank criterion

Let $G$ be an irreducible row generator with stationary initial law $\pi$, independent of the original Brownian motions, and let $G^*=D_\pi^{-1}G^\top D_\pi$. Compare both orientations with the same fixed $v_0>0$, $X_0=0$, and coefficients in

$$dv=(a_Y-\kappa_Yv)dt+\xi_Y\sqrt v\,dB,\qquad dX=-vdt/2+\sqrt v(\rho_YdB+\sqrt{1-\rho_Y^2}\,dW).$$

Assume well-defined nonnegative variance, a true stock martingale, and finite share-weighted log-return means at every finite horizon. Write $k=\kappa-\rho\xi$, $A=G-\operatorname{diag}k$, and use the stationary inner product.

$$\mathcal L_a(M)=\langle a,M\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,Ma\rangle_\pi,\qquad D_a(T)=\mathbb E_G[e^{X_T}X_T]-\mathbb E_{G^*}[e^{X_T}X_T] =\tfrac12\int_0^T(T-u)\mathcal L_a(e^{uA})du.$$

Under the continuous stock numéraire, regime intensities remain $G$, variance reversion becomes $k$, and the log-return drift becomes $v/2$. The variance-weighted masses satisfy $z'=(G^\top-\operatorname{diag}k)z+\operatorname{diag}(a)\pi^\top$, $z(0)=v_0\pi^\top$. Weighted adjointness cancels the common initial-variance term; two integrations give the displayed identity.

$$B_{j,i}=\mathcal L_{e_i}(A^j),\qquad j=2,\ldots,n-1,\quad i=0,\ldots,n-1.$$

If $A$ has entirely real spectrum and $\operatorname{rank}B=n-2$, then for any distinct positive nodes $\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{n-3}$ there is a nonzero signal $D_a$ whose only positive zeros are those nodes, all simple. The resulting real vector $a$ may be shifted by a sufficiently large constant without changing the signal. The spectral requirement is on the share-moment matrix $A$, not on the regime generator $G$.

Why the rank criterion prescribes the zeros

Stationarity gives $\mathcal L_a(I)=\mathcal L_a(A)=0$ for every $a$. Cayley–Hamilton and $D_a''=\mathcal L_a(e^{TA})/2$ show that the signal space $\mathcal V=\{D_a:a\in\mathbb R^n\}$ has dimension exactly $\operatorname{rank}B$.

Each signal solves $\partial_T^2\det(\partial_T I-A)D=0$, an equation of order $n+2$ with real characteristic roots. It has a zero of multiplicity at least four at the origin. The real-exponential Rolle argument bounds every nonzero solution by $n+1$ zeros on any finite closed interval, counted with multiplicity, hence bounds its positive zeros by $n-3$.

For completeness, divide a solution by one positive exponential and differentiate; the differential equation loses one characteristic root while Rolle's theorem loses at most one zero. Induction proves the bound, including repeated roots; an identically zero derivative leaves a nonzero exponential with no zeros.

The $n-3$ homogeneous conditions $D(\tau_j)=0$ on the $(n-2)$-dimensional space $\mathcal V$ admit a nonzero solution. The zero bound excludes every additional positive zero and every multiple prescribed zero.

Finally $\mathcal L_{\mathbf1}(M)=0$ for every matrix $M$, so $D_{a+c\mathbf1}=D_a$. Also $D_k\equiv0$: $A\mathbf1=A^*\mathbf1=-k$ and commutation of $A$ with $e^{uA}$ give $\mathcal L_k(e^{uA})=0$. Thus, under the rank condition, the entire cancellation kernel is $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,k\}$.

A full-rank real-spectrum family in every dimension

Index the states by $i=0,\ldots,n-1$, let $C_{i,i+1}=1$ cyclically and $C_{ii}=-1$, and set

$$G_\varepsilon=\varepsilon C,\quad \pi_i=1/n,\quad k_i=3+i,\quad A_\varepsilon=-\operatorname{diag}k+\varepsilon C,\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1/2.$$

The characteristic polynomial is $\prod_i(z+k_i+\varepsilon)-\varepsilon^n$. At $z=-k_i-\varepsilon\pm\varepsilon$, the product has opposite signs and magnitude greater than $\varepsilon^n$, since every other factor has magnitude at least $1-\varepsilon>\varepsilon$. There is exactly one simple negative root in each disjoint interval, so the spectrum is entirely real.

Consider the square minor of $B_\varepsilon$ using the interior columns $i=1,\ldots,n-2$. It is a polynomial in $\varepsilon$ whose first nonzero coefficient is explicit:

$$\det B_\varepsilon[:,1:n-1]=c_n\varepsilon^{n-2}+O(\varepsilon^{n-1}),\qquad c_n={(-1)^{(n-2)(n-1)/2}(n-2)!(n-1)!\over n^{n-2}} \prod_{j=1}^{n-3}j!\ne0.$$

To check this, put $h_m(x,y)=\sum_{\ell=0}^m x^\ell y^{m-\ell}$. The coefficient of $\varepsilon$ in row $j=m+1$ and interior column $i$ is $(-1)^m[h_m(k_i,k_i+1)-h_m(k_i-1,k_i)]/n$.

As a polynomial in $k_i$ it has degree $m-1$ and leading coefficient $(-1)^m m(m+1)/n$. The determinant of these rows is the product of their leading coefficients times the Vandermonde determinant at the consecutive interior nodes, giving $c_n$.

Consequently the rank is $n-2$ for every sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, and indeed for all $\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)$ except possibly finitely many roots of this minor. Choose one such speed before choosing the prescribed maturity nodes; no explicit dimension-uniform speed threshold is asserted.

Realization with positive CIR variance and a true stock martingale

For this family fix $\kappa_i=2$, $\rho_i=-1/2$ and $\xi_i=2(i+1)$, giving $k_i=3+i$ exactly. After solving the node constraints, add a common constant to $a$ until $2a_i>\xi_i^2$ in every regime. This ensures strictly positive variance from $v_0>0$ and does not alter $A$, its rank, or $D_a$.

The martingale and integrability conditions hold for every such finite $a$. Set $b=\delta=1/(4n^2)$ and take return moments $s=-\delta$ and $s=1+\delta$; their exponential supersolutions have variance coefficient bounded by

$$\tfrac12\xi_i^2b^2+(\rho_i\xi_i s-2)b+\tfrac12(s^2-s) \le{-8n^2+2n+1\over32n^4}<0.$$

Indeed $\xi_i\le2n$ and $\rho_i\xi_i s\le n\delta$, which give the displayed bound directly. With $C_s=\max_i a_i b$, localization bounds $\mathbb E e^{sX_t+bv_t}$ by $e^{bv_0+C_st}$. The positive moment above one makes the localized stocks uniformly integrable on every finite horizon; the negative moment supplies the other side of the moment strip.

Five regimes with exactly two reversals: an exact fixture

Take $n=5$, $\varepsilon=1/10$, $v_0=.04$ and

$$a=(60,59.665,61,59.241,60),\quad k=(3,4,5,6,7),\quad \xi=(2,4,6,8,10).$$

The interior minor is $361/156250$, and $c_5=288/125$. The strict Feller margin is $\min_i(2a_i-\xi_i^2)=20$; the five spectral intervals have centers $-3.1,-4.1,-5.1,-6.1,-7.1$ and radius $.1$.

$$D(.5)\in[0.000000077818,0.000000077819],\qquad D(1)\in[-0.000000556804,-0.000000556803],$$ $$D(2)\in[0.000001800425,0.000001800426].$$

The two positive zeros are therefore unique in $(.5,1)$ and $(1,2)$, and there are no others. Their numerical estimates are $0.59042656$ and $1.54165209$; only the brackets and global zero count are certified claims.

Degree-160 rational Taylor actions of the two seven-dimensional forward systems enclose each mean after removing the common constant $60$ and the common initial-variance contribution, both of which cancel exactly. If $M$ is the centered system matrix and $L=\lceil\|TM\|_\infty\rceil$, each directional output error is at most $3^L L^{161}/(2\cdot161!)$. The summed errors are below $9.2\times10^{-189}$, $2.2\times10^{-138}$ and $4.3\times10^{-91}$ at the three horizons.

The certificate also checks the exact leading minor and a nonzero minor at speed $.1$ for $n=3,\ldots,10$, together with spectral intervals and CIR supersolutions. Independent doubled-integral semigroups and the polynomial-moment implementation on main agree with the rational fixture within $3\times10^{-9}$.

Literature and scope

The real-exponential zero bound and the underlying Chebyshev-space terminology are classical; see Aldaz, Kounchev and Render (2009), Theorem 9 and its real-root proof in the authors' preprint. The contribution here is the full-rank cyclic construction and its realization with strictly positive CIR variance, not a new general Chebyshev theorem.

This is an exact fixed-parameter result, not a fast-switching asymptotic or a complex-spectrum global zero bound. It does not settle the sharp call-crossing problem in issue 204. Equal share-weighted means at a node do not imply equal option-price curves there.
