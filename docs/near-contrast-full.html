Robust exponential-age ranks near one CDF contrast

A uniform statewise CDF error of $\eta$ costs at most $2\eta$ in marginal rank coverage, without a sample-size multiplier.

Let $Z_t$, $t\in\mathbb R$, be a jointly measurable strictly stationary latent process. Conditional on its full path and the sampling times, score emissions are independent with fixed continuous CDFs $F_{Z_t}$. Suppose there are continuous reference CDFs on one affine line, with a bounded real function $h$ satisfying $\mathbb E h(Z_0)=0$:

$$G_z(s)=\bar G(s)+h(z)H(s),\qquad \sup_s|F_z(s)-G_z(s)|\le\eta,\quad 0\le\eta\le1.$$

The error bound is uniform in latent state, up to a stationary null set, and each $G_z$ must be a genuine CDF. It is not merely closeness of the pooled marginal CDFs. No Markov, mixing, ergodicity or density lower-bound assumption is needed.

The sampling rule and coverage budget

Choose $\beta>0$ and independently draw chronological gaps $D_j\sim\operatorname{Exp}(j\beta)$, $1\le j\le N$, where the argument is a rate. Calibrate on the scores at $0,D_1,\ldots,D_1+\cdots+D_{N-1}$ and test on the next score at $D_1+\cdots+D_N$. If $Q_k$ is the $k$th calibration order statistic, then

$$\boxed{\left|\Pr(S_{\rm test}\le Q_k)-\frac{k}{N+1}\right|\le2\eta,\qquad1\le k\le N.}$$

This is a deterministic model-approximation budget, not an estimate of $\eta$ from calibration data. The sampling rule needs neither the reference CDFs nor the latent dynamics, but interpreting its error requires a justified uniform comparison. The bound is conservative; no optimality of its constant is claimed.

A more precise finite-sample envelope

Write $p=k/(N+1)$ and $\phi(u)=\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(N,u)\le k-1\}$. The proof gives the asymmetric bounds, clipped to $[0,1]$,

$$p-\eta-\int_0^\eta\phi(u)\,du \le\Pr(S_{\rm test}\le Q_k) \le p+\eta+\int_{1-\eta}^1[1-\phi(u)]\,du.$$

Each integral is at most $\eta$, proving the displayed sample-size-independent budget. For evaluation, with $I_x$ the regularized incomplete beta function,

$$\int_0^\eta\phi(u)\,du=\frac1{N+1}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}I_\eta(r+1,N-r+1),$$ $$\int_{1-\eta}^1[1-\phi(u)]\,du=\frac1{N+1}\sum_{r=k}^{N}I_\eta(N-r+1,r+1).$$

At $N=k=9$ and $\eta=0.01$, the refined envelope is $[0.880000000000,0.919561792499]$, compared with the simpler $[0.88,0.92]$.

Proof: condition on the past, then sandwich CDFs

By stationarity put the test at zero, and draw labeled calibration ages iid $\operatorname{Exp}(\beta)$. Reversing their independent order-statistic spacings gives the chronological schedule above. Define

$$U_t=\beta\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta a}h(Z_{t-a})\,da,\qquad G_U(s)=\bar G(s)+U_0H(s).$$

The bounded stationary filter has absolutely continuous paths and derivative $\beta(h(Z_t)-U_t)$ almost everywhere. For every continuously differentiable $\psi$ and $T>0$, the chain rule and bounded Fubini give

$$0=\mathbb E[\psi(U_T)-\psi(U_0)] =\beta T\mathbb E[\psi'(U_0)(h(Z_0)-U_0)],\qquad \mathbb E[h(Z_0)\mid U_0]=U_0.$$

The final identity follows by taking all continuous derivatives on the compact range of $U_0$. Consequently $\mathbb E[G_{Z_0}(s)\mid U_0]=G_U(s)$.

Given the full latent past including $Z_0$, the age-labeled calibration scores are iid with CDF $A(s)=\beta\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta a}F_{Z_{-a}}(s)\,da$. Thus $\|A-G_U\|_\infty\le\eta$ and actual conditional coverage is $\int\phi(A(s))\,dF_{Z_0}(s)$.

The function $s\mapsto\phi(A(s))$ is decreasing with total variation one. Stieltjes integration by parts therefore changes this integral by at most $\eta$ when $F_{Z_0}$ is replaced by $G_{Z_0}$. Monotonicity then sandwiches the resulting integrand between $\phi([G_U+\eta]_0^1)$ and $\phi([G_U-\eta]_0^1)$, where brackets denote clipping.

These two envelopes depend on the past only through $U_0$. Averaging $dG_{Z_0}$ conditional on $U_0$ replaces it by $dG_U$; the continuous probability integral transform then makes $G_U(S)$ uniform. Integrating the two shifted binomial tails over $[0,1]$ yields the finite-sample bounds because $\int_0^1\phi(u)du=p$.

In particular, when $\eta=0$ this proves exact marginal ranks for the one-contrast reference model. Conditional iid structure concerns the unsorted age labels; the chronologically ordered scores need not be exchangeable.

A second contrast breaks exactness, even with an unchanged pooled CDF

Take the stationary chain with uniform invariant law and

$$Q=\tfrac16\begin{pmatrix}-5&1&4\\1&-5&4\\4&4&-8\end{pmatrix},\quad h=(-1,0,1),\quad r=(1/2,-1,1/2),$$ $$G_i(s)=s+\tfrac14h_i(s^2-s),\qquad F_i(s)=G_i(s)+\delta r_i(s^3-s),\quad0\le s\le1.$$

Extend the CDFs by zero and one outside this interval. At $\delta=1/10$ they have positive densities and uniform statewise CDF error $\eta=2\delta/(3\sqrt3)$. Their pooled CDF is exactly $s$, the same as the reference pooled CDF, but at $N=k=2$ and $\beta=1$ exact coverage is $48384001/72576000=2/3+1/72576000$.

The certificate checks 810 chronological multi-contrast coverage budgets through $N=29$, 184 binomial-envelope identities, the exact rational witness, and zero-perturbation controls. These examples verify the formulas, not sharpness of the error constant.

Scope and relation to classical robustness bounds

The guarantee is stationary marginal coverage over the latent path, emissions and randomized schedule, not coverage conditional on observed gaps, calibration scores or current state. It does not cover same-panel score refitting, overlapping path emissions or anytime calibration reuse. A small pooled-CDF error alone cannot replace the uniform statewise hypothesis, as the rational witness shows.

Kolmogorov-distance coverage control is classical: Clarkson et al. (2025), Lemma 3.1, treat independent contaminated calibration scores and clean test scores. Here the specialization is the stationary latent one-contrast reference and randomized-age panel; ordinary CDF perturbation inequalities are not claimed as new. General concentration and decoupling guarantees remain separate; see Oliveira et al. (2024).
