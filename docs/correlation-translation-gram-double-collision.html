Three tails create two successive Gaussian collision blocks

At the next boundary, two tail derivatives meet two Gaussian modes at different scales.

The first open window ends when the first tail derivative catches the first post-primary Gaussian feature. For three tails, the second derivative then meets the following Gaussian feature one order later, producing two distinct rank-two limits.

Double-collision theorem

Use the three-tail triangular law, positive weights, distinct centered offsets, and notation of the multi-tail confluence theorem. Assume $1\le r\le k-6$ and

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n}\longrightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty).$$

Let $P_1$ project away from $L_1=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_r,v_0\}$ and set $g_1=P_1\phi_{r+1}$ and $b_1=P_1v_1$. Define

$$M_1=h_{r+1}g_1g_1^\top+\eta_1\rho^2b_1b_1^\top.$$

If $\alpha_1\ge\alpha_2>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+2+j}(K_n)=\alpha_j\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)}),\qquad j=1,2.$$

Next let $P_2$ project away from $L_2=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+1},v_0,v_1\}$ and set $g_2=P_2\phi_{r+2}$ and $b_2=P_2v_2$. Define

$$M_2=h_{r+2}g_2g_2^\top+\eta_2\rho^4b_2b_2^\top.$$

If $\beta_1\ge\beta_2>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+4+j}(K_n)=\beta_j\varepsilon_n^{2(r+2)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+2)}),\qquad j=1,2.$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+2)})$.

Why there are two blocks

The first tail feature has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n$, matching $\varepsilon_n^{r+1}$. Its orthogonal label coordinate and the Gaussian residual contribute the two summands of $M_1$.

After their complete domain and output spans are removed, the remaining tail feature has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^2$, matching $\varepsilon_n^{r+2}$. The label residual contributes $\eta_2\rho^4$ at covariance scale.

Both matrices have rank two because the polynomial/exponential evaluation system through $x^{r+2}$ and $x^2e^{\tau x}$ is extended Chebyshev at the $k\ge r+6$ distinct nodes.

Seven-output certificate

Take $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3)$, centered offsets $(-4/3,-1/3,2/3)$, nodes $(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3)$, $\rho=3/2$, and $\delta_n=\rho\varepsilon_n$.

The first collision coefficients are $(82.878648476347436,0.017345179990531)$ and the second are $(0.259769760019158,0.000032854891863)$. At $n=4096$, their exact normalized ratios are

$$(0.998218744222,0.999874455440,0.998076359749,1.000465647028).$$

The 260-digit certificate also checks the primary block, exact rare weights, full confluent rank, positivity, convergence, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for three tails and fixed $k,r,q,\gamma,$ and $\rho$ at one fixed positive maturity. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not a finite-$n$ bound or uniformity over moving dimensions, offsets, or boundary ratios.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
