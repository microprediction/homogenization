Fourier envelopes for switched CIR returns

Explicit density, CDF and damped-call truncation bounds, uniform in switching speed.

Let $B,W$ be independent Brownian motions and $Y$ an independent finite-state chain with any conservative row generator $mQ$, $m\ge0$. Take any initial regime prior and fixed $v_0\ge0$, $X_0=0$.

$$dv_t=(a_{Y_t}-\kappa_{Y_t}v_t)dt+\xi_{Y_t}\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t,$$ $$dX_t=-\tfrac12v_tdt+\sqrt{v_t}\left(\rho_{Y_t}dB_t+ \sqrt{1-\rho_{Y_t}^2}\,dW_t\right).$$

Assume $a_i,\kappa_i,\xi_i>0$ and put $a_* =\min_i a_i$, $K=\max_i\kappa_i$, $\Xi=\max_i\xi_i$ and $\eta=\min_i(1-\rho_i^2)>0$. These bounds do not require irreducibility, stationarity or the statewise Feller inequality. If the variance mean level is parametrized by $\theta_i$, use $a_i=\kappa_i\theta_i$.

A scalar Laplace supersolution

For $s\ge0$, let $b'=s-Kb-\Xi^2b^2/2$, $b(0)=0$, and $A'=-a_*b$, $A(0)=0$. Then, for $V_T=\int_0^T v_tdt$,

$$\mathbb E e^{-sV_T}\le w_s(T,v_0):=e^{A(T)-b(T)v_0}\le1.$$ $$d=\sqrt{K^2+2\Xi^2s},\quad D=K+d+(d-K)e^{-dT},\quad b(T)={2s(1-e^{-dT})\over D},$$ $$A(T)={2a_*\over\Xi^2}\left\{\log{2d\over D}+{(K-d)T\over2}\right\}.$$

Indeed, for the variance generator $\mathcal L_i$,

$$ { (\partial_T-\mathcal L_i+sv)w_s\over w_s} =(a_i-a_*)b+(K-\kappa_i)bv+\tfrac12(\Xi^2-\xi_i^2)b^2v\ge0.$$

The switching term vanishes because $w_s$ does not depend on the regime. Itô's formula, localization and Fatou's lemma applied to $e^{-s\int_0^t v_rdr}w_s(T-t,v_t)$ prove the bound. This is a transform supersolution, not pathwise comparison of diffusions with different vol-of-vol.

Real-axis decay and density truncation

Condition on $B,Y$ and write $X_T=M+\int_0^T\sqrt{(1-\rho_{Y_t}^2)v_t}\,dW_t$. The remaining Gaussian variance is $J\ge\eta V_T$, so for $\varphi_T(u)=\mathbb E e^{iuX_T}$,

$$|\varphi_T(u)|\le w_{\eta u^2/2}(T,v_0)\le C_Te^{-b_T|u|},\qquad C_T=2^{2a_*/\Xi^2}e^{a_*KT/\Xi^2},\quad b_T={a_*T\sqrt\eta\over\Xi}.$$

The last inequality uses $D\ge K+d$, $2d/(K+d)\le2$, $d\ge\Xi\sqrt\eta|u|$, and discards the nonpositive initial-variance exponent. Thus at every fixed $T>0$ the return density extends analytically to $|\operatorname{Im}x|<b_T$.

Fourier inversion truncated to $|u|\le U$ gives density $f_U$ and CDF $F_U$ with

$$\sup_x|f_T(x)-f_U(x)|\le {C_Te^{-b_TU}\over\pi b_T},\qquad \sup_x|F_T(x)-F_U(x)|\le {C_Te^{-b_TU}\over\pi b_TU}.$$

These are truncation bounds for exact integrals; quadrature and floating-point error are separate. The CDF bound follows from the integrable $\varphi_T(u)/u$ tail in the inversion formula.

Damped contours from a positive Riccati bound

For $r>1$ define $a^*=\max_i a_i$ and $\ell_r=\max_i(r\rho_i\xi_i-\kappa_i)$. Let $\beta_r(0)=A_r(0)=0$ solve

$$\beta_r'=\tfrac12\Xi^2\beta_r^2+\ell_r\beta_r+\tfrac12(r^2-r),\qquad A_r'=a^*\beta_r.$$

If this scalar solution is finite throughout $[0,T]$, it is nonnegative and the same generator argument applied to $e^{rX_t+A_r(T-t)+\beta_r(T-t)v_t}$ gives $\mathbb E e^{rX_T}\le M_r:=e^{A_r(T)+\beta_r(T)v_0}$. Finiteness is sufficient, not a necessary description of the model's full moment strip.

One simple all-time certificate is $\ell_r<0$ and $\ell_r^2\ge\Xi^2(r^2-r)$: the solution stays below the smaller nonnegative Riccati root. The benchmark below meets this condition at $r=4$.

Choose damping $\gamma>1$ and $p>1$ with this bound finite at $r=p\gamma$; set $q=p/(p-1)$. Conditional Gaussian integration gives $H=\mathbb E[e^{\gamma X_T}\mid B,Y]=e^{\gamma M+\gamma^2J/2}$, with $\mathbb E H^p\le M_{p\gamma}$.

Hölder's inequality therefore proves the speed-uniform vertical-contour envelope

$$|\mathbb E e^{(\gamma+iu)X_T}| \le M_{p\gamma}^{1/p}w_{q\eta u^2/2}(T,v_0)^{1/q} \le C_{\gamma,p}e^{-b_{\gamma,p}|u|},$$ $$C_{\gamma,p}=M_{p\gamma}^{1/p}C_T^{1/q},\qquad b_{\gamma,p}=b_T/\sqrt q.$$

For $\alpha=\gamma-1$ and $C(k)=\mathbb E(e^{X_T}-e^k)_+$, elementary integration of $e^{\alpha k}C(k)$ gives Fourier transform $\mathbb E e^{(\gamma+iu)X_T}/\{(\alpha+iu)(\alpha+1+iu)\}$. Since the denominator's modulus is at least $u^2$, the call inversion truncated at $U>0$ has error

$$|C(k)-C_U(k)|\le {e^{-\alpha k}C_{\gamma,p}e^{-b_{\gamma,p}U} \over\pi b_{\gamma,p}U^2}.$$

The affine Riccati/transform framework is classical; see Duffie, Pan and Singleton (2000), Proposition 1. The inequalities above are proved for the switching model directly; they do not assume that simultaneous coefficient switching preserves a single exponential-affine equality.

Perfect leverage is a genuine boundary

Take one regime, $a=\xi=\rho=1$, $\kappa=1/2$, and $v_0>0$. This strict-Feller variance stays positive, but $X_T=v_T-v_0-T$.

$$h=1-e^{-T/2},\qquad |\varphi_T(u)|={\exp\{-h u^2e^{-T/2}v_0/(1+h^2u^2)\}\over1+h^2u^2},$$ $$u^2|\varphi_T(u)|\longrightarrow h^{-2}e^{-e^{-T/2}v_0/h}>0.$$

Thus an exponential Fourier envelope need not hold at perfect correlation, even with strict positivity. At $T=1$, $v_0=.04$, the scaled modulus is $6.072946479$ at $u=3200$, approaching $6.072950074$.

Checks and scope

The certificate checks 48 heterogeneous Laplace and 32 leveraged deterministic-path bounds, independent scalar Riccati solves, and 240 statewise leveraged real/damped transform inequalities. It also checks the perfect-leverage formula against an independent affine solve.

For $a=(.2,.5)$, $\kappa=1.4$, $\xi=.8$, $\rho=-.6$, $T=1$, $v_0=.04$, $p=2$ and $\gamma=2$, the real and damped decay constants are $0.2$ and $0.141421356$. At $|k|\le.2$, the density/CDF/call tail budgets at $U=40$ are $(0.00127529853,0.0000318824633,0.0000137527801)$; at $U=80$ they are $(4.27814997\times10^{-7},5.34768746\times10^{-9},1.20112974\times10^{-8})$.

All bounds fix the model parameters and maturity while allowing every switching speed and initial regime prior; no uniformity as $T\downarrow0$, $a_*\downarrow0$, or $\eta\downarrow0$ is claimed. Damped inversion additionally requires the stated finite positive-Riccati certificate.

This controls Fourier truncation of the exact transform, not the global error of an inverse-speed expansion or the numerical integration error. The floating checks are regression tests, not outward-rounded interval enclosures. The separate global price-expansion target is recorded in issue 197.
