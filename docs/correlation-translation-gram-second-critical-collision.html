Rare-tail Gram modes collide again at the next scale

At tail separation of order $\varepsilon^2$, the contrast meets the following Gaussian mode.

Between the first two collision scales, a Gaussian mode precedes the coalescing-tail contrast. At the lower boundary they have equal size and form another explicit rank-two covariance block.

Second-collision theorem

Fix $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, $1\le r\le k-5$, a slope $\tau>0$, and weights $q_1,q_2>0$. Put $Q=q_1+q_2$, $\gamma_1=-q_2/Q$, and $\gamma_2=q_1/Q$.

Let $\varepsilon_n,\delta_n\downarrow0$ satisfy $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n^2\to\rho\in(0,\infty)$. Use the same Gaussian-plus-two-atom triangular law as in the preceding results, with

$$\tau_{\ell n}=\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n,\qquad A_{\ell n}=\exp(\tau_{\ell n}/\varepsilon_n),\qquad p_{\ell n}=q_\ell\varepsilon_n^{2r}A_{\ell n}^{-2c},$$

and let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$.

Write $\phi_a=(x_j^a/a!)_j$, $v=(e^{\tau x_j})_j$, and $\dot v=(x_je^{\tau x_j})_j$. Let $P_1$ project orthogonally away from

$$L_1=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+1},v\},$$

and put $a=P_1\phi_{r+2}$ and $b=P_1\dot v$.

For $G\sim N(0,1)$, center $Z_j=|G|^c(\log|G|)^j$ and define

$$h_{r+2}=\left\|Z_{r+2} -\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}\{Z_0,\ldots,Z_{r+1}\}}Z_{r+2}\right\|_{L^2}^2.$$

The second critical matrix is

$$M_\rho=h_{r+2}aa^\top+\frac{q_1q_2}{Q}\rho^2bb^\top.$$

It has rank two. If $\nu_1\ge\nu_2>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+3+j}(K_n)=\nu_j\varepsilon_n^{2r+4} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r+4}),\qquad j=1,2.$$

The intervening eigenvalue remains

$$\lambda_{r+3}(K_n)=h_{r+1}\|P_0\phi_{r+1}\|^2\varepsilon_n^{2r+2} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r+2}),$$

where $P_0$ projects away from $\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_r,v\}$. Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2r+4})$.

Proof

The preceding Schur complement removes $L_1$, including the order-$\varepsilon_n^{r+1}$ Gaussian feature. Gaussian Gram–Schmidt next leaves $\varepsilon_n^{r+2}\sqrt{h_{r+2}}\,a$.

Weighted centering of the two tail labels leaves $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n\sqrt{q_1q_2/Q}\,b$. Division by $\varepsilon_n^{r+2}$ makes this feature converge to $\rho\sqrt{q_1q_2/Q}\,b$.

The latent Gaussian and label-contrast features are orthogonal, so their covariance matrices add. The scaled Schur complement therefore converges to $M_\rho$, and finite-dimensional spectral perturbation gives the displayed eigenvalue limits.

The vectors $a$ and $b$ are independent. Otherwise a nonzero combination of $1,x,\ldots,x^{r+2},e^{\tau x},xe^{\tau x}$ would vanish at $k\ge r+5$ distinct nodes, contradicting the extended Chebyshev property.

Seven-output certificate

Take $c=\tau=q_1=1$, $q_2=2$, $x=(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3)$, $r=2$, $\rho=3/2$, and $\varepsilon_n=1/n$. The two predicted eighth-order coefficients are

$$(\nu_1,\nu_2)=(0.098789647380103,0.000079884359623).$$

At $n=2048$, the exact coefficient ratios are $(0.999478273903,1.002655309467)$. The 240-digit certificate also checks the earlier fourth- and sixth-order modes, monotone convergence, positivity, and full confluent rank.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for the displayed triangular laws and nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It gives a limiting block for fixed $\rho$, not a finite-$n$ error bound or a uniform statement over moving $\rho$.

The collision-staircase theorem extends the same block construction to every integer boundary $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n^m\to\rho\in(0,\infty)$.

The result concerns the output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies the classical column-confluence geometry, Kato supplies finite-dimensional self-adjoint perturbation theory, and Karlin and Studden supply the zero-count argument.
