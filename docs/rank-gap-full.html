Canceling the first rank error by gap design

In a stationary binary terminal-emission model, a shorter final test gap cancels the first-order marginal coverage error at every calibration rank.

Let $Y_0,\ldots,Y_N\in\{-1,+1\}$ be balanced stationary binary Markov states at deterministic observation times, with edge correlations $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_N\in[0,1]$. Given the states, the scores are independent with the same state-specific continuous CDFs at every time. Freeze the score map, use the first $N\ge2$ scores for calibration, and let $Q_k$ be their $k$th order statistic, $1\le k\le N$.

The final edge joins the last calibration score to the test score $S_N$. Write $F$ for the stationary mixture CDF, $U=F(S)$, $r(u)=\mathbb E[Y\mid U=u]$, $R(u)=\int_0^u r(v)\,dv$, and $b_{n,j}(u)={n\choose j}u^j(1-u)^{n-j}$, zero outside $0\le j\le n$. Since $U$ is uniform and the state is balanced, $R(0)=R(1)=0$.

The cancellation identity

The terminal-threshold calculation gives two first-order coefficients:

$$A_{CC}=\int_0^1 R(u)^2\{b_{N-2,k-1}(u)-b_{N-2,k-2}(u)\}\,du,\qquad A_{CT}=-\int_0^1r(u)R(u)b_{N-1,k-1}(u)\,du.$$

They are not independent: integration by parts, using $(R^2)'=2rR$ and the zero endpoint values, gives

$$A_{CT}=\frac12\int_0^1R^2 b'_{N-1,k-1}\,du =-\frac{N-1}{2}A_{CC}.$$

This holds for every rank and every pair of continuous emission laws; Gaussian emissions and symmetric score distributions are not required. At the iid-state baseline the rank event is symmetric in the calibration indices, so every calibration–calibration edge has coefficient $A_{CC}$ and the final edge has coefficient $A_{CT}$. Therefore, with $C(\rho)=\Pr(S_N\le Q_k)$,

$$C(\rho)=\frac{k}{N+1} +A_{CC}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\rho_j-\frac{N-1}{2}\rho_N\right\} +\mathcal E(\rho).$$

A finite remainder, not only an asymptotic claim

$$p_0=\frac{k}{N+1},\quad \omega_k=\min(p_0,1-p_0),\qquad |\mathcal E(\rho)|\le\omega_k\left\{\prod_{j=1}^N(1+\rho_j)-1-\sum_{j=1}^N\rho_j\right\}.$$

Indeed the state-path likelihood relative to independent balanced states is $\prod_{j=1}^N(1+\rho_jY_{j-1}Y_j)$. The empty subset gives iid rank coverage and singleton subsets give the displayed first-order term. Every nonempty edge subset has a leaf, so its product of state signs is a nonconstant, mean-zero, $\{-1,+1\}$-valued character under the independent-state law.

If $H(y)\in[0,1]$ is the rank-event probability conditional on the state path, then $\mathbb E_0H=p_0$ and each such character $\chi$ satisfies $|\mathbb E_0[H\chi]|=|\mathbb E_0[(1-H)\chi]|\le\min(p_0,1-p_0)=\omega_k$. Summing absolute coefficients over subsets of size at least two proves the bound. Thus the remainder estimate is finite-sample and uniform over the emission laws, but explicitly depends on $N$.

In particular, choose calibration correlations $\rho_1=\cdots=\rho_{N-1}=\rho$ and final correlation $\rho_N=2\rho$, with $0\le\rho\le1/2$. The linear term vanishes for every emission law and rank simultaneously, and

$$\left|C-\frac{k}{N+1}\right|\le \min\!\left\{1,\omega_k\big[(1+\rho)^{N-1}(1+2\rho)-1-(N+1)\rho\big]\right\} =O_N(\rho^2).$$

What this means for observation times

For a stationary telegraph chain flipping at rate $m$, put $a=2m$; an observation gap $h$ has correlation $e^{-ah}$. With equal calibration gaps $h$, the balanced final gap is $g=h-(\log2)/a$, positive when $h>(\log2)/a$. For fixed total span $T=(N-1)h+g$, this is

$$h=\frac{T+(\log2)/a}{N},\qquad g=\frac{T-(N-1)(\log2)/a}{N},\qquad T>\frac{(N-1)\log2}{a}.$$

Thus the second-order comparison need not spend more calendar time: calibration gaps become slightly longer and the final gap shorter. The rule requires the true decay rate, not an unchecked plug-in estimate. If instead $\rho_N=(2+e)\rho$ with fixed nonzero $e$, the remaining linear term is $-(N-1)e\rho A_{CC}/2$, so the second-order conclusion is generally lost.

This does not contradict mixing-based coverage bounds such as Oliveira et al. (2024): those bounds control dependence without using this signed, model-specific cancellation. Here the claim is stationary marginal rank coverage for terminal emissions at fixed $N$, not calibration-conditional PAC coverage, feature-conditional coverage or an anytime result. It neither treats overlapping path windows nor proves a globally optimal sampling design.

Numerical certificate

The certificate checks 44 regular-gap and 18 irregular-gap exact rational coverage polynomials for separated uniform emissions, plus 186 finite remainder inequalities. It checks the integration-by-parts identity and zero derivative in 38 Gaussian cases, including unequal emission variances, and independently integrates the test density to audit the Markov count recursion.

For $S_j=Y_j+0.5Z_j$, $N=k=9$, the coefficients are $A_{CC}=-0.0027134858$ and $A_{CT}=0.0108539431$; equal gaps leave the coefficient $-0.0108539431$. At balanced calibration correlation $\rho=0.025$, the same-total-span equal-gap error is $-0.0003037256$, while the balanced error is $+0.0000018428$. The last measured balanced-error order is $2.060318$; the explicit finite bound is conservative, not fitted to these values.
