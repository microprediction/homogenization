A logarithmic layer resolves rational spectral resonance

A power moving by $1/\log(1/\varepsilon)$ changes the collision coefficient, not the collision order.

The fixed-power theorems are not uniform near a rational exponent. The exact transition has logarithmic width: a finite scaled displacement simply renormalizes the rational collision ratio.

Moving-power resonance theorem

Use the fixed $s$-tail triangular law, positive weights, distinct centered offsets, and notation of the rational spectral-merge theorem. Fix coprime $p,q\ge1$, put $\alpha_0=p/q$, $L_n=\log(1/\varepsilon_n)$, and suppose

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n^{\alpha_n}}\longrightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty), \qquad L_n(\alpha_n-\alpha_0)\longrightarrow\theta\in\mathbb R.$$

Keep $s,p,q,k,r,c,\tau$, weights, offsets, nodes, and the positive maturity fixed, with $k\ge r+\lfloor\alpha_0(s-1)\rfloor+s+1$. Then every conclusion of the rational theorem holds with

$$\widehat\rho=\rho e^{-\theta}$$

in place of $\rho$. In particular, the event order and indices are those of the rational power $\alpha_0$, including every collision at $(a,j)=(p\ell,q\ell)$.

At such a collision, let $u_\ell,b_\ell$ be the residual directions from the rational theorem. The two leading coefficients are the positive eigenvalues of

$$M_\ell(\theta)=h_{r+p\ell}u_\ell u_\ell^\top +\eta_{q\ell}(\rho e^{-\theta})^{2q\ell}b_\ell b_\ell^\top.$$

Every separated tail coefficient is changed in the same way, from $\eta_j\rho^{2j}\|b_j\|^2$ to $\eta_j(\rho e^{-\theta})^{2j}\|b_j\|^2$. The Gaussian coefficients are unchanged.

Proof and the sharp scale

The reduction is exact at the normalization level:

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n^{\alpha_0}} =\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n^{\alpha_n}} \exp\{-L_n(\alpha_n-\alpha_0)\} \longrightarrow\rho e^{-\theta}.$$

The rational theorem now applies directly with effective ratio $\widehat\rho$. Thus the layer has width $1/L_n$: a smaller exponent displacement is invisible at leading order, while a displacement of this size produces the finite factor $e^{-\theta}$.

This argument is sequencewise for finite $\theta$. It does not assert a uniform remainder over unbounded $\theta$, and it does not identify the outer crossover when $L_n(\alpha_n-\alpha_0)$ diverges.

Three-tail certificate

Take $\alpha_0=3/2$, $s=3$, $k=8$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, the weights, offsets, and nodes of the rational certificate, and set $\rho=3/2$, $\theta=\log(3/2)$, and $\alpha_n=3/2+\theta/\log(1/\varepsilon_n)$. Then $\widehat\rho=1$ exactly.

The predicted coefficients are $(0.395991167289368,7.880500958110479,0.009192895225548,0.007697443444958,0.000016326690235)$. At $n=8192$, $\alpha_n=1.544997115440$ and the five normalized ratios are $(1.013673056823,0.986223189321,1.000444848035,0.999983882741,1.001043373353)$.

The 260-digit calculation also checks the effective-ratio identity at every refinement, exact rare weights, positivity, convergence toward all five coefficients, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

The result gives the same sequencewise little-$o$ expansions as the rational theorem, not finite-$n$ error bounds. It is not uniform over moving dimensions, tail counts, nodes, offsets, maturities, boundary ratios, or unbounded scaled displacement.

This concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram at one fixed maturity. It asserts neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
