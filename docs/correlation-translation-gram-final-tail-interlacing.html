A Gaussian mode interlaces the final tail derivative

Below the last collision, the next Gaussian feature separates between two tail scales.

At the critical boundary, the final tail derivative and next Gaussian feature share one scale. In the adjacent open window, the Gaussian mode moves ahead of that last derivative but remains behind every earlier tail mode.

Final-tail interlacing theorem

Use the $s$-tail triangular law, weights, centered distinct offsets, and notation of the multi-tail confluence theorem. Assume $s\ge3$, $1\le r\le k-s-2$, and

$$\frac{\delta_n^{s-1}}{\varepsilon_n}\longrightarrow0, \qquad \frac{\varepsilon_n}{\delta_n^{s-2}}\longrightarrow0.$$

Equivalently, $\delta_n^{s-1}\ll\varepsilon_n\ll\delta_n^{s-2}$.

For $1\le j\le s-2$, the earlier tail modes retain their ladder coefficients:

$$\lambda_{r+2+j}(K_n)= \eta_j\|R_jv_j\|_2^2\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2j} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2j}).$$

Let $P_G$ project orthogonally away from

$$L_G=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_r,v_0,\ldots,v_{s-2}\},$$

and set $g=P_G\phi_{r+1}$. If $h_{r+1}$ is the residual variance of $Z_{r+1}=|G|^c(\log|G|)^{r+1}$ after projection onto $Z_0,\ldots,Z_r$, then

$$\lambda_{r+s+1}(K_n)= h_{r+1}\|g\|_2^2\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)}).$$

Let $P_T$ project away from $L_T=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+1},v_0,\ldots,v_{s-2}\}$ and set $b=P_Tv_{s-1}$. The final tail mode is

$$\lambda_{r+s+2}(K_n)= \eta_{s-1}\|b\|_2^2\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2(s-1)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2(s-1)}).$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2(s-1)})$.

Why the order separates

The first condition makes the final tail feature smaller than the Gaussian feature: $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^{s-1}\ll\varepsilon_n^{r+1}$. The second makes every earlier tail feature larger.

After the earlier Schur complements, Gaussian Gram–Schmidt leaves $\varepsilon_n^{r+1}\sqrt{h_{r+1}}\,g$. Removing that direction leaves the final weighted label coordinate $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^{s-1}\sqrt{\eta_{s-1}}\,b$.

Both vectors are nonzero by the extended-Chebyshev independence of the corresponding polynomial/exponential evaluation systems. The next Gaussian feature is smaller because $\varepsilon_n^2/\delta_n^{s-1}\to0$ under the two displayed assumptions.

Three-tail certificate

Take $s=3$, $k=6$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3)$, centered offsets $(-4/3,-1/3,2/3)$, nodes $(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2)$, and $\delta_n=\varepsilon_n^{2/3}$.

The first-tail, interlaced-Gaussian, and last-tail coefficients are

$$(3.038546555413267,\ 0.003619140256121,\ 0.001322737315070).$$

At $n=262144$, their exact normalized ratios are $(0.999482786612,1.003103317250,0.996063092098)$. The 260-digit certificate also checks the primary block, full confluent rank, monotone convergence, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for fixed $s,k,r,q,$ and $\gamma$ at one fixed positive maturity. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not a finite-$n$ bound or uniformity over moving dimensions, offsets, or separation windows.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
