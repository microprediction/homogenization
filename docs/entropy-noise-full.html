Sharp entropy recovery under quote noise

Even the entire call curve, known within a uniform absolute error, leaves an unavoidable logarithmic loss.

Back to cycle direction · Papers · certificate.

The sharp limit

Fix $M>1$ and let $\mathcal P_M$ contain all laws on $[0,\infty)$ with $\mathbb ES=1$ and $\mathbb ES^2\le M$. The target is $H(P)=\mathbb E_P[S\log S]$, with $0\log0=0$, and the call curve is $C_P(K)=\mathbb E_P(S-K)_+$.

For a prescribed finite strike set $\mathcal K$, observe $y_K=C_P(K)+e_K$ with $|e_K|\le\eta$. Define the deterministic minimax absolute error

$$R_M^{\mathrm{fin}}(\eta)=\inf_{\substack{\mathcal K\ \mathrm{finite}\\\widehat H}} \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_M}\ \sup_{|e_K|\le\eta} |\widehat H((C_P(K)+e_K)_{K\in\mathcal K})-H(P)|.$$

The design and estimator may depend on $M,\eta$, but not on the unknown law. Define $R_M^{\mathrm{all}}$ by allowing observation of a function $y(K)$ for every $K\ge0$, with $\sup_K|y(K)-C_P(K)|\le\eta$.

$$\boxed{\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\frac{R_M^{\mathrm{all}}(\eta)}{\eta\log(1/\eta)} =\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\frac{R_M^{\mathrm{fin}}(\eta)}{\eta\log(1/\eta)}=2.}$$

This is a bounded-adversarial-error result, not a Gaussian-noise, sampling or coverage theorem. The limit holds for each fixed $M>1$, not uniformly as $M\downarrow1$.

It sharpens the quote-error question left by the finite strips and noiseless fixed-grid rate: the logarithmic amplification is not merely an artifact of a chosen strip.

A finite-strip upper bound

For $0<\eta<1$, put $L=\eta$, $U=\eta^{-1}$ and $N=\lceil\log(U/L)/\log(1+\sqrt{8\eta})\rceil$, and set $h=\log(U/L)/N$, $K_j=Le^{jh}$. There are $N+1$ quotes; this simple construction is not claimed quote-count optimal.

Interpolate $f(s)=s\log s-s+1$ on these knots and extend by the endpoint tangent lines outside $[L,U]$. The slope-jump weights are $w_0=d_h$, $w_j=h$ for $0<j<N$, $w_N=h-d_h$, where $d_h=h/(1-e^{-h})-1$.

$$\widehat H(y)=1-L+\log L+\sum_{j=0}^Nw_j y_{K_j}+\frac{M\eta}{4},\qquad |\widehat H(y)-H(P)|\le2\eta\log(1/\eta)+(1+M/4)\eta.$$

Indeed the weights are positive and sum to $\log(U/L)$, so the noise contributes at most $2\eta\log(1/\eta)$. On a cell $[a,b]$, $f''\le1/a$ bounds the chord error by $(b-a)^2/(8a)\le\eta s$.

Below $L$, the tangent deficit is at most $L$, including an atom at zero; above $U$ it is at most $s^2/(2U)$. Thus each law's signed expected interpolation error lies in $[-\eta(1+M/2),\eta]$, and adding $M\eta/4$ centers that interval.

The affine term follows from the lower tangent $f(L)+\log L(s-L)$ and the known mean one. This proves the finite-design upper bound, hence also the whole-curve bound, in exact arithmetic.

An indistinguishable pair using both tails

Set $\lambda=(M-1)/(4M)$, $c=(M-1)/8$, and $\bar\delta=\min(1/4,\lambda/2,\sqrt{c\lambda/2})$. For $0<\delta\le\bar\delta$, define

$$q=\frac{2\delta^2}{c},\quad U_*=\frac c\delta,\quad \epsilon=\frac\delta\lambda,\quad d=\frac{1-2\delta}{1-\lambda-q},$$ $$R=\lambda\delta_0+q\delta_{U_*}+(1-\lambda-q)\delta_d,\qquad P=\lambda\delta_\epsilon+q\delta_{U_*/2}+(1-\lambda-q)\delta_d.$$

Here $\delta_x$ denotes a point mass at $x$. Both laws have mean one and nonnegative masses, since $q\le\lambda<1/4$.

$$\mathbb E_R S^2=2c+\frac{(1-2\delta)^2}{1-\lambda-q} \le1+\frac34(M-1)<M,\qquad \mathbb E_P S^2-\mathbb E_R S^2=\frac{\delta^2}{\lambda}-\frac{3c}{2}\le-c.$$

For the first bound use $q\le\lambda$, $(1-2\delta)^2\le1$, and $1/(1-2\lambda)=1+(M-1)/(M+1)\le1+(M-1)/2$. Both laws therefore belong to $\mathcal P_M$.

Because $\epsilon\le U_*/2$, their call difference is the nonnegative trapezoid

$$C_R(K)-C_P(K)= \begin{cases}\lambda K,&0\le K\le\epsilon,\\ \delta,&\epsilon\le K\le U_*/2,\\ q(U_*-K),&U_*/2\le K\le U_*,\\0,&K\ge U_*. \end{cases}$$ $$\|C_R-C_P\|_\infty=\delta,\qquad H(R)-H(P)=\delta\log\!\frac{2\lambda c}{\delta^2}.$$

The entropy identity follows directly from the atoms: the common $d$ term cancels, the lower transfer adds mean $\delta$, and the upper transfer removes the same mean. The atom at zero and the remote upper tail each contribute one logarithmic term.

For $\delta=2\eta\le\bar\delta$, the entire midpoint curve $y=(C_R+C_P)/2$ is within $\eta$ of either law. It is itself an arbitrage-free call curve of a law in $\mathcal P_M$, so projecting observations onto valid call curves cannot remove the obstruction.

$$R_M^{\mathrm{all}}(\eta)\ge\frac{H(R)-H(P)}2 =2\eta\log(1/\eta)+\eta\log(\lambda c/2).$$

Every estimator sees identical data and must err by at least half the target gap on one law. Since $R_M^{\mathrm{all}}\le R_M^{\mathrm{fin}}$, this lower bound and the finite-strip upper bound prove both limits, with explicit additive $O_M(\eta)$ bounds.

Interpretation and certificate

The constant two is sharp for this moment class and uniform absolute quote-error norm, not necessarily for a restricted stochastic-volatility family, relative quote errors, or quotes confined to a fixed market range. These atomic laws are not asserted to arise as a switched-CIR reversal pair; sharp CIR crossing counts remain separate.

For $M=1$, the only law is $S=1$ almost surely and the recovery error is zero, so the strict condition $M>1$ matters. Errors in weights, summation, the moment bound and forward normalization require separate budgets.

For $M=2$, $\eta=10^{-6}$, the lower and upper bound formulas evaluate to approximately $2.2778990852\times10^{-5}$ and $2.91310211159\times10^{-5}$ respectively, in normalized payoff units. These are evaluations of the proved formulas, not a fitted rate or an outward-rounded numerical enclosure.

The certificate checks rational masses, means, second moments and the complete piecewise-linear call gap exactly, and independently evaluates the entropy identity and noisy-strip bounds. It also tests the common midpoint observation and reports the two asymptotic coefficients; the proof, not a fitted slope, supplies the continuum and minimax conclusions.

Classical vanilla replication is reviewed in Bossu, Carr and Papanicolaou (2020), section 2.1. Our entropy target is a stock-weighted moment, not differential entropy or the maximum-entropy density fitting problem studied by Orozco Rodriguez and Santosa (2012); no claim of priority for general inverse-problem instability is made.
