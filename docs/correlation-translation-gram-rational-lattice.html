Rational powers merge the full multi-tail spectrum

For $\delta\asymp\varepsilon^{p/q}$, arithmetic alone decides which tail and Gaussian modes collide.

The integer collision lattice is only the $q=1$ slice. For every positive rational power, one sorted merge gives every separated mode, every collision doublet, its eigenvalue index, and the complete dimension threshold.

Rational spectral-merge theorem

Use the fixed $s$-tail triangular law, positive weights, distinct centered offsets, and notation of the multi-tail confluence theorem. Fix coprime integers $p,q\ge1$, put $\alpha=p/q$ and $A=\lfloor\alpha(s-1)\rfloor$, assume $k\ge r+A+s+1$, and let

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n^\alpha}\longrightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty).$$

The first $r$ Gaussian modes and the two primary modes are unchanged.

For a Gaussian order $1\le a\le A$ that is not a collision, set $J_a=\lceil aq/p\rceil-1$, let $P_a$ project away from

$$\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+a-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{J_a}\},$$

and put $g_a=P_a\phi_{r+a}$. Then

$$\lambda_{r+a+J_a+2}(K_n)=h_{r+a}\|g_a\|^2\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)}).$$

For a tail derivative $1\le j\le s-1$ that is not a collision, put $A_j=\lfloor\alpha j\rfloor$, let $R_j$ project away from

$$\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+A_j},v_0,\ldots,v_{j-1}\},$$

and set $b_j=R_jv_j$. Its mode is

$$\lambda_{r+A_j+j+2}(K_n)=\eta_j\rho^{2j}\|b_j\|^2 \varepsilon_n^{2(r+\alpha j)}+o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+\alpha j)}).$$

Collisions occur exactly at $a=p\ell$, $j=q\ell$, with $1\le q\ell\le s-1$. Let $Q_\ell$ project away from

$$\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+p\ell-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{q\ell-1}\},$$

put $u_\ell=Q_\ell\phi_{r+p\ell}$ and $b_\ell=Q_\ell v_{q\ell}$, and define

$$M_\ell=h_{r+p\ell}u_\ell u_\ell^\top+eta_{q\ell}\rho^{2q\ell}b_\ell b_\ell^\top.$$

If $\nu_{\ell,1}\ge\nu_{\ell,2}>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+(p+q)\ell+t}(K_n)=\nu_{\ell,t}\varepsilon_n^{2(r+p\ell)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+p\ell)}),\qquad t=1,2.$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+\alpha(s-1))})$.

Proof: sort, residualize, count

Gaussian order $a$ has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^{r+a}$; tail derivative $j$ has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^j\sim\rho^j\varepsilon_n^{r+\alpha j}$. Thus equality means $aq=pj$, hence $a=p\ell$ and $j=q\ell$ because $p,q$ are coprime.

A Gaussian order $a$ has exactly $J_a$ positive tail derivatives before it; a noncollision tail derivative $j$ has exactly $A_j$ positive Gaussian orders before it. Successive Schur complements therefore give the stated projectors and indices. At equality the two independent residual directions survive together as $M_\ell$.

The final evaluation system consists of polynomials through degree $r+A$ and exponential derivatives through $x^{s-1}e^{\tau x}$: exactly $r+A+s+1$ functions. Their extended-Chebyshev property makes every residual nonzero and every collision block rank two, while finite-dimensional spectral perturbation gives the displayed little-$o$ expansions.

Three-tail, three-halves certificate

Take $p/q=3/2$, $s=3$, $k=8$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3)$, centered offsets $(-4/3,-1/3,2/3)$, nodes $(-4,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3)$, $\rho=5/4$, and $\delta_n=\rho\varepsilon_n^{3/2}$. The sorted events are Gaussian $a=1$, tail $j=1$, Gaussian $a=2$, then the collision $(a,j)=(3,2)$.

The predicted separated coefficients are $0.395991167289368$, $12.313282747047625$, and $0.009192895225548$; the collision pair is $(0.017257550126017,0.000017778927881)$.

At $n=8192$, their five normalized ratios in spectral order are $(1.021370098696,0.978789340366,1.000445519559,0.999906318169,1.001118746197)$.

The 260-digit certificate checks centered offsets, exact rare weights, both primary modes, full confluent rank, positivity, refinement toward all five coefficients, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is sequencewise for fixed $s,p,q,k,r,c,\tau$, weights, offsets, nodes, and $\rho$ at one fixed positive maturity. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not finite-$n$ error bounds or uniformity over moving powers, dimensions, tail counts, nodes, offsets, or boundary ratios.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It asserts neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
