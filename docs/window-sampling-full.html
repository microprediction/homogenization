Confidence budgets from one stationary trajectory

Ordered window products from one trajectory are dependent, even when their observation intervals do not overlap. An independently justified mixing bound gives simultaneous finite-sample covariance error budgets, which can then feed a deterministic generator-error certificate.

Observation model and theorem

Let $Y$ be a stationary finite irreducible row-generator chain with known stationary law $\pi$. Fix $T>0$, $N\ge1$, $r\ge1$, $0<\delta<1$, and a known feature matrix $U\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}$ with $\pi U=0$ before observing the data. Observe the path occupations exactly over $[0,3NT]$ and put

$$X_{kj}=\int_{(3k+j)T}^{(3k+j+1)T}U^Te_{Y_t}dt,\quad \widehat J_i=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X_{k0}X_{k,i+1}^T,\quad i=0,1.$$

The expectation $J_i=\mathbb E\widehat J_i$ is the ordered adjacent or one-gap window covariance, since the feature means are known to vanish. Do not subtract fitted sample means or symmetrize these products.

Assume a supplied deterministic upper bound $0\le\alpha<1$ on the Dobrushin coefficient

$$\frac12\max_{x,y}\sum_z|P_{xz}-P_{yz}|\le\alpha,\qquad P=e^{3TQ}.$$

Write $M_a=\max_x|U_{xa}|$ and define $c_0=0$, $c_{m+1}=1+\alpha c_m$, and

$$H_N=c_N^2+\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}(1+c_m)^2,\qquad \eta=\frac{T^2\sum_{a=1}^rM_a^2}{N}\sqrt{2H_N\log\frac{4r^2}{\delta}}.$$
$$\boxed{\Pr\!\left(\|\widehat J_0-J_0\|_F\le\eta, \ \|\widehat J_1-J_1\|_F\le\eta\right)\ge1-\delta.}$$

For fixed design and confidence level, these budgets are $O(N^{-1/2})$, since $H_N\le(1-\alpha)^{-2}+N\{1+(1-\alpha)^{-1}\}^2$. No reversibility or diagonalizability is needed.

Proof for bounded path-block rewards

Apply the same fixed scalar path functional to every block, giving rewards $Z_k$ bounded by $B$ in absolute value. Let $S_k=Y_{3kT}$, $g(x)=\mathbb E[Z_k\mid S_k=x]$, and $V_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}P^jg$, with $V_0=0$.

The Markov property and oscillation contraction give $\operatorname{osc}(V_m)\le2Bc_m$. Reveal $S_0$ first and then each complete path block; the Doob martingale for $\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}Z_k$ has initial conditional range at most $2Bc_N$.

When revealing block $k$, the only random part of the updated conditional expectation is $Z_k+V_{N-k-1}(S_{k+1})$. Its conditional range is at most $2B(1+c_{N-k-1})$, including dependence between the reward and its endpoint.

The conditional Hoeffding lemma gives a centered-increment moment bound $\exp(\lambda^2w^2/8)$ for an interval of length $w$. Multiplying these bounds, including the initial-state increment, gives

$$\mathbb E\exp\!\left\{\lambda\left(\sum_kZ_k-\mathbb E\sum_kZ_k\right)\right\} \le\exp(\lambda^2B^2H_N/2),$$ $$\Pr\!\left(\left|N^{-1}\sum_kZ_k-\mathbb EZ_0\right|\ge t\right) \le2\exp\{-N^2t^2/(2B^2H_N)\}.$$

Stationarity supplies the common mean in the last line. Apply it to each ordered product entry with $B=T^2M_aM_b$, and take a union bound over both matrices and all $r^2$ entries. Summing the squared entry budgets proves the displayed Frobenius bound; a zero feature column gives zero entries exactly.

Where a usable mixing bound comes from

The unknown $Q$ must not be replaced by its fitted estimate inside the confidence calculation without a separate justification. One sufficient known model-class restriction is $Q_{xy}\ge\rho>0$ for every $x\ne y$, giving $\alpha=e^{-3n\rho T}$.

Indeed, decompose $Q=\rho(\mathbf1\mathbf1^T-nI)+R$, where $R$ is a row generator. At independent rate $n\rho$, reset the state to a uniform label; coupling two chains at the first shared reset and then using common randomness proves the contraction bound. The abstract theorem also permits sparse chains when another certified contraction bound is available.

Using the budgets without a selection-coverage mistake

For complete weighted-orthonormal features, the exact model obeys $L=-U^TD_\pi QU$, $J_0=L^{-2}(I-e^{-TL})^2$, and $J_0^{-1}J_1=e^{-TL}$. The residual-aware deterministic reconstruction bound accepts $\eta_0=\eta_1=\eta$ from this theorem.

On the single simultaneous event above, every candidate satisfying that deterministic test has its claimed error bound, even if the candidate is chosen using the same data. Thus the probability of reporting an incorrect certified radius is at most $\delta$, allowing abstention when the test fails. This is not a claim of $1-\delta$ coverage conditional on the procedure reporting a radius.

The observation design, features and mixing bound must remain fixed or have separately justified simultaneous control. The result does not cover an estimated stationary law, hidden feature embedding, discretization or measurement error, data-dependent stopping, or choosing the window length after inspecting the data.

A design illustration and checks

For the symmetric two-state chain with flip rate $1/2$, use $U=(1,-1)^T$ and $T=\log2$. Then $\alpha=1/8$; at $N=10^6$ and $\delta=0.05$, the budget is $\eta=0.003047870$.

At the population input values $J_0=1/4$, $J_1=1/8$ and candidate $L=1$, the reconstruction test with this positive budget gives radius $0.045377255$. This is a design illustration at population values, not a claim that the same radius holds for every realized sample.

The certificate checks $96$ conditional-range inequalities exactly on dependent three-state block rewards, plus exact geometric-sum identities and finite-law tail and moment-generating-function comparisons. It also checks the continuous-time ordered-product moments and reset contraction bound in $60$ cases. Floating-point checks support the implementation, not the probability theorem or interval certification.

Classical concentration machinery

Hoeffding bounds for uniformly ergodic Markov chains and their martingale proofs are classical; see Glynn and Ormoneit (2002), especially Section 2. The proof here treats path-block rewards, keeps their endpoint dependence and the initial-state increment, and specializes the resulting budgets to ordered covariance recovery.
