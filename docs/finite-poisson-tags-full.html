Finite Poisson tags cannot identify arbitrary clocks

A local population alias, even with known clock moments and complete endpoint labels.

Setup and result

Let $P=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/2$ and $Q_c=c(P-I)$, $c>0$, on two known labeled states, with the same uniform starting law. Draw $T>0$ independently of the whole chain and an independent Poisson process $N$ of known physical-time rate $s>0$. Retain only $(Y_0,Y_T,J)$, where $J=\min(N_T,M)$ for a fixed finite integer $M\ge1$.

Fix an integer $r\ge0$, a baseline speed $c_0\ne s$, and any baseline density $f_0$ positive almost everywhere on an open interval inside $(0,\infty)$, with finite raw moments through $r$. For every $c$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $c_0$, there is a positive density $f_c$ giving exactly the same full endpoint/tag law and the same raw moments through $r$ as $(c_0,f_0)$. Moreover $f_c=f_0$ outside a fixed compact interval and $\|f_c/f_0-1\|_\infty=O(|c-c_0|)$ on its support.

Thus no finite cap universally identifies even the speed of a reversible two-state chain when the clock family is unrestricted. The alias exists arbitrarily close to every Gamma density at $c_0\ne s$, not merely between exotic atomic clocks. Positivity means that the density ratio stays positive wherever $f_0$ is positive; no new support is required.

For $r\ge1$ the mean duration is preserved too, so this is not just an unknown choice of physical time unit.

What the record law fixes

Write $w_j(t)=e^{-st}(st)^j/j!$ for $j<M$, and $w_M(t)=1-\sum_{j<M}w_j(t)$. Since $e^{tQ_c}=P+e^{-ct}(I-P)$, the joint subprobability matrix for tag $j$ is

$$R_j=p_jP+v_j(I-P),\qquad p_j=\int w_j(t)f(t)\,dt,\quad v_j=\int e^{-ct}w_j(t)f(t)\,dt.$$

Hence the entire law, including the capped tail, is fixed by integrals against the following vector $\Phi_c$: normalization and the optional known moments are included explicitly.

$$\Phi_c(t)=\big(1,t,\ldots,t^r;\ (t^je^{-st})_{j=0}^{M-1};\ e^{-ct}; (t^je^{-(s+c)t})_{j=0}^{M-1}\big)^\top.$$

The vector has $d=r+2M+2$ components. Matching it preserves the lower-tag values $p_j,v_j$ and also the two tail values, because $\sum p_j=1$ and $\sum v_j=\int e^{-ct}f(t)dt$. No within-tag normalization or conditional independence of $T$ and $J$ is assumed.

A constructive local proof

The components of $\Phi_{c_0}$ are linearly independent on every open interval. Indeed they are exponential polynomials with distinct decay rates $0,s,c_0,s+c_0$; an identity on an interval extends analytically, and letting $t\to\infty$ successively removes the polynomial at each ordered decay rate. The exclusion $c_0=s$ is necessary for this argument and for the conclusion below.

Choose a bounded nonnegative smooth bump $b$ with compact support in the positive-density interval and $b>0$ on a smaller interval. Put $u=\Phi_{c_0}$ and define

$$A(c)=\int \Phi_c(t)u(t)^\top b(t)f_0(t)\,dt,\quad m(c)=\int\Phi_c(t)f_0(t)\,dt,\quad \theta(c)=A(c)^{-1}\{m(c_0)-m(c)\}.$$

The matrix $A(c_0)$ is a positive definite Gram matrix: a zero quadratic form would make a nonzero exponential polynomial vanish on an interval. All integrals are finite and continuously differentiable near $c_0$; the polynomial moments are constant in $c$, while derivatives of the decaying terms are bounded on the positive axis. Consequently $A(c)$ remains invertible locally and $\theta(c)=O(|c-c_0|)$.

$$f_c(t)=f_0(t)\{1+b(t)u(t)^\top\theta(c)\},\qquad \int\Phi_c(t)f_c(t)\,dt=m(c_0).$$

The compactly supported factor $bu$ is bounded, so for sufficiently small $|c-c_0|$ its relative perturbation is less than one. This proves positivity, unit mass, equality of the required integrals and the stated density-ratio bound. If $f_0$ is smooth, the same construction gives smooth $f_c$.

A quantitative sufficient test uses $D=\sup_t\|b(t)u(t)\|_2$ and $\eta(c)=\|A(c_0)^{-1}\{A(c)-A(c_0)\}\|_2$. If $\eta(c)<1$, the Neumann bound gives

$$\left\|\frac{f_c}{f_0}-1\right\|_\infty\le \rho(c):=\frac{D\|A(c_0)^{-1}\|_2\|m(c_0)-m(c)\|_2}{1-\eta(c)}.$$

Thus $\rho(c)<1$ certifies positivity and $\|f_c-f_0\|_{\rm TV}\le\rho(c)/2$. Here the density ratio is interpreted on $\{f_0>0\}$; $\rho(c)=O(|c-c_0|)$ for fixed baseline, cap, moments and bump.

For $c\ne c_0$, the clock really changes: keeping $f_0$ would change $\int e^{-ct}f_0(t)dt$, which is strictly decreasing for $T>0$. Starting from a Gamma density, $f_c$ cannot be another Gamma density, since it agrees with $f_0$ on an entire tail but is not equal everywhere. The construction therefore does not contradict inversion within a stipulated Gamma family.

The rate collision and the scope

At $c_0=s$ the conclusion fails for the speed. The observed equality $\sum_jv_j=p_0$ says $L(c)=L(s)$ for the candidate clock's Laplace transform $L$. Strict decrease of $L$ forces $c=s$, whatever the positive clock law.

This is one independently sampled duration per record, with a fresh auxiliary counter, not a record of every tick, intermediate states or shared durations across multiple transitions. It is an exact population ambiguity, not a claim about finite-sample error or an alias for every possible finite tag design. The finite-cap proof does not cover an infinite collection of known clock moments.

For the fixed centered feature $h=(1,-1)^\top$, the one-sided symmetric Green–Kubo value is $\mathcal K_c(h,h)=\int_0^\infty e^{-ct}dt=1/c$, so the aliased speeds have different strengths but the same fixed-feature rank. No regime-count statement is inferred from integrating different maturity loadings.

Finite generalized-moment constraints are classical; Winkler (1988) studies the extreme points of such moment sets. Only the publisher's abstract and bibliographic metadata were checked here; the local moving-speed construction is proved above rather than imported from that paper.

An uncapped count recovers timestamp information in population

Now retain $N_T$ itself, with no cap, for any finite generator $Q$ on a complete known labeled space and any independent clock law $\mu$ on $(0,\infty)$. The initial law is independent of the clock and has full support; the known tick rate is $s>0$. No density, duration-moment assumption, irreducibility or diagonalizability is needed.

The population law of $(Y_0,Y_T,N_T)$ identifies exactly the same clock and semigroup information as $(Y_0,Y_T,T)$. This means equality of the two count-record laws is equivalent to equality of the two timestamp-record laws, not that an individual count reveals its duration. To prove it, divide by the observed initial probabilities and form the joint count matrices

$$R_k=\int e^{-st}\frac{(st)^k}{k!}e^{tQ}\,\mu(dt),\qquad H(z)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty z^kR_k =\int e^{-s(1-z)t}e^{tQ}\,\mu(dt),\quad 0<z<1.$$

Knowing $s$ is essential for recovery in physical time. If $s$ is unknown, put $U=sT$, $G=Q/s$ and let $\nu$ be the law of $U$; then

$$H(z)=\int e^{-(1-z)u}e^{uG}\,\nu(du).$$

The record identifies $\nu$ and its matrix measures $(e^{uG})_{ij}\nu(du)$; under the same grid-free support condition below it identifies $G$. It cannot separate the physical scale: for every $a>0$, replacing $(s,Q,T)$ by $(as,aQ,T/a)$ leaves the entire labeled count law unchanged. Thus, under grid-free support, an unknown tick rate identifies exactly the dimensionless pair $(Q/s,\operatorname{Law}(sT))$, not $s$, $Q$ and the duration law separately; on a grid, the generator alias below remains.

Row sums of $H(z)$ give the clock's Laplace transform on $(0,s)$. Uniqueness of that transform identifies $\mu$; entrywise uniqueness then identifies every finite measure $(e^{tQ})_{ij}\mu(dt)$. These are precisely the timestamp joint measures, while the reverse implication follows by adding the known conditional Poisson counter.

For completeness, a finite signed measure with Laplace transform zero on an open positive interval has transform zero on the entire right half-plane by analyticity. Its pushforward under $x=e^{-t}$ therefore has every nonnegative integer moment zero, including mass by dominated convergence as the Laplace argument decreases to zero. Polynomial density in $C[0,1]$ forces that measure to vanish, proving the uniqueness used here without any raw duration moments.

If $\operatorname{supp}\mu$ is not contained in $h\mathbb Z$ for any $h>0$, the uncapped law identifies $Q$ as well. Indeed continuity extends equality of candidate semigroups to the support, and $\{t\in\mathbb R:e^{tQ}=e^{tG}\}$ is a closed additive subgroup: equality is preserved under differences by matrix inversion. A proper such subgroup is a grid or $\{0\}$; the stated support excludes both, so differentiation at zero gives $Q=G$.

The grid exception is genuine, even for an uncapped counter. On three states put $P_3=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3$, $E_3=I-P_3$ and

$$J_3=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q_0=-\frac{4\pi}hE_3,\quad Q_1=Q_0+\frac{2\pi}hJ_3.$$

Both have strictly positive off-diagonal rates and $e^{khQ_0}=e^{khQ_1}$ for every integer $k$, since $J_3^2=-E_3$ and its rotation completes a full turn at $h$. Every clock supported on $h\mathbb Z$ consequently gives identical timestamp and uncapped-count laws for this pair. This is failure of universal identification over classes including three states, not ambiguity at every grid-sampled generator or a two-state converse.

Count-mixture identification is classical; Teicher (1961) gives an identification theorem for one-parameter additively closed families. Its publisher abstract and metadata were checked, not the full text; the matrix-measure extension and grid argument above are self-contained.

Equivalence concerns the entire infinite count distribution, not finitely many observed count frequencies or stable analytic continuation. Even for the reset family with one fixed known clock, the full uncapped record laws satisfy $\|\mathcal L(Q_c)-\mathcal L(Q_{2c})\|_{\rm TV}=\{L(c)-L(2c)\}/2\to0$ while $\|Q_c-Q_{2c}\|_\infty=c$.

For each count the contrast coefficient decreases with $c$; summing its nonnegative differences gives the displayed identity. Thus neither uncapping nor population identification supplies a uniform inverse-continuity or finite-sample guarantee.

The certificate constructs compact density tilts around a Gamma clock and checks both signs of speed change, moment preservation, positivity bounds and every endpoint/tag probability by independent quadrature. It also checks uncapped matrix generating functions, exact physical-time rescaling, an infinite-mean clock and the grid alias, with explicit omitted-count bounds. These are high-precision floating checks, not outward-rounded existence certificates; the Gram argument supplies existence for every finite cap.
