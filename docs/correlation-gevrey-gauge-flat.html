Gevrey-gauge covariance

The index-zero profile boundary has a fractional-power rate.

The positive-index inverse-profile theorem stops sharply at index zero. A logarithmic profile produces an ordinary Gevrey-flat map and a new fractional power of the switching speed.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain setup of the tangent-gauge theorem. Let $r(w)/\rho(w)\to1$ as $w\to0$, where $\rho$ is a continuous positive homogeneous gauge, and define

$$\Xi_{q,b,r}(w)=\exp\{-b/r(w)^q\},\qquad w\ne0, \qquad \Xi_{q,b,r}(0)=0,$$

for $q,b>0$. For $n=mT\to\infty$, uniformly over arbitrary initial priors,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Xi_{q,b,r}(W_{m,T})) \sim K_{q,b,\rho,\Gamma},n^{q/(q+2)},$$ $$K_{q,b,\rho,\Gamma} =\frac{q+2}{2q}(2qb)^{2/(q+2)} \Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)^{-q/(q+2)}.$$

Here $\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)=\max_{\|z\|_2=1}\rho(\Gamma^{1/2}z)^2$. This is a fixed-feature, fixed-maturity result; it makes no nonlinear-rank or maturity-integrated identification claim.

Moderate-deviation contraction

Put $a_n=n^{1/(q+2)}$ and $v_n=n/a_n^2=n^{q/(q+2)}$. The spectral moderate-deviation principle for $a_nW_{m,T}$ has speed $v_n$ and quadratic rate $y^\top\Gamma^+y/2$ on the range of $\Gamma$.

For the $p$th moment, homogeneity and the tangent relation reduce the limiting contraction to

$$C_p=\inf_y\left\{\frac{pb}{\rho(y)^q} +\frac12y^\top\Gamma^+y\right\}. $$

At fixed $s=\rho(y)$, the quadratic cost is $s^2/(2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma))$. Minimizing over $s>0$ gives

$$C_p=\frac{q+2}{2q}(qpb)^{2/(q+2)} \Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)^{-q/(q+2)}.$$

Because $C_p$ is proportional to $p^{2/(q+2)}$, $C_2<2C_1$. The second moment therefore dominates the squared mean, and the variance constant is $K_{q,b,\rho,\Gamma}=C_2$.

The profile phase boundary

Writing $H(x)=\log b+q\log x$ gives $\exp{-\exp(H(1/r))\}=\Xi_{q,b,r}$. This $H$ is regularly varying with index zero, after an immaterial bounded-range extension, so the positive-index hypothesis in the inverse-profile theorem cannot be dropped.

The earlier scalar telegraph result is the one-dimensional symmetric specialization. The theorem above adds arbitrary finite chains, vector features, asymmetric gauges and tangent-equivalent local scales.

Numerical certificate

Literature boundary

Varadhan (1966) supplies the classical Laplace-principle framework, while Gärtner (1977) supplies the spectral Markov large-deviation principle. The singular homogeneous contraction and its gauge constant are derived above.
