Arbitrary-rank rare contamination

A vanishing atom reaches the last confluent direction through one divided difference.

For any number of coalescing power outputs, the rare-tail boundary has an exact coefficient. The two-output hyperbolic-cosine term is the first case of a normalized barycentric divided difference.

Rank-$k$ boundary theorem

Fix $c>0$, an integer $k\ge2$, and distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$. Let $G$ be standard Gaussian and

$$P_n=(1-p_n)\mathcal L(G)+p_n\delta_{A_n},\qquad \varepsilon_n\downarrow0,\quad A_n\longrightarrow\infty,\quad p_n\longrightarrow0.$$

Suppose, for finite $\tau,q\ge0$, that

$$\varepsilon_n\log A_n\longrightarrow\tau,\qquad \frac{p_nA_n^{2c}}{\varepsilon_n^{2(k-1)}}\longrightarrow q.$$

Let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$ under $P_n$. Define $w_j=\{\prod_{\ell\ne j}(x_j-x_\ell)\}^{-1}$, $u=w/\|w\|_2$, and $v_\tau=(e^{\tau x_1},\ldots,e^{\tau x_k})^\top$.

Let $(H_c)_{rs}=\operatorname{Cov}(|G|^c(\log|G|)^r, |G|^c(\log|G|)^s)$ for $0\le r,s<k$. Write $H_c=R_cR_c^\top$ and $V_xV_x^\top=L_xL_x^\top$ for their Cholesky factorizations, where $(V_x)_{rj}=x_j^r/r!$.

$$\frac{\lambda_{\min}(K_n)}{\varepsilon_n^{2(k-1)}}\longrightarrow \{(R_c)_{kk}(L_x)_{kk}\}^2+q(u^\top v_\tau)^2.$$

For $k=2$ and $x=(-1/2,1/2)$, the second term is $q\{\cosh(\tau)-1\}$. Thus the earlier two-output formula is recovered exactly.

Why barycentric weights appear

The weights satisfy $\sum_jw_jx_j^r=0$ for $0\le r<k-1$. Hence $u$ is the unique unit direction annihilated by the first $k-1$ rows of the confluent Vandermonde matrix.

The projection $w^\top v_\tau$ is exactly the exponential divided difference at the nodes. It vanishes at $\tau=0$ and is nonzero for $\tau>0$ because the relevant derivative of the exponential is positive.

Proof

Set $M_a=\mathbb E|G|^a$. For exponents $a_i=c+\varepsilon_nx_i$, the exact mixture identity is

$$K_n=(1-p_n)K_n^G+p_n(1-p_n)(A_n^{a_i}-M_{a_i})_{i} (A_n^{a_j}-M_{a_j})_{j}^{\top}.$$

After division by $\varepsilon_n^{2(k-1)}$, the second term converges to $qv_\tau v_\tau^\top$. Every cross term containing a Gaussian moment vanishes because $A_n^c\to\infty$.

The Gaussian Gram has successive eigenvalue orders $1,\varepsilon_n^2,\ldots, \varepsilon_n^{2(k-1)}$. Its last unit eigenvector converges to $u$, while the preceding eigenvalue is of order $\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}$.

This spectral separation reduces the last-scale perturbation to its Rayleigh quotient on $u$. The Gaussian coefficient is $\{(R_c)_{kk}(L_x)_{kk}\}^2$ and the rare atom adds $q\,u^\top v_\tau v_\tau^\top u$, proving the claim.

Four-output certificate

Take $c=1$, $x=(0,1,2,3)$, $\varepsilon_n=1/n$, $A_n=e^n$, and $p_n=e^{-2n}/n^6$. The Gaussian coefficient is $0.002603117321985$, while

$$q(u^\top v_1)^2=\frac{(e-1)^6}{20}=1.286875071194561.$$

The contaminated limit is $1.289478188516545$, or $495.359228578087$ times the Gaussian prediction. The exact coefficient at $n=1024$ is $1.285213007165$, which is $0.996692319894$ of that limit.

Here the excess fixed $2c$ moment is only $n^{-6}$. In the derivative Gram, the rare contribution has order $n^{r+s-6}$, so it survives only in the top $(3,3)$ entry and therefore exactly at the fourth-output scale.

Scope and literature

This theorem concerns triangular laws and nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It neither changes fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor creates rank by integrating different maturity loadings.

The sequence violates the strict moment buffer: every higher power moment eventually explodes. The result is asymptotic and gives no finite-$n$ error rate without quantitative convergence rates in the three displayed hypotheses.

At the next-heavier rare-tail scale, contamination can lift the smallest eigenvalue order rather than only change its coefficient.

Berrut and Trefethen (2004) review barycentric interpolation and divided differences. Gautschi (1978) treats inverse confluent-Vandermonde geometry; the contamination coefficient above is derived here.
