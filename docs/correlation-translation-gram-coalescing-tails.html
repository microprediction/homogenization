Coalescing rare tails split a Gram mode quadratically

The direction lost when two tail slopes merge reappears at the square of their separation.

Distinct rare-tail slopes promote distinct Gram modes at one scale. When two slopes approach each other, their second mode does not disappear abruptly: a confluent exponential derivative determines its smaller eigenvalue and exact coefficient.

Two-scale splitting theorem

Fix $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, $1\le r\le k-3$, a slope $\tau>0$, and weights $q_1,q_2>0$. Put $Q=q_1+q_2$ and

$$\gamma_1=-\frac{q_2}{Q},\qquad \gamma_2=\frac{q_1}{Q}.$$

For sequences $\varepsilon_n,\delta_n\downarrow0$ satisfying $\varepsilon_n/\delta_n\to0$, define

$$\tau_{\ell n}=\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n,\qquad A_{\ell n}=\exp(\tau_{\ell n}/\varepsilon_n),\qquad p_{\ell n}=q_\ell\varepsilon_n^{2r}A_{\ell n}^{-2c},$$

and let $X_n$ have law $(1-p_{1n}-p_{2n})\mathcal L(G)+p_{1n}\delta_{A_{1n}}+p_{2n}\delta_{A_{2n}}$, where $G\sim N(0,1)$. Let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$.

With $\phi_a=(x_j^a/a!)_j$, let $P_r$ project onto $\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r-1}\}^\perp$, and set

$$a=\frac{P_r\phi_r}{\|P_r\phi_r\|_2},\qquad w=P_r(e^{\tau x_j})_j,\qquad d=P_r(x_je^{\tau x_j})_j.$$

Let $R$ project orthogonally away from $\operatorname{span}\{a,w\}$. If $\mu_1\ge\mu_2>0$ are the two positive eigenvalues of

$$M_0=c_{r+1}aa^\top+Qww^\top,$$

then, for the decreasing eigenvalues of $K_n$,

$$\lambda_{r+j}(K_n)=\mu_j\varepsilon_n^{2r}+o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}),\qquad j=1,2,$$ $$\lambda_{r+3}(K_n)=\frac{q_1q_2}{Q}\|Rd\|_2^2 \varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2+o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2).$$

Every remaining eigenvalue, when present, is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2)$.

The first $r$ Gaussian eigenvalue coefficients are unchanged. Thus two distinct tails still supply two additional directions, but the second promoted direction moves down by the quadratic factor $\delta_n^2$.

Why the coefficient is positive

The weighted centering $q_1\gamma_1+q_2\gamma_2=0$ cancels the first-order change in $\sum_\ell q_\ell w(\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n)w(\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n)^\top$. Its compression away from $\operatorname{span}\{a,w\}$ is

$$\delta_n^2\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^2q_\ell\gamma_\ell^2\right)(Rd)(Rd)^\top +o(\delta_n^2),\qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^2q_\ell\gamma_\ell^2=\frac{q_1q_2}{Q}.$$

The vector $Rd$ is nonzero. Otherwise a nontrivial combination of $1,x,\ldots,x^r,e^{\tau x},xe^{\tau x}$ would vanish at the $k\ge r+3$ nodes, contrary to the extended Chebyshev property.

Schur-complement elimination gives the displayed small eigenvalue. The next Gaussian block is smaller by $\varepsilon_n^2/\delta_n^2=o(1)$, while rare-weight cross terms are exponentially smaller; this is exactly where the rate condition $\varepsilon_n/\delta_n\to0$ is used.

Six-output certificate

Take $c=\tau=q_1=1$, $q_2=2$, $x=(-5,-3,-1,1,3,5)/2$, $r=2$, $\varepsilon_n=1/n$, and $\delta_n=n^{-1/2}$. The coefficient is

$$\frac23\|Rd\|_2^2=0.114633532958465.$$

At $n=4096$, the exact fifth eigenvalue divided by $\varepsilon_n^4\delta_n^2$ is $0.109502136454$, a ratio $0.955236514380$. The certificate also checks the two primary fourth-order modes and full rank of the concrete confluent evaluation matrix.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for the displayed triangular laws and nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It is not uniform over arbitrary slope arrays, and it claims no finite-$n$ error bound beyond the stated little-$o$ remainder.

If $\delta_n$ is comparable to or smaller than $\varepsilon_n$, the next Gaussian block interacts with the split tail mode and the coefficient above is not asserted. The result changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank after integrating distinct maturity loadings.

Karlin and Studden supply the extended Chebyshev zero-count argument. Gautschi treats confluent-Vandermonde geometry, and Kato supplies the spectral perturbation framework.
