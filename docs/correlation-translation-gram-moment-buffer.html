Moment buffers for translation-Gram limits

One strict moment beyond the target power supplies every local Hilbert-space hypothesis.

The uniform joint Gram theorem is stated in Hilbert-space derivatives. For random power features, a simple higher-moment bound implies those assumptions and rules out the rare-tail boundary layer.

Moment-buffer theorem

Fix $c>0$, an integer $k\ge1$, and distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$. Suppose

$$X_n\Rightarrow X,\qquad \sup_n\mathbb E|X_n|^{2(c+\eta)}<\infty$$

for some $\eta>0$. Define the centered $L^2$ maps

$$f_n(a)=|X_n|^a-\mathbb E|X_n|^a$$

and the limiting derivative Gram

$$(H_c)_{rs}=\operatorname{Cov}\left( |X|^c(\log|X|)^r,\ |X|^c(\log|X|)^s\right), \qquad 0\le r,s<k.$$

Assume $H_c\succ0$, write $H_c=R_c^\top R_c$, and let $V_xV_x^\top=L_xL_x^\top$ for $(V_x)_{rj}=x_j^r/r!$. If $K_{n,\varepsilon}$ is the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon x_j}$, then for every $n_\ell\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon_\ell\downarrow0$, at arbitrary relative rates,

$$\frac{\lambda_j(K_{n_\ell,\varepsilon_\ell})} {\varepsilon_\ell^{2(j-1)}}\longrightarrow \{(R_c)_{jj}(L_x)_{jj}\}^2,\qquad 1\le j\le k.$$

Positive definiteness is automatic, for example, when the support of $|X|$ contains an interval in $(0,\infty)$. The theorem also applies to discrete limits whenever the displayed derivative Gram is positive definite.

Why one extra moment is enough

Choose $0<\delta<\min(c,\eta)$. For $|a-c|\le\delta$ and $0\le r\le k$,

$$|x|^{2a}|\log|x||^{2r}\le C\{1+|x|^{2(c+\eta)}\}.$$

Near zero this follows because $a>0$; at infinity the strict exponent gap absorbs every fixed log power. Hence $a\mapsto f_n(a)$ is $k$ times strongly differentiable in $L^2$, with a derivative bound uniform in $n$ and $a$.

The same strict gap makes all products $|X_n|^{2c}(\log|X_n|)^{r+s}$ and the corresponding first moments uniformly integrable. Weak convergence therefore gives convergence of every derivative-Gram entry to $H_c$.

The uniform Taylor and derivative-Gram hypotheses of the joint theorem now hold. Its relative $O(\varepsilon)$ eigenvalue estimate and continuity of Cholesky factors give the displayed arbitrary-rate limit.

Sharpness and certificate

The rare-contamination example proves that convergence of the $2c$ moment itself is not enough. A strict power buffer is sufficient, not necessary: derivative-Gram convergence only needs uniform integrability of the displayed log-weighted moments, while a separate local $k$th-derivative bound controls the Taylor remainder.

For a concrete boundary check, take $c=1$, $\eta=1/4$, $\varepsilon_n=1/n$, $A_n=e^n$, and mix a Gaussian with an atom at $A_n$ of probability $p_n=e^{-2(c+\eta)n}$. Its contribution to the $2(c+\eta)$ moment is exactly one, while the dangerous derivative-tail scale is $n^2e^{-n/2}\to0$.

The exact mixture certificate gives $\lambda_{\min}(K_{64,1/64})/[\{D_1/(2V_1)\}(1/64)^2]=0.999876369294$. At $n=16$ the same ratio is $2.191977560290$, illustrating that the theorem is asymptotic and supplies no finite-$n$ coefficient rate without a moment-convergence rate.

Scope and literature

This is a sufficient criterion for nonlinear-output Grams at one fixed positive maturity. It neither changes fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor integrates distinct maturity loadings.

von Bahr (1965) studies the classical role of higher moments in moment convergence under a central limit theorem. The log-weighted moment-buffer criterion and its joint coalescing consequence are derived here.
