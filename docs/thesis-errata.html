Errata for the 2001 thesis

Corrections to Chapter 3 and Appendix B of the thesis listed on the papers page.

Page numbers are the printed ones; the page of the PDF is ten higher. The site's own formulas do not use the expressions corrected here.

Chapter 3: the switching rate

The generator printed on p. 85 has off-diagonal rate $\lambda/2$, so the regime contrast decays like $e^{-\lambda s}$ and a regime lasts $2/\lambda$ on average. On p. 125, $\lambda = 5$ is described as five switches a year, a rate $\lambda$ out of each state and a contrast decaying like $e^{-2\lambda s}$. The simulations on pp. 126 and 128 follow the second convention, as does this site.

Chapter 3: the dynamic mean Vasicek formula

Eq. (3.7) on p. 106 replaces the level and the variance by their means given the starting regime. A coefficient at calendar time $s$ is weighted by the remaining maturity, so with $b(u) = (1 - e^{-\kappa u})/\kappa$ and rate $\lambda$ out of each state the exponent is

$$a_{\mathrm{dmv}}(T) = \kappa\int_0^T\big(\bar\theta \pm \tilde\theta\,e^{-2\lambda s}\big)\,b(T - s)\,ds - \frac12\int_0^T\big(\bar s \pm \tilde s\,e^{-2\lambda s}\big)\,b(T - s)^2\,ds ,$$

with $\tilde\theta$ and $\tilde s$ the half-differences of the level and the variance, and the upper sign for a start in the first regime. As printed, Eq. (3.7) pairs $e^{-\lambda s}$ with $b(s)$. The remaining maturity is needed under either convention for $\lambda$.

The two examples have levels 0.35 and 0.15 and a negligible volatility. The size of the antisymmetric correction, $|P_1 - P_2|$ over twice the mean Vasicek price, is:

Two smaller points in the second example. The table of time scales at the foot of p. 127 is carried over from the first example and should read $1/\kappa = 0.04$, $1/\lambda = 1$, $T = 5$. The antisymmetric entries on p. 128 are magnitudes: the first regime has the higher level and the cheaper bond, as the negative entries on p. 126 show.

Appendix B.4: the deterministic shift

The short rate is $\mathit{fudge}(u) + \beta\cdot X(u)$, and $\Lambda(t, u) = \exp\big(-\int_t^u \mathit{fudge}\big)$ scales the bond from $t$ to $u$.

  • Caplet and floorlet, pp. 172–173. The shifted formulas carry $\Lambda(T, \tau)$ and need $\Lambda(t, T)\,\Lambda(T, \tau) = \Lambda(t, \tau)$: the discount from today to the quote day is missing.
  • Swaplet, p. 175. The second bond matures at $\tau + \Delta\tau$ and takes $\Lambda(t, \tau + \Delta\tau)$, so $$\mathit{swaplet}^\Lambda = \Lambda(t, \tau)\,B(t, \tau) - (1 + \Delta\tau K)\,\Lambda(t, \tau + \Delta\tau)\,B(t, \tau + \Delta\tau) .$$
  • Futures, p. 177. Under the risk-neutral expectation the recursion is $f_{i-1} = B(T_{i-1}, T_i)^{-1}\,\mathbb E\big[e^{-\int r}f_i\big]$, with a negative exponent. If the expectation is already under the forward measure, neither the discount nor $B^{-1}$ appears.

A check with $X = 0$ and a shift of 5%: $t = 0$, $T = \tau = 1$, $\Delta\tau = 1$, $K = 0$. The caplet and the swaplet are both worth $e^{-0.05} - e^{-0.10} = 0.046392$. The printed caplet formula gives 0.048771, too high by the factor $e^{0.05}$, and the printed swaplet formula gives zero.
