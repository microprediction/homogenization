Multi-tail confluence creates a collision cascade

On the diagonal scale, every tail derivative meets its matching Gaussian residual.

The two blocks in the three-tail example are the first nontrivial instance of a complete pattern. With $s$ coalescing tails and $\delta_n$ proportional to $\varepsilon_n$, there are $s-1$ successive rank-two collisions.

Diagonal collision-cascade theorem

Use the $s$-tail triangular law, positive weights, distinct centered offsets, and notation of the multi-tail confluence theorem. Assume $s\ge2$, $1\le r\le k-2s$, and

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n}\longrightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty).$$

The first $r$ Gaussian modes and the two primary modes remain those of the common-slope theorem.

For $1\le j\le s-1$, let $P_j$ project away from

$$L_j=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+j-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{j-1}\},$$

set $g_j=P_j\phi_{r+j}$ and $b_j=P_jv_j$, and define

$$M_j=h_{r+j}g_jg_j^\top+\eta_j\rho^{2j}b_jb_j^\top.$$

This matrix has rank two. If $\nu_{j,1}\ge\nu_{j,2}>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+2j+a}(K_n)=\nu_{j,a}\varepsilon_n^{2(r+j)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+j)}),\qquad a=1,2.$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+s-1)})$.

Why the cascade is complete

The $j$th weighted-label residual has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^j$, while the matching Gaussian residual has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^{r+j}$. Their ratio tends to $\rho^j$, giving the coefficient $\eta_j\rho^{2j}$ at covariance scale.

After each complete domain and output pair is removed, the same argument repeats one order later. The final extended-Chebyshev system contains the polynomial columns through $x^{r+s-1}$ and exponential columns through $x^{s-1}e^{\tau x}$.

It has $r+2s$ functions, so the condition $k\ge r+2s$ makes every projected pair independent and every $M_j$ rank two. Once all $s-1$ label residuals are used, only smaller Gaussian features remain.

Four-tail certificate

Take $s=4$, $k=9$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3,4)$, centered offsets $(-2,-1,0,1)$, nodes $(-4,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,4)$, $\rho=5/4$, and $\delta_n=\rho\varepsilon_n$.

The three pairs of collision coefficients are

$$(2323.618616766171272,0.166908704432684),$$ $$(40.386754915255587,0.002700798513326),$$ $$(0.055286932164863,0.000004257341527).$$

At $n=2048$, their exact normalized ratios are

$$(0.995208746790,1.000160327261,0.995151921100,$$ $$1.001101070337,0.995771614089,1.003392086245).$$

The 260-digit certificate also checks centered offsets, exact rare weights, primary modes, full confluent rank, positivity, convergence, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for fixed $s,k,r,q,\gamma,$ and $\rho$ at one fixed positive maturity. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not a finite-$n$ bound or uniformity over moving dimensions, tail counts, offsets, or boundary ratios.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
