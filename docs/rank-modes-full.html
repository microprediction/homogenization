A two-mode limit to final-gap calibration

One emission-blind final gap cannot restore exact marginal rank coverage for two decay modes, even if the gap is randomized independently.

Observe a stationary finite-state chain with row generator $Q$ at times $0,d,d+G$. The first two scores calibrate the threshold $\max(S_0,S_d)$ and the third is the test score, whose iid rank-coverage value is $2/3$. The gap $G\ge0$ is drawn independently of the chain and all score noise, using the same law in both emission experiments below.

An exact rank identity, not a mixing expansion

For a centered state signal $h$, $\pi h=0$, let the conditionally independent score emissions have densities

$$f_i(s)=1+\varepsilon h_i(2s-1),\qquad 0\le s\le1,\qquad |\varepsilon h_i|<1.$$

All stationary score marginals are uniform and continuous. Write $r_h(t)=\langle h,e^{tQ}h\rangle_\pi$ and let $C_h(d,G)$ average the rank event over the calibration panel, test score, chain and gap randomization.

$$\boxed{C_h(d,G)-\frac23=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{60} \{\mathbb E r_h(G)+\mathbb E r_h(d+G)-2r_h(d)\}.}$$

To prove this, condition on the three state signals and abbreviate their density coefficients by $a,b,c$. Direct integration of $f_{\rm test}(s)\{1-F_0(s)F_d(s)\}$ gives

$$\frac23+\frac{a+b}{12}-\frac c6-\frac{ab}{30}+\frac{ac+bc}{60}.$$

The cubic coefficient vanishes, and stationarity kills the linear terms. The three pair expectations give the boxed identity, without reversibility or a large-gap approximation.

Two incompatible Laplace moments on one reversible chain

$$Q=\frac16\begin{pmatrix}-5&1&4\\1&-5&4\\4&4&-8\end{pmatrix},\qquad \pi=\tfrac13(1,1,1),\qquad d=\log2,\qquad\varepsilon=\tfrac14.$$

Compare $h_1=(1,-1,0)$ and $h_2=(1,1,-2)$, separately, with all other design choices unchanged. Their decay rates are $1,2$, the minimum off-diagonal rate is $1/6$, and both score-density families are at least $1/2$ on $[0,1]$.

Set $y=\mathbb E e^{-G}$ and $z=\mathbb E e^{-2G}$. Since $r_{h_1}(t)=\tfrac23e^{-t}$ and $r_{h_2}(t)=2e^{-2t}$, the exact coverage biases are

$$C_{h_1}-\frac23=\frac{y-2/3}{960},\qquad C_{h_2}-\frac23=\frac{z-2/5}{384}.$$

Simultaneous exact coverage would require $y=2/3$, $z=2/5$. But Jensen gives $z\ge y^2$, whereas $2/5<4/9$. Thus no independently randomized final-gap law works for both emission laws, even with $Q$ known.

A sharp minimax absolute error

The full optimization over such gap laws has an explicit solution:

$$\inf_{\mathcal L(G)}\max_{j=1,2}\left|C_{h_j}(d,G)-\frac23\right| =E_*:=\frac{13-\sqrt{159}}{14400} =2.711665188066\times10^{-5}.$$ $$y_*:=\frac{\sqrt{159}-3}{15},\qquad G_*=-\log y_*=0.445295905181.$$

Indeed $(2/3-y_*)/960=(y_*^2-2/5)/384=E_*$. If $y\le y_*$, the first absolute error is at least $E_*$; if $y\ge y_*$, then $z\ge y^2\ge y_*^2$ makes the second error at least $E_*$. The deterministic gap $G_*$ attains equality, so independent randomization cannot improve this two-experiment minimax value.

Scope and certificate

This is an exact finite-sample statement about one fixed rank threshold and a precommitted, emission-blind final-gap law with a fixed calibration gap. It does not rule out emission-specific gaps, score-dependent or state-adaptive designs, changing the threshold, or increasing the calibration spacing. The loss is absolute deviation from exact nominal coverage, not a lower bound on undercoverage or an impossibility of conservative one-sided validity.

Coverage here is stationary marginal coverage, not coverage conditional on the realized calibration scores or the current regime. The calculation concerns terminal emissions, not overlapping path-score windows.

The certificate checks the exact conditional polynomial and 54 state-path kernels, then compares 60 independent matrix-exponential/resolvent and score-quadrature calculations. It also checks 36 general nonreversible cases and 2,000 randomized-gap laws and independently optimizes the deterministic minimax gap; the universal lower bound follows from the proof, not the mesh.

For general concentration and decoupling bounds under dependence, see Oliveira, Orenstein, Ramos and Romano (2024). The signed rank polynomial and elementary Laplace-moment obstruction here are a separate model-specific calculation, not attributed to that general theory.
