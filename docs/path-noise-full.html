When smoothing noise vanishes

A noise-uniform telegraph calculation: the starting-state coverage gap crosses from inverse horizon to inverse square root.

Setup and result

Let $Y_s\in\{-1,+1\}$ flip at rate one, put $A_c=c^{-1}\int_0^cY_sds$, and consider $S_c=\mu A_c+\sigma Z$, with fixed $\mu>0$, independent $Z\sim N(0,1)$, and any $\sigma\ge0$. Write $F_\pm$ for its fixed-start CDFs, put $F_\pi=(F_++F_-)/2$, and let $q_c(\sigma)$ be its $p$-quantile for fixed $0<p<1$.

Define the Gaussian comparison scale $\tau_c(\sigma)=\sqrt{\sigma^2+\mu^2/c}$ and $z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)$. This is not the exact standard deviation: $\operatorname{Var}_\pi A_c=c^{-1}-(1-e^{-2c})/(2c^2)$. As $c\to\infty$,

$$F_\pm(q_c(\sigma);c,\sigma)-p =\mp{\mu\phi(z_p)\over2c\tau_c(\sigma)} +o\!\left({\mu\over c\tau_c(\sigma)}\right),\qquad\text{uniformly over }\sigma\ge0.$$

At zero noise the pooled law has endpoint atoms, but every fixed interior $p$ lies in its continuous part for sufficiently large $c$. For all such $c$, pooling gives exact marginal population coverage; the displayed discrepancy is conditional on the latent starting state. It is not an empirical-calibration or distribution-free conditional guarantee.

An exact CDF-gap identity

Conditioning on the Poisson number of flips gives the Poisson–Beta occupation law in the companion note. Summing that series, the densities on $-1<a<1$ are

$$f_\pm(a)={ce^{-c}\over2}\left[I_0(cr)+{1\pm a\over r}I_1(cr)\right],\qquad r=\sqrt{1-a^2},$$

with a mass $e^{-c}$ at the starting endpoint. Since $I_0'(x)=I_1(x)$, integrating the difference from the pooled density, including the negative endpoint atom, gives

$$\Pr_+(A_c\le a)-\Pr_\pi(A_c\le a)=-\tfrac12e^{-c}I_0(c\sqrt{1-a^2}),\qquad -1\le a<1.$$

The difference is zero outside that half-open interval, and the negative-start gap has the opposite sign. Thus the atoms are retained, not silently replaced by Gaussian tails.

Put $\lambda=\sigma\sqrt c/\mu$, $t=q\sqrt c/\mu$, and define

$$k_c(u)=\sqrt c\,e^{-c}I_0(c\sqrt{1-u^2/c})\,\mathbf1_{[-\sqrt c,\sqrt c)}(u).$$

If $\phi_\lambda$ denotes the $N(0,\lambda^2)$ density, with convolution by $\phi_0$ meaning the identity, then exactly

$$F_+(q;c,\sigma)-F_\pi(q;c,\sigma)=-{(k_c*\phi_\lambda)(t)\over2\sqrt c}.$$

Here $\int k_c(u)du=1-e^{-2c}$. In particular the fixed-start discrepancy can be evaluated without subtracting nearly equal CDFs.

A finite-horizon bound uniform over noise levels

Let $j_c$ be the continuous density of $\sqrt c A_c$ under a stationary start, extended by zero outside its support:

$$j_c(u)={\sqrt c\,e^{-c}\over2}\left[I_0(cr)+{I_1(cr)\over r}\right],\qquad r=\sqrt{1-u^2/c},\quad |u|<\sqrt c.$$

The two remaining atoms each have mass $e^{-c}/2$. Define exact deterministic one-dimensional error budgets by

$$\begin{aligned} \eta_c&=\tfrac12\{\|j_c-\phi\|_1+e^{-c}\},\\ L_c&=\|k_c-\phi\|_1,\\ D_c&=\int_0^{\sqrt c}|k_c'(u)+u\phi(u)|du+\sqrt c\,e^{-c}+\phi(\sqrt c),\\ \epsilon_c&=\sqrt2\max\{D_c,L_c/\sqrt{2\pi}\}. \end{aligned}$$

The derivative in $D_c$ is taken only inside the support. Its jump and Gaussian-tail terms give $\|k_c-\phi\|_\infty\le D_c$. Young's convolution inequalities, using the supremum bound for $\lambda\le1$ and the $L^1$ bound for $\lambda\ge1$, imply

$$\sup_{t\in\mathbb R,\lambda\ge0}\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\, |(k_c*\phi_\lambda)(t)-\phi_{\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}}(t)|\le\epsilon_c.$$

In this display $t$ ranges over all real numbers. Total variation contracts under Gaussian convolution and rescaling, so the pooled standardized CDF is within $\eta_c$ of $\Phi$, independently of $\sigma$. If $\eta_c<\min(p,1-p)$, put

$$\Delta_c(p)=\max\{z_p-z_{p-\eta_c},z_{p+\eta_c}-z_p\}.$$

Quantile bracketing and $\sup|\phi'|=1/\sqrt{2\pi e}$ now give the finite-$c$ bound

$$\left|F_\pm(q_c(\sigma);c,\sigma)-p \pm{\mu\phi(z_p)\over2c\tau_c(\sigma)}\right| \le{\mu\over2c\tau_c(\sigma)} \left[\epsilon_c+{\Delta_c(p)\over\sqrt{2\pi e}}\right].$$

These budgets use explicit Bessel functions, absolute integrals and normal quantiles; there is no hidden inverse power of $\sigma$. Their numerical quadratures in the certificate are regression checks, not outward-rounded enclosures.

Why the error budgets vanish

The classical fixed-order Bessel asymptotics give $k_c\to\phi$, $k_c'\to\phi'$, and $j_c\to\phi$ locally uniformly. The integral representation of $I_0$ gives $I_0(x)\le2e^x/\sqrt{1+x}$ for $x\ge0$, and its power series gives $I_1(x)\le(x/2)I_0(x)$. Also $I_1(x)\le I_0(x)$ by the same integral representation.

On $|u|\le\sqrt c/2$, these inequalities bound $k_c$ and $|k_c'|$ by constant multiples of $e^{-u^2/2}$ and $|u|e^{-u^2/2}$. On the rest of the support, their integrals are bounded by a polynomial in $c$ times $e^{-(1-\sqrt3/2)c}$. Hence $L_c,D_c\to0$ by splitting at a fixed large $|u|$.

The continuous mass of $j_c$ tends to one, so its local limit and Scheffé's lemma give $\eta_c\to0$ as well. Consequently $\Delta_c(p)\to0$, proving the noise-uniform little-$o$ statement. No numerical decay exponent for this remainder is needed or claimed.

The noise-scale crossover

For $\sigma_c=s c^{-\beta}$ with fixed $s>0$ and $\beta\ge0$, the positive-start leading gap is:

Each table entry has relative error tending to zero; the negative-start entry changes sign. The fixed-positive-noise $c^{-1}$ theorem remains correct, but is not uniform as its smoothing parameter vanishes.

Literature and scope

The telegraph law and its Gaussian scaling limit are classical; see the inspected primary preprint of Bogachev and Ratanov, equation (1.9) and Theorem 1.1, published in 2011 in Stochastic Processes and their Applications 121:1816–1844, DOI 10.1016/j.spa.2011.03.016. Its positive-half-line occupation functional is different from occupation of the velocity states used here.

Bessel inputs are DLMF 10.32.1 and 10.40.1; the CDF-gap identity and convolution budget are derived above. The certificate checks the identity against independent Poisson–Beta CDFs, the finite-noise budgets and the four power-law regimes. This note treats a symmetric two-state driver, a linear occupation score, independent Gaussian noise, and population pooling only.
