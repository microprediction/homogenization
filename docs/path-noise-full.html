When smoothing noise vanishes

A noise-uniform telegraph calculation, including unequal switching rates and endpoint atoms.

Setup and result

Let $Y_s\in\{-1,+1\}$ jump from $+$ to $-$ at fixed rate $a>0$ and back at fixed rate $b>0$. Put $r=a+b$, $\pi=(b/r,a/r)$, $\bar y=(b-a)/r$, and $v=8ab/r^3$. Consider $A_c=c^{-1}\int_0^cY_sds$ and $S_c=\mu A_c+\sigma Z$, with fixed $\mu>0$, independent $Z\sim N(0,1)$, and any $\sigma\ge0$.

Write $F_\pm$ for its fixed-start CDFs, $F_\pi=(bF_++aF_-)/r$, and $q_c(\sigma)$ for the pooled $p$-quantile at fixed $0<p<1$. Define $z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)$ and the Gaussian comparison scale $\tau_c(\sigma)=\sqrt{\sigma^2+\mu^2v/c}$. The exact stationary variance is $\operatorname{Var}_\pi A_c=v/c-v(1-e^{-rc})/(rc^2)$, so $\tau_c$ is not the exact standard deviation.

$$\begin{pmatrix}F_+(q_c(\sigma))-p\\F_-(q_c(\sigma))-p\end{pmatrix} ={2\mu\phi(z_p)\over r^2c\tau_c(\sigma)}\begin{pmatrix}-a\\b\end{pmatrix} +o\!\left({\mu\over c\tau_c(\sigma)}\right), \qquad\text{uniformly over }\sigma\ge0.$$

For $a=b=1$ this is the symmetric coefficient $\mp\mu\phi(z_p)/(2c\sqrt{\sigma^2+\mu^2/c})$. For unequal rates the two errors have ratio $-a/b$, not $-1$; their stationary-weighted sum is exactly zero at every $c$ where the pooled quantile attains $p$. This is marginal population coverage, not empirical-calibration or distribution-free conditional validity.

At zero noise there are endpoint atoms, but every fixed interior $p$ lies in the continuous part for sufficiently large $c$. Rates $a,b$ are held fixed; no uniformity as either rate vanishes is claimed.

An exact CDF-gap identity

For $-1<x<1$, write $R=\sqrt{1-x^2}$, $E=c\{r+(a-b)x\}/2$, and $B=c\sqrt{ab}R$. The classical telegraph densities are

$$f_+(x)={ce^{-E}\over2}\left[aI_0(B)+{\sqrt{ab}(1+x)\over R}I_1(B)\right],\qquad f_-(x)={ce^{-E}\over2}\left[bI_0(B)+{\sqrt{ab}(1-x)\over R}I_1(B)\right].$$

The remaining masses are $e^{-ac}$ at $+1$ for a positive start and $e^{-bc}$ at $-1$ for a negative start. Defining $g_c(x)=e^{-E}I_0(B)$ on $[-1,1)$ and zero outside, differentiation gives $f_+-f_-=-g_c'$ inside the support. Including the endpoint atoms therefore gives the exact identity

$$\Pr_+(A_c\le x)-\Pr_-(A_c\le x)=-g_c(x).$$

Consequently $F_+-F_\pi$ is $-a/r$ times the Gaussian convolution of $g_c$, while $F_--F_\pi$ is $+b/r$ times the same convolution. This proves the unequal error ratio exactly, not merely to leading order.

A normalized kernel and its finite-horizon budgets

Set $x(u)=\bar y+\sqrt{v/c}\,u$, $\ell=(-1-\bar y)\sqrt{c/v}$, $h=(1-\bar y)\sqrt{c/v}$, and $H=r\sqrt{vc}/2$. Define $k_c(u)=H g_c(x(u))$, supported on $[\ell,h)$, and let $j_c(u)=\sqrt{v/c}\{bf_+(x(u))+af_-(x(u))\}/r$ be the continuous stationary density, extended by zero. The stationary atom mass is $\omega_c=(be^{-ac}+ae^{-bc})/r$.

The exact conditional-mean difference gives $\int_{-1}^1g_c(x)dx=2(1-e^{-rc})/(rc)$, so $\int k_c=1-e^{-rc}$. Put $\lambda=\sigma\sqrt{c/v}/\mu$, $t=(q-\mu\bar y)\sqrt{c/v}/\mu$, and let $\phi_\lambda$ be the $N(0,\lambda^2)$ density, with convolution by $\phi_0$ meaning the identity. Then exactly

$$F_+(q)-F_\pi(q)=-{a\over rH}(k_c*\phi_\lambda)(t),\qquad F_-(q)-F_\pi(q)={b\over rH}(k_c*\phi_\lambda)(t).$$

The following exact one-dimensional budgets retain both endpoint jumps:

$$\begin{aligned} \eta_c&=\tfrac12\{\|j_c-\phi\|_1+\omega_c\},\qquad L_c=\|k_c-\phi\|_1,\\ D_c&=\tfrac12\left\{\int_\ell^h|k_c'(u)+u\phi(u)|du +H(e^{-ac}+e^{-bc})+\phi(\ell)+\phi(h)\right\},\\ \epsilon_c&=\sqrt2\max\{D_c,L_c/\sqrt{2\pi}\}. \end{aligned}$$

The derivative is taken only inside the support. Since a function vanishing at both infinities has supremum at most half its total variation, $\|k_c-\phi\|_\infty\le D_c$. Young's inequalities, using this bound for $\lambda\le1$ and the $L^1$ bound for $\lambda\ge1$, imply

$$\sup_{t\in\mathbb R,\lambda\ge0}\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\, |(k_c*\phi_\lambda)(t)-\phi_{\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}}(t)|\le\epsilon_c.$$

The exact total-variation distance $\eta_c$ contracts under Gaussian convolution and rescaling. Hence the pooled standardized CDF is within $\eta_c$ of $\Phi$, independently of $\sigma$. If $\eta_c<\min(p,1-p)$, put $\Delta_c(p)=\max\{z_p-z_{p-\eta_c},z_{p+\eta_c}-z_p\}$ and $w_+=a,w_-=b$.

$$\left|F_\pm(q_c(\sigma))-p\pm{2\mu w_\pm\phi(z_p)\over r^2c\tau_c(\sigma)}\right| \le {2\mu w_\pm\over r^2c\tau_c(\sigma)} \left[\epsilon_c+{\Delta_c(p)\over\sqrt{2\pi e}}\right].$$

This follows by quantile bracketing and $\sup|\phi'|=1/\sqrt{2\pi e}$. At zero noise the stated $\eta_c$ condition also excludes quantiles in the endpoint atoms. The budgets use explicit Bessel functions and absolute integrals; certificate quadratures are regression checks, not outward-rounded enclosures.

Why the budgets vanish for every fixed pair of rates

The exponential part of $g_c$ is $e^{cS(x)}$, where

$$S(x)=-\tfrac12\{r+(a-b)x\}+\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{1-x^2},\qquad S(\bar y)=S'(\bar y)=0,\quad S''(\bar y)=-1/v.$$

Moreover $S''(x)=-\sqrt{ab}(1-x^2)^{-3/2}\le-\sqrt{ab}$. Fixed-order Bessel asymptotics therefore give $k_c\to\phi$, $k_c'\to\phi'$, and $j_c\to\phi$ locally uniformly in $u$. The normalization $H$ is what makes the first limit have mass one.

On $|x-\bar y|\le(1-|\bar y|)/2$, concavity and the Bessel expansions bound $k_c$ and $|k_c'|$ by $C(1+|u|)e^{-du^2}$ for fixed-rate constants $C,d>0$ and all sufficiently large $c$. In the derivative, $I_1(B)/I_0(B)=1+O(c^{-1})$ cancels the apparent $\sqrt c$ term because $S'(\bar y)=0$. Outside this interior interval, $I_0(B)\le2e^B/\sqrt{1+B}$ and $I_1(B)\le(B/2)I_0(B)$ bound the integrals by a polynomial in $c$ times an exponentially decaying factor.

Thus $L_c,D_c\to0$; the remaining Gaussian tails and endpoint terms vanish as well. The continuous mass of $j_c$ tends to one, so Scheffé's lemma gives $\eta_c\to0$. Hence $\Delta_c(p)\to0$, proving the noise-uniform little-$o$ result without asserting an unproved polynomial remainder rate.

The noise-scale crossover

For $\sigma_c=s c^{-\beta}$ with fixed $s>0$ and $\beta\ge0$, the positive-start leading gap is:

noise decayconditional gap
$0\le\beta<1/2$$-2\mu a\phi(z_p)c^{\beta-1}/(r^2s)$
$\beta=1/2$$-2\mu a\phi(z_p)c^{-1/2}/(r^2\sqrt{s^2+\mu^2v})$
$\beta>1/2$, or $\sigma_c=0$$-2a\phi(z_p)c^{-1/2}/(r^2\sqrt v)$

Each entry has relative error tending to zero; multiply by $-b/a$ for the negative-start gap. The fixed-positive-noise $c^{-1}$ theorem remains correct but is not uniform as its smoothing parameter vanishes.

Literature and scope

The unequal-rate density, including its atoms, is classical: López and Ratanov (2014), equation (2.14), inspected in the primary publisher PDF. Bessel inputs are DLMF 10.32.1 and 10.40.1. The CDF-gap identity and convolution error transfer are derived above; no novelty claim is made for the density or Gaussian limit.

The certificate retains the symmetric Poisson–Beta comparisons and adds unequal-rate matrix Feynman–Kac comparisons, finite-noise bounds and stationary-weighted cancellation. The model has two states, fixed positive rates, a linear occupation score, independent Gaussian noise and population pooling only. There is no distribution-free feature-conditional coverage claim.
