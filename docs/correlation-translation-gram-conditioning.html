Conditioning of the translation-hull Gram

Distinct power outputs have full rank, with explicit spectral constants as their exponents coalesce.

The translation-hull Gram can be positive definite and still nearly singular. For powers of one Gaussian feature, a confluent-Vandermonde expansion gives the determinant and every eigenvalue scale.

Coalescing-exponent theorem

Let $G\sim N(0,1)$, fix $c>0$, and take distinct real numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_k$. For sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, define

$$F_{\varepsilon,j}=|G|^{c+\varepsilon x_j}-M_{c+\varepsilon x_j}, \qquad M_p=\mathbb E|G|^p.$$

Let $K_\varepsilon$ be their covariance Gram matrix and put

$$Y_r=|G|^c(\log|G|)^r-M_c^{(r)},\qquad H_c=(\mathbb E[Y_rY_s])_{r,s=0}^{k-1},$$ $$(H_c)_{rs}=M^{(r+s)}(2c)-M^{(r)}(c)M^{(s)}(c),$$ $$\Delta(x)=\prod_{i<j}(x_j-x_i).$$

Then $H_c\succ0$ and

$$\det K_\varepsilon= \varepsilon^{k(k-1)} \left\{\frac{\Delta(x)}{\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}r!}\right\}^{\!2} \det H_c\{1+O(\varepsilon)\}.$$

If the eigenvalues are ordered decreasingly, then

$$\lambda_j(K_\varepsilon)=\Theta(\varepsilon^{2(j-1)}), \qquad 1\le j\le k.$$

Exact spectral constants

Define the factorial-scaled Vandermonde matrix $V_x=(x_j^r/r!)_{0\le r\le k-1,\,1\le j\le k}$. Take the positive-diagonal Cholesky factors

$$H_c=R_c^\top R_c,\qquad V_xV_x^\top=L_xL_x^\top,$$

where $R_c$ is upper triangular and $L_x$ is lower triangular. Then every order constant is explicit:

$$\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0} \frac{\lambda_j(K_\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^{2(j-1)}} =\{(R_c)_{jj}(L_x)_{jj}\}^2,\qquad 1\le j\le k.$$

The product of these constants is $\det(H_c)\{\Delta(x)/\prod r!\}^2$, so the determinant theorem is recovered without losing its leading coefficient.

Proof

The centered map $f(a)=|G|^a-M_a$ is analytic into $L^2$ near $c$, with $f^{(r)}(c)=Y_r$. Thus its $k$ sampled columns have the $L^2$ expansion

$$f(c+\varepsilon x_j)= \sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon^rx_j^r}{r!}Y_r+O_{L^2}(\varepsilon^k).$$

The leading exterior product is $\varepsilon^{k(k-1)/2}\Delta(x)(\prod r!)^{-1}Y_0\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_{k-1}$. Squaring its norm gives the determinant formula.

No nonzero combination of the $Y_r$ is almost surely zero: it would make $z^cP(\log z)$ constant on $(0,\infty)$, and the limit at zero first makes the constant zero and then forces $P=0$. Hence $H_c\succ0$.

Write the derivative frame as $Q_cR_c$ and $V_x=L_xU_x$, with $Q_c$ isometric and $U_x$ orthogonal. Up to isometries, the sampled-column operator is $R_c\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon,\ldots,\varepsilon^{k-1})L_x+O(\varepsilon^k)$.

In its $j$th exterior power, the unique least exponent uses the first $j$ diagonal scales. The surviving rank-one term has norm $\prod_{i=1}^j(R_c)_{ii}(L_x)_{ii}$; consecutive product limits give the individual singular-value constants, and squaring gives the eigenvalue formula.

Uniform finite-law theorem

The same constants admit a joint limit. Let $f_n:(c-\eta,c+\eta)\to\mathcal H_n$ be centered maps into possibly different real Hilbert spaces, fix the nodes $x_j$, and suppose the maps are $k$ times strongly differentiable with

$$\sup_n\sup_{|a-c|\le\eta}\|f_n^{(k)}(a)\|_{\mathcal H_n}<\infty.$$

Assume also that the derivative Grams $H_{n,c}=(\langle f_n^{(r)}(c),f_n^{(s)}(c)\rangle)_{r,s=0}^{k-1}$ converge to a positive-definite matrix $H_c$. Write $H_{n,c}=R_{n,c}^{\top}R_{n,c}$ and retain $V_xV_x^\top=L_xL_x^\top$ from above.

For the Gram $K_{n,\varepsilon}$ of the sampled vectors $f_n(c+\varepsilon x_j)$, there are $n_0,\varepsilon_0,C>0$ such that, for $n\ge n_0$, $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, and every $j$,

$$\left|\frac{\lambda_j(K_{n,\varepsilon})} {\{(R_{n,c})_{jj}(L_x)_{jj}\}^2\varepsilon^{2(j-1)}}-1\right| \le C\varepsilon.$$

Consequently, if $n_\ell\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon_\ell\downarrow0$ at arbitrary relative rates, then

$$\frac{\lambda_j(K_{n_\ell,\varepsilon_\ell})} {\varepsilon_\ell^{2(j-1)}}\longrightarrow \{(R_c)_{jj}(L_x)_{jj}\}^2.$$

Why the estimate is uniform

Uniform Hilbert-space Taylor expansion gives $f_n(c+\varepsilon x_j)=\sum_{r<k}\varepsilon^rx_j^rf_n^{(r)}(c)/r!+E_{n,j}$ with $\|E_{n,j}\|\le C_0\varepsilon^k$. Positive definiteness of $H_c$ keeps the Cholesky factors $R_{n,c}$ in a compact set of invertible upper-triangular matrices.

The exterior-power argument above is therefore uniform in $n$: its unique least-degree minor stays bounded away from zero, every competing minor has at least one extra power of $\varepsilon$, and the Taylor remainder is one order beyond the smallest singular scale. This proves the displayed relative $O(\varepsilon)$ bound.

Exact telegraph joint certificate

Let $W_m$ be the unit-time average of the stationary symmetric telegraph chain switching at rate $m$, and put $Z_m=\sqrt m\,W_m$. Its exact moment-generating function is

$$\mathbb E e^{tZ_m}=e^{-m}\left\{\cosh s_m+\frac{m}{s_m}\sinh s_m\right\}, \qquad s_m=\sqrt{m^2+mt^2}.$$

It converges to $e^{t^2/2}$ and is bounded by $e^{t^2/2}$. Thus fixed positive moments and their log-weighted derivatives are uniformly integrable, so the centered maps $f_m(a)=|Z_m|^a-\mathbb E|Z_m|^a$ satisfy the theorem on compact positive exponent intervals and $H_{m,c}\to H_c$.

For $c=1$, two symmetric powers, and the deliberately joint choice $\delta=m^{-1/2}$, the certificate integrates the exact atom-plus-Bessel law. At $m=4096$, the finite-speed coefficient, local coalescence ratio, and full Gaussian joint ratio are respectively

$$(0.999541633, 0.999876393, 0.999418083).$$

The last number is $\lambda_{\min}(K_{m,\delta})/[\{D_1/(2V_1)\}\delta^2]$; it tests both limits simultaneously rather than substituting the Gaussian law before coalescing the exponents.

Sharp two-output constants

For the symmetric pair $c-\delta/2,c+\delta/2$, write $V_c=\operatorname{Var}(|G|^c)$ and

$$D_c=\det\operatorname{Gram} \left(|G|^c-M_c, |G|^c\log|G|-M_c'\right)>0.$$

Symmetry removes the odd remainder terms, so

$$\det K_\delta=D_c\delta^2+O(\delta^4),\qquad \lambda_{\min}(K_\delta)=\frac{D_c}{2V_c}\delta^2+O(\delta^4),$$ $$\operatorname{cond}_2K_\delta=\frac{4V_c^2}{D_c}\delta^{-2}\{1+O(\delta^2)\}.$$

At $c=1$, the certificate gives

$$V_1=0.363380227632,\qquad D_1=0.024978898995,$$ $$\frac{D_1}{2V_1}=0.034370195591,\qquad \frac{4V_1^2}{D_1}=21.145077668713.$$

At $\delta=0.025$, the exact determinant, smallest eigenvalue and scaled condition number are respectively $0.999854950$, $0.999683519$ and $1.000488120$ times these asymptotic predictions.

Four-output certificate

For $c=1$ and $x=(0,1,2,3)$, the four eigenvalue constants are

$$(1.453520910530,\ 0.343701955912,\ 0.041753467553,\ 0.002603117322).$$

At $\varepsilon=0.003125$, the exact eigenvalues divided by these predictions are $(1.008501938,1.004160346,0.999045171,0.993601303)$. Their product gives the determinant coefficient $0.000054298718305$, and the exact determinant ratio is $1.005256943$.

Between $\varepsilon=0.00625$ and $0.003125$, the measured eigenvalue exponents are $(0.012231,2.005936,3.998635,5.990805)$, approaching $(0,2,4,6)$.

Scope

This is conditioning of a nonlinear-output Gram at one fixed positive maturity. It neither changes fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor integrates distinct maturity loadings.

The joint estimate is uniform only for families satisfying the displayed $L^2$ Taylor bound and positive-definite derivative-Gram limit; weak convergence alone is insufficient. Its $O(\varepsilon)$ error is relative after division by each eigenvalue's own vanishing scale, while convergence of the leading coefficient is carried separately by $H_{n,c}\to H_c$.

A rare-contamination counterexample shows that even total-variation convergence and convergence of the fixed-exponent second moment do not replace these local assumptions.

Halperin (1962) supplies the Hilbert-space Gram identity, while Gautschi (1978) treats inverse conditioning of ordinary and confluent Vandermonde matrices. Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir (2010) give the Bessel occupation density used by the certificate; the uniform joint theorem is derived here.
