Rare tails can lift a Gram eigenvalue order

At the penultimate scale, one vanishing atom makes two eigenvalues coalesce in order.

Rare contamination need not merely change the last confluent coefficient. A slightly heavier atom lifts the smallest eigenvalue by two powers of the node spacing, even though its fixed-power moment still vanishes.

Order-lifting theorem

Fix $c>0$, an integer $k\ge3$, and distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$. Let $G$ be standard Gaussian and

$$P_n=(1-p_n)\mathcal L(G)+p_n\delta_{A_n},\qquad \varepsilon_n\downarrow0,\quad A_n\longrightarrow\infty,\quad p_n\longrightarrow0.$$

Suppose, for finite $\tau\ge0$ and $q>0$, that

$$\varepsilon_n\log A_n\longrightarrow\tau,\qquad \frac{p_nA_n^{2c}}{\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}}\longrightarrow q.$$

Let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$. Put $\phi_r=(x_j^r/r!)_{j=1}^k$ and

$$N=\{y:\phi_r^\top y=0, 0\le r<k-2\},\qquad \dim N=2.$$

Let $P_N$ be orthogonal projection onto $N$, set $a=P_N\phi_{k-2}/\|P_N\phi_{k-2}\|_2$, and let $u$ be the normalized barycentric-weight vector. Then $(a,u)$ is an orthonormal basis of $N$.

Let $(H_c)_{rs}=\operatorname{Cov}(|G|^c(\log|G|)^r,|G|^c(\log|G|)^s)$ and $H_c=R_cR_c^\top$. Also let $V_xV_x^\top=L_xL_x^\top$, where $(V_x)_{rj}=x_j^r/r!$, and write $c_{k-1}=\{(R_c)_{k-1,k-1}(L_x)_{k-1,k-1}\}^2$.

For $v_\tau=(e^{\tau x_j})_j$, define $\alpha=a^\top v_\tau$, $\beta=u^\top v_\tau$, and

$$T=c_{k-1}+q(\alpha^2+\beta^2),\qquad \mu_\pm=\frac{T\pm\sqrt{T^2-4qc_{k-1}\beta^2}}2.$$
$$\lambda_{k-1}(K_n)=\mu_+\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}+o(\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}),\qquad \lambda_k(K_n)=\mu_-\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}+o(\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}).$$

Eigenvalues are ordered decreasingly. If $\tau>0$, then $\beta$ is a nonzero exponential divided difference, so $\mu_->0$ and the uncontaminated smallest order $\varepsilon_n^{2(k-1)}$ is lifted to $\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}$.

Proof

Eliminate the first $k-2$ polynomial directions by a Schur complement. The Gaussian effective block on $N$, divided by $\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}$, converges to $c_{k-1}aa^\top$.

The exact mixture identity shows that the projected rare block converges to $q(P_Nv_\tau)(P_Nv_\tau)^\top$. Its coupling through the eliminated block is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)})$ and does not enter the displayed limit.

Since $P_Nv_\tau=\alpha a+\beta u$, the limiting two-dimensional matrix in the basis $(a,u)$ is

$$\begin{pmatrix} c_{k-1}+q\alpha^2 & q\alpha\beta\\ q\alpha\beta & q\beta^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Its trace is $T$ and its determinant is $qc_{k-1}\beta^2$, giving $\mu_\pm$. Spectral separation from the first $k-2$ eigenvalues and finite-dimensional self-adjoint perturbation complete the proof.

Four-output certificate

Take $c=1$, $x=(0,1,2,3)$, $\varepsilon_n=1/n$, $A_n=e^n$, and $p_n=e^{-2n}/n^4$. Here

$$a=\frac{(1,-1,-1,1)}2,\qquad u=\frac{(-1,3,-3,1)}{\sqrt{20}}.$$

The Gaussian penultimate coefficient is $0.041753467553130$. The two lifted coefficients are

$$(\mu_+,\mu_-)=(31.457133750367350, 0.001708086215243).$$

At $n=1024$, exact division of the third and fourth eigenvalues by $\varepsilon_n^4$ gives $(31.351218989988979, 0.001707940378909)$, or ratios $(0.996633044790, 0.999914620039)$ to the theorem.

The excess fixed $2c$ moment is $n^{-4}+o(n^{-4})$. Thus fixed-power moment convergence does not preserve even the smallest eigenvalue exponent when four outputs coalesce.

Scope and literature

This is a triangular-law theorem for nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It does not change fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank and does not infer rank by integrating different maturity loadings.

The displayed remainders are pointwise along sequences satisfying the two convergence assumptions. No finite-$n$ rate is claimed without quantitative rates for those assumptions.

Kato gives the classical self-adjoint spectral perturbation framework, while Gautschi treats confluent-Vandermonde geometry. The two-scale order-lifting boundary above is derived here and extends to a complete intermediate-scale staircase.
