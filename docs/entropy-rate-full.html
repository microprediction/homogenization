Rate-optimal finite quotes for terminal entropy

A second-moment bound removes the logarithmic quote-count factor; indistinguishable atomic laws prove the remaining power cannot improve.

Back to cycle direction · Papers · certificate.

Setup and result

Let $P,R$ be laws on $[0,\infty)$ with $\mathbb E_P S=\mathbb E_R S=1$ and $\max(\mathbb E_P S^2,\mathbb E_R S^2)\le M<\infty$. Write $C_P(K)=\mathbb E_P(S-K)_+$ and $D=\mathbb E_P[S\log S]-\mathbb E_R[S\log S]$, with $0\log0=0$.

For an integer $n\ge2$, use the ordered knot set $\{j^2/n^2:0\le j\le n\}\cup\{n^2/\ell^2:1\le\ell<n\}$. There are $2n-1$ positive strikes, from $n^{-2}$ to $n^2$, and no quote at zero is needed.

Let $f(s)=s\log s-s+1$, $f(0)=1$, and let $f_n$ be its chord interpolant through all knots, continued by the tangent to $f$ at $U=n^2$ above $U$. At each positive knot $K$, let $w_K$ be the upward jump of the slope of $f_n$.

$$A_n=\sum_Kw_K\{C_P(K)-C_R(K)\},\qquad W_n=\sum_Kw_K=1+4\log n,\qquad |A_n-D|\le E_n:=\frac{9/8+5M}{n^2}.$$

All weights are positive, so $\max_K|C_P(K)-C_R(K)|\ge (|D|-E_n)_+/W_n$. Thus $n=\max(2,\lceil\sqrt{(9/8+5M)/\varepsilon}\rceil)$ gives error at most $\varepsilon$ with $O(\varepsilon^{-1/2})$ quotes per curve at fixed $M$.

This improves the geometric-grid construction in the finite entropy-strip work when a second moment is available. It does not replace that result under only a positive moment of order strictly between one and two.

Explicit replication and signed error

If the knots are $0=K_0<K_1<\cdots<K_J=U$, put $b_i=(f(K_i)-f(K_{i-1}))/(K_i-K_{i-1})$. Then $b_1=\log K_1-1$, $w_{K_i}=b_{i+1}-b_i$ for $i<J$, and $w_U=\log U-b_J$.

$$f_n(s)=1+b_1s+\sum_Kw_K(s-K)_+.$$

Convexity gives the positivity of the weights and their telescoping sum $W_n$. Equal means cancel the cash and forward terms, while $\mathbb E f(S)=\mathbb E[S\log S]$.

On the first interval $[0,n^{-2}]$, the interpolation error is $s\log(n^{-2}/s)\le1/(en^2)$, including the zero endpoint by continuity. On any other interval $[a,b]$, the chord error is at most $(b-a)^2/(8a)$, since $f''(s)=1/s\le1/a$.

For the lower knots $a=j^2/n^2$, $b=(j+1)^2/n^2$, $j\ge1$, this gives $(b-a)^2/(8a)\le9/(8n^2)$. For upper knots $a=n^2/\ell^2$, $b=n^2/(\ell-1)^2$, $\ell\ge2$, use

$$\ell\left\{\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\right)^2-1\right\} =\frac{\ell(2\ell-1)}{(\ell-1)^2}\le6$$

to obtain $(b-a)^2/(8a)\le9s^2/(2n^2)$ throughout the cell. Above $U$, the tangent error is negative and its magnitude is at most $s^2/(2U)$: integrate $1/x\le1$ twice for $x=s/U\ge1$.

Consequently, for either law $L$, $-M/(2n^2)\le\mathbb E_L(f_n-f)\le(9/8+9M/2)/n^2$. Subtract these signed intervals to get $E_n$, rather than adding two complete absolute-error budgets.

For a single law define $\widehat H_n=1+b_1+\sum_Kw_K C(K)$. Subtracting $(9/16+2M)/n^2$ centers its error interval and gives worst-case entropy error at most $E_n/2$.

A matching fixed-design information lower bound

Fix $M>1$, set $\lambda=\min(1/4,M-1)$, and prescribe any $N$ nonnegative strikes, before observing the law. Among their gaps in $[1/2,3/2]$, including the two endpoints, choose one $[a,b]$ of width $h\ge1/(N+1)$ whose interior contains no strike.

Put $c=(a+b)/2$, $d=(1-\lambda c)/(1-\lambda)$, and construct $R=\lambda\delta_c+(1-\lambda)\delta_d$ and $P=(\lambda/2)(\delta_a+\delta_b)+(1-\lambda)\delta_d$. Both are nonnegative mean-one laws, and their calls agree exactly at every prescribed strike, since a call payoff is affine on $[a,b]$ unless its strike lies in the interior.

$$\mathbb E_R S^2=1+\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}(c-1)^2,\qquad \mathbb E_P S^2=\mathbb E_R S^2+\frac{\lambda h^2}{4} \le1+\frac{7\lambda}{12}\le M.$$

On this interval $f''\ge2/3$, so strong convexity gives $\mathbb E_P[S\log S]-\mathbb E_R[S\log S]\ge\lambda h^2/12$. Every estimator of one law's entropy from these exact quotes therefore has worst-case absolute error at least

$$\frac{\lambda}{24(N+1)^2}.$$

The estimator receives identical input for $P$ and $R$, hence must err by at least half their entropy gap on one of them. Together with the centered interpolant, this proves the optimal exponent $N^{-2}$ for noiseless, deterministic, fixed-strike designs on the stated moment class; constants and finite-$N$ designs are not asserted optimal.

This is a deterministic information bound, not a statistical sampling rate or a result about adaptive strike selection. The atomic witnesses need not be realizable as two reversed switched-CIR models, so the theorem does not settle the CIR crossing count in issue 204.

Quote errors and checks

If every call in each curve has error at most $\eta$, the exact-weight strip has additional error at most $2\eta W_n$. Thus reducing interpolation error indefinitely at fixed quote error is not guaranteed to improve this bound; errors in numerical weights, summation, moment bounds and forward normalization require separate budgets.

For illustration, $M=4.6$ and $\varepsilon=46\times10^{-6}$ give $n=725$, or $1449$ positive strikes, with $E_n\approx45.897741\times10^{-6}$ and $W_n\approx27.344687$. The strike range is approximately $[1.902497\times10^{-6},525625]$, a conservative theoretical design, not a market-feasibility claim.

The certificate checks exact rational moment bounds and quote aliasing for adversarial laws on arbitrary designs, numerical entropy separation, independent chord/vanilla replication and signed-error tests including default atoms, and worst-case quote perturbations. The rate assertion follows from the proof, not a fitted numerical slope.

Finite vanilla replication is classical: Bossu, Carr and Papanicolaou (2020), section 2.1, review the continuum identity and the finite-strike limitation. Ma, Deng and Zheng (2016), Theorem 3.1, already prove second-order spline replication on a fixed positive interval in a density-weighted norm.

Here the error is uniform over a moment class including atoms, the zero endpoint and an unbounded upper tail, and the lower bound concerns all estimators from fixed call quotes, not only splines. These displayed bounds are proved directly; there is no priority claim for static hedging or the classical spline order.
