One sufficient speed, even with missing transitions

For every finite irreducible regime chain, sufficiently fast switching gives a unique constant-fraction optimum below log utility, simultaneously at every positive horizon and every initial prior. A block minorization of the exact tilted chain supplies an explicit sufficient speed without requiring all direct transition rates to be positive.

The theorem

Let $Q$ be any irreducible finite row generator on at least two states and let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, $m>0$. Fix $0<\gamma<1$, $p=1-\gamma$, common constant interest rate $r$, excess returns $b_i$, and variances $s_i\ge s_{\min}>0$.

With Brownian stock noise independent of $Y$, hold one deterministic unrestricted fraction $w$ throughout the horizon and maximize its exact certainty-equivalent rate

$$h_i(w)=r+b_iw-\gamma s_iw^2/2,\qquad J_{m,T,\nu}(w)=\frac{\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{p\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)dt}}{pT}.$$

Put $q_i(w)=b_i-\gamma ws_i$ and $I=[L,U]$, with $L=\min_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)$ and $U=\max_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)$. Every maximizer lies in $I$: the derivative is positive below $L$ and negative above $U$, while the quadratic upper bound $J(w)\le r+\max_i|b_i||w|-\gamma s_{\min}w^2/2$ gives existence.

If $L=U$, the common fraction is already uniquely optimal at every speed. Otherwise compute the finite maxima

$$B=\max_{w\in I}\operatorname{osc}q(w),\qquad D=\max_{w\in I}\operatorname{osc}h(w),\qquad \delta=pD.$$

The maximum for $B$ uses interval endpoints; the maximum for $D$ also checks interior critical points of each quadratic difference $h_i-h_j$. Fix any block duration $\theta>0$ in the unscaled chain and any certified $a_0>0$ such that

$$P=e^{\theta Q},\qquad 0<a_0\le\min_j\frac{\min_i P_{ij}}{\max_i P_{ij}}\le1.$$

Irreducibility makes $P$ strictly positive, even for a sparse generator. The constant $a_0$ must be bounded rigorously, for example by nonnegative uniformization sums; a floating matrix exponential alone does not certify it.

For a constructive bound, choose $\lambda\ge\max_i(-Q_{ii})$, $\lambda>0$, and $F=I+Q/\lambda$. Any positive $i$-to-$j$ path of length $k$ in $F$ gives

$$P_{ij}\ge e^{-\lambda\theta}\frac{(\lambda\theta)^k}{k!} \prod_{\text{path edges }(u,v)}F_{uv}>0.$$

Use the length-zero path for $i=j$ and a positive path for every other pair. The minimum of these finitely many bounds is a valid $a_0$, since every $P_{ij}\le1$; columnwise upper bounds can sharpen it.

$$\tau=\theta/m,\qquad d=\delta\theta,\qquad \alpha_m=a_0e^{-d/m},\qquad \kappa_m=\gamma s_{\min}-\frac{pB^2\theta e^{d/m}}{2ma_0}.$$ $$J_{m,T,\nu}''(w)\le-\kappa_m\quad(w\in I),\qquad \text{for all }T>0\text{ and every prior }\nu.$$

In particular define $C=pB^2\theta/(2a_0\gamma s_{\min})$. Since $e^x\le(1-x)^{-1}$ for $0\le x<1$,

$$m>d+C\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \kappa_m\ge\gamma s_{\min}\left(1-\frac{C}{m-d}\right)>0.$$

Thus $m>d+C$ guarantees one global optimum for every horizon and prior. This is a conservative sufficient threshold, not a sharp transition; the optimizer may still depend on the horizon and prior, and concavity outside $I$ is not asserted.

Minorize the exact tilted block

Fix $w\in I$, set $A=mQ+pD_{h(w)}$, $z(u)=e^{uA}\mathbf1$, and $H=e^{\tau A}$. The Feynman–Kac formula entry by entry gives

$$e^{p\min h\,\tau}P_{ij}\le H_{ij}\le e^{p\max h\,\tau}P_{ij}.$$

Under the full path tilt proportional to $e^{p\int_0^T h(Y_t)dt}$, the exact kernel is $K^T_{s,t}=D_{z(T-s)}^{-1}e^{(t-s)A}D_{z(T-t)}$. Its initial law is $\widehat\nu_i=\nu_i z_i(T)/(\nu z(T))$, not generally $\nu$.

For a complete block ending at $t=s+\tau\le T$, let $v_j=\max_iP_{ij}$, $g=z(T-t)>0$, and $\mu_j=v_jg_j/\sum_kv_kg_k$. Its rows satisfy the common minorization

$$K^T_{s,t}(i,j)=\frac{H_{ij}g_j}{(Hg)_i} \ge e^{-\delta\tau}a_0\frac{v_jg_j}{\sum_kv_kg_k}=\alpha_m\mu_j.$$

The reference law $\mu$ depends on the block and the remaining horizon, but the mass $\alpha_m$ does not. Subtracting this common row part contracts oscillations by $1-\alpha_m$; incomplete blocks cannot increase oscillations, so

$$\operatorname{osc}(K^T_{s,t}f)\le (1-\alpha_m)^{\lfloor(t-s)/\tau\rfloor}\operatorname{osc}f.$$

When $\alpha_m=1$, use the convention $0^0=1$. This argument uses the exact finite-horizon kernel, not a stationary Perron approximation or an assumption that the tilted start remains stationary.

Integrate covariance, not a whole-path likelihood ratio

Write $R_w=\int_0^Tq_{Y_t}(w)dt$, $V=\int_0^Ts_{Y_t}dt$ and $\mathbb E_w$ for the tilted law. Direct differentiation gives $J''=-\gamma\mathbb E_w V/T+p\operatorname{Var}_w(R_w)/T$.

Conditioning at $s\le t$, Cauchy–Schwarz and the variance bound by one quarter of the squared range give

$$|\operatorname{Cov}_w(q(Y_s),q(Y_t))|\le \frac{B^2}{4}(1-\alpha_m)^{\lfloor(t-s)/\tau\rfloor}.$$

This holds for any initial law, including the reweighted prior above. Integrating over the ordered time triangle and then enlarging the lag interval gives

$$\frac{\operatorname{Var}_w(R_w)}{T}\le\frac{B^2}{2} \int_0^\infty(1-\alpha_m)^{\lfloor u/\tau\rfloor}du =\frac{B^2\tau}{2\alpha_m}.$$

Combining this with $V\ge Ts_{\min}$ proves the theorem. Strong concavity on $I$ and localization give uniqueness of the global optimum without any reversibility or diagonalization assumption.

A sparse rational example

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\0&-1&1\\1&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad b=(3/25,-2/25,1/20),\quad s=(1/25,9/100,1/16),\quad\gamma=1/2.$$

Take $\theta=1$ and write $F=Q+I$, a cyclic permutation matrix. The first three terms of $e^Q=e^{-1}\sum_{k\ge0}F^k/k!$ put every entry between $e^{-1}/2$ and $e^{-1}(1+2/9)$: the remaining series is at most $(1/6)\sum_{j\ge0}4^{-j}=2/9$.

$$a_0=9/22,\quad I=[-16/9,6],\quad B=7/20,\quad D=33/20,\quad d=33/40,\quad C=539/144,\quad d+C=3289/720<5.$$

Every $m\ge5$ therefore gives a unique optimum for every positive horizon and prior, with curvature modulus at least $311/150300$. Every $m\ge9$ has modulus at least $1/100$, since $d+2C=374/45<9$.

Certificate, literature and scope

The certificate checks the rational bounds and compares differentiated Feynman–Kac, tilted-moment and canonical-block calculations on sparse and defective generators. Floating consistency checks do not prove an all-horizon theorem or certify a numerically estimated transition lower bound; the comparison and geometric-series proof above do.

Block contraction for tilted Feynman–Kac kernels is classical; see Del Moral and Miclo (2002), Theorem 3.2 and Propositions 4.3–4.4. This note spells out a finite-state column-envelope specialization and its portfolio curvature constant, without a priority claim for the stability mechanism.

The result concerns one constant fraction and common constant $r$, not adaptive trading or filtered information. It is an exact finite-speed uniqueness bound, not a price approximation, an optimal sufficient speed, or a claim that the optimum is independent of maturity.
