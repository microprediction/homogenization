A three-valued tag identifies Gamma-sampled generators

An independent Poisson counter, stopped after its second tick, replaces a stored timestamp by one of three symbols.

Observation scheme and result

Let $Q$ be any finite time-homogeneous row generator on a complete known labeled state space, with a full-support initial law. Draw $T\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha,\lambda)$ independently of the entire chain, with unknown real shape $\alpha>0$ and rate $\lambda>0$. Run an independent Poisson process $N$ of known rate $s>0$, fixed in physical-time units, and retain only $(Y_0,Y_T,J)$ with $J=\min(N_T,2)$.

The population law identifies $(\alpha,\lambda,Q)$, with no shape bound or integer restriction. Reducible, absorbing and defective generators are included, without a rate cap, reversibility or diagonalizability. This is not recovery from one record, a minimal-category claim or a finite-sample guarantee.

No intermediate regime labels or Poisson tick times are retained. Equivalently, given $T=t$, assign symbols zero, one and two with probabilities $e^{-st}$, $st e^{-st}$ and $1-(1+st)e^{-st}$, then discard $T$. This is randomized duration tagging, not deterministic time binning or endogenous observation of regime jumps.

Calibrate both clock parameters

Let $M_j(i,k)=\Pr(Y_T=k,J=j\mid Y_0=i)$ for $j=0,1$. These are joint subprobability matrices, not transition matrices normalized within a tag. Their row sums, equivalently the marginal tag probabilities, are

$$p_0=\left(\frac\lambda{\lambda+s}\right)^\alpha,\qquad p_1=\frac{\alpha s}{\lambda+s}p_0.$$

Set $x=p_1/p_0$, $z=-\log(p_0)/x$, and $y=s/(\lambda+s)$. Then

$$z=\phi(y):=\frac{-\log(1-y)}y,\quad 0<y<1,\qquad \alpha=\frac xy,\quad \lambda=s\frac{1-y}y.$$

The function $\phi$ increases strictly from one to infinity, so this is a unique scalar inverse. Indeed $y^2\phi'(y)=y/(1-y)+\log(1-y)$ starts at zero and has derivative $y/(1-y)^2>0$. Every stipulated Gamma law has $p_0,p_1>0$ and $z>1$; these inequalities alone do not test the Gamma-family assumption.

A direct generator inverse

Put $C=(\lambda+s)I-Q$. Gamma integration gives

$$M_0=\frac{\lambda^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}e^{-tC}\,dt, \qquad M_1=\frac{s\lambda^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^\infty t^\alpha e^{-tC}\,dt.$$

All eigenvalues of $C$ have positive real part. Integration by parts therefore has vanishing boundary terms at zero and infinity for every $\alpha>0$, and yields $CM_1=\alpha sM_0$.

To see invertibility without assuming a simple spectrum, put $C$ in complex triangular form. The diagonal entries of the two matrix integrals are the nonzero scalar Gamma integrals $\Gamma(\alpha)c^{-\alpha}$ and $\Gamma(\alpha+1)c^{-\alpha-1}$ for $\Re c>0$. Thus both $M_0$ and $M_1$ are invertible, including for Jordan blocks.

$$\boxed{Q=(\lambda+s)I-\alpha sM_0M_1^{-1}.}$$

No matrix logarithm, fractional root, eigenvalue matching or block-Hankel inversion is needed in this reconstruction. The Gamma–Poisson count mixture is classical; see Hilbe (2007), Introduction, and Higham (2008) for the matrix-function background. The displayed specialization is proved directly, without a priority claim for those ingredients.

An exact discarded-time alias is separated

For $\alpha=7$, $\lambda=s=1$, let $P=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3$, $E=I-P$ and

$$J_0=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \theta=\pi/7,\quad a=10\cos\theta-1,\quad b=10\sin\theta,\quad Q_\pm=-aE\pm bJ_0.$$

Both generators have positive off-diagonal rates, since $a>\sqrt3 b$. The identities $J_0^2=-E$ and $PJ_0=J_0P=0$ give $(I-Q_\pm)^{-7}=P-10^{-7}E$, so discarding time and tags produces identical endpoint kernels. The zero-tick matrices instead have infinity-norm gap $3.451335327169\times10^{-8}$.

The certificate recovers the generator from the tagged law. In this example $\log_{10}\operatorname{cond}_\infty(M_1)=6.015$; this is an example-specific value, not a uniform conditioning bound. The pair are time reversals, so no difference in their symmetric Green–Kubo strengths or fixed-feature ranks is claimed.

Scope and an exact instability boundary

Even with a fixed known Gamma clock and known $s$, the inverse is not uniformly continuous over unbounded generator speeds. For $Q_c=c(P-I)$ on two states, $P=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/2$, and the same uniform initial law, the full endpoint/tag laws obey

$$\|\mathcal L(Q_c)-\mathcal L(Q_{2c})\|_{\rm TV} =\frac12\left\{\left(\frac\lambda{\lambda+c}\right)^\alpha- \left(\frac\lambda{\lambda+2c}\right)^\alpha\right\}\longrightarrow0, \qquad \|Q_c-Q_{2c}\|_\infty=c\longrightarrow\infty.$$

For each tag $j$, its endpoint matrix is $p_jP+v_j(c)(I-P)$ with $v_j(c)=\mathbb E[e^{-cT}\Pr(J=j\mid T)]$ nonnegative and decreasing in $c$. Summing the nonnegative differences across all three tags gives the displayed Laplace-transform difference. Hence exact invertibility alone gives no uniform accuracy or sample-efficiency guarantee.

The Gamma-family, independence and complete-label assumptions are essential to the stated derivation, not fitted conclusions. If the auxiliary rate $s$ is unknown too, simultaneously scaling $(Q,\lambda,s)$ by the same positive constant preserves the record law and loses physical-time scale. State-dependent or adaptive clocks, elapsed-time stopping, noisy labels and arbitrary unknown duration families are outside scope.

The result concerns full labeled endpoint laws, not an identification of a chain from Green–Kubo contractions alone. It changes neither fixed-feature Gram-rank bounds nor the distinction from rank after integrating maturity-dependent loadings.

The certificate checks an exact rational defective case, real-shape clock fits, independent tag integrals, joint recoveries and the alias and reset examples. It ends with literal PASS and uses portable assertion tolerances; its high-precision floating checks are not outward-rounded interval certificates.
