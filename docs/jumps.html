Vasicek with jumps at a switching intensity

An intensity that jumps upward, more often in one regime than the other.

The model

A default intensity $x_t$ mean-reverts, diffuses, and jumps upward. The jumps arrive at a rate set by a hidden regime $y_t \in \{1, 2\}$, as do the mean level and volatility:

$$dx_t = \kappa\,(\theta_{y_t} - x_t)\,dt + \sigma_{y_t}\,dW_t + dJ_t .$$

While $y_t = i$, $J$ jumps at rate $\ell_i$ by exponentially distributed amounts with mean $m$.

The regime switches at rate $\lambda$ in each direction. The intensity reverts over $1/\kappa$ and the regime changes every $1/\lambda$.

Parameters: $\kappa = 2$, $\theta = (0.05, 0.02)$, $\sigma = (0.02, 0.01)$, $\ell = (3, 0.2)$ and $m = 0.03$: the first regime is a stressed one, with frequent jumps.

What is computed

The survival probability

$$u_i(t,x) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{-\int_0^t x_r\,dr} \mid x_0 = x,\ y_0 = i\big].$$

Averaging

When the regime switches infinitely fast the limit is a Vasicek intensity with averaged mean and variance, plus jumps at the average rate $\bar\ell = \tfrac12(\ell_1 + \ell_2)$.

Reduction to a linear system

By the Feynman–Kac formula the prices $u_i(t,x)$, one for each starting regime, solve the coupled equations

$$\partial_t u_i = \kappa(\theta_i - x)\,\partial_x u_i + \tfrac12\sigma_i^2\,\partial_{xx}u_i - x\,u_i + \ell_i\,\mathbb{E}\big[u_i(t, x + J) - u_i(t, x)\big] + \sum_j Q_{ij}\,u_j, \qquad u_i(0,x) = 1.$$

The last term is the regime switching: it couples the equation for regime $i$ to the others at the switching rates. Try the Vasicek form $u_i = e^{-B(t)x}a_i(t)$. Then $\partial_x u_i = -B\,u_i$ and $\partial_{xx}u_i = B^2u_i$, and after dividing by $e^{-Bx}$ the terms proportional to $x$ are

$$-B'\,x = \kappa B\,x - x .$$

They cancel for every regime when $B' = 1 - \kappa B$, that is $B(t) = (1 - e^{-\kappa t})/\kappa$. Because $\kappa$ is the same in every regime, one $B$ works for all of them. What remains involves $t$ only.

The jump term needs one more step. A jump of size $J$ multiplies $e^{-Bx}$ by $e^{-BJ}$, and for exponential jumps with mean $m$

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-BJ}\big] = \frac{1}{1 + mB}.$$

So the jumps change only the time function. The prices are $u_i = e^{-B(t)x}a_i(t)$ with $a' = (Q + \operatorname{diag} g)a$, $a(0) = \mathbf 1$ and

$$g_i(t) = -\kappa\theta_i B + \tfrac12\sigma_i^2 B^2 + \ell_i\Big(\frac{1}{1 + m B} - 1\Big).$$

The expansion

Let the regime switch at rate $\lambda$ in each direction, and write

$$\varepsilon = \frac1\lambda, \qquad \bar g = \tfrac12(g_1 + g_2), \qquad \tilde g = \tfrac12(g_1 - g_2),$$

where $g_i$ is the function above. The average $\bar g$ is what the averaged model sees. The half-difference $\tilde g$ measures how much the two regimes disagree.

First order

To first order in $\varepsilon$,

$$a_i(t) = \exp\Big(\int_0^t \bar g + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^t \tilde g^{\,2}\Big)\Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde g(t)\Big) + O(\varepsilon^2),$$

with the upper sign for a start in regime 1. The three factors have separate meanings, explained on the idea page.

  • $e^{\int\bar g}$ is the averaged model.
  • $\frac\varepsilon2\int\tilde g^2$ is half the variance of the fluctuating integral of $g$. It is the same for both starting regimes, and it grows with $t$.
  • The bracket is the memory of the starting regime. The switch stays near its starting state for about $1/(2\lambda)$, which shifts the integral by $\pm\tilde g/(2\lambda)$.

Every order

Add and subtract the two equations for $a_1$ and $a_2$. The mean $m = \tfrac12(a_1 + a_2)$ and the normalized difference $\omega = (a_1 - a_2)/(a_1 + a_2)$ then satisfy

$$\omega' = \tilde g\,(1 - \omega^2) - 2\lambda\,\omega, \qquad \omega(0) = 0,$$ $$\log m(t) = \int_0^t \big(\bar g + \tilde g\,\omega\big), \qquad a_{1,2} = m\,(1 \pm \omega).$$

The first equation has a large restoring term $-2\lambda\omega$: the switch pulls the two regimes together at rate $2\lambda$, while $\tilde g$ pushes them apart. So $\omega$ stays close to the value that balances the two, and expanding in $\varepsilon$ finds that value order by order. Writing $\omega = \sum_n \varepsilon^n\omega_n$ and matching powers gives

$$\omega_1 = \frac{\tilde g}{2}, \qquad \omega_{n+1} = -\frac12\Big(\omega_n' + \tilde g\sum_{i+j=n}\omega_i\,\omega_j\Big).$$

This is the two-state case of the solvability argument: at each order the restoring term can be inverted, which here is a division by $2\lambda$.

The series does not satisfy $\omega(0) = 0$. An initial layer of width about $1/(2\lambda)$ repairs the start and is solved order by order in the same way. The engine page gives the version for any number of regimes.

The jump term is not a sum of exponentials, so the engine holds $g_i$ as a Chebyshev series.
