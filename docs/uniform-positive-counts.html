A positive count approximation at every maturity

A uniform marginal error bound, not a full-process approximation.

Let a finite irreducible chain have generator $mQ$, with arrival intensities $0<a\leq\ell_i\leq b$. At each horizon $T$, replace the integrated intensity by a gamma variable with its exact prior-specific mean and variance.

The resulting negative-binomial Cox count is nonnegative and normalized and matches the exact first two count moments. Constant intensity is exactly Poisson.

$$\sup_{T>0}\sup_\nu d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L_\nu(N_T),\mathcal L(N_{*,T,\nu})) \leq\min\left\{1,\frac{A_2}{(m\gamma)^2} +\frac{A_3}{(m\gamma)^3}+\frac{A_4}{(m\gamma)^4}\right\}.$$

The full note gives explicit constants from a semigroup envelope $\|e^{tQ}-\mathbf1\pi\|_\infty\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}$. No reversibility, diagonalizability or stationary start is required.

The certificate checks 168 count laws over six horizons, including a defective chain. For symmetric switching, intensities $(3,1)$ and speed 80, the common all-horizon bound is $0.0125223590$; the pure first-state error at $T=100$ is $0.0000006892$.

The full note extends the uniform rate to nonnegative intensities, including zero-intensity regimes, with separate explicit constants. Constants depend on fixed mixing data and a positive stationary mean; no vanishing-gap uniformity is claimed. Even from stationarity, simultaneous batches obstruct second-order path-TV accuracy for the standard independent-increment negative-binomial process.
