Two factors and two-name credit

Default dependence created by a common hidden regime, and where it enters the expansion.

The model

Two companies have default intensities $x_1$ and $x_2$. Each follows its own Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, driven by independent Brownian motions, but both take their parameters from one hidden regime $y_t \in \{1, 2\}$, a credit cycle:

$$dx_{j} = \kappa_j\,(\theta_{j,y_t} - x_j)\,dt + \sigma_{j,y_t}\,dW_j, \qquad j = 1, 2 .$$

The regime switches at rate $\lambda$ in each direction.

There are two time scales: each intensity reverts over $1/\kappa_j$, and the cycle turns every $1/\lambda$.

Parameters: $\kappa = (1, 1.5)$, the first company has $\theta_1 = (0.06, 0.01)$, the second $\theta_2 = (0.05, 0.015)$, both have $\sigma = (0.02, 0.005)$ in the two regimes, and $x_0 = (0.03, 0.03)$.

The same algebra prices two-factor Gaussian short rates.

The quantity

Survival of each company and of both, to time $t = 3$,

$$S_c(t) = \mathbb{E}\Big[e^{-\int_0^t (c_1 x_1 + c_2 x_2)\,dr}\Big], \qquad c = (1,0),\ (0,1),\ (1,1),$$

and from them the correlation of the two default indicators, $(p_{12} - p_1p_2)/\sqrt{p_1(1-p_1)\,p_2(1-p_2)}$, with $p_1 = 1 - S_{(1,0)}$, $p_2 = 1 - S_{(0,1)}$ and $p_{12} = 1 - S_{(1,0)} - S_{(0,1)} + S_{(1,1)}$.

Averaging

When the cycle turns infinitely fast each intensity is a Vasicek process with averaged parameters, and the two are independent. The averaged model has no default correlation at all.

Reduction to a linear system

The prices solve a Feynman–Kac equation in $x_1$, $x_2$ and the regime, with the potential $c_1x_1 + c_2x_2$. Try one Vasicek factor per intensity, $S_c = e^{-B_1x_1 - B_2x_2}\,a(t)$. The terms in $x_j$ cancel when $B_j' = c_j - \kappa_jB_j$, which gives

$$B_j(t) = c_j\,\frac{1 - e^{-\kappa_j t}}{\kappa_j}.$$

Neither $B_j$ depends on the regime, because the mean-reversion speeds do not switch. What remains is

$$a' = \big(Q + \operatorname{diag} g(t)\big)\,a, \quad a(0) = \mathbf 1, \qquad g_i = \sum_j\big(-\kappa_j\theta_{j,i}B_j + \tfrac12\sigma_{j,i}^2B_j^2\big).$$

The expansion

Let the regime switch at rate $\lambda$ in each direction, and write

$$\varepsilon = \frac1\lambda, \qquad \bar g = \tfrac12(g_1 + g_2), \qquad \tilde g = \tfrac12(g_1 - g_2),$$

where $g$ is the function above for the chosen $c$. The average $\bar g$ is what the averaged model sees. The half-difference $\tilde g$ measures how much the two regimes disagree.

First order

To first order in $\varepsilon$,

$$a_i(t) = \exp\Big(\int_0^t \bar g + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^t \tilde g^{\,2}\Big)\Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde g(t)\Big) + O(\varepsilon^2),$$

with the upper sign for a start in regime 1. The three factors have separate meanings, explained on the idea page.

  • $e^{\int\bar g}$ is the averaged model.
  • $\frac\varepsilon2\int\tilde g^2$ is half the variance of the fluctuating integral of $g$. It is the same for both starting regimes, and it grows with $t$.
  • The bracket is the memory of the starting regime. The switch stays near its starting state for about $1/(2\lambda)$, which shifts the integral by $\pm\tilde g/(2\lambda)$.

Every order

Add and subtract the two equations for $a_1$ and $a_2$. The mean $m = \tfrac12(a_1 + a_2)$ and the normalized difference $\omega = (a_1 - a_2)/(a_1 + a_2)$ then satisfy

$$\omega' = \tilde g\,(1 - \omega^2) - 2\lambda\,\omega, \qquad \omega(0) = 0,$$ $$\log m(t) = \int_0^t \big(\bar g + \tilde g\,\omega\big), \qquad a_{1,2} = m\,(1 \pm \omega).$$

The first equation has a large restoring term $-2\lambda\omega$: the switch pulls the two regimes together at rate $2\lambda$, while $\tilde g$ pushes them apart. So $\omega$ stays close to the value that balances the two, and expanding in $\varepsilon$ finds that value order by order. Writing $\omega = \sum_n \varepsilon^n\omega_n$ and matching powers gives

$$\omega_1 = \frac{\tilde g}{2}, \qquad \omega_{n+1} = -\frac12\Big(\omega_n' + \tilde g\sum_{i+j=n}\omega_i\,\omega_j\Big).$$

This is the two-state case of the solvability argument: at each order the restoring term can be inverted, which here is a division by $2\lambda$.

The series does not satisfy $\omega(0) = 0$. An initial layer of width about $1/(2\lambda)$ repairs the start and is solved order by order in the same way. The engine page gives the version for any number of regimes.

For two names the first-order term gives the dependence directly. The function $g$ for $c = (1,1)$ is the sum of the single-name functions, so $\tilde g_{(1,1)} = \tilde g_1 + \tilde g_2$ and

$$\log\frac{S_{(1,1)}}{S_{(1,0)}\,S_{(0,1)}} = \frac{1}{\lambda}\int_0^t \tilde g_1(r)\,\tilde g_2(r)\,dr + O(\lambda^{-2}),$$

where $\tilde g_j$ is company $j$'s regime half-difference. It is positive when the cycle moves both companies the same way.
