A positive Cox-count corrector

Keep the overdispersion correction without losing a probability distribution.

For a Cox count $N\mid\Lambda\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda)$, write $\mu=\mathbb E\Lambda>0$ and $v=\operatorname{Var}\Lambda$. Replace $\Lambda$ by a gamma variable with the same mean and variance; the resulting count is negative binomial, with generating function

$$G_*(z)=\left(1+\frac v\mu(1-z)\right)^{-\mu^2/v}.$$

This is nonnegative and normalized at every speed and matches $\mathbb EN=\mu$ and $\operatorname{Var}N=\mu+v$ exactly. If $v=0$, use the exact Poisson law instead.

A second-order distributional error

For a stationary two-state chain flipping at rate $\lambda$ in each direction, with nonnegative intensities $\ell_1,\ell_2$, put $\bar\ell=(\ell_1+\ell_2)/2>0$ and $\delta=(\ell_1-\ell_2)/2$. At horizon $T>0$, use the exact moments

$$\mu=\bar\ell T,\qquad v=\delta^2\left\{\frac T\lambda-\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda T}}{2\lambda^2}\right\}.$$
$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(N),\mathcal L(N_*))\leq \min\left\{1,\frac{\delta^4}{\lambda^2} \left(2T^2+\frac{4T}{3\bar\ell}\right) +\frac{2\delta^6T}{\bar\ell^2\lambda^3}\right\}.$$

Thus the approximation is $O(\lambda^{-2})$ at fixed intensities and horizon, while remaining a probability law for every $\lambda>0$. The constants are conservative, not sharp, and this is not a maturity-uniform bound.

The full note also proves the same second-order rate uniformly over all starting priors under symmetric switching, with a separate explicit bound and exact prior-specific mean and variance.

With intensities $(8,1)$, stationary start and $T=1$, at $\lambda=40$ the Poisson approximation has total-variation error $0.0160856124$, versus $0.0005052802$ for the negative binomial. The rigorous explicit bound there is $0.2182043065$; the last measured error order is $1.962215$.

The full note proves a general moment comparison bound, then specializes it to the telegraph rate. This is a fixed-horizon count approximation, not an exact gamma law for the integrated intensity, or a model for the full arrival process; unequal switching rates are outside the explicit speed bounds.
