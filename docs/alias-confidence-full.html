Alias-safe finite-state snapshot confidence

Labeled snapshots can certify a finite-state generator without a supplied mixing rate or speed cap, when an observable gate excludes logarithm aliases. The certificate combines anytime transition-row intervals, a determinant lower bound and a finite matrix-logarithm series with an explicit tail. It deliberately abstains outside a sufficient local identification region.

Setup and the computable certificate

Let $Q$ be a fixed finite irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ known states, and observe $X_k=Y_{kh}$ at fixed known spacing $h>0$. The initial state is arbitrary, not necessarily stationary; put $P=e^{hQ}$. Every norm below is the induced maximum absolute row-sum norm $\|\cdot\|_\infty$.

Fix $0<\delta<1$ before observing data, and suppose a stochastic estimate $\widehat P_K$ and a data-dependent budget $\eta_K$ satisfy $\Pr_{Q,x}\{\|\widehat P_K-P\|_\infty\le\eta_K\text{ for all }K\ge0\}\ge1-\delta$ for every initial state $x$. The transition-count construction below supplies this event without unknown model parameters.

$$\rho_K=\|I-\widehat P_K\|_\infty,\qquad d_K=\det\widehat P_K-n\eta_K,\qquad \boxed{\rho_K+\eta_K<1,\quad d_K>e^{-\pi}.}$$

Report only when both strict tests pass. For any integer $m\ge1$, possibly chosen from the record, define

$$L_{K,m}=-\frac1h\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{(I-\widehat P_K)^j}{j},\qquad R_{K,m}=\frac{\eta_K}{h(1-\rho_K-\eta_K)} +\frac{\rho_K^{m+1}}{h(m+1)(1-\rho_K)}.$$
$$\boxed{\|L_{K,m}-Q\|_\infty\le R_{K,m},\qquad \max_i(-Q_{ii})\le-\frac{\log d_K}{h}.}$$

These conclusions hold simultaneously at every passing sample count on the original confidence event. $L_{K,m}$ has zero row sums but may have negative off-diagonal entries. If a valid row generator $\overline Q$ is required, clip its off-diagonals to nonnegative values and reset the diagonal, then report the budget $R_{K,m}+\|\overline Q-L_{K,m}\|_\infty$.

Why the determinant excludes aliases

For stochastic matrices $A,B$, multilinearity telescopes $\det A-\det B$ into $n$ determinants with one row difference each. All other rows have Euclidean norm at most one, while each difference row has Euclidean norm at most $\|A-B\|_\infty$. Hadamard's inequality therefore gives $|\det A-\det B|\le n\|A-B\|_\infty$.

On the confidence event, $\det P\ge d_K>e^{-\pi}$. For any compatible row generator $G$ with $e^{hG}=P$, the real identity $\det P=e^{h\operatorname{tr}G}$ implies $h\sum_i(-G_{ii})=-\log\det P\le-\log d_K<\pi$.

Thus $h\max_i(-G_{ii})<\pi$, and Gershgorin's disks put every eigenvalue of $hG$ in the strip $|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi$. The principal-logarithm uniqueness theorem gives $hG=\log P$, including repeated eigenvalues and Jordan blocks. In particular the true $Q$ is the unique compatible generator, and the displayed exit-rate bound follows.

This is the classical determinant/strip argument, not a new embedding theorem. See Davies (2010), Theorem 11, and Higham (2008), Theorem 1.31 and Chapter 11 for principal-logarithm uniqueness without a simple-spectrum assumption.

The error bound does not assume commuting matrices

Suppress $K$ and write $A=I-\widehat P$, $B=I-P$, $\beta=\rho+\eta<1$. The power-series logarithms converge and $A^j-B^j=\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}A^r(A-B)B^{j-1-r}$ gives $\|A^j-B^j\|_\infty\le j\eta\beta^{j-1}$.

Consequently $\|\log\widehat P-\log P\|_\infty\le\eta/(1-\beta)$. The omitted tail at $\widehat P$ is at most $\sum_{j>m}\rho^j/j\le\rho^{m+1}/\{(m+1)(1-\rho)\}$. Divide by $h$ and use $\log P=hQ$ to obtain $R_{K,m}$.

Anytime transition-matrix budgets from the snapshots

Let $V_i(K)=\sum_{k<K}\mathbf1\{X_k=i\}$ and $N_{ij}(K)=\sum_{k<K}\mathbf1\{X_k=i,X_{k+1}=j\}$, including $j=i$. For each of the $n^2$ entries, use

$$M_{ij,K}(u)=\frac{\mathrm B(N_{ij}+1,V_i-N_{ij}+1)}{u^{N_{ij}}(1-u)^{V_i-N_{ij}}},\qquad [l_{ij},r_{ij}]=\{u\in[0,1]:M_{ij,K}(u)\le n^2/\delta\}.$$

Use continuous endpoint limits with $0^0=1$, and $[0,1]$ for an unvisited row. The log mixture is convex, so the interval endpoints are scalar root solves, with explicit endpoints for zero or all successes. Set $\widehat P_{ij}=N_{ij}/V_i$ when $V_i>0$, and use the identity row $e_i^T$ otherwise.

$$\eta_K=\min\left(2,\ \max_i\sum_j \max\{|\widehat P_{ij}-l_{ij}|,|\widehat P_{ij}-r_{ij}|\}\right).$$

At the true $u=P_{ij}\in(0,1)$, each fixed alternative $v\in(0,1)$ multiplies by $v/u$ when the selected row is $i$ and the next state is $j$, by $(1-v)/(1-u)$ on the other outcomes in that row, and by one otherwise. The conditional mean is one in $\sigma(X_0,\ldots,X_K)$ because row selection is predictable. Integrating uniformly over $v$ produces $M_{ij,K}(P_{ij})$, a nonnegative martingale initially one.

Ville's inequality and the $n^2$-entry union bound give simultaneous inclusion at all $K$ with probability at least $1-\delta$. Summing entry errors yields the stated norm event; the cap two is the diameter of stochastic rows. Neither row independence nor a binomial law conditional on the random visit count is assumed.

This specializes classical mixture confidence sequences; see Howard et al. (2021), Section 3.2 and Proposition 7. The mixing distribution is fixed in advance and is not a prior on the true transition probabilities.

Usefulness, checks and limits

For a fixed model with $\|I-P\|_\infty<1$ and $\det P>e^{-\pi}$, ergodicity gives $V_i\to\infty$ and $N_{ij}/V_i\to P_{ij}$ almost surely. For $\widehat u=N_{ij}/V_i$, the identity $\log M(u)=\log M(\widehat u)+V_i\operatorname{kl}(\widehat u,u)$ and Stirling's formula $\log M(\widehat u)=-\tfrac12\log V_i+O(1)$ give interval widths $O(\sqrt{\log V_i/V_i})$, so $\eta_K\to0$ and the gate eventually passes almost surely. Choosing $m\to\infty$ then makes the reported generator radius tend to zero, including the clipping cost.

Every fixed finite generator satisfies those strict population tests for sufficiently small fixed $h$. No uniform time-to-accuracy claim, eventual passage at arbitrary spacing, or identification outside the gate is asserted. Repeated reports and data-selected stopping are valid on the simultaneous event, not conditionally on the event of reporting.

With cyclic three-state transition counts $(900000,80000,20000)$ per row, $V_i=1000000$, $h=.2$, $\delta=.05$ and $m=24$, the certificate gives $\eta=.003588796$, $\rho=.2$, $d=.714433613>e^{-\pi}=.043213918$ and generator radius $.022531047$ (display-rounded). The balanced count matrix is realizable by an Eulerian transition record; these are prescribed counts, not a coverage simulation.

The certificate checks 378 exact rational martingale means, 42 first-passage laws over horizons 1–7 and 48 exact determinant perturbations. Its 63 matrix-logarithm checks cover seven dense, sparse nonreversible and exactly defective chains; the largest error/budget ratio is $.986121506$. A three-cycle snapshot alias is also checked and rejected by the gate.

Floating evaluations are not outward-rounded arithmetic certificates; roundoff is not part of the statistical budget. Known labels, the complete state space, fixed spacing and a time-homogeneous generator are essential assumptions here. This is model-based initial-state-conditional confidence, not feature-conditional conformal coverage or a grid-to-continuous occupation correction.
