A smooth clock can erase a generator

One known phase-type clock makes a continuum of distinct three-state generators produce the same iid sampled labels, while their physical-time Green–Kubo strengths differ.

Observation protocol and result

Sample a finite continuous-time chain at iid positive intervals, independently of the chain, retaining all regime labels including repeats. The interval law is known, but realized durations, clock-component tags and total elapsed time are discarded; the number of observations is fixed. The sampled transition kernel is $R(Q)=\mathbb E e^{\tau Q}$.

Use the fixed clock

$$\tau\sim\frac1{4097}\operatorname{Erlang}(1,1) +\frac{4096}{4097}\operatorname{Erlang}(13,1),$$

where the second argument is a rate. Its density is strictly positive and analytic on $(0,\infty)$, with all moments finite:

$$f_\tau(t)=\frac{e^{-t}}{4097}\left(1+\frac{4096t^{12}}{12!}\right),\qquad \mathbb E\tau=\frac{53249}{4097},\quad \mathbb E\tau^2=\frac{745474}{4097}.$$

There is a continuum of strictly positive-rate generators $Q_\epsilon$, all with uniform stationary law, for which

$$\boxed{R(Q_\epsilon)=P,\qquad P=\tfrac13\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top, \qquad |\epsilon|\le1/100.}$$

Thus stationary sampled labels are exactly iid uniform. From any common initial distribution $\nu$, every fixed-count label-path probability is $\nu_{i_0}\prod_{j=1}^m(1/3)$, regardless of $\epsilon$. This is exact loss of generator information, not merely fast mixing or a small numerical difference.

Explicit generator and cancellation

Put $\theta=\pi/12$, $a=2\cos\theta-1$, $b=2\sin\theta$ and

$$u=a/3+b/\sqrt3,\qquad v=a/3-b/\sqrt3,\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}-u-v&u&v\\v&-u-v&u\\u&v&-u-v\end{pmatrix}.$$

Both rates are positive: $u=0.609475708249$ and $v=0.011758726803$. With $E=I-P$ and $J=(Q+aE)/b$, direct multiplication gives $J^\top=-J$, $J^2=-E$, $JP=PJ=0$.

The rate-one exponential-clock kernel $C=(I-Q)^{-1}$ is

$$C=P+\tfrac12(\cos\theta\,E+\sin\theta\,J),\qquad C^{12}=P-\frac{E}{4096}.$$

An Erlang clock is a sum of independent exponential stages, so its sampled kernel is the corresponding power of $C$. Hence

$$R(Q)=\frac{C+4096C^{13}}{4097}=P.$$

Equivalently, the clock transform $F(z)=[(1+z)^{-1}+4096(1+z)^{-13}]/4097$ vanishes at $z=a\pm ib$. A smooth positive sampling density therefore need not prevent complete spectral cancellation.

A continuum, not just reversal of a cycle

Let $h=(1,0,-1)^\top$, $H=hh^\top/2$, $S_\epsilon=I+\epsilon H$ and

$$Q_\epsilon=S_\epsilon Q S_\epsilon^{-1}.$$

Since $S_\epsilon\mathbf1=\mathbf1$ and $\mathbf1^\top S_\epsilon=\mathbf1^\top$, row sums and the uniform stationary law are preserved. Also $HJH=0$, giving

$$Q_\epsilon-Q=b\left(\epsilon HJ-\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}JH\right).$$

For $g=(1,-2,1)^\top$, one has $HJ=-hg^\top/(2\sqrt3)$ and $JH=gh^\top/(2\sqrt3)$.

Every entry changes in magnitude by at most $b(|\epsilon|+|\epsilon|/|1+\epsilon|)/\sqrt3$. For the entire displayed interval every off-diagonal rate is therefore at least

$$v-\frac b{\sqrt3}\left(\frac1{100}+\frac1{99}\right) =0.005751369263>0.$$

Similarity commutes with resolvents and powers, and $S_\epsilon P S_\epsilon^{-1}=P$. Consequently $R(Q_\epsilon)=P$ throughout the interval. The family is nonconstant and isospectral, hence its distinct members are not proportional generators.

The same fixed feature has different physical-time strength

For the fixed centered feature $f=(1,-1,0)^\top$, define the stationary Green–Kubo variance rate

$$\Gamma_\epsilon =2\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(f(Y_0),f(Y_t))dt =\frac23 f^\top\{(P-Q_\epsilon)^{-1}-P\}f.$$

Writing $s=1+\epsilon$, direct inversion on the centered plane gives

$$\Gamma_\epsilon= \frac{4a+\sqrt3b(s^{-1}-s)}{3(a^2+b^2)}.$$

This is strictly decreasing in $\epsilon$ on the displayed interval. At $\epsilon=-0.01,0,0.01$ the strengths are respectively $1.098723804540$, $1.093437018467$ and $1.088202839848$.

These are fixed-feature strengths, not ranks after integrating different maturity loadings. The fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank theorem remains valid: the same feature span and stationary law are used throughout, so no change of fixed-feature rank is claimed.

A clock tag restores identification; small weight errors give near-erasure

If the Erlang-component tag is retained, the conditional kernel for the positive-probability shape-one component is $C_\epsilon=(I-Q_\epsilon)^{-1}$. It identifies $Q_\epsilon=I-C_\epsilon^{-1}$ without timestamps. No sample-efficiency claim is made for this rare component.

For any valid perturbed shape-one weight $w$, put $w_*=1/4097$ and $R_w=wC_\epsilon+(1-w)C_\epsilon^{13}$. Since both powers are stochastic,

$$\|R_w-P\|_\infty\le2|w-w_*|.$$

Two members of the family under the same perturbed clock have row total-variation distance at most $2|w-w_*|$. Sequential coupling therefore bounds their $m$-transition label-path total variation by $\min\{1,2m|w-w_*|\}$ from any common initial law.

Certificate, literature and scope

The certificate checks the rotation/resolvent cancellation and clock moments exactly, plus 21 generators, an independent augmented phase-chain resolvent, three direct semigroup integrals and 63 clock-weight perturbations. It checks the strength formula and prints every numerical headline with portable tolerances, ending in literal PASS.

The clock is a 13-phase Coxian absorption time: after the first rate-one exponential stage it stops with probability $1/4097$, otherwise completing twelve more stages. Bladt (2005), Definition 3.1 and Examples 3.2–3.4, provides the classical phase-type framework, distinct from a positive mixture of exponential rates. The cancellation and similarity family above are derived directly, not attributed to that review.

This counterexample concerns known iid clocks with discarded realized times and tags, not unknown-clock confounding or finite-sample estimation. It does not contradict generator identification for known positive exponential-rate mixtures; the phase-type transform here has complex zeros. Elapsed-time stopping, retained durations and state-dependent clocks are outside the observation protocol.
