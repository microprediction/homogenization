Rare tails create a Gram spectral staircase

At every intermediate scale, one rare direction promotes exactly one extra eigenvalue.

The penultimate-scale order lift is one step of a complete hierarchy. A rank-one tail at scale $\varepsilon^{2r}$ leaves the first $r$ eigenvalue coefficients intact, but makes both of the next two eigenvalues live at that scale.

Intermediate-scale theorem

Fix $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, and an integer $1\le r\le k-2$. Let $G$ be standard Gaussian and

$$P_n=(1-p_n)\mathcal L(G)+p_n\delta_{A_n},\qquad \varepsilon_n\downarrow0,\quad A_n\longrightarrow\infty,\quad p_n\longrightarrow0.$$

Suppose, for finite $\tau>0$ and $q>0$, that

$$\varepsilon_n\log A_n\longrightarrow\tau,\qquad \frac{p_nA_n^{2c}}{\varepsilon_n^{2r}}\longrightarrow q.$$

Let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$. Put $\phi_s=(x_j^s/s!)_{j=1}^k$,

$$E_r=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r-1}\},\qquad N_r=E_r^\perp,$$

and let $P_r$ be orthogonal projection onto $N_r$. Define

$$a_r=\frac{P_r\phi_r}{\|P_r\phi_r\|_2},\qquad w_r=P_rv_\tau,\qquad v_\tau=(e^{\tau x_j})_{j=1}^k.$$

Let $c_j=\{(R_c)_{jj}(L_x)_{jj}\}^2$ be the Gaussian spectral constants, with one-based indices, from the exact coalescing-Gram theorem. Set $\alpha_r=a_r^\top w_r$, $\beta_r^2=\|w_r\|_2^2-\alpha_r^2$, and

$$T_r=c_{r+1}+q\|w_r\|_2^2,\qquad \mu_{r,\pm}=\frac{T_r\pm\sqrt{T_r^2-4qc_{r+1}\beta_r^2}}2.$$
$$\frac{\lambda_j(K_n)}{\varepsilon_n^{2(j-1)}}\longrightarrow c_j\quad(1\le j\le r),$$ $$\lambda_{r+1}(K_n)=\mu_{r,+}\varepsilon_n^{2r}+o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}),\qquad \lambda_{r+2}(K_n)=\mu_{r,-}\varepsilon_n^{2r}+o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}).$$

Eigenvalues are ordered decreasingly. Exponential divided differences give $\beta_r^2>0$, so the Gaussian order $\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)}$ of $\lambda_{r+2}$ is promoted to $\varepsilon_n^{2r}$.

Why the staircase has two steps at one level

Eliminate $E_r$ by a Schur complement. After division by $\varepsilon_n^{2r}$, the Gaussian effective block on $N_r$ converges to $c_{r+1}a_ra_r^\top$.

The exact mixture covariance identity adds $q w_rw_r^\top$; every coupling through $E_r$ is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2r})$. The limiting effective matrix is therefore

$$M_r=c_{r+1}a_ra_r^\top+qw_rw_r^\top.$$

Its only nonzero eigenvalues are $\mu_{r,+}$ and $\mu_{r,-}$. They are both positive because $w_r$ is not collinear with $a_r$: otherwise the exponential data at the distinct nodes would agree with a polynomial of degree at most $r$, contradicting positivity of the next divided difference.

Weyl's inequality preserves the first $r$ Gaussian coefficients because the rare block has norm $O(\varepsilon_n^{2r})$. Spectral separation and finite-dimensional self-adjoint perturbation then give the displayed limits.

Five-output middle-step certificate

Take $c=1$, $x=(-2,-1,0,1,2)$, $r=2$, $\varepsilon_n=1/n$, $A_n=e^n$, and $p_n=e^{-2n}/n^4$. Here

$$a_2=\frac{(2,-1,-2,-1,2)}{\sqrt{14}},\qquad c_3=0.146137136435956.$$

The predicted coefficients for the third and fourth eigenvalues are

$$(\mu_{2,+},\mu_{2,-})=(7.894885176600677, 0.012431942202026).$$

At $n=1024$, exact division by $\varepsilon_n^4$ gives $(7.872514362209916,0.012434054561630)$, or ratios $(0.997166416751,1.000169913885)$ to the theorem.

The excess fixed $2c$ moment is $n^{-4}+o(n^{-4})$. Repeating the construction for $r=1,\ldots,k-2$ places the order lift at any chosen step of the coalescing spectrum.

Scope and literature

This is a triangular-law theorem for nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained by integrating different maturity loadings.

Every remainder is pointwise along sequences satisfying the two displayed convergence assumptions. No finite-$n$ rate is asserted without quantitative rates for those assumptions.

Kato supplies the self-adjoint perturbation framework, and Gautschi treats confluent-Vandermonde geometry. The intermediate rare-tail staircase is derived here.
