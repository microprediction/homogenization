A hard cutoff is not a Green–Kubo Gram

A sharp twelve-state threshold within stationary-normal chains, and what the Bartlett window preserves.

Let $Q$ be an irreducible finite row generator, $\pi$ its stationary law, and $F$ fixed real centered features. For the stationary population correlation $C(t)=F^TD_\pi e^{tQ}F$, distinguish

$$K=\int_0^\infty C(t)dt,\qquad R_T=\int_0^T C(t)dt,\qquad B_T=\int_0^T(1-t/T)C(t)dt.$$

The symmetric part of $R_T$ can be negative, even without sampling error. In contrast $2T\operatorname{sym}B_T$ is exactly the covariance of $\int_0^T F(Y_s)ds$; its positivity and fixed-feature kernel are the existing finite-horizon covariance result. The triangular Bartlett window is the finite-time Einstein–Helfand identity, not a new positivity principle.

Sharp hard-cutoff threshold for normal chains

Assume now that $Q$ is normal in $L^2(\pi)$: $QQ^*=Q^*Q$, where $Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^TD_\pi$. Normality is stronger than diagonalizability but does not require reversibility. If the chain has at most eleven states, then $\operatorname{sym}R_T\succeq0$ for every $T>0$ and every real $F$.

Twelve is sharp in this class: a uniform one-way twelve-cycle already has a negative scalar $R_T$. This is not a minimum-state assertion for nonnormal chains.

Indeed, the scalar spectral contributions are positive multiples of $e^{-at}\cos(bt)$, $a>0$. For $b>0$,

$$r_T(a,b)=\int_0^T e^{-at}\cos(bt)dt =\frac{a+e^{-aT}[-a\cos(bT)+b\sin(bT)]}{a^2+b^2}.$$

The first local minimum is at $T=3\pi/(2b)$ and all later local minima are larger. Thus $r_T(a,b)\ge0$ for every $T\ge0$ exactly when

$$\kappa e^{-3\pi/(2\kappa)}\le1,\qquad\kappa=b/a.$$

Real eigenvalues contribute a positive integral. The classical generator eigenvalue sector gives $|b|/a\le\cot(\pi/n)$; see Davies (2010), proof of Theorem 9, equations (5)–(6). Normality makes the scalar spectral weights nonnegative, so this eigenvalue statement suffices here.

Here are elementary strict bounds certifying the integer threshold, without a floating root decision. For $0<x<\pi/2$, $\tan x>x+x^3/3$, and $\pi>31/10$ gives $\cot(\pi/11)<7/2$. A finite exponential series with a geometric tail proves $7/2<e^{13/10}<37/10$.

$$\cot(\pi/11)\log\cot(\pi/11)<91/20<3\pi/2, \qquad (2+\sqrt3)\log(2+\sqrt3)>481/100>3\pi/2.$$

The last comparison uses $2+\sqrt3>37/10$ and $\pi<22/7$. Since $\cot(\pi/12)=2+\sqrt3$, the twelve-cycle crosses the threshold; every longer one-way cycle does too.

Exact twelve-cycle benchmark

Let $Q=P-I$, where $P$ advances once around twelve states, take uniform $\pi$, and set $f_j=\cos(\pi j/6)$. Then $C_f(t)=\tfrac12e^{-at}\cos(bt)$, with $a=1-\sqrt3/2$, $b=1/2$.

$$T=3\pi,\qquad K_f=1/4,\qquad R_{T,f}=\frac{a-\tfrac12e^{-3\pi a}}{2(a^2+1/4)}\approx-0.01394427, \qquad B_{T,f}\approx0.44947098.$$

The Bartlett excess is strict analytically: for $z=a-ib$, $\operatorname{Re}[(1-e^{-Tz})/z^2]=(a^2-b^2-2ab e^{-aT})/(a^2+b^2)^2<0$. Adding reverse cycle rate $1/1000$ and uniform refresh rate $1/1000$ makes every off-diagonal rate positive and retains the negative hard cutoff at its first minimum. Its mode ratio exceeds $369/100>e^{13/10}$, so the same rational sign argument applies.

Thus a population hard cutoff can violate positivity, while the positive Bartlett form can exceed the long-run form. The reversible upper bound $\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(Z_T)\preceq2T\operatorname{sym}K$ therefore does not extend even to normal irreversible chains. Main already restricts that upper bound to reversible chains; this is a sharp counterexample to the stronger extension, not an error in main.

The Bartlett sector survives for normal chains

For the same normal-chain assumption, write $G_T=\operatorname{sym}B_T$, $A_T=\operatorname{skew}B_T$ and $\kappa_Q=\max_{-a+ib\in\operatorname{spec}Q,\,a>0}|b|/a$. Then for every $T>0$ and every real feature embedding,

$$-\kappa_QG_T\preceq iA_T\preceq\kappa_QG_T, \qquad\kappa_Q\le\cot(\pi/n).$$

The inequalities are Hermitian and hold also when the feature Gram is singular. In particular, two independent features retain $|(A_T)_{12}|\le\cot(\pi/n)\sqrt{\det G_T}$. No such finite-window sector theorem is asserted here for nonnormal generators.

To prove it set $z=a-ib$ and

$$b_T(z)=\frac1z-\frac{1-e^{-Tz}}{Tz^2},\qquad s(T)=\int_0^T e^{-at}\sin(bt)dt\ge0\quad(b>0).$$

The nonnegativity follows by checking local minima at $2k\pi/b$. Fubini gives $\operatorname{Im}b_T=T^{-1}\int_0^T s(u)du\ge0$, and direct algebra gives $b\operatorname{Re}b_T-a\operatorname{Im}b_T=s(T)/T\ge0$. Hence $|\operatorname{Im}b_T|\le(|b|/a)\operatorname{Re}b_T$; normal spectral calculus and feature congruence prove the matrix inequalities.

Scope and checks

These are population, stationary, fixed-feature statements, not positivity guarantees for arbitrary empirical lag estimates. They do not count regimes from rank after integrating different maturity loadings. Normal spectral calculus is essential to the stated threshold and sector proofs.

The certificate checks the rational threshold bounds, the exact cycle formula against independent matrix quadrature, circulant and nonuniform-stationary normal chains, and a strictly positive-rate perturbation of the negative example. Floating consistency checks are not interval enclosures; the threshold proof is analytic.

The classical covariance/correlation conversion is discussed in Viscardy and Gaspard (2003), Appendix B. Neither that conversion nor the generator eigenvalue sector is claimed new.
