Finite-history calibration without a panel-size penalty

Truncated exponential ages give a marginal rank-error budget independent of calibration size, under a stationary one-contrast emission model.

Setup and coverage envelope

Let $X_t$, $t\in\mathbb R$, be a bounded, jointly measurable, strictly stationary real signal with $\mathbb E X_0=0$. Conditional on the full signal and the sampling times, all score emissions are independent with genuine continuous CDFs

$$F_{X_t}(s)=\bar F(s)+X_tH(s).$$

The functions $\bar F,H$ are fixed, and $\bar F$ is the stationary pooled CDF. Any stationary binary regime process with two continuous emission CDFs has this form; three or more arbitrary CDFs need not. No Markov property, ergodicity, mixing or path continuity is required.

Fix $N\ge1$, $\beta,L>0$ and $1\le k\le N$. Independently of the signal, draw labeled calibration ages $A_i$ iid from $\operatorname{Exp}(\beta)$ conditioned on $A_i\le L$, where $\beta$ is a rate. Observe calibration scores at $-A_i$ and a test score at zero, and let $Q_k$ be their $k$th calibration order statistic.

Put $\delta=e^{-\beta L}$ and $p=k/(N+1)$. Then the marginal coverage $C=\Pr(S_{\rm test}\le Q_k)$ satisfies

$$\boxed{(1-\delta)p\le C\le(1-\delta)p+\delta,\qquad -\delta p\le C-p\le\delta(1-p).}$$

In fact the test-rank law is $(1-\delta)\operatorname{Unif}\{1,\ldots,N+1\}+\delta\nu$ for some probability law $\nu$ on those ranks. Its total-variation distance from the uniform law is at most $\delta$. These are valid envelopes, not a claim of optimality over the model class.

Finite-window filter identity

Define the bounded stationary filter

$$U_t=\frac{\beta}{1-\delta}\int_0^L e^{-\beta a}X_{t-a}\,da,\qquad \dot U_t=\frac{\beta}{1-\delta} [X_t-\delta X_{t-L}-(1-\delta)U_t]\quad\hbox{almost everywhere}.$$

The convolution has absolutely continuous paths. Write the expression in square brackets as $D_t$; for any continuously differentiable $\varphi$ and $T>0$, the integrated chain rule and Fubini give

$$0=\mathbb E[\varphi(U_T)-\varphi(U_0)] =\frac{\beta}{1-\delta}\int_0^T\mathbb E[\varphi'(U_t)D_t]\,dt =\frac{\beta T}{1-\delta}\mathbb E[\varphi'(U_0)D_0].$$

The last equality uses joint stationarity, not differentiation at a prescribed exceptional time. Every continuous function on the compact range of $U$ is a derivative of such a $\varphi$, so these identities imply $\mathbb E[D_0\mid U_0]=0$. Consequently

$$\mathbb E[X_0\mid U_0]=(1-\delta)U_0+\delta\mathbb E[X_{-L}\mid U_0].$$

Why only the boundary weight enters

Conditional on the entire signal, independent labeled ages and emission noise make the calibration scores iid with continuous CDF $G_U=\bar F+U_0H$. This conditional joint law depends on the signal only through $U_0$, so calibration and test scores remain independent given $U_0$. The test CDF conditional on $U_0$ is exactly

$$\mathbb E[F_{X_0}(s)\mid U_0] =(1-\delta)G_U(s)+\delta B_U(s),\qquad B_U(s)=\bar F(s)+\mathbb E[X_{-L}\mid U_0]H(s).$$

Both $G_U$ and $B_U$ are genuine continuous CDFs: each is a conditional mixture of emission laws. In the first mixture component, all $N+1$ scores are iid given $U_0$, so the test rank is uniform without ties. The second component has an arbitrary rank law $\nu_U$; averaging over $U_0$ proves the stated mixture and coverage envelope.

Sorting the ages changes neither $Q_k$ nor the result, but the chronological score vector need not be exchangeable. The probability averages over signal, schedule and scores: it is not coverage conditional on the observed calibration sample, realized times or current regime. Nonstationary starts, overlapping path scores and a score map fitted using this panel are outside the assumptions.

Size of the improvement and a nonzero bias

At $\beta=1$, $L=10$, $N=k=9$, the boundary weight is $4.53999297625\times10^{-5}$. The undercoverage budget is $4.08599367862\times10^{-5}$ and the overcoverage budget is $4.53999297625\times10^{-6}$, giving

$$0.8999591400632\le C\le0.9000045399930.$$

Coupling the entire truncated age panel with untruncated iid exponential ages gives the valid but looser absolute-error budget $1-(1-\delta)^N$. In this example it is $0.0004085251741918$; the filter argument avoids charging once per calibration point.

Truncation does not generally retain exact ranks. For the stationary two-state chain $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-7&7\\3&-3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with emissions uniform on $[0,1]$ and $[2,3]$, $\beta=1$, $L=\log2$ and $N=k=2$, the exact coverage is $6708133/10137600=0.661708195234$, not $2/3$.

Certificate and literature

The certificate checks 27 exact filter-moment identities and 108 exact rank-mixture identities through $N=8$, using rational matrix-polynomial evaluation at $L\in\{\log2,2\log2,5\log2\}$. An independent floating-point matrix exponential checks the moment calculations, and the certificate prints every numerical headline above.

For $Z_t=\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=0\}}$ and $V_t=\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)}Z_s\,ds$, the row moments satisfy $m_j'=m_j(Q-jI)+jm_{j-1}\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$, with separate initial rows retaining the boundary state. Integrating the binomial rank tail over the two uniform emission intervals gives exact polynomials in $W=V_L/(1-\delta)$. The all-$N$ statement follows from the proof, not these finite checks.

Barber, Candès, Ramdas and Tibshirani (2023) bound conformal coverage loss using total-variation distances under swaps of training and test observations. The argument here instead identifies a model-specific boundary mixture; it is not a distribution-free guarantee for arbitrary stationary scores.
