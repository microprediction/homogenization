Endpoint interaction in slow-fast chains

Finite-rate bounds that distinguish endpoint weights from the interaction transmitting filter memory.

A finite-state theorem

Let $Q$ be an irreducible generator on finitely many hidden states, with stationary row $\pi$, and $P_0=\mathbf1\pi$. Suppose known constants $A,g>0$ satisfy $\|e^{tQ}-P_0\|_\infty\le Ae^{-gt}$ for every $t\ge0$. Throughout, $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is maximum absolute row sum.

The observed state $x$ also takes finitely many values. In observed-state-major ordering, let $G_m=m(I\otimes Q)+L$ for $m>0$, where $L$ is any fixed Markov generator on the joint state space and $\ell=\|L\|_\infty$. This includes regime-specific observed jump generators and bounded simultaneous observed/hidden jumps.

Write $S(t)=e^{tG_m}$, $P=I\otimes P_0$, $C=I-P$, $\alpha=mg$ and $u=A\ell/\alpha$. Let $\bar L_{xy}=\pi L_{xy}\mathbf1$ and $R(t)=e^{t\bar L}\otimes P_0$. Both $S$ and $R$ are stochastic, so their infinity norms are one.

$$\begin{aligned} \|CS(t)\|_\infty&\le Ae^{-\alpha t}+u(1-e^{-\alpha t}),\\ \|CS(t)P\|_\infty,\ \|PS(t)C\|_\infty&\le u,\\ \|CS(t)C\|_\infty&\le b(t) :=Ae^{-\alpha t}+A^2\ell t e^{-\alpha t} +u^2[1-e^{-\alpha t}-\alpha t e^{-\alpha t}],\\ \|S(t)-R(t)\|_\infty&\le\eta(t) :=Ae^{-\alpha t}+u(2+\ell t). \end{aligned}$$

The doubly centered block is second order away from the initial layer, although each singly centered block is only first order. No reversibility or diagonalizability of $Q$ is required; a spectral gap without a valid prefactor $A$ is not a substitute for the stated semigroup bound.

Two Duhamel formulas prove the bounds

Put $U(t)=e^{mt(I\otimes Q)}$. The left and right variation-of-constants identities are

$$S(t)=U(t)+\int_0^tU(t-s)LS(s)\,ds =U(t)+\int_0^tS(s)LU(t-s)\,ds.$$

Since $\|CU(t)\|_\infty=\|U(t)C\|_\infty\le Ae^{-\alpha t}$, the first identity and $\|S(s)\|_\infty=1$ give the displayed bound on $CS(t)$. Multiplication by $P$ removes the unperturbed term for $CS(t)P$; the second identity does the same for $PS(t)C$. The resulting convolution is at most $u$ in either case.

Centering the second identity on both sides gives

$$\|CS(t)C\|_\infty\le Ae^{-\alpha t} +A\ell\int_0^t[Ae^{-\alpha s}+u(1-e^{-\alpha s})]e^{-\alpha(t-s)}\,ds=b(t).$$

Finally, $R(t)=e^{tPLP}P$ and

$$PS(t)-R(t)=\int_0^tR(t-s)LCS(s)\,ds.$$

Bounding this integral and adding $CS(t)$ proves $\eta(t)$. The proof uses bounded finite matrices, not an unbounded diffusion generator or a formal eigenvalue expansion.

From joint transitions to an observation kernel

For an observed transition $x\to y$ over time $t$, the hidden-state observation matrix is the block $K=S(t)_{xy}$. Choose the positive reference probability $p=(e^{t\bar L})_{xy}$, and set $M=K/p$, $a=M\mathbf1$, $b=\pi M$, $s=\pi a$. The interaction remaining after removing endpoint weights is $D=M-ab/s$.

With $C_0=I-P_0$, the exact algebraic identity is

$$K-\frac{(K\mathbf1)(\pi K)}{\pi K\mathbf1} =C_0KC_0-\frac{(C_0K\mathbf1)(\pi KC_0)}{\pi K\mathbf1}.$$

Extracting the relevant blocks of the semigroup bounds therefore gives

$$\delta:=\|M-P_0\|_\infty\le\frac{\eta(t)}p,\qquad \|D\|_\infty\le\frac1p\left[b(t)+\frac{u^2}{\pi K\mathbf1}\right].$$

Thus the second-order interaction is a consequence of the model, not an extra tangent assumption. In particular, if $M=P_0+A_1/m+O(m^{-2})$ exists, then $C_0A_1C_0=0$ automatically for fixed positive observation spacing and reference probability.

A uniform panel budget

Restrict all observation spacings to $[\tau,T]$ with $\tau>0$, and all used reference probabilities to $p\ge p_*>0$. The following constants are computable before seeing the hidden filter:

$$\delta_*=\frac{Ae^{-\alpha\tau}+u(2+\ell T)}{p_*},\qquad \Gamma_*=\frac{A(1+A\ell T)e^{-\alpha\tau} +u^2\left[1+\{p_*(1-\delta_*)\}^{-1}\right]}{p_*}.$$

If $\delta_*<1/3$, every used kernel satisfies $\delta\le\delta_*$ and $\|D\|_\infty\le\Gamma_*$, because $\pi K\mathbf1\ge p_*(1-\delta_*)$. Put $\rho=2\delta_* /(1-\delta_*)$ and $\xi=\min\{\rho,\Gamma_* /(1-\delta_*)^2\}$.

For a panel of $N$ transitions and integer memory $r\ge0$, keep the first $r+1$ predictive factors exact. Thereafter predict with the filter obtained from just the preceding $r$ kernel updates, restarted from $\pi$. For any specified initial hidden prior, the exact and restarted log likelihoods obey

$$|\ell_F-\ell_r|\le\frac{(N-r-1)_+}{2}\rho^2\xi^r.$$

Indeed, $F(\nu)=\nu M/(\nu a)=b/s+\nu D/(\nu a)$ has row $\ell^1$ Lipschitz constant at most $\Gamma_* /(1-\delta_*)+\delta_*\Gamma_* /(1-\delta_*)^2=\Gamma_* /(1-\delta_*)^2$, as well as the generic bound $\rho$. The exact filter is within $\rho$ of $\pi$ after any update, and each predictive log factor is $\rho/2$-Lipschitz. Restarting therefore costs at most $\rho\xi^r$ in the filter and $\rho^2\xi^r/2$ in each remaining log factor.

Every restarted predictive factor sums to one over the next observed state, so the construction defines a normalized observation law, positive on the paths under consideration.

For fixed $\tau,T,p_*,Q,L$ and fixed $r$, the budget is $O(Nm^{-2r-2})$, uniformly in panel length. One retained interval gives a quartic rather than cubic inverse-speed bound; it uses two transitions and hence three consecutive observed states. This is an approximation theorem, not an efficiency or conditional-coverage guarantee.

Verification and limits

The certificate checks all semigroup and block inequalities, including a nonreversible cycle and simultaneous jumps, and compares matrix exponentials with independent uniformization. A switched three-state observation model has measured interaction and one-interval log-gap orders near two and four. Floating computations are regression checks, not outward-rounded enclosures.

The finite-dimensional averaging framework is classical; see Yin and Zhang (2013), whose title, authors, edition and contents were checked on the publisher's page. The bounds above are proved directly, without importing a theorem from the inaccessible full book or asserting priority.

The constants are not uniform as a diffusion discretization is refined: $\ell$ may diverge and $p_*$ may vanish. Nor is fixed-spacing forgetting uniform at spacings of order $m^{-1}$: even with $L=0$, $K=e^{t mQ}$ retains an order-one initial layer there. Extending this result to a continuous-state diffusion requires separate density estimates.
