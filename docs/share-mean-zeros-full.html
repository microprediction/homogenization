How often can the share-mean reversal vanish?

An exact maturity-zero bound from a finite moment system; four regimes can have exactly one positive zero.

For a fixed switching speed, if the share-measure first-moment matrix has real spectrum, a nonzero share-weighted log-return mean gap has at most $n-3$ positive maturity zeros, counted with multiplicity. This is a bound on one scalar observable as maturity varies, not on call-price crossings as strike varies.

Model and exact scalar reduction

Let a finite irreducible chain have row generator $mQ$, fixed $m>0$, stationary initial law $\pi$, and be independent of the original Brownian motions. Compare it with $mQ^*$, where $Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^\top D_\pi$, at the same fixed variance $v_0$ and with

$$dv_t=(a_{Y_t}-\kappa_{Y_t}v_t)dt+\xi_{Y_t}\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t,\qquad dX_t=-\tfrac12v_tdt+\sqrt{v_t}(\rho_{Y_t}dB_t+\sqrt{1-\rho_{Y_t}^2}\,dW_t).$$

Assume positive $a_i,\kappa_i,\xi_i$, $|\rho_i|\le1$, a well-defined nonnegative variance process, a true stock martingale $e^{X_t}$, and finite share-weighted log-return means at the maturities considered. For an all-maturity conclusion require these integrability and martingale conditions at every finite maturity.

$$k_i=\kappa_i-\rho_i\xi_i,\quad A=mQ-\operatorname{diag}k,\quad \mathcal L(M)=\langle a,M\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,Ma\rangle_\pi,$$ $$D(T)=\mathbb E_Q[e^{X_T}X_T]-\mathbb E_{Q^*}[e^{X_T}X_T] =\tfrac12\int_0^T(T-u)\mathcal L(e^{uA})du.$$

Here $\langle x,y\rangle_\pi=x^\top D_\pi y$. Under the stock numéraire, the regime intensities stay $mQ$, variance reversion becomes $k_i$, and the log-return drift becomes $v/2$.

Indeed the share-measure variance-weighted regime masses satisfy $z'=(mQ^\top-\operatorname{diag}k)z+\operatorname{diag}(a)\pi^\top$, $z(0)=v_0\pi^\top$. Weighted adjointness cancels the $v_0$ contribution between orientations; integrating the forcing twice proves the formula.

A bound including repeated eigenvalues and Jordan blocks

Let $p_A$ be the minimal polynomial of $A$ and $r=\deg p_A\le n$. If all roots of $p_A$ are real, then either $D\equiv0$, or $r\ge3$ and

$$\#\{T>0:D(T)=0\}\ \text{counted with multiplicity}\ \le r-3\le n-3.$$

No normality or diagonalizability is assumed. The spectral hypothesis is on $A$, not merely on the un-tilted generator $Q$, and the speed remains fixed.

For the proof put $F(T)=\mathcal L(e^{TA})$. The identity $p_A(A)=0$ gives $p_A(\partial_T)F=0$; since $D''=F/2$,

$$\partial_T^2p_A(\partial_T)D=0.$$

Stationarity gives $\mathcal L(I)=\mathcal L(A)=0$, so $D(0)=D'(0)=D''(0)=D'''(0)=0$. Hence every nonzero $D$ has a zero of order at least four at the origin.

A real constant-coefficient differential equation of order $N$ with real characteristic roots has at most $N-1$ zeros of a nonzero solution on any finite interval, counting multiplicities. To see this, divide by one positive exponential and differentiate: the order falls by one, while Rolle's theorem loses at most one zero. Induction proves the claim, including repeated roots; if the derivative is identically zero, the original solution is a nonzero exponential and has no zeros.

Apply this with $N=r+2$ on $[0,H]$ and subtract the four origin zeros. Since $H$ is arbitrary, the bound holds on the whole positive half-line. In particular $r\le2$ forces $D\equiv0$.

There is a sharper finite test: if $\ell=\min\{j:\mathcal L(A^j)\ne0\}$, then $2\le\ell\le r-1$ and the origin order is $\ell+2$. The positive-zero bound improves to $r-\ell-1$; if all $j=0,\ldots,r-1$ vanish, then $D\equiv0$.

With complex spectrum put $\Omega=\max_{\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}A}|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|$. The same bound holds on $(0,H]$ whenever $H\Omega<\pi$, by the extended-Chebyshev theorem of Aldaz, Kounchev and Render (2009), Theorem 9 in the authors' preprint. This is a bounded-window conclusion, not a global complex-spectrum zero count.

For the unit one-way four-cycle at speed one, the skew part of $A$ has norm one, independent of the real killing vector $k$. Writing $K=(Q-Q^\top)/2$, the matrix $K^\top K$ is a nonzero orthogonal projection, so $\Omega\le1$ by the eigenvector Rayleigh identity. Hence a nonzero signal has at most one zero on $(0,\pi)$ without a real-spectrum assumption; the certificate checks the opposite signs at $.6$ and $.8$ for the earlier four-cycle fixture.

Complete four-regime criterion under stable real spectrum

For four states assume additionally that $A$ has strictly negative real eigenvalues; $k_i>0$ is sufficient for stability. Set $\alpha=\mathcal L(A^2)$ and $\beta=\mathcal L(A^3)-\operatorname{tr}(A)\alpha$.

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-zT}D(T)dT ={\alpha z+\beta\over2z^2\det(zI-A)},\qquad z>0.$$

The numerator has degree at most one because $\mathcal L(I)=\mathcal L(A)=0$; its two coefficients follow by expanding the resolvent at infinity. The zero-count theorem implies that a nonzero $D$ has at most one positive zero, and any such zero is simple.

If $\alpha\ne0$, its initial sign is the sign of $\alpha$, since $D(T)=\alpha T^4/48+O(T^5)$. If $\beta\ne0$, its eventual sign is the sign of $\beta$, since $D(T)=\beta T/[2\det(-A)]+O(1)$.

Consequently there is exactly one positive zero if and only if $\alpha\beta<0$. If one coefficient is zero and the other is nonzero there is no positive zero: the initial sign is $\beta$ when $\alpha=0$, while the limiting constant is $\alpha/[2\det(-A)]$ when $\beta=0$. Both coefficients zero is precisely $D\equiv0$.

A strict-Feller example attaining the four-state bound

Take the one-way cycle with exit rates $(1/100,1,2,3)$, so $\pi=(600,6,3,2)/611$, and fix $m=1$, $v_0=.04$,

$$\kappa_i=2,\quad\rho_i=-3/5,\quad\xi=(1/5,2/5,1,3/2),\quad a=(5,2,8,2),\quad k=(53/25,56/25,13/5,29/10).$$

The Feller margin is $\min_i(2a_i-\xi_i^2)=7/4>0$. For return moment $s$ and constant variance loading $b$, the supersolution coefficient is $\xi_i^2b^2/2+(\rho_i\xi_i s-\kappa_i)b+(s^2-s)/2$.

At $(s,b)=(2,1/2)$ and $(-1/10,1/10)$ its maxima are $-23/200$ and $-499/4000$. Thus $e^{bv+C_sT}$, with $C_s=\max_i a_i b$, bounds the localized moment process at every finite horizon. These give a two-sided moment strip and uniform integrability of the localized stock, verifying the assumed martingale and moment scope.

$$\det(zI-A)=\prod_{r\in\{213/100,81/25,23/5,59/10\}}(z+r)-3/50.$$

At each pair $z=-r\pm1/10$ this polynomial has opposite signs. The four disjoint negative intervals therefore contain all four roots, proving simple entirely real spectrum by exact rational inequalities, not an eigensolver.

$$\alpha=-297/15275<0,\qquad\beta=1323/58750>0,$$ $$D(1)\in[-0.000014866529,-0.000014866528],\qquad D(2)\in[0.000006419034,0.000006419035].$$

There is therefore exactly one positive maturity zero, in $(1,2)$. Its numerical estimate is $1.8471791789$; uniqueness comes from the theorem, not a sampled sign plot.

The certificate encloses the two means using independent six-dimensional forward systems and degree-160 rational Taylor actions. Each directional output error is at most $3^L L^{161}/(2\cdot161!)$, $L=\lceil\|TM\|_\infty\rceil$, using initial norm at most one and output norm $1/2$. The summed bounds are below $5.4\times10^{-115}$ and $5.2\times10^{-65}$ at the two horizons.

Checks, literature and limits of the result

Thirty-six additional cases compare a doubled-integral semigroup, the forward first-moment system, and the independent polynomial-moment implementation on main. A repeated-eigenvalue defective chain checks cancellation; exact arithmetic checks the fixture's characteristic identity, spectral intervals, numerator, Feller condition and moment supersolutions.

Finite-dimensional moment closure is classical; see Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012). The real-exponent Rolle argument is also classical and appears in the proof of the cited extended-Chebyshev theorem; the application here reserves four zeros at the origin of this reversal observable.

This is an exact fixed-speed theorem, not a truncated homogenization expansion. It does not prove a sharp global call-crossing count in issue 204, nor identify full call curves from their share-weighted means. Complex-spectrum signals may have additional zeros outside the stated short window.
