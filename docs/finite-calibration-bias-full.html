Finite calibration and latent path bias

An exact rank baseline, a beta-averaged first correction, and a computable finite-horizon remainder.

For independent stationary calibration paths, the empirical order statistic preserves exact marginal rank coverage but changes the leading starting-regime bias. A beta average replaces the population Gaussian density factor; it strictly attenuates the leading bias at the same marginal level without eliminating it.

Independent paths, not overlapping calibration windows

Fix a finite irreducible row generator $Q$, its invariant row law $\pi$, and a real state feature $f$. For each independent path define

$$A_c={1\over c}\int_0^c f(Y_t)dt,\qquad S_c=\mu A_c+\sigma Z, \quad Z\sim N(0,1),\quad\sigma>0,\quad a=\mu/\sigma.$$

The Gaussian noises are mutually independent and independent of the chains. Take $N$ iid calibration scores with initial law $\pi$, and an independent test path whose initial state is $i$. Let $\widehat q_k$ be the $k$th calibration order statistic, $1\le k\le N$, and put

$$C_i(c;N,k)=\Pr_i(S_c\le\widehat q_k),\qquad p_N={k\over N+1}.$$

This probability averages over the calibration sample and the test path while conditioning on the test's starting regime. It is not coverage conditional on the realized calibration panel, test features or an entire observed history. The score map is fixed; fitting on calibration labels and dependent or overlapping calibration paths are excluded.

The exact rank-to-bias transfer

Let $F_i$ be the test CDF, $F_\pi=\sum_i\pi_iF_i$, and $q_\pi(u)=F_\pi^{-1}(u)$. Positive Gaussian smoothing makes $F_\pi$ continuous and strictly increasing. Uniform order statistics give

$$U=F_\pi(\widehat q_k)\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,N+1-k),$$ $$\boxed{C_i-p_N=\mathbb E\big[F_i(q_\pi(U))-U\big]},\qquad \sum_i\pi_iC_i=p_N\quad\text{exactly}.$$

The beta identity and marginal rank validity are classical, not new coverage guarantees. If a nominal level $p$ is implemented with $k=\lceil(N+1)p\rceil\le N$, the exact marginal baseline is $p_N$, not $p$. The usual $k=N+1$ convention uses $+\infty$, giving coverage one and zero latent gap instead of a beta law.

The first latent correction

$$\widetilde f=f-(\pi f)\mathbf1,\qquad h=Q^\#\widetilde f,\qquad QQ^\#=Q^\#Q=I-\mathbf1\pi,\quad\pi Q^\#=0.$$

For fixed $Q,f,a,N$ as $c\to\infty$, uniformly over states and the finite set of ranks,

$$\boxed{C_i(c;N,k)-p_N={a h_i\over c}J_{N,k}+O(c^{-2})},\qquad J_{N,k}=\mathbb E\,\phi(\Phi^{-1}(U)).$$

The population threshold at level $p_N$ has factor $\phi(z_{p_N})$ instead. Put $j(u)=\phi(\Phi^{-1}(u))$ with $j(0)=j(1)=0$; then $j'(u)=-\Phi^{-1}(u)$ and $j''(u)=-1/\phi(\Phi^{-1}(u))<0$.

$$0<J_{N,k}<\phi(z_{p_N}),\qquad J_{N,N+1-k}=J_{N,k},\qquad J_{1,1}={1\over2\sqrt\pi}.$$

Strict Jensen proves attenuation because the beta law is nondegenerate. If $k/(N+1)\to p\in(0,1)$, beta concentration and bounded continuity give $J_{N,k}\to\phi(z_p)$. Smaller calibration samples also have greater calibration-conditional variability; this attenuation is not a recommendation to reduce sample size.

An explicit bound at any finite horizon and calibration size

Write $W_c=A_c-\pi f$, $m_{r,i}=\mathbb E_iW_c^r$, $m_{r,\pi}=\sum_i\pi_i m_{r,i}$ and $\delta_{r,i}=m_{r,i}-m_{r,\pi}$. Define

$$E_i(c)={|a|\over c}|(e^{cQ}h)_i| +{a^2\over2}|\delta_{2,i}|+{|a|^3\over6}|\delta_{3,i}| +{a^4\over24}(m_{4,i}+m_{4,\pi}),$$ $$\eta={a^2m_{2,\pi}\over2},\qquad B_N(\eta)=\min\{\phi(0)\min(1,N\eta),\ j(\min(\eta,1/2))\},$$ $$\boxed{\left|C_i-p_N-{a h_i\over c}J_{N,k}\right| \le E_i(c)+{|a h_i|\over c}B_N(\eta)}.$$

This bound holds for every $c>0$, finite $N$ and admissible rank, not just asymptotically. It requires no unknown conditional quantile or empirical coverage estimate. The displayed moments follow from finite-dimensional linear ODEs.

To prove it, expand $\Phi(z-aW_c)$ through degree three, with $z=(x-\mu\pi f)/\sigma$. The Gaussian Fourier integral gives $\|\phi^{(j)}\|_\infty\le1$ for $j=0,1,2,3$; moreover $m_{1,i}=[(e^{cQ}-I)h]_i/c$ and $m_{1,\pi}=0$.

$$\sup_x\left|F_i(x)-F_\pi(x)-{a h_i\over c}\phi(z)\right|\le E_i(c).$$

Next compare a stationary standardized score $Z+aW_c$ with $Z$. Its mean shift is zero, and Taylor expansion of the Gaussian density in $L^1$, using $\|\phi''\|_1\le2$, bounds their total variation by $a^2m_{2,\pi}/2$.

Tensorization costs at most $N$ times this distance for the calibration panel; applying the order-statistic map cannot increase it. The expectation of $\phi$ at the standardized order statistic therefore differs from its iid Gaussian value $J_{N,k}$ by at most $\phi(0)\min\{1,Na^2m_{2,\pi}/2\}$. This gives the first term in $B_N$.

There is also an $N$-independent bound. Taylor expansion gives $\sup_z|F_\pi(\mu\pi f+\sigma z)-\Phi(z)|\le\eta$; concavity and symmetry imply $|j(u)-j(v)|\le j(\min(\eta,1/2))$ whenever $|u-v|\le\eta$.

At the actual threshold, $U=F_\pi(\widehat q_k)$ and $\Phi((\widehat q_k-\mu\pi f)/\sigma)$ therefore differ by at most $\eta$ pointwise. This proves the second term in $B_N$. Integrating the uniform CDF-contrast bound against the threshold law proves the boxed inequality.

Why the remainder is second order

The Feynman–Kac moment hierarchy for $V_r(t)=\mathbb E_i(\int_0^t\widetilde f(Y_s)ds)^r$ is $V_r'=QV_r+r\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde f)V_{r-1}$, with $V_0=\mathbf1$, $V_r(0)=0$ for $r>0$. Then $m_{r,i}=V_r(c)_i/c^r$.

Equivalently, the analytic dominant eigenvalue of $Q+s\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde f)$ is $\lambda(s)=\Gamma s^2/2+O(s^3)$, $\Gamma=-2\langle\widetilde f,h\rangle_\pi$, with projector $P(s)\mathbf1=\mathbf1+O(s)$. The remaining spectral component and its fixed-order derivatives decay exponentially, allowing defective generators.

Differentiating through order four gives $m_{2,i}=\Gamma/c+O(c^{-2})$, $m_{3,i}=O(c^{-2})$, $m_{4,i}=O(c^{-2})$. Thus $\delta_{2,i}=O(c^{-2})$, $E_i=O(c^{-2})$, and $m_{2,\pi}=O(c^{-1})$. For fixed $N$ the explicit bound is $O(c^{-2})$.

The modulus term also gives an absolute $O(c^{-2}\sqrt{\log c})$ remainder uniformly over every finite $N\ge1$ and $1\le k\le N$. Indeed $j(\eta)=O(\eta\sqrt{\log(1/\eta)})$: integration by parts bounds $\Phi(-z)\ge\phi(z)z/(z^2+1)$, while the Gaussian Chernoff bound gives $z\le\sqrt{2\log(1/\eta)}$ for $z=-\Phi^{-1}(\eta)$. Thus arbitrary calibration-size sequences are allowed, but the assertion is not relative to a vanishing leading coefficient at extreme ranks, nor uniform as $\sigma\downarrow0$ or the chain's mixing gap degenerates.

Numerical checks and interpretation

At common marginal level $p_N=.9$, the factors for $(N,k)=(9,9),(19,18),(99,90)$ are $.1538752731,.1637539578,.1729791136$, versus the population factor $.1754983319$. For $(N,k)=(1,1),(3,2)$ they are $.2820947918,.3315966888$.

The nonreversible fixture is $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1.1&.8&.3\\.4&-1.4&1\\.7&.5&-1.2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $f=(-1,.35,1.4)$, $\mu=1$, $\sigma=.5$. With $c=256,N=k=9$, the three coverages are $(.90097071,.89971826,.89931746)$: their stationary weighted average is exactly $.9$.

The certificate checks 30 three-state cases from $c=32$ to $1024$, the finite moment budgets, beta symmetry and strict attenuation. Fourier/spatial refinement changes coverages by at most $7.8\times10^{-12}$; final residual orders lie between $1.993$ and $1.995$, using a portable $5\times10^{-8}$ allowance. Independent Poisson–Beta occupation mixtures for the unit-rate symmetric binary chain, with the same $\mu,\sigma$, give one-calibration coverages $.4481267575$ and $.4841628650$ at $c=4,16$.

The theorem and finite bound are analytic; these Fourier and mixture values are numerical consistency checks, not rigorous interval enclosures. Exact rank coverage and its marginal scope are covered by Lei et al. (2018), Theorem 2.2; the beta order-statistic identity follows directly from iid uniform transforms. The new calculation quantifies the model-specific latent-state correction, not distribution-free conditional validity.

The result fixes positive Gaussian smoothing and independent calibration paths. It does not cover vanishing noise, critical nonlinear scores, temporal calibration dependence, same-panel training or a chain whose mixing gap degenerates with $c$. Marginal rank validity, latent-start bias and calibration-conditional random coverage are three distinct quantities.
