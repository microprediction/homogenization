Quantitative switched Vasicek density bounds

A Gaussian envelope makes the hidden endpoint interaction second order after Fourier inversion.

Model and computable constants

Let $dX_t=\kappa(\theta_{Y_t}-X_t)\,dt+\sigma_{Y_t}\,dW_t$, with common $\kappa>0$, independent Brownian and regime noise, and $\min_i\sigma_i^2=s_->0$. The hidden chain has generator $mQ$, $m>0$, where $Q$ is finite and irreducible, with stationary row $\pi$ and a supplied mixing bound $\|e^{tQ}-\mathbf1\pi\|_\infty\le Ae^{-gt}$. All matrix norms below are maximum absolute row sum.

Write $P=\mathbf1\pi$, $C=I-P$, $\bar\theta=\pi\theta$, $\bar s=\pi\sigma^2$, and $v_s(t)=s(1-e^{-2\kappa t})/(2\kappa)$. The averaged density $p_0(x,x';t)$ is Gaussian with mean $\mu=e^{-\kappa t}x+\bar\theta(1-e^{-\kappa t})$ and variance $v_{\bar s}(t)$. Restrict $x,x'$ to $I=[l,h]$ and $t$ to $[\tau,T]$, with $\tau>0$.

$$\begin{gathered} \beta=v_{s_-}(\tau)/2,\quad a=\kappa\max_i|\theta_i-\bar\theta|, \quad b=(\max_i\sigma_i^2-s_-)/2,\\ J_j=\frac{\Gamma((j+1)/2)}{2\pi\beta^{(j+1)/2}},\quad H_1=aJ_1+bJ_2,\quad H_2=a^2J_2+2abJ_3+b^2J_4,\\ B=\max\{h-\min(l,\bar\theta),\max(h,\bar\theta)-l\},\qquad p_*=\frac{\exp[-B^2/(2v_{\bar s}(\tau))]}{\sqrt{2\pi v_{\bar s}(T)}}. \end{gathered}$$

The mean stays between $x$ and $\bar\theta$, so $p_0\ge p_*>0$ on this set. The constants may be conservative but involve only the specified model and observation set.

Finite-rate density and interaction bounds

Let $K=K_m(x,x';t)$ be the regime-resolved density matrix. Put $\alpha=mg$, $E=e^{-\alpha\tau}$ and

$$e_m=AJ_0E+\frac{A(2H_1+TH_2)}{\alpha},\quad u_m=\frac{AH_1}{\alpha},\quad b_m=A(J_0+ATH_1)E+\frac{A^2H_2}{\alpha^2}.$$
$$\|K-p_0P\|_\infty\le e_m,\qquad \|CKP\|_\infty,\ \|PKC\|_\infty\le u_m,\qquad \|CKC\|_\infty\le b_m.$$

For $\delta_m=e_m/p_*<1$, the normalized kernel $M=K/p_0$ therefore satisfies

$$\left\|M-\frac{(M\mathbf1)(\pi M)}{\pi M\mathbf1}\right\|_\infty \le\gamma_m:=\frac{b_m+u_m^2/[p_*(1-\delta_m)]}{p_*}.$$

This follows from the exact identity $K-(K\mathbf1)(\pi K)/(\pi K\mathbf1)=CKC-(CK\mathbf1)(\pi KC)/(\pi K\mathbf1)$. In particular, the endpoint weights can be first order while their remaining interaction is second order.

A fully explicit sufficient speed is

$$m_0=\max\left\{1,\frac{8A(2H_1+TH_2)}{gp_*}, \frac{\log_+(8AJ_0/p_*)}{g\tau}\right\},\qquad \log_+z=\max(0,\log z).$$

For every $m\ge m_0$, $\delta_m\le1/4<1/3$. Thus the bounds do not assume an unquantified matrix-density expansion.

Proof: contract first, then invert

The exact joint characteristic matrix has the form $e^{iu\mu-v_{s_-}(t)u^2/2}Z_u(t)$, where

$$Z_u'=(mQ+H_u(t))Z_u,\quad Z_u(0)=I,\qquad (H_u)_{ii}=iu\kappa(\theta_i-\bar\theta)e^{-\kappa t} -\tfrac12u^2(\sigma_i^2-s_-)e^{-2\kappa t}.$$

Substitution of this factorization into the backward Kolmogorov equation gives the displayed matrix ODE, with terminal regime indicators as its columns.

Every diagonal real part is nonpositive, so its two-parameter propagator contracts in infinity norm, by products of killed Markov propagators. Also $\|H_u(t)\|_\infty\le q(u):=a|u|+bu^2$. Left and right Duhamel formulas on any subinterval then give, with $U=Aq(u)/\alpha$,

$$\begin{aligned} \|CZ_u(t)\|_\infty&\le Ae^{-\alpha t}+U(1-e^{-\alpha t}),\\ \|CZ_u(t)P\|_\infty,\ \|PZ_u(t)C\|_\infty&\le U,\\ \|CZ_u(t)C\|_\infty&\le Ae^{-\alpha t}+A^2q(u)t e^{-\alpha t}+U^2,\\ \|Z_u(t)-r_u(t)P\|_\infty&\le Ae^{-\alpha t}+U(2+q(u)t), \quad r_u(t)=e^{\int_0^t\pi H_u(s)\mathbf1\,ds}. \end{aligned}$$

For the double centering, the right Duhamel integral is bounded by $Aq(u)\int_0^t[Ae^{-\alpha(t-s)}+U(1-e^{-\alpha(t-s)})]e^{-\alpha s}\,ds$. The scalar projection equation gives the final estimate, since $|r_u(t)/r_u(s)|\le1$. These statements apply to the nonautonomous propagator, not a frozen-time exponential.

After multiplication by the Gaussian envelope, every bound is integrable: $(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}|u|^j e^{-\beta u^2}\,du=J_j$. Fourier inversion and the triangle inequality now give exactly $e_m,u_m,b_m$; the scalar comparison inverts to $p_0P$. Thus the proof controls all frequencies, rather than interchanging a fixed-frequency asymptotic series with an uncontrolled integral.

All panel lengths and a numerical error budget

For $m\ge m_0$, put $\rho=2\delta_m/(1-\delta_m)$ and $\xi=\min\{\rho,\gamma_m/(1-\delta_m)^2\}$. For integer memory $r\ge0$, retain the first $r+1$ predictive factors exactly, then restart from $\pi$ and apply only the preceding $r$ kernel updates. For every panel of $N$ transitions in the specified set and every initial hidden prior,

$$|\ell_F-\ell_r|\le (N-r-1)_+\rho^2\xi^r/2=O(Nm^{-2r-2})$$

for fixed $r$ as $m\to\infty$. Writing $D=M-(M\mathbf1)(\pi M)/(\pi M\mathbf1)$ gives the exact update identity $F(\nu)=\pi M/(\pi M\mathbf1)+\nu D/(\nu M\mathbf1)$.

Subtracting updates bounds their row $\ell^1$ Lipschitz constant by $\min\{\rho,\gamma_m/(1-\delta_m)^2\}=\xi$. Each output is within $\rho$ of $\pi$, and each predictive log factor is $\rho/2$-Lipschitz. Summing the resulting per-factor bound proves the claim.

In particular one retained interval has a quartic bound, without a separate tangent hypothesis. Its two transitions involve three consecutive observations. The first $r+1$ exact factors preserve an arbitrary initial prior; all restarted predictive factors normalize.

The certificate independently freezes $H_u$ at time-cell midpoints, multiplies exact constant-cell exponentials, and sums frequencies $u_j=j\omega$, $-n\le j\le n$. With $J$ equal time cells, $\beta_t=v_{s_-}(t)/2$, $F=n\omega$, $L=2\pi/\omega$, $v_+=v_{\max\sigma_i^2}(t)$ and $B_0=\max\{h-\min(l,\min_i\theta_i),\max(h,\max_i\theta_i)-l\}<L$, its exact-arithmetic density error is at most

$$\frac{t^2}{4J}\frac{\omega}{2\pi}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}e^{-\beta_tu_j^2} [\kappa a|u_j|+2\kappa b u_j^2] +\frac{\operatorname{erfc}(F\sqrt{\beta_t})}{\sqrt{2\pi v_{s_-}(t)}} +\frac{2e^{-(L-B_0)^2/(2v_+)}}{\sqrt{2\pi v_{s_-}(t)} [1-e^{-L(L-B_0)/v_+}]}.$$

The first term is a contractive Duhamel bound using $\|H_u'\|\le\kappa a|u|+2\kappa bu^2$ and integrated midpoint distances $t^2/(4J)$. The second bounds the omitted Gaussian lattice tail. For the third, conditional Gaussianity and Poisson summation identify the full lattice sum as a periodized density; the nonzero images are bounded by a geometric series.

A sharper time budget uses the midpoint cancellation and the diagonal potential's commutator with $Q$. Define $r_\theta=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}|\theta_j-\theta_i|$ and $r_s=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}|\sigma_j^2-\sigma_i^2|$, so

$$L_1(u)=\kappa a|u|+2\kappa bu^2,\quad L_2(u)=\kappa^2a|u|+4\kappa^2bu^2,\quad \|[Q,H_u'(s)]\|_\infty\le R(u):=\kappa^2r_\theta|u|+\kappa r_su^2.$$

The time-error term above may be replaced by

$$\frac{\omega}{2\pi}\sum_{j=-n}^n e^{-\beta_tu_j^2} \left\{\frac{t^3}{J^2}\left[\frac{L_2(u_j)}{24}+\frac{mR(u_j)}{12}\right] +\frac{t^4L_1(u_j)^2}{64J^3}\right\}.$$

On a cell of width $d$ centered at $c$, expand $H_u(s)-H_u(c)$ as $(s-c)H_u'(c)$ plus a remainder bounded by $L_2(u)(s-c)^2/2$. In the linear Duhamel term, let $F(s)$ be the exact left propagator times $H_u'(c)$ times the frozen right propagator. Diagonality gives $\|F'(s)\|_\infty\le mR(u)+L_1(u)^2|s-c|$.

The constant $F(c)$ integrates to zero against $s-c$. Integrating $F(s)-F(c)$ and the Taylor remainder bounds the cell error by $d^3[L_2(u)/24+mR(u)/12]+d^4L_1(u)^2/64$. A contractive telescoping sum over the cells proves the displayed global budget.

Take the smaller of the two proven time budgets. The sharper bound is second order in time resolution at fixed switching speed; its constant grows with $m$, so this is not a stiffness-uniform estimate. Exponential integration with commutator error analysis is classical (Iserles, Marthinsen and Nørsett 1999); these constants are proved here.

This budget includes time discretization, Fourier truncation and aliasing, but not floating-point roundoff. The implementation uses portable numerical allowances, not outward-rounded exponentials, and is not a validated density enclosure. The theorem concerns exact densities and does not depend on the numerical checks.

Literature and scope

The conditional Gaussian representation is classical: Huang et al. (2016), Theorem 2.1 in its inspected arXiv preprint. Transition-density expansions also predate this note (Il'in, Khasminskii and Yin 1999); its metadata were checked, but full-text access was unavailable. The estimates above are proved directly; no novelty claim or uninspected theorem is imported.

The constants are not uniform in tail observations, as $\tau\downarrow0$, or as the minimum volatility vanishes. Common mean reversion and independent noise are essential to this proof; it does not cover switched $\kappa$, leverage or arbitrary diffusions. No estimator-efficiency, conditional-conformal-coverage or regime-count claim follows.
