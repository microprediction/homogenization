Second-order arbitrary-prior correlation prices

The mean boundary, occupation skew and Gaussian kurtosis produce a uniform third-order remainder.

A nonstationary initial regime changes a fast-switching option price at first order. Keeping that exact mean boundary and the next occupation moments gives a second-order price formula whose remainder is $O(m^{-3})$, uniformly over every initial regime prior at fixed positive maturity.

The theorem

Let the calm-to-crisis and crisis-to-calm rates be $ma$ and $mb$. Put

$$r=a+b,\quad p_1=\frac ar,\quad p_0=\frac br,\quad u=p_0p_1,\quad d=p_0-p_1,\quad \delta=\eta-p_1,\quad \Delta\rho=\rho_1-\rho_0,$$

where $\eta=\mathbb P(Y_0=1)$ and $\bar\rho=p_0\rho_0+p_1\rho_1$. Define the exact mean boundary

$$b_m(\eta)=\Delta\rho\,\delta\frac{1-e^{-mrT}}{mrT}.$$

If $T>0$ is fixed and $C$ has six continuous derivatives on the interval between $\rho_0$ and $\rho_1$, then, uniformly over $\eta\in[0,1]$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\eta C(\hat\rho_T)={}&C(\bar\rho)+b_m(\eta)C'(\bar\rho) +\frac{u(\Delta\rho)^2}{mrT}C''(\bar\rho)\\ &+\frac{1}{m^2r^2T^2}\Big[(\Delta\rho)^2(\delta d-u)C''(\bar\rho) +u(\Delta\rho)^3(d+\delta)C'''(\bar\rho)\\ &\hspace{43mm}+\tfrac12u^2(\Delta\rho)^4C''''(\bar\rho)\Big] +O_{T,C}(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

The bound is uniform even for a prior $\eta=eta_m$ that changes with speed. It is not uniform as maturity tends to zero or infinity, and it is a two-state scalar-correlation result rather than a general finite-chain formula.

Proof

Write $A_m=\int_0^T(\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=1\}}-p_1)dt$, so $\hat\rho_T-\bar\rho=\Delta\rho A_m/T$. The exact arbitrary-start occupation cumulants give, uniformly in $\eta$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E A_m&=\delta\frac{1-e^{-mrT}}{mr},\\ \mathbb E A_m^2&=\frac{2uT}{mr}+\frac{2(\delta d-u)}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^3&=\frac{6u(d+\delta)T}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^4&=\frac{12u^2T^2}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^5&=O(m^{-3}),\qquad \mathbb E A_m^6=O(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

The second line combines the stationary covariance integral with its exact endpoint correction. In the third line, the $d$ term is the stationary third occupation cumulant and the $\delta$ term is the product of the leading variance and mean boundary; the fourth line is the three-pair contribution $3(2uT/(mr))^2$.

The finite-state spectral cumulant expansion controls orders five and six uniformly because the initial-prior coefficients are continuous on the compact simplex; analyticity follows from Kato's spectral projection perturbation theory. Taylor expansion of $C(\bar\rho+\Delta\rho A_m/T)$ through degree five, with the sixth derivative bounding the remainder, gives the theorem.

Exchange-option implementation

For Margrabe's price $M(V)$, let $k=\log(S_1/(qS_2))$ and

$$h(V)=\frac{k^2}{2V^2}-\frac1{2V}-\frac18.$$

Starting from $M'(V)=S_1\phi(d_1)/(2\sqrt V)$, repeated differentiation gives

$$M''=hM',\qquad M'''=(h^2+h')M',\qquad M''''=(h^3+3hh'+h'')M'.$$

Since $V(\rho)$ is affine, $C^{(j)}(\rho)=(-2\sigma_1\sigma_2T)^jM^{(j)}(V(\rho))$. This supplies the variance, skew and kurtosis terms without numerical differentiation.

Numerical certificate

The certificate uses 21 equally spaced initial priors, all nine displayed exchange strikes, and speeds $m=4,8,16,32,64$. The maximum error falls from $5.36315810\times10^{-5}$ to $1.22709523\times10^{-8}$, with last-doubling order $3.006199$.

Independent arbitrary-precision differentiation checks the first four variance derivatives with maximum normalized error $2.59\times10^{-14}$. The observed order supports the implementation; the moment argument above proves the fixed-maturity rate.

Literature boundary

The atom-plus-Bessel occupation law is classical; see Pedler (1971) and Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir (2010). The displayed price expansion follows directly from those occupation moments and Taylor's theorem; no priority claim is made for the occupation law.
