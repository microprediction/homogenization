Green–Kubo geometry of joint default

Positive CIR intensities, an identifiable instantaneous matrix, and a maturity-integrated rank trap.

Research note, 23 September 2026; extended 30 September 2026. The constant-hazard example gives the starting point; this note changes the loadings while retaining nonnegative intensities.

A positive-intensity model

Conditionally independent Cox defaults have intensities $X_j(t)$, $j=1,\ldots,d$. Specifically, let $E_j$ be independent unit-exponential variables, independent of the Brownian motions and the regime, and set $\tau_j=\inf\{t:\int_0^tX_j(s)ds\ge E_j\}$. A common irreducible $n$-state chain $y_t$ with stationary law $\pi$ switches their CIR mean levels:

$$dX_j=\kappa_j(\theta_{j,y_t}-X_j)dt+ \sigma_j\sqrt{X_j}\,dW_j,\qquad y_t\ \text{has generator }mQ_0.$$

Assume $\kappa_j>0$, $\sigma_j>0$, $X_j(0)=x_j>0$, independent Brownian motions and the independent default triggers just defined. Taking $2\kappa_j\min_i\theta_{j,i}>\sigma_j^2$ keeps each intensity strictly positive over every finite time interval, even as regimes switch. For a subset $A$ of names the joint survival price is $S_A(T)=\mathbb E\exp(-\int_0^T\sum_{j\in A}X_j(t)\,dt)$.

The CIR Riccati loading $B_j$ and the regime-dependent factor obey

$$B_j'=1-\kappa_jB_j-\tfrac12\sigma_j^2B_j^2,\qquad B_j(0)=0,$$ $$S_A^\nu(T)=e^{-\sum_{j\in A}B_j(T)x_j}\,\nu a_A(T),\qquad a_A'=\left(mQ_0+\operatorname{diag} \left[-\sum_{j\in A}\kappa_j\theta_j B_j(t)\right]\right)a_A,\quad a_A(0)={\bf1}.$$

This is an exact finite-dimensional reduction for any initial regime prior $\nu$. The loadings $B_j$ vary with maturity and can differ across names, although $Q_0$ is shared.

The first-order matrix

Let $h_j=-\kappa_j(\theta_j-\bar\theta_j{\bf1})$, where $\bar\theta_j=\pi\theta_j$, and define the fixed Green–Kubo matrix and the loading integrals

$$K_{jk}=\frac1m\mathcal K^{\rm sym}_0(h_j,h_k),\qquad J_{jk}(T)=\int_0^T B_j(t)B_k(t)\,dt.$$

Here $\mathcal K^{\rm sym}_0$ uses $Q_0$ and is the half-asymptotic covariance of the two centered additive functionals. On each fixed interval $0\le T\le L$, the stationary-start fast-switching expansion gives

$$\log S_A(T)=-\sum_{j\in A} \left[B_j(T)x_j+\kappa_j\bar\theta_j\int_0^T B_j(t)\,dt\right] +\sum_{j,k\in A}D_{jk}(T)+O_L(m^{-2}),\qquad D_{jk}(T)=K_{jk}J_{jk}(T).$$

The $O_L(m^{-2})$ error is uniform in $T$ for fixed model parameters. It follows by separating the constant and centered regime components in the $a_A$ equation: the centered component is $-Q_0^\#\tilde g/m$ plus an $O_L(m^{-2})$ remainder. Since all $B_j(0)=0$, the first-order initial-layer amplitude vanishes. Substitution into the stationary component gives the quadratic Green–Kubo term. This is an asymptotic statement, not an exact low-rank statement at finite $m$.

An arbitrary initial regime prior

Hiddenness does not imply a stationary start. Let $\nu$ be any initial prior for the regime; if the displayed initial intensities are observed from an already-running system, $\nu$ can instead be the posterior conditional on those observations. Put $H=-Q_0^\#$ and

$$f_A(t)=\sum_{j\in A}B_j(t)h_j,\qquad h_j=-\kappa_j(\theta_j-\bar\theta_j{\bf1}).$$

The same outer expansion, now retained before multiplication by the initial prior, gives

$$\log S_A^\nu(T)=\log S_A^\pi(T)+\frac1m\,\nu Hf_A(T)+O_L(m^{-2}).$$

This estimate is uniform on every fixed $0\le T\le L$: because $B_j(0)=0$, one has $f_A(0)=0$, so the first-order outer corrector already satisfies the initial condition and no order-$m^{-1}$ boundary layer is needed. The term is genuine start-regime memory and vanishes for $\nu=\pi$, since $\pi H=0$.

A useful cancellation follows from $f_{\{j,k\}}=f_{\{j\}}+f_{\{k\}}$:

$$\log\frac{S_{\{j,k\}}^\nu(T)}{S_{\{j\}}^\nu(T)S_{\{k\}}^\nu(T)} =2K_{jk}J_{jk}(T)+O_L(m^{-2}).$$

Thus the first-order pairwise log-survival ratio and the identification of $K_{jk}$ are robust to an unknown common prior, even though every marginal survival curve changes at order $m^{-1}$. Since default correlation is itself order $m^{-1}$, its change with the prior begins at order $m^{-2}$. The certificate checks all three statements directly against the finite-rate ODE: the measured rates are $2.004$ for survival after the endpoint correction, $2.010$ for the prior-independent pair formula, and $1.998$ for the prior effect on correlation.

For two distinct names, the observable pairwise survival ratio is

$$\log\frac{S_{\{j,k\}}(T)}{S_{\{j\}}(T)S_{\{k\}}(T)} =2K_{jk}J_{jk}(T)+O_L(m^{-2}).$$

Its first-order sign is the sign of $K_{jk}$. Off-diagonal values can be negative while the entire $K$ matrix remains positive semidefinite. Different states having high instantaneous hazards do not, by themselves, determine that sign: the integrated lagged covariance does.

Two names suffice to detect cycle direction

Unordered joint survival sees only the symmetric Green–Kubo matrix because hazard multiplication operators commute. The identity of the first defaulter is different: it is an absorbing-channel label and retains time orientation. Let two names have positive regime-dependent hazards $h_1,h_2$, put $h=h_1+h_2$, and start the regime in stationarity. The exact probability that name 1 defaults first is

$$p_1=\pi^\top(\operatorname{diag}h-mQ_0)^{-1}h_1.$$

Define the unscaled oriented matrix

$$K^{(0)}_{jk}=-\pi\cdot\left[(h_j-\bar h_j{\bf1})\odot Q_0^\#(h_k-\bar h_k{\bf1})\right].$$

A singular resolvent expansion gives

$$p_1=\frac{\bar h_1}{\bar h} +\frac1{m\bar h}\left\{ \frac{\bar h_1}{\bar h}K^{(0)}_{h,h}-K^{(0)}_{h,1}\right\} +O(m^{-2}),$$ $$p_1(Q_0)-p_1(Q_0^{\rm rev}) =\frac{K^{(0)}_{12}-K^{(0)}_{21}}{m\bar h}+O(m^{-2}).$$

For a direct proof, solve $(\operatorname{diag}h-mQ_0)x=h_1$ with $x=x_0{\bf1}+m^{-1}x_1+\cdots$. Solvability at order one gives $x_0=\bar h_1/\bar h$ and $x_1=Q_0^\#(x_0h-h_1)+a_1{\bf1}$. Solvability at the next order, $\pi\operatorname{diag}(h)x_1=0$, gives $a_1=(x_0K^{(0)}_{h,h}-K^{(0)}_{h,1})/\bar h$. Since $p_1=\pi x=x_0+a_1/m+O(m^{-2})$, this proves the first display. Time reversal transposes $K^{(0)}$ and gives the second.

Here $K^{(0)}_{h,1}=K^{(0)}_{11}+K^{(0)}_{21}$ and $K^{(0)}_{h,h}=\sum_{j,k}K^{(0)}_{jk}$. Thus only two event types are required; at least three hidden states are needed for a finite irreducible chain to violate detailed balance.

For the three-state cycle with clockwise/backward rates $(2,0.1)$ and hazards

$$h_1=(0.07,0.01,0.01),\qquad h_2=(0.01,0.07,0.01),$$

one obtains $\bar h_1=\bar h_2=0.03$, $K^{(0)}_{12}=-9.50119\times10^{-6}$ and $K^{(0)}_{21}=-1.90024\times10^{-4}$. The predicted clockwise-minus-counterclockwise gap is therefore $0.0030087094/m+O(m^{-2})$.

What a multi-name race identifies

With $d$ competing causes, one unweighted race does not generally identify the whole oriented matrix. Let $w_a>0$ scale cause $a$, put $\bar h(w)=\sum_a w_a\bar h_a$, and decompose the unscaled oriented matrix as

$$K^{(0)}=G+A,\qquad G=\tfrac12(K^{(0)}+K^{(0)\top}),\qquad A=\tfrac12(K^{(0)}-K^{(0)\top}).$$

The exact cause-probability vector is

$$p_a(w)=\pi^\top\left(\operatorname{diag}\!\left\{\sum_jw_jh_j\right\} -mQ_0\right)^{-1}w_ah_a.$$

Applying the same singular-resolvent calculation componentwise gives

$$p_a(w)=\frac{w_a\bar h_a}{\bar h(w)}+\frac{c_a(w)}m+O(m^{-2}),$$ $$c_a(w)=\frac1{\bar h(w)}\left\{ \frac{w_a\bar h_a}{\bar h(w)}w^\top Gw-w_a(w^\top K^{(0)})_a\right\}.$$

Unordered marginal and joint survivals identify $G$. If $c_a^G(w)$ denotes the coefficient predicted by setting $A=0$, the remaining cause-order signal is exactly

$$c_a(w)-c_a^G(w)=\frac{w_a(Aw)_a}{\bar h(w)},$$ $$p_a(w;Q_0)-p_a(w;Q_0^{\rm rev}) =\frac{2w_a(Aw)_a}{m\bar h(w)}+O(m^{-2}).$$

Thus one unweighted race sees only $A\mathbf1$, a $(d-1)$-dimensional contrast. For $d\ge3$ it can be zero even when $A\ne0$. Antisymmetry makes the sharp multi-design result stronger than ordinary column-by-column recovery. If the exposure designs span a $q$-dimensional subspace $W$, then the kernel of

$$A\longmapsto(Aw^{(1)},\ldots,Aw^{(q)})$$

is exactly the space of skew maps supported on $W^\perp$, and hence has dimension

$$\binom{d-q}{2}.$$

Indeed, a kernel element vanishes on $W$; skew-adjointness then makes $W^\perp$ invariant, while every skew map on $W^\perp$ gives such an element. Therefore $q=d-1$ linearly independent positive designs identify every antisymmetric $d\times d$ matrix, and this number is minimal: with $q\le d-2$ there is always a nonzero invisible skew map on $W^\perp$. This identification result keeps the fixed oriented parameter matrix separate from any maturity-integrated rank claim.

Which rank bound survives?

For fixed centered feature vectors $h_1,\ldots,h_d$, the matrix $K\succeq0$ has rank at most $n-1$. In fact its rank equals the dimension spanned by those centered vectors for an irreducible finite chain. At a single maturity, the matrix of correction densities is $\operatorname{diag}(B(t))K\operatorname{diag}(B(t))$, and it has the same rank for $t>0$.

For completeness, if $f$ is centered and $w=-Q_0^\#f$, then $2\mathcal K_0(f,f)=\sum_{i,\ell\ne i}\pi_i(Q_0)_{i\ell} (w_\ell-w_i)^2$. Irreducibility makes this zero only when $w$ is constant, equivalently $f=0$. Thus the symmetric Green–Kubo form is an inner product on the $(n-1)$-dimensional centered space, which proves the claimed rank of $K$.

The integrated matrix $D(T)=K\circ J(T)$ is also positive semidefinite, because it is a time integral of positive semidefinite matrices. Its rank need not be at most $n-1$. Time-dependent diagonal rescalings rotate a low-dimensional range as $t$ varies. In particular, a rank test applied directly to $D(T)$ is not a lower bound on the number of regimes. This distinction is absent for constant hazards: all $B_j(t)=1$ and $D(T)=TK$.

The sharp Hadamard-rank theorem

There is nevertheless an exact replacement for the false regime-count bound. Let $r=\operatorname{rank}K$ and $s=\operatorname{rank}J(T)$, where $s$ is the dimension of the span of the loading functions $B_1,\ldots,B_d$ in $L^2[0,T]$. Then

$$\operatorname{rank}D(T) \le\min\{d,rs\}.$$

Indeed, factor $K=UU^\top$ and $J=VV^\top$, and denote their $j$th rows by $u_j$ and $v_j$. The Hadamard product has the Gram factorization

$$D_{jk}=\langle u_j,u_k\rangle\langle v_j,v_k\rangle =\langle u_j\otimes v_j,u_k\otimes v_k\rangle.$$

Thus the rank is exactly the dimension spanned by the $d$ rowwise tensor products $u_j\otimes v_j$ in an $rs$-dimensional space. The bound is sharp: generic factor pairs have rowwise tensor rank $\min(d,rs)$. Positivity of $D$ is the classical Schur product theorem; the tensor factorization also supplies the rank statement directly.

The corrected regime-count lower bound

If the fixed features are driven by an irreducible $n$-state chain, then $r=\operatorname{rank}K\le n-1$. Consequently the integrated rank $R=\operatorname{rank}D(T)$ and the known loading-span dimension $s=\operatorname{rank}J(T)$ imply

$$n\ge 1+\left\lceil {R\over s}\right\rceil.$$

If only an upper bound $s\le q$ is known, the still-valid conclusion is $n\ge1+\lceil R/q\rceil$. This is the strongest possible lower bound from these ranks alone: for every $r,s\ge1$, generic Gram factors with $d\ge rs$ attain $R=rs$, and hence equality with $n=r+1$. A complete-graph $n$-state chain realizes an arbitrary rank-$r$ centered feature Gramian, while $s$ independent deterministic loading shapes realize the second factor.

The endpoints explain the apparent contradiction. With one common loading shape, $s=1$ and the familiar rule $n\ge R+1$ survives. With $d$ independent loading shapes, a full-rank $d\times d$ integrated matrix says only $n\ge2$, and the two-regime CIR construction below attains that bound. This inference uses the rank of the loading family; it is not a claim that $D(T)$ itself is a fixed-feature Green–Kubo matrix.

Two regimes can create arbitrarily large integrated rank

The two-name example is not a dimension-two accident. For any number $d$ of names and any $T>0$, a two-regime model can have $\operatorname{rank}K=1$ but $\operatorname{rank}D(T)=d$. Choose every centered regime feature on the single two-state contrast, with nonzero coefficient $a_j$, so $K=aa^\top$ up to scale. At zero vol-of-vol and distinct $\kappa_j>0$,

$$B_j(t)=\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_jt}}{\kappa_j}.$$

These functions are linearly independent on every positive interval: differentiating a vanishing linear combination gives a vanishing combination of distinct exponentials $e^{-\kappa_jt}$, whose Vandermonde system is nonsingular. Hence $J(T)$ is positive definite and

$$D(T)=\operatorname{diag}(a)J(T)\operatorname{diag}(a)$$

has full rank $d$. The conclusion persists for sufficiently small strictly positive CIR volatilities because the Riccati solutions and the Gram determinant depend continuously on $\sigma_j^2$. Mean levels can simultaneously be chosen with a uniform strict Feller margin. Therefore no bound depending only on the number of regimes survives after maturity integration.

An exact full-rank criterion

The preceding rank-one construction is a special case of a sharp criterion that removes any numerical genericity assumption. Fix a common CIR volatility $\sigma\ge0$, pairwise distinct mean-reversion rates $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_d$, and any $T>0$. The CIR loading independence proved below makes $J(T)$ positive definite. For every positive semidefinite fixed-parameter Green–Kubo matrix $K$,

$$D(T)=K\circ J(T)\succ0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K_{ii}>0 \text{ for every }i.$$

To prove sufficiency, factor $K=FF^\top$ and write $f_\ell$ for the columns of $F$. The Schur product has the exact decomposition

$$K\circ J(T)=\sum_\ell \operatorname{diag}(f_\ell)J(T) \operatorname{diag}(f_\ell).$$

If a vector $x$ has zero quadratic form, positivity of $J(T)$ forces $\operatorname{diag}(f_\ell)x=0$ for every $\ell$. But $K_{ii}=\sum_\ell f_{\ell i}^2>0$, so some feature is nonzero in each coordinate and hence $x=0$. Conversely, if $K_{ii}=0$, positive semidefiniteness forces the entire $i$th row and column of $K$ to vanish, making $D(T)$ singular.

Thus heterogeneous maturity loadings create exact full rank at every positive maturity if and only if every name has nonzero fixed-parameter Green–Kubo variance. The rank of $K$ itself may be one. This does not increase fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank; it characterizes the rank created only after integrating different deterministic maturity loadings.

A five-name certificate uses the same positive CIR loading Gramian with fixed Green–Kubo ranks one, two, and three. All three integrated matrices have rank five; their smallest eigenvalues are respectively $1.57035185\times10^{-7}$, $7.36817240\times10^{-5}$, and $3.91143441\times10^{-2}$. Setting one feature row to zero makes the corresponding diagonal entry vanish and reduces the integrated rank exactly to four.

Exact rank under repeated loading shapes

The distinct-rate criterion has an exact extension when some names share a mean-reversion rate. Partition the name indices into clusters $C_1,\ldots,C_g$, where $\kappa_i=\alpha_c$ for $i\in C_c$, and assume the cluster centers $\alpha_c$ are distinct. For common CIR volatility and every $T>0$,

$$\operatorname{rank}\{K\circ J(T)\} =\sum_{c=1}^g\operatorname{rank}K_{C_c,C_c}.$$

Indeed, factor $K=FF^\top$ and let $f_i$ be row $i$ of $F$. If $b_c(t)=B_{\alpha_c}(t)$, then $K\circ J(T)$ is the Gram matrix in $\mathbb R^r\otimes L^2[0,T]$ of the rowwise tensors

$$f_i\otimes b_{c(i)}.$$

The distinct CIR loading functions $b_1,\ldots,b_g$ are linearly independent. Hence the tensor spans belonging to different clusters form a direct sum. The span within cluster $C_c$ has dimension $\operatorname{rank}F_{C_c}=\operatorname{rank}K_{C_c,C_c}$, proving the formula. This rowwise tensor factorization is the transpose form of the product introduced by Khatri and Rao (1968).

Consequently, the integrated matrix is positive definite exactly when

$$K_{C_c,C_c}\succ0\qquad(c=1,\ldots,g).$$

The endpoints clarify the rank distinction. If every loading is distinct, each cluster is a singleton and the condition reduces to $K_{ii}>0$. If every loading is identical, there is one cluster and integration preserves $\operatorname{rank}K$. Thus positive diagonal entries alone are not sufficient once loading shapes collide.

A six-name certificate with collision sizes $(3,2,1)$ uses fixed Green–Kubo ranks one, two, and three. The within-cluster rank sums are respectively $1+1+1$, $2+2+1$, and $3+2+1$, and the observed integrated ranks are exactly $3,5,6$. In the rank-one case every diagonal entry of $K$ is positive, yet the integrated rank is only the number of distinct loading shapes, three.

A complete heterogeneous two-name criterion

Clustering by mean reversion alone is valid only under the preceding common-volatility assumption. For two names allow separate CIR parameter pairs $(\kappa_i,\sigma_i)$, with $\kappa_i>0$ and $\sigma_i\ge0$, and let $B_i$ solve

$$B_i'=1-\kappa_iB_i-\tfrac12\sigma_i^2B_i^2, \qquad B_i(0)=0.$$

For every $T>0$, the two-name loading Gramian $J(T)$ is positive definite exactly when

$$(\kappa_1,\sigma_1)\ne(\kappa_2,\sigma_2).$$

Indeed, a singular two-function Gramian makes the analytic loadings proportional on $[0,T]$. Since $B_i'(0)=1$, the proportionality constant must be one. The next two derivatives are

$$B_i''(0)=-\kappa_i,\qquad B_i'''(0)=\kappa_i^2-\sigma_i^2,$$

so equality of the loadings forces equality of both parameter pairs; the converse follows from uniqueness of the Riccati equation.

Exact two-name rank law. If the parameter pairs are distinct and $K\succeq0$, then

$$\operatorname{rank}\{K\circ J(T)\} =\#\{i:K_{ii}>0\}.$$

If the pairs coincide, then $J(T)=j_T\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$ with $j_T>0$, and therefore

$$K\circ J(T)=j_TK,\qquad \operatorname{rank}\{K\circ J(T)\}=\operatorname{rank}K.$$

For the distinct-pair case, positive diagonal entries and the Schur decomposition above imply positive definiteness; a zero diagonal in a positive semidefinite matrix removes that coordinate. Equivalently, strict Cauchy–Schwarz for the two different loadings makes the determinant positive. This gives the complete heterogeneous-parameter answer in dimension two without assuming a common volatility.

The certificate fixes $\kappa_1=\kappa_2=1.1$ but takes $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(0.18,0.46)$. The loading-Gram determinant is $9.27359551\times10^{-4}$. A rank-one positive-diagonal $K$ produces integrated eigenvalues $(1.73191413\times10^{-4},3.42690265)$, hence rank two. When the entire parameter pair is repeated, the same $K$ remains rank one, while a positive-definite $K$ remains rank two. Equal mean reversion by itself is therefore not a loading collision.

A heterogeneous many-name family

There is also an exact $d$-name extension away from common volatility. Hold the full Riccati decay rate

$$\gamma=\sqrt{\kappa_i^2+2\sigma_i^2}$$

fixed across names, allow both $\kappa_i$ and $\sigma_i$ to vary, and put $q_i=(\gamma-\kappa_i)/(\gamma+\kappa_i)$. Then

$$B_i(t)={1+q_i\over\gamma} {1-e^{-\gamma t}\over1+q_i e^{-\gamma t}}.$$

Distinct full parameter pairs give distinct $q_i$. These loading functions are linearly independent on every positive interval. Indeed, with $z=e^{-\gamma t}$, a vanishing linear combination becomes, after removing the common factor $1-z$,

$$\sum_i{d_i\over1+q_i z}=0.$$

Multiplying by $\prod_j(1+q_jz)$ gives a polynomial that vanishes on an interval, hence identically. Evaluating that identity at the distinct poles $z=-1/q_i$ kills every coefficient with $q_i>0$; a possible $q_i=0$ coefficient then also vanishes. This elementary partial-fraction proof avoids a genericity assumption.

Iso-decay cluster law. Partition names into clusters $C_c$ having identical full pairs $(\kappa_i,\sigma_i)$. If all names share $\gamma$ and $K\succeq0$, then for every $T>0$,

$$\operatorname{rank}\{K\circ J(T)\} =\sum_c\operatorname{rank}K_{C_c,C_c}.$$

The proof is the same rowwise tensor direct-sum argument as for the common-volatility cluster law, now justified by the rational loading family above. In particular, if every full pair is distinct, positive diagonal entries of $K$ are necessary and sufficient for full integrated rank. This theorem is restricted to a common $\gamma$; it does not claim arbitrary collections of heterogeneous CIR loadings are linearly independent.

The four-name certificate takes $\gamma=2$ and $\kappa=(0.2,0.2,1.0,1.8)$, with $\sigma_i=\sqrt{(4-\kappa_i^2)/2}$. Thus there are three distinct full parameter pairs although both parameters vary. Fixed Green–Kubo ranks one and two give predicted and observed integrated ranks three and four. Their smallest positive eigenvalues are respectively $8.45331965\times10^{-7}$ and $1.74806811\times10^{-3}$.

Long maturity restores effective rank one

Full algebraic rank also does not mean uniform information across maturities. Let the distinct CIR loadings have positive limits

$$b_j=\lim_{t\to\infty}B_j(t) ={2\over\sqrt{\kappa_j^2+2\sigma^2}+\kappa_j}, \qquad b=(b_1,\ldots,b_d)^\top,$$

for a common fixed $\sigma\ge0$, and put $r(t)=B(t)-b$. The Riccati formula gives $r(t)=O(e^{-\rho t})$ for some $\rho>0$, hence

$$J(T)=\int_0^TB(t)B(t)^\top dt =Tbb^\top+C+O(e^{-\rho T}),\qquad C=\int_0^\infty\{B(t)B(t)^\top-bb^\top\}\,dt.$$

Let $P_b$ be orthogonal projection onto $b^\perp$ and define, on that $(d-1)$-dimensional space,

$$H=P_b\left\{\int_0^\infty r(t)r(t)^\top dt\right\}P_b.$$

The cross terms in $C$ vanish after compression because $P_bb=0$. Moreover $H$ is positive definite: if $z\perp b$ and $z^\top Hz=0$, then $z^\top r(t)=0$ for every $t$, hence $z^\top B(t)=0$; the CIR loading independence proved below forces $z=0$.

Long-maturity spectrum. Order the eigenvalues of $J(T)$ as $\lambda_1(T)\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d(T)$ and those of $H$ as $\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_{d-1}>0$. Then

$$\lambda_1(T)=\|b\|^2T+O(1),\qquad \lambda_{j+1}(T)=\mu_j+O(T^{-1}),$$ $$\operatorname{cond}_2J(T) ={\|b\|^2\over\mu_{d-1}}T+O(1),\qquad \det J(T)=\|b\|^2\det(H)T+O(1).$$

In an orthonormal basis beginning with $b/\|b\|$, the first block of $J(T)$ is $T\|b\|^2+O(1)$, the lower block tends to $H$, and the off-diagonal block is bounded. A Schur complement gives the displayed $O(T^{-1})$ bounded-eigenvalue corrections. The same theorem holds for the rank-one Green–Kubo correction $D(T)=\operatorname{diag}(a)J(T)\operatorname{diag}(a)$ when every $a_j\ne0$, after replacing $B,b,r$ by their coordinatewise products with $a$.

The conclusion is deliberately two-sided: heterogeneous maturity loadings create exact full rank for every finite $T>0$, but at the long end only their common stationary levels accumulate linearly. The remaining $d-1$ directions contain a finite amount of transient information, so relative conditioning deteriorates like $T$. This is a deterministic loading-Gram theorem at fixed CIR parameters. It does not make the earlier $O_L(m^{-2})$ pricing remainder uniform as $T\to\infty$ and does not interchange the switching-speed and maturity limits.

For four positive-volatility loadings with $\kappa=(0.35,0.8,1.7,3.2)$ and $\sigma=0.18$, the limiting bounded eigenvalues are

$$(2.74707297\times10^{-4},\quad 1.19392590\times10^{-2},\quad 4.59242675\times10^{-1}).$$

At $T=2048$ their measured values are $(2.74570818\times10^{-4},1.19298558\times10^{-2}, 4.58596743\times10^{-1})$. The four convergence orders, including the scaled leading eigenvalue, are $(1.005479,1.004346,1.002275,0.999965)$. The measured and predicted limits are respectively $30747.39631$ and $30780.38173$ for $\operatorname{cond}_2J(T)/T$, and $1.26818689\times10^{-5}$ and $1.27360280\times10^{-5}$ for $\det J(T)/T$.

The inverse information has a rank-one long-end correction

The spectrum identifies the scales but not the limiting covariance or the stationary–transient coupling. Write $\beta=\|b\|$, $e=b/\beta$, $P=I-ee^\top$, and let $H^+$ denote the Moore–Penrose inverse of $H$ on $\mathbb R^d$ (the inverse on $b^\perp$ and zero on $\operatorname{span}\{b\}$). With $C$ as above, define

$$g=PCe,\qquad w=e-H^+g.$$

Inverse-information expansion. For fixed distinct CIR parameters and $T\to\infty$,

$$J(T)^{-1}=H^++{ww^\top\over\beta^2T}+O(T^{-2})$$

in operator norm. Consequently, for every fixed exposure $x$,

$$x^\top J(T)^{-1}x =x^\top H^+x+{(x^\top w)^2\over\beta^2T}+O(T^{-2}), \qquad T\,b^\top J(T)^{-1}b\longrightarrow1.$$

Indeed, in an orthonormal basis $(e,U)$ with columns of $U$ spanning $b^\perp$, exponential convergence of $B(t)$ gives

$$[e\ U]^\top J(T)[e\ U] =\begin{pmatrix} \beta^2T+c_{00}&(U^\top g)^\top\\ U^\top g&U^\top HU \end{pmatrix}+O(e^{-\rho T}).$$

The lower block is positive definite. Its block inverse is $H^+$ plus the outer product of $e-H^+g$ divided by the scalar Schur complement $\beta^2T+c_{00}-g^\top H^+g+O(e^{-\rho T})$. Expanding that reciprocal proves the operator-norm remainder. Thus long maturity supplies unbounded precision in only one asymptotic direction. Every orthogonal exposure retains the finite transient-information floor $x^\top H^+x$; the vector $w$, rather than $e$ alone, records the finite coupling to the stationary direction.

For the four-name parameters above, the certificate evaluates the exact finite-$T$ loading Gramian by quadrature. At $T=2048$, the operator-norm error after the rank-one inverse correction is $7.98785874\times10^{-3}$, with observed order $2.006271$ between the last two doubled maturities. It also gives $T b^\top J(T)^{-1}b=1.00427059$ versus limit $1$, and transient inverse trace $3728.05152$ versus $\operatorname{tr}(H^+)=3726.17300$.

This is again a fixed-parameter deterministic loading-Gram statement. It neither changes the fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor asserts a maturity-uniform pricing remainder, and it does not interchange the long-maturity and fast-switching limits.

Fixed Green–Kubo rank determines the number of long-end directions

The rank-one conclusion has a sharp finite-rank extension. Let $K$ be any fixed positive semidefinite Green–Kubo matrix of rank $r$, put $\Delta_t=\operatorname{diag}B(t)$ and $\Delta_\infty=\operatorname{diag}b$, and consider the integrated correction

$$D(T)=K\circ J(T)=\int_0^T\Delta_tK\Delta_t\,dt.$$

This Gram representation is the continuous loading version of the Schur product theorem.

Because every $b_j>0$, the stationary matrix

$$A=\Delta_\infty K\Delta_\infty$$

also has rank $r$. Exponential loading convergence gives

$$D(T)=TA+C_K+O(e^{-\rho T}),\qquad C_K=K\circ C =\int_0^\infty(\Delta_tK\Delta_t-A)\,dt.$$

Let $Q$ project onto $\operatorname{ran}A$, put $P=I-Q$, and define on $\ker A$

$$H_K=PC_KP.$$

Rank-$r$ long-maturity theorem. Suppose $D(T_0)$ is positive definite for some $T_0>0$. If $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_r>0$ are the positive eigenvalues of $A$ and $\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_{d-r}>0$ are the eigenvalues of $H_K$ on $\ker A$, then

$$\lambda_j(D(T))=Ta_j+O(1),\quad 1\le j\le r,$$ $$\lambda_{r+j}(D(T))=\mu_j+O(T^{-1}), \quad 1\le j\le d-r,$$ $$\operatorname{cond}_2D(T)={a_1\over\mu_{d-r}}T+O(1), \qquad \det D(T)=T^r\det_+(A)\det(H_K)+O(T^{r-1}).$$

Under the distinct-mean-reversion assumptions of the full-rank criterion above, the premise $D(T_0)\succ0$ is equivalent simply to $K_{ii}>0$ for every name, and then holds for every $T_0>0$.

The finite-maturity full-rank premise supplies the missing positivity, rather than merely assuming it. For $z\in\ker A$,

$$z^\top H_Kz =\int_0^\infty(\Delta_tz)^\top K(\Delta_tz)\,dt\ge0.$$

If this vanishes, analyticity of the CIR loadings implies $\Delta_tz\in\ker K$ for every $t>0$, hence $D(T_0)z=0$ and therefore $z=0$. Thus $H_K$ is positive definite. The displayed spectrum and determinant then follow from the $(\operatorname{ran}A,\ker A)$ block Schur complement.

There is also a matrix-level inverse limit. Let $A^+$ and $H_K^+$ be the Moore–Penrose inverses on their complementary supports and set

$$L=Q-H_K^+PC_KQ.$$
$$D(T)^{-1}=H_K^++{1\over T}LA^+L^\top+O(T^{-2})$$

in operator norm.

This follows by inverting the same two blocks: the Schur complement on $\operatorname{ran}A$ is $TA+O(1)$, while the lower block tends to $H_K$. The correction has rank $r$. Hence maturity integration may make $D(T)$ exactly full rank at every finite maturity, but precisely $\operatorname{rank}K$ information directions accumulate without bound at the long end. The preceding theorem is the $r=1$ specialization.

A five-name certificate takes the rank-two matrix $K=FF^\top$ with $F=(\mathbf1,x)$ and $x=(-2,-0.7,0.2,1.1,2.3)^\top$, and distinct positive-volatility CIR loadings with $\kappa=(0.25,0.6,1.2,2.4,5)$ and $\sigma=0.18$, and obtains full integrated rank five. The limiting transient eigenvalues are

$$(7.28134479\times10^{-4},\ 2.20648314\times10^{-2},\ 7.86025259\times10^{-1}),$$

while the two stationary eigenvalues are $(1.95826087,57.5035564)$. At $T=2048$, all five scaled convergence orders lie between $0.999991$ and $1.006072$; $\operatorname{cond}_2D(T)/T$ is $78840.4105$ versus limit $78973.8133$, and $\det D(T)/T^2$ is $1.41432970\times10^{-3}$ versus limit $1.42204704\times10^{-3}$. The inverse-expansion remainder is $3.06155141\times10^{-3}$ with observed order $2.006827$.

The result concerns a fixed $K$ and fixed CIR parameters. It explicitly distinguishes the $r$ stationary directions inherited from fixed Green–Kubo rank from the additional finite information created by heterogeneous maturity loadings; it asserts no switching-rate-uniform price error and no interchange of maturity and switching limits.

Full rank becomes singular at a quantified rate

Algebraic full rank does not make nearby loading parameters stably identifiable. This can be made sharp. Fix $T>0$, a common $\sigma\ge0$, distinct real numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_d$, and put $\kappa_j=\kappa_0+\epsilon x_j$. Let $B_\kappa$ solve

$$B_\kappa'=1-\kappa B_\kappa-\tfrac12\sigma^2B_\kappa^2, \qquad B_\kappa(0)=0,$$

and define $J_\epsilon=(\langle B_{\kappa_i},B_{\kappa_j}\rangle_{L^2[0,T]})$. If its eigenvalues are ordered $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d$, then, for fixed $d$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$,

$$\lambda_j(J_\epsilon)=\Theta(\epsilon^{2(j-1)}),\qquad \operatorname{cond}_2J_\epsilon=\Theta(\epsilon^{-2(d-1)}).$$

The leading determinant is explicit. With indices $r,s=0,\ldots,d-1$, set

$$G^{(d)}_{rs}=\int_0^T \partial_\kappa^rB_{\kappa_0}(t) \partial_\kappa^sB_{\kappa_0}(t)\,dt.$$

Then

$$\det J_\epsilon =\epsilon^{d(d-1)} \frac{\prod_{i<j}(x_j-x_i)^2}{\prod_{r=0}^{d-1}(r!)^2} \det G^{(d)}\{1+O(\epsilon)\}.$$

To prove it, Taylor-expand the $d$ vectors $B_{\kappa_0+\epsilon x_j}$ in $L^2[0,T]$ and take their exterior product. Its first nonzero term is the Vandermonde determinant times $B_{\kappa_0}\wedge\partial_\kappa B_{\kappa_0}\wedge\cdots\wedge \partial_\kappa^{d-1}B_{\kappa_0}$, with power $\epsilon^{d(d-1)/2}$; squaring its norm gives the formula. The derivative vectors are independent because the Riccati equation gives

$$\partial_\kappa^rB_{\kappa_0}(t) =\frac{(-1)^r}{r+1}t^{r+1}+O(t^{r+2}).$$

Divided-difference column operations then give singular values of the loading synthesis map of orders $1,\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon^{d-1}$, which proves the individual eigenvalue powers. This is the confluent counterpart of the classical Vandermonde conditioning studied by Gautschi (1978). If $K=aa^\top$ with fixed nonzero coordinates, the congruence $D_\epsilon=\operatorname{diag}(a)J_\epsilon\operatorname{diag}(a)$ has the same powers. Thus two regimes still give full integrated rank for every $\epsilon>0$, while the weakest identified direction vanishes as $\epsilon^{2(d-1)}$. This is a fixed-$d$, fixed-$T$ statement; it is not uniform in the number of names or maturity.

The constants multiplying those powers are explicit as well. Let $W:L^2[0,T]\leftarrow\mathbb R^d$ have columns $\partial_\kappa^rB_{\kappa_0}$, $0\le r<d$, and define the scaled Vandermonde matrix

$$V_{rj}={x_j^r\over r!},\qquad 0\le r<d,\quad 1\le j\le d.$$

Take the Hilbert-space QR factorization $W=U_WR_W$ and the LQ factorization $V=L_VU_V$. Equivalently, $R_W$ is the upper Cholesky factor of $G^{(d)}$ and $L_V$ is the lower Cholesky factor of $VV^\top$. Then

$$\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0} {\lambda_{r+1}(J_\epsilon)\over\epsilon^{2r}} =\left|(R_W)_{r+1,r+1}(L_V)_{r+1,r+1}\right|^2, \qquad 0\le r<d.$$

Indeed, Taylor expansion gives the loading synthesis map as $WD_\epsilon\{V+O(\epsilon)\}$, with $D_\epsilon=\operatorname{diag}(1,\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon^{d-1})$. After the two orthogonal changes of basis, the unique lowest-power minor in each exterior power uses the first rows and columns. Consecutive exterior-product limits give the individual singular-value constants; squaring gives the displayed Gram-eigenvalue constants. Their product is exactly the determinant coefficient above.

The certificate uses positive volatilities $\sigma_j=0.02$, distinct $\kappa_j$ between $0.2$ and $5$, and strict Feller mean levels for $2\le d\le8$. Every integrated matrix is full rank. At $d=8$ its smallest eigenvalue is $1.4691\times10^{-8}$ and its condition number is $6.7541\times10^9$. This is an important distinction: algebraic rank can be maximally amplified while statistical identification becomes extremely ill-conditioned. A separate 70-digit coalescence check at $d=4$ finds eigenvalue log--log slopes $(0.000316,2.000484,4.000156,5.999654)$. The explicit leading eigenvalue constants are $(3.250068477,0.02564049977,0.0007991812818,0.00001379064292)$; at $\epsilon=1/256$ their largest relative error is $1.67\times10^{-5}$. The determinant ratio is $1.000021036$ against the displayed constant, and the condition-number slope is $-5.999901$.

Partial collisions have a cluster spectrum

The one-center hierarchy has a precise multi-center extension. Let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_g$ be distinct, let cluster $c$ contain $m_c$ distinct fixed nodes $x_{c,1},\ldots,x_{c,m_c}$, and put $\kappa_{c,j}=\alpha_c+\epsilon x_{c,j}$. Assume the finite jet family

$$\left\{\partial_\kappa^rB_{\alpha_c}: 1\le c\le g,\ 0\le r<m_c\right\}$$

is linearly independent in $L^2[0,T]$. If $r_1\le\cdots\le r_d$ is the sorted multiset $\biguplus_{c=1}^g\{0,1,\ldots,m_c-1\}$, then the descending eigenvalues of the loading Gram matrix satisfy, as $\epsilon\downarrow0$ with $g$, the cluster data, and $T$ fixed,

$$\lambda_j(J_\epsilon)=\Theta(\epsilon^{2r_j}), \qquad 1\le j\le d=\sum_c m_c.$$

Thus exactly $g$ eigenvalues stay of constant order. If $m_{\max}=\max_c m_c$, then

$$\operatorname{cond}_2J_\epsilon =\Theta(\epsilon^{-2(m_{\max}-1)}),\qquad \det J_\epsilon =\Theta\!\left(\epsilon^{2\sum_c {m_c\choose2}}\right).$$

The determinant coefficient is explicit. Order the jet vectors by $(c,r)$ and let $G_{\rm jet}$ be their Gram matrix. Then

$$\det J_\epsilon= \epsilon^{2\sum_c {m_c\choose2}}\det G_{\rm jet} \prod_{c=1}^g {\prod_{i<j}(x_{c,j}-x_{c,i})^2 \over\prod_{r=0}^{m_c-1}(r!)^2}\{1+O(\epsilon)\}.$$

For the proof, Taylor-expand separately at each $\alpha_c$ and right-multiply each cluster by its fixed scaled-Vandermonde inverse. The transformed columns are $\epsilon^r\{\partial_\kappa^rB_{\alpha_c}+O(\epsilon)\}$. Jet independence bounds the resulting synthesis map above and below by the diagonal map with entries $\epsilon^r$, proving all singular exponents; squaring gives the Gram exponents. Taking the exterior product gives the coefficient above. This is the finite-dimensional derivative-matching structure introduced by Hermite (1878) and is consistent with the confluent-Vandermonde bounds of Gautschi (1978).

For an arbitrary analytic loading family, the theorem remains conditional on the displayed joint jet independence. For the CIR Riccati family, however, that condition is automatic. Write

$$B_\kappa(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}c_n(\kappa)t^n.$$

Substitution in the Riccati equation gives $c_1=1$ and

$$(n+1)c_{n+1}(\kappa) =-\kappa c_n(\kappa) -\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1} c_j(\kappa)c_{n-j}(\kappa).$$

Induction shows that $c_n$ is a polynomial of degree $n-1$ with leading coefficient $(-1)^{n-1}/n!$. For $d=\sum_cm_c$, form the Taylor-jet matrix

$$M_{n,(c,r)}=\partial_\kappa^r c_n(\alpha_c),qquad 1\le n\le d,\quad 0\le r<m_c.$$

If the distinct centers are ordered increasingly, triangular change of polynomial basis and the confluent Vandermonde determinant give

$$\det M= \left\{\prod_{n=1}^d\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!}\right\} \left\{\prod_{c=1}^g\prod_{r=0}^{m_c-1}r!\right\} \prod_{c<e}(\alpha_e-\alpha_c)^{m_cm_e}\ne0.$$

Any $L^2[0,T]$ relation among the CIR jets is an analytic identity in $t$; its first $d$ Taylor coefficients are annihilated by $M$. The displayed determinant therefore forces every coefficient in the relation to vanish. Thus, for fixed common $\sigma\ge0$ and every $T>0$, distinct CIR cluster centers alone imply joint jet independence and make $G_{\rm jet}$ positive definite. This is precisely the derivative interpolation structure in Hermite's original 1878 paper; no numerical genericity assumption is needed for CIR.

The exact rational certificate applies the recurrence to cluster sizes $(3,2,1)$ and centers $(4/5,2,4)$. The resulting six-by-six Taylor-jet determinant is $-1536/48828125=-3.145728\times10^{-5}$, exactly equal to the formula above and independent of the lower-degree volatility terms. The positive-CIR quadrature certificate then checks the asymptotic constants at 80-digit precision for the same cluster sizes, centers $(0.8,2,4)$, common volatility $0.02$, and $T=4$. It finds eigenvalue slopes $(0.000007,0.000058,0.000344,1.999615,2.000232,3.999713)$, determinant slope $7.999970$, condition-number slope $-3.999706$, and determinant ratio $0.999999999999953$ against the explicit coefficient. Consequently, one rank-one fixed-feature Green--Kubo matrix with nonzero loadings still yields full integrated rank for every sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$; cluster multiplicities, rather than the number of regimes, determine the asymptotic conditioning.

The explicit two-name benchmark

Take a symmetric two-state generator $Q_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $\pi=(1/2,1/2)$, and two CIR names with

name$\kappa_j$$\sigma_j$$(\theta_{j,1},\theta_{j,2})$$x_j$
110.1$(0.06,0.02)$0.03
220.1$(0.05,0.015)$0.03

The smallest Feller margin $\min_{i,j}(2\kappa_j\theta_{j,i}-\sigma_j^2)$ is $0.03>0$. With $d_j=(\theta_{j,1}-\theta_{j,2})/2$, the one-dimensional centered space gives $K_{jk}=\kappa_jd_j\kappa_kd_k/(2m)$. Thus $K$ has rank one. Yet $B_1$ and $B_2$ are linearly independent on every positive maturity interval: both have $B_j'(0)=1$, while $B_j''(0)=-\kappa_j$ differ. Their Gram matrix $J(T)$ is positive definite, and multiplication by the nonzero diagonal $(\kappa_1d_1,\kappa_2d_2)$ proves $D(T)$ has rank two.

For $T=5$ and $m=1$, the eigenvalues of $K$ are $(0,\,8.125\times10^{-4})$, whereas those of $D(5)$ are approximately $(4.143\times10^{-6},\,1.343\times10^{-3})$. The certificate checks the Feller condition and eigenvalues, then solves the exact joint-survival ODE. The residual of the pairwise formula multiplied by $m^2$ stabilizes at approximately $-8.6\times10^{-5}$ as $m$ increases from 10 to 80.

What the curves identify

Suppose $x_j,\kappa_j,\sigma_j$ are known, every name shares the same stationary-start chain, and ideal first-order marginal and pairwise survival curves are available over a maturity interval. The marginal curve for $j$, after subtracting $-B_jx_j$, has the coefficients $-\kappa_j\bar\theta_j$ on $\int B_j$ and $K_{jj}$ on $\int B_j^2$. Those two functions are independent because $B_j$ is nonconstant. The pairwise log ratio then determines $K_{jk}$ through $2K_{jk}J_{jk}(T)$, with $J_{jk}(T)>0$ for $T>0$. Thus these curves identify $\bar\theta_j$ and the whole rate-scaled matrix $K$ at first order, although they do not identify $Q_0$, $\theta_{j,i}$, or $m$ separately.

After removing the known loadings, the correct regime-count restriction is $\operatorname{rank}K\le n-1$, or equivalently $\operatorname{rank}[\operatorname{diag}(B(t))^{-1} D'(t)\operatorname{diag}(B(t))^{-1}]\le n-1$ for $t>0$. The corresponding bound on $\operatorname{rank}D(T)$ is false. Estimating derivatives from noisy curves may be unstable; the entrywise division $K_{jk}=D_{jk}(T)/J_{jk}(T)$ uses integrated loadings instead.

Scope: fixed CIR $\kappa_j,\sigma_j$, switching mean levels, independent Brownian motions and Cox triggers, arbitrary initial regime prior independent of the specified $x_j$, and first-order fast-switching accuracy. Switching $\kappa$ or $\sigma$ changes the Riccati reduction and requires its own identification argument.
