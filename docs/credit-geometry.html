Green–Kubo geometry of joint default

Positive CIR intensities, an identifiable instantaneous matrix, and a maturity-integrated rank trap.

Research note, 23 September 2026. The constant-hazard example gives the starting point; this note changes the loadings while retaining nonnegative intensities.

A positive-intensity model

Conditionally independent Cox defaults have intensities $X_j(t)$, $j=1,\ldots,d$. Specifically, let $E_j$ be independent unit-exponential variables, independent of the Brownian motions and the regime, and set $\tau_j=\inf\{t:\int_0^tX_j(s)ds\ge E_j\}$. A common irreducible $n$-state chain $y_t$ with stationary law $\pi$ switches their CIR mean levels:

$$dX_j=\kappa_j(\theta_{j,y_t}-X_j)dt+ \sigma_j\sqrt{X_j}\,dW_j,\qquad y_t\ \text{has generator }mQ_0.$$

Assume $\kappa_j>0$, $\sigma_j>0$, $X_j(0)=x_j>0$, independent Brownian motions and the independent default triggers just defined. Taking $2\kappa_j\min_i\theta_{j,i}>\sigma_j^2$ keeps each intensity strictly positive over every finite time interval, even as regimes switch. For a subset $A$ of names the joint survival price is $S_A(T)=\mathbb E\exp(-\int_0^T\sum_{j\in A}X_j(t)\,dt)$.

The CIR Riccati loading $B_j$ and the regime-dependent factor obey

$$B_j'=1-\kappa_jB_j-\tfrac12\sigma_j^2B_j^2,\qquad B_j(0)=0,$$ $$S_A^\nu(T)=e^{-\sum_{j\in A}B_j(T)x_j}\,\nu a_A(T),\qquad a_A'=\left(mQ_0+\operatorname{diag} \left[-\sum_{j\in A}\kappa_j\theta_j B_j(t)\right]\right)a_A,\quad a_A(0)={\bf1}.$$

This is an exact finite-dimensional reduction for any initial regime prior $\nu$. The loadings $B_j$ vary with maturity and can differ across names, although $Q_0$ is shared.

The first-order matrix

Let $h_j=-\kappa_j(\theta_j-\bar\theta_j{\bf1})$, where $\bar\theta_j=\pi\theta_j$, and define the fixed Green–Kubo matrix and the loading integrals

$$K_{jk}=\frac1m\mathcal K^{\rm sym}_0(h_j,h_k),\qquad J_{jk}(T)=\int_0^T B_j(t)B_k(t)\,dt.$$

Here $\mathcal K^{\rm sym}_0$ uses $Q_0$ and is the half-asymptotic covariance of the two centered additive functionals. On each fixed interval $0\le T\le L$, the stationary-start fast-switching expansion gives

$$\log S_A(T)=-\sum_{j\in A} \left[B_j(T)x_j+\kappa_j\bar\theta_j\int_0^T B_j(t)\,dt\right] +\sum_{j,k\in A}D_{jk}(T)+O_L(m^{-2}),\qquad D_{jk}(T)=K_{jk}J_{jk}(T).$$

The $O_L(m^{-2})$ error is uniform in $T$ for fixed model parameters. It follows by separating the constant and centered regime components in the $a_A$ equation: the centered component is $-Q_0^\#\tilde g/m$ plus an $O_L(m^{-2})$ remainder. Since all $B_j(0)=0$, the first-order initial-layer amplitude vanishes. Substitution into the stationary component gives the quadratic Green–Kubo term. This is an asymptotic statement, not an exact low-rank statement at finite $m$.

An arbitrary initial regime prior

Hiddenness does not imply a stationary start. Let $\nu$ be any initial prior for the regime; if the displayed initial intensities are observed from an already-running system, $\nu$ can instead be the posterior conditional on those observations. Put $H=-Q_0^\#$ and

$$f_A(t)=\sum_{j\in A}B_j(t)h_j,\qquad h_j=-\kappa_j(\theta_j-\bar\theta_j{\bf1}).$$

The same outer expansion, now retained before multiplication by the initial prior, gives

$$\log S_A^\nu(T)=\log S_A^\pi(T)+\frac1m\,\nu Hf_A(T)+O_L(m^{-2}).$$

This estimate is uniform on every fixed $0\le T\le L$: because $B_j(0)=0$, one has $f_A(0)=0$, so the first-order outer corrector already satisfies the initial condition and no order-$m^{-1}$ boundary layer is needed. The term is genuine start-regime memory and vanishes for $\nu=\pi$, since $\pi H=0$.

A useful cancellation follows from $f_{\{j,k\}}=f_{\{j\}}+f_{\{k\}}$:

$$\log\frac{S_{\{j,k\}}^\nu(T)}{S_{\{j\}}^\nu(T)S_{\{k\}}^\nu(T)} =2K_{jk}J_{jk}(T)+O_L(m^{-2}).$$

Thus the first-order pairwise log-survival ratio and the identification of $K_{jk}$ are robust to an unknown common prior, even though every marginal survival curve changes at order $m^{-1}$. Since default correlation is itself order $m^{-1}$, its change with the prior begins at order $m^{-2}$. The certificate checks all three statements directly against the finite-rate ODE: the measured rates are $2.004$ for survival after the endpoint correction, $2.010$ for the prior-independent pair formula, and $1.998$ for the prior effect on correlation.

For two distinct names, the observable pairwise survival ratio is

$$\log\frac{S_{\{j,k\}}(T)}{S_{\{j\}}(T)S_{\{k\}}(T)} =2K_{jk}J_{jk}(T)+O_L(m^{-2}).$$

Its first-order sign is the sign of $K_{jk}$. Off-diagonal values can be negative while the entire $K$ matrix remains positive semidefinite. Different states having high instantaneous hazards do not, by themselves, determine that sign: the integrated lagged covariance does.

Two names suffice to detect cycle direction

Unordered joint survival sees only the symmetric Green–Kubo matrix because hazard multiplication operators commute. The identity of the first defaulter is different: it is an absorbing-channel label and retains time orientation. Let two names have positive regime-dependent hazards $h_1,h_2$, put $h=h_1+h_2$, and start the regime in stationarity. The exact probability that name 1 defaults first is

$$p_1=\pi^\top(\operatorname{diag}h-mQ_0)^{-1}h_1.$$

Define the unscaled oriented matrix

$$K^{(0)}_{jk}=-\pi\cdot\left[(h_j-\bar h_j{\bf1})\odot Q_0^\#(h_k-\bar h_k{\bf1})\right].$$

A singular resolvent expansion gives

$$p_1=\frac{\bar h_1}{\bar h} +\frac1{m\bar h}\left\{ \frac{\bar h_1}{\bar h}K^{(0)}_{h,h}-K^{(0)}_{h,1}\right\} +O(m^{-2}),$$ $$p_1(Q_0)-p_1(Q_0^{\rm rev}) =\frac{K^{(0)}_{12}-K^{(0)}_{21}}{m\bar h}+O(m^{-2}).$$

For a direct proof, solve $(\operatorname{diag}h-mQ_0)x=h_1$ with $x=x_0{\bf1}+m^{-1}x_1+\cdots$. Solvability at order one gives $x_0=\bar h_1/\bar h$ and $x_1=Q_0^\#(x_0h-h_1)+a_1{\bf1}$. Solvability at the next order, $\pi\operatorname{diag}(h)x_1=0$, gives $a_1=(x_0K^{(0)}_{h,h}-K^{(0)}_{h,1})/\bar h$. Since $p_1=\pi x=x_0+a_1/m+O(m^{-2})$, this proves the first display. Time reversal transposes $K^{(0)}$ and gives the second.

Here $K^{(0)}_{h,1}=K^{(0)}_{11}+K^{(0)}_{21}$ and $K^{(0)}_{h,h}=\sum_{j,k}K^{(0)}_{jk}$. Thus only two event types are required; at least three hidden states are needed for a finite irreducible chain to violate detailed balance.

For the three-state cycle with clockwise/backward rates $(2,0.1)$ and hazards

$$h_1=(0.07,0.01,0.01),\qquad h_2=(0.01,0.07,0.01),$$

one obtains $\bar h_1=\bar h_2=0.03$, $K^{(0)}_{12}=-9.50119\times10^{-6}$ and $K^{(0)}_{21}=-1.90024\times10^{-4}$. The predicted clockwise-minus-counterclockwise gap is therefore $0.0030087094/m+O(m^{-2})$.

What a multi-name race identifies

With $d$ competing causes, one unweighted race does not generally identify the whole oriented matrix. Let $w_a>0$ scale cause $a$, put $\bar h(w)=\sum_a w_a\bar h_a$, and decompose the unscaled oriented matrix as

$$K^{(0)}=G+A,\qquad G=\tfrac12(K^{(0)}+K^{(0)\top}),\qquad A=\tfrac12(K^{(0)}-K^{(0)\top}).$$

The exact cause-probability vector is

$$p_a(w)=\pi^\top\left(\operatorname{diag}\!\left\{\sum_jw_jh_j\right\} -mQ_0\right)^{-1}w_ah_a.$$

Applying the same singular-resolvent calculation componentwise gives

$$p_a(w)=\frac{w_a\bar h_a}{\bar h(w)}+\frac{c_a(w)}m+O(m^{-2}),$$ $$c_a(w)=\frac1{\bar h(w)}\left\{ \frac{w_a\bar h_a}{\bar h(w)}w^\top Gw-w_a(w^\top K^{(0)})_a\right\}.$$

Unordered marginal and joint survivals identify $G$. If $c_a^G(w)$ denotes the coefficient predicted by setting $A=0$, the remaining cause-order signal is exactly

$$c_a(w)-c_a^G(w)=\frac{w_a(Aw)_a}{\bar h(w)},$$ $$p_a(w;Q_0)-p_a(w;Q_0^{\rm rev}) =\frac{2w_a(Aw)_a}{m\bar h(w)}+O(m^{-2}).$$

Thus one unweighted race sees only $A\mathbf1$, a $(d-1)$-dimensional contrast. For $d\ge3$ it can be zero even when $A\ne0$. Antisymmetry makes the sharp multi-design result stronger than ordinary column-by-column recovery. If the exposure designs span a $q$-dimensional subspace $W$, then the kernel of

$$A\longmapsto(Aw^{(1)},\ldots,Aw^{(q)})$$

is exactly the space of skew maps supported on $W^\perp$, and hence has dimension

$$\binom{d-q}{2}.$$

Indeed, a kernel element vanishes on $W$; skew-adjointness then makes $W^\perp$ invariant, while every skew map on $W^\perp$ gives such an element. Therefore $q=d-1$ linearly independent positive designs identify every antisymmetric $d\times d$ matrix, and this number is minimal: with $q\le d-2$ there is always a nonzero invisible skew map on $W^\perp$. This identification result keeps the fixed oriented parameter matrix separate from any maturity-integrated rank claim.

A sharp three-cause example uses the same directed three-state cycle and cyclic hazards

$$h_1=(0.07,0.01,0.01),\quad h_2=(0.01,0.07,0.01),\quad h_3=(0.01,0.01,0.07).$$

Here $\|A\|_F=2.21094\times10^{-4}$ but $A\mathbf1=0$, so the equal-weight race is exactly unchanged by cycle reversal. At $w=(1,2,4)$ the predicted reversal-gap coefficients for the three causes are $(-0.0017192625,0.0051577876,-0.0034385251)$. Exact resolvents give second-order residual rates $1.998$ for both the probability vector and reversal gap. The equal-weight design alone is the sharp $q=d-2$ counterexample. Adding just one further positive design recovers $A$ with maximum error $1.36\times10^{-19}$. The certificate also verifies the kernel-dimension formula for every $3\le d\le7$ and $1\le q\le d-1$.

Which rank bound survives?

For fixed centered feature vectors $h_1,\ldots,h_d$, the matrix $K\succeq0$ has rank at most $n-1$. In fact its rank equals the dimension spanned by those centered vectors for an irreducible finite chain. At a single maturity, the matrix of correction densities is $\operatorname{diag}(B(t))K\operatorname{diag}(B(t))$, and it has the same rank for $t>0$.

For completeness, if $f$ is centered and $w=-Q_0^\#f$, then $2\mathcal K_0(f,f)=\sum_{i,\ell\ne i}\pi_i(Q_0)_{i\ell} (w_\ell-w_i)^2$. Irreducibility makes this zero only when $w$ is constant, equivalently $f=0$. Thus the symmetric Green–Kubo form is an inner product on the $(n-1)$-dimensional centered space, which proves the claimed rank of $K$.

The integrated matrix $D(T)=K\circ J(T)$ is also positive semidefinite, because it is a time integral of positive semidefinite matrices. Its rank need not be at most $n-1$. Time-dependent diagonal rescalings rotate a low-dimensional range as $t$ varies. In particular, a rank test applied directly to $D(T)$ is not a lower bound on the number of regimes. This distinction is absent for constant hazards: all $B_j(t)=1$ and $D(T)=TK$.

Two regimes, rank two

The smallest Feller margin $\min_{i,j}(2\kappa_j\theta_{j,i}-\sigma_j^2)$ is $0.03>0$. With $d_j=(\theta_{j,1}-\theta_{j,2})/2$, the one-dimensional centered space gives $K_{jk}=\kappa_jd_j\kappa_kd_k/(2m)$. Thus $K$ has rank one. Yet $B_1$ and $B_2$ are linearly independent on every positive maturity interval: both have $B_j'(0)=1$, while $B_j''(0)=-\kappa_j$ differ. Their Gram matrix $J(T)$ is positive definite, and multiplication by the nonzero diagonal $(\kappa_1d_1,\kappa_2d_2)$ proves $D(T)$ has rank two.

For $T=5$ and $m=1$, the eigenvalues of $K$ are $(0,\,8.125\times10^{-4})$, whereas those of $D(5)$ are approximately $(4.143\times10^{-6},\,1.343\times10^{-3})$. The certificate checks the Feller condition and eigenvalues, then solves the exact joint-survival ODE. The residual of the pairwise formula multiplied by $m^2$ stabilizes at approximately $-8.6\times10^{-5}$ as $m$ increases from 10 to 80.

What the curves identify

Suppose $x_j,\kappa_j,\sigma_j$ are known, every name shares the same stationary-start chain, and ideal first-order marginal and pairwise survival curves are available over a maturity interval. The marginal curve for $j$, after subtracting $-B_jx_j$, has the coefficients $-\kappa_j\bar\theta_j$ on $\int B_j$ and $K_{jj}$ on $\int B_j^2$. Those two functions are independent because $B_j$ is nonconstant. The pairwise log ratio then determines $K_{jk}$ through $2K_{jk}J_{jk}(T)$, with $J_{jk}(T)>0$ for $T>0$. Thus these curves identify $\bar\theta_j$ and the whole rate-scaled matrix $K$ at first order, although they do not identify $Q_0$, $\theta_{j,i}$, or $m$ separately.

After removing the known loadings, the correct regime-count restriction is $\operatorname{rank}K\le n-1$, or equivalently $\operatorname{rank}[\operatorname{diag}(B(t))^{-1} D'(t)\operatorname{diag}(B(t))^{-1}]\le n-1$ for $t>0$. The corresponding bound on $\operatorname{rank}D(T)$ is false. Estimating derivatives from noisy curves may be unstable; the entrywise division $K_{jk}=D_{jk}(T)/J_{jk}(T)$ uses integrated loadings instead.

Scope: fixed CIR $\kappa_j,\sigma_j$, switching mean levels, independent Brownian motions and Cox triggers, arbitrary initial regime prior independent of the specified $x_j$, and first-order fast-switching accuracy. Switching $\kappa$ or $\sigma$ changes the Riccati reduction and requires its own identification argument.
