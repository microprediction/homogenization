Exact cyclic state count for two skew modes

A Fourier and compression theorem for translation-invariant chains, with a sharp nine-state bound and an exact ten-state witness.

A cyclically translation-invariant chain with $5\le n\le8$ has at most one intrinsic Green–Kubo skew frequency larger than one. At nine states the exact largest possible second frequency is $\rho_9=1.245891659322\ldots<\cot(\pi/5)$, while a directed ten-cycle realizes two copies of the target exactly; ten is therefore the exact minimum in this architecture.

The chain and its full intrinsic frequencies

Write the state space as $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ and let

$$Qf(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}r_j\{f(x+j)-f(x)\},\qquad r_j\ge0.$$

Assume that the jumps with positive rates generate the group, so the chain is irreducible and its stationary law is uniform. The support may be dense and noncactus; nearest-neighbour or reversible symmetry is not assumed.

On centered real potentials put

$$L=-Q,\qquad H={L+L^*\over2},\qquad A={L-L^*\over2},\qquad \mathcal A=H^{-1/2}AH^{-1/2}.$$

The positive intrinsic skew frequencies are the positive singular values of the real skew matrix $\mathcal A$, one for each conjugate Fourier pair. With $1\le k<n/2$, set

$$a_k=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}r_j\{1-\cos(2\pi kj/n)\},\qquad b_k=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}r_j\sin(2\pi kj/n).$$
$$\boxed{\kappa_k={|b_k|\over a_k}}.$$

Irreducibility makes every $a_k$ positive. A possible real Fourier mode at $k=n/2$ has zero skew frequency; the discrete-Fourier diagonalization is the classical circulant construction in Davis (2012).

The small-cycle exclusion

$$5\le n\le8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \#\{k:\kappa_k>1\}\le1.$$

For an orientation $\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}$ define the contribution of jump $j$ to the inequality $\epsilon b_k>a_k$ by

$$h_{k,\epsilon}(j)=\epsilon\sin(2\pi kj/n)-\{1-\cos(2\pi kj/n)\}.$$

If $\kappa_k>1$, one orientation has $\sum_jr_jh_{k,\epsilon}(j)>0$. To rule out two such modes it suffices, for every pair and both orientations, to find positive $c$ with

$$c\,h_{k,\epsilon}(j)+h_{\ell,\delta}(j)\le0 \quad\hbox{for every }j.$$

Multiplication by $r_j$ and summation would otherwise contradict two strict positive inequalities. The finite certificates are especially short:

The sharp nine-state boundary

$$n=9\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \kappa_{(2)}\le\rho_9, \qquad 1.245891659322<\rho_9<1.245891659323,$$

Here $\kappa_{(2)}$ is the second largest of the four full frequencies and $\rho_9$ is exact, not a fitted decimal. Put

$$h^{\rho}_{k,\epsilon}(j)=\epsilon\sin(2\pi kj/9)-\rho\{1-\cos(2\pi kj/9)\}$$

and define $\rho_9$ as the unique positive root of the quadratic

$$D(\rho)=h^{\rho}_{1,+}(1)h^{\rho}_{4,-}(2) -h^{\rho}_{1,+}(2)h^{\rho}_{4,-}(1).$$

The leading coefficient of $D$ is negative and its constant coefficient is positive, so its two roots have opposite signs; outward rational trigonometric bounds give the displayed isolating interval for the positive one.

For every pair of modes and orientations outside one six-element symmetry orbit, the jumpwise separator is simply

$$h^{\rho_9}_{k,\epsilon}(j)+h^{\rho_9}_{\ell,\delta}(j)<0.$$

The orbit is generated from $(k,\ell,\epsilon,\delta)=(1,4,+,-)$ by multiplying jumps by units modulo nine and by reversal. For that representative choose

$$c=-{h^{\rho_9}_{4,-}(1)\over h^{\rho_9}_{1,+}(1)}>0.$$

Then $c h^{\rho_9}_{1,+}(j)+h^{\rho_9}_{4,-}(j)$ vanishes at $j=1,2$ and is strictly negative at the other six jumps. The first equality defines $c$ and the second is exactly $D(\rho_9)=0$. Summing against nonnegative rates proves that two signed mode ratios cannot both exceed $\rho_9$, hence $\kappa_{(2)}\le\rho_9$.

The bound is attained. Take only jumps one and two, with

$$r_1={-h^{\rho_9}_{1,+}(2)\over h^{\rho_9}_{1,+}(1)-h^{\rho_9}_{1,+}(2)} =0.112960633715\ldots,\qquad r_2=1-r_1.$$

Both weights are positive, while the two weighted signed margins vanish by the determinant identity. Thus modes one and four both have frequency exactly $\rho_9$, proving sharpness.

The certificate checks 300 rationally enclosed Fourier inequalities, including every non-equality case in the sharp proof. It derives a rational enclosure of $\pi$ from Machin's formula and then uses alternating rational Taylor enclosures for sine and cosine, so neither the root isolation nor the exclusions rest on floating rounding.

Why features cannot evade the full spectrum

Let $F$ be any centered $n\times4$ feature matrix of rank four and let $U=L^{-1}_0F$ be its centered Poisson potentials. Up to transposition, which only reverses the skew sign, its Green–Kubo block is

$$K=U^*LU,\qquad S=\operatorname{sym}K=U^*HU,\qquad D=\operatorname{skew}K=U^*AU.$$

After $H$-orthonormalizing the columns of $U$, the normalized feature skew matrix $S^{-1/2}DS^{-1/2}$ is a four-dimensional compression of $\mathcal A$. Apply Cauchy interlacing to the Hermitian matrix $i\mathcal A$: the second positive eigenvalue of the compression is at most the second positive eigenvalue of the full matrix.

Thus a full cyclic chain through eight states cannot produce a four-feature block with two frequencies above one; at nine states its second feature frequency is at most the sharp constant $\rho_9$. This is a compression statement, not an inference from the largest frequency alone.

The exact cyclic state count for the two-mode target

Consider the fixed-parameter population block

$$K_*=I_4+\cot(\pi/5)\operatorname{diag}(J,J),\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

Its two positive intrinsic skew frequencies are both $\cot(\pi/5)=1.376381920471\ldots>\rho_9$. Therefore no translation-invariant chain on $5,6,7,8,$ or $9$ cyclic states realizes $K_*$, regardless of its nonnegative jump rates or the four centered features.

Fewer than five states are already impossible by rank. At ten states take the unit-rate directed cycle and its energy-normalized real Fourier potentials $\widehat c_k,\widehat s_k$ for modes $k=1,2$, together with the energy-normalized alternating mode $\widehat z$.

Put $\alpha=1/\sqrt5$ and choose the four potential columns

$$U=\left(\alpha\widehat c_1+\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\widehat z, \widehat s_1,\widehat c_2,\widehat s_2\right).$$

Distinct Fourier modes are orthogonal in the symmetric energy form, and the alternating mode has zero skew energy. Consequently $U^*HU=I_4$ and the first skew block has frequency $\alpha\cot(\pi/10)$, while the second has frequency $\cot(\pi/5)$.

The exact identity

$$\alpha\cot(\pi/10)=\cot(\pi/5)$$

follows from $\cot^2(\pi/10)=5+2\sqrt5$ and the double-angle formula. With $L=-Q$ and $F=LU$, the Green–Kubo convention gives

$$F^*L^{-1}_0F=U^*L^*U =I_4+\cot(\pi/5)\operatorname{diag}(J,J),$$

after choosing the sine orientations displayed by the certificate. Thus the minimum number of states among all cyclically translation-invariant realizations of $K_*$ is exactly ten.

This does not settle the unrestricted minimum-state problem. In particular, the known nine-state shared-hub realization is necessarily non-translation-invariant; a general overlapping-cycle generator could have different intrinsic geometry.

Certificate and scope

The certificate also checks the sharp nine-state two-jump realization, the exact ten-cycle feature block, and 200 dense random circulant generators by constructing the full normalized skew matrix independently. Its Fourier frequencies agree with the matrix singular values to $5.2\times10^{-15}$, using a portable assertion tolerance of $2\times10^{-11}$.

The theorem concerns one fixed generator and one fixed feature block. It does not identify a hidden generator, convert rank after integrating maturity-dependent loadings into a regime count, or make a statement about marginal versus conditional conformal coverage.
