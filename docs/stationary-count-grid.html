Count bins decouple one order faster

A joint finite-grid theorem, uniform over initial priors and bin lengths.

Let a finite irreducible chain with generator $mQ$ and any initial prior $\nu$ drive nonnegative arrival intensities $\ell_i$. For successive bins of lengths $T_j$, let $X_j$ count arrivals and $p_{\nu,j}$ be its exact unconditional transient marginal law.

$$d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathcal L_\nu(X_1,\ldots,X_r),\bigotimes_jp_{\nu,j}\right) \leq\min\left\{1,\frac{4(r-1)C^2F^2}{m^2\gamma^2}\right\}.$$

Here $F=\|\ell-(\pi\ell)\mathbf1\|_\infty$, and $(C,\gamma)$ bounds exponential mixing of $Q$. Two $O(m^{-1})$ count/state memory defects multiply without conditioning on rare boundary states; stationarity permits a sharper reversed-chain constant.

The bound holds at all speeds and uniformly over bin lengths, with zero intensities and defective generators allowed. Replacing each marginal by an approximation adds at most the sum of its TV errors, giving a second-order joint-grid approximation whenever the marginal errors are second order.

For a stationary start, two unit bins, a unit-rate symmetric base chain, intensities $(3,1)$ and speed 80, the independence error is $0.0000057234$, below the bound $0.0000781250$. The full note proves the rate is sharp: here $m^2$ times the independence error tends to $0.0366312778$, with an explicit remainder.

The bound grows with $r$; a bounded number of bins is needed for the second-order rate. No full-path approximation, filtered conditional-law bound or refinement consistency is claimed.
