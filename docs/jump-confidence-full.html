Shrinking anytime confidence from observed jumps

A complete labeled jump record supplies generator confidence without a mixing bound, stationary start or rate cap. A fixed gamma mixture gives anytime-valid intervals that shrink as state occupation accumulates, including under data-dependent stopping. This uses transition counts as well as holding times; it does not infer a generator from occupation-window moments alone.

Setup and confidence sequence

Let $Y$ have any fixed finite row generator $Q$ on $n\ge2$ known states and any initial law; irreducibility is not needed for coverage. Observe the full continuous path, including jump times and destinations. For $i\ne j$, write

$$S_i(t)=\int_0^t\mathbf1_{\{Y_s=i\}}ds,\qquad N_{ij}(t)=\#\{i\to j\text{ jumps in }[0,t]\},\qquad m=n(n-1).$$

Fix $0<\delta<1$ and gamma shape $a>0$, rate $b>0$ before observing the path; $b$ has units of time. For a candidate transition rate $u>0$, with $N=N_{ij}(t)$ and $S=S_i(t)$, define

$$M_{ij,t}(u)=\frac{b^a\Gamma(a+N)}{\Gamma(a)(b+S)^{a+N}} \frac{e^{uS}}{u^N},\qquad C_{ij}(t)=\{u\ge0:M_{ij,t}(u)\le m/\delta\}.$$

At $u=0$, define $M_{ij,t}(0)=(b/(b+S))^a$ if $N=0$, and $+\infty$ if $N>0$. These conventions retain genuinely zero rates.

$$\boxed{\inf_x\Pr_x\!\left(\forall t\ge0,\ \forall i\ne j: q_{ij}\in C_{ij}(t)\right)\ge1-\delta.}$$

The same event covers every unknown initial mixture and any stopping rule. The gamma distribution is a fixed likelihood-mixture device, not a prior assumption that the true rates are random. No independence of the transition counts is assumed.

Likelihood proof, including the zero-rate boundary

Conditional on any initial state, fix one true edge rate $q=q_{ij}>0$ and an alternative $v>0$. Replacing only that edge by $v$ and adjusting its row diagonal gives another finite generator with the same path support. Its likelihood ratio against the true path law is

$$L_t(v,q)=(v/q)^{N_{ij}(t)}\exp\{-(v-q)S_i(t)\}.$$

This is a nonnegative mean-one martingale in the full path filtration. Integrating it against the fixed density $b^av^{a-1}e^{-bv}/\Gamma(a)$ gives exactly $M_{ij,t}(q)$; conditional Tonelli preserves the martingale property. If $q=0$, then $N_{ij}(t)=0$ for all $t$ almost surely and $M_{ij,t}(0)$ is nonincreasing, hence a supermartingale starting at one.

Ville's inequality bounds the probability that the true rate is ever excluded on one edge by $\delta/m$. A union bound over the $m$ edges proves the displayed event. In particular, do not replace this joint counting-process argument by a claim that $N_{ij}\mid S_i$ is Poisson.

For example, in a two-state chain started in state one, stop at its first departure. Then $N_{12}=1$ deterministically while $S_1$ is exponential with rate $q_{12}$. Conditioning on that random exposure does not produce a Poisson count.

Computing the intervals and a generator radius

Put $K=\log(m/\delta)$. If $S=0$, necessarily $N=0$ almost surely, and $C_{ij}=[0,\infty)$. If $S>0$ and $N=0$,

$$C_{ij}=\left[0,\frac{K+a\log(1+S/b)}{S}\right].$$

If $N>0$, the log mixture is strictly convex in $u$, diverges at both endpoints of $(0,\infty)$, and has minimum at $\widehat q=N/S$. The mixture at that minimum is at most one, since each likelihood is bounded by its maximum. Thus the two roots of $\log M(u)=K$ bound a nonempty finite interval containing $\widehat q$.

For stable numerical root finding use $z=\log(u/\widehat q)$ and

$$N(e^z-1-z)=K-\log M(\widehat q).$$

When all $S_i>0$, set $\widehat q_{ij}=N_{ij}/S_i$ and choose diagonals to give zero row sums. If $C_{ij}=[l_{ij},u_{ij}]$, put $r_{ij}=\max(|\widehat q_{ij}-l_{ij}|,|u_{ij}-\widehat q_{ij}|)$. On the same anytime event,

$$\|\widehat Q(t)-Q\|_2\le\|\widehat Q(t)-Q\|_F \le\left\{\sum_{i\ne j}r_{ij}^2+\sum_i\left(\sum_{j\ne i}r_{ij}\right)^2\right\}^{1/2}.$$

This follows by bounding off-diagonal errors entrywise and diagonal errors by their row sums. The sharper induced-norm and box-vertex bounds in the existing generator-radius theorem apply too. Stationary-law, group-inverse and skew-energy propagation still require their own sufficient tests; the estimated generator need not be irreducible.

Why the mixture is a substantive sharpening

The existing fixed-tilt intervals can already be made anytime-valid by applying Ville to their exponential martingales. But with one fixed finite nonempty $\Theta\subset(0,\infty)$, $\theta_* =\min\Theta$, fixed confidence and $N/S\to q>0$, their limits are

$$l\longrightarrow\frac{q\theta_*}{e^{\theta_*}-1},\qquad u\longrightarrow\frac{q\theta_*}{1-e^{-\theta_*}},\qquad u-l\longrightarrow q\theta_*>0.$$

Here $\theta/(e^\theta-1)$ decreases and $\theta/(1-e^{-\theta})$ increases on $(0,\infty)$, so both limiting extrema use $\theta_*$. This is a limitation of a fixed finite tilt set, not an error in its finite-path guarantee.

By Laplace's formula or Stirling's expansion, the gamma mixture instead has $\log M(\widehat q)=-\tfrac12\log S+O(1)$ whenever $S\to\infty$ and $\widehat q\to q>0$. Expanding $e^z-1-z$ at zero gives interval width $O(\sqrt{\log S/S})$. At a true zero rate, $N=0$ almost surely and the upper endpoint is $O(\log S/S)$.

For an irreducible finite chain, occupation-time ergodicity and the compensated-count strong law give these consistency conditions for every row almost surely. No uniform calendar-time rate over all generators, or optimal iterated-logarithm constant, is asserted.

Why an unvisited row must remain unbounded

Fix a horizon $H>0$ and start state one in the irreducible two-state family

$$Q_{\varepsilon,R}=\begin{pmatrix}-\varepsilon&\varepsilon\\R&-R\end{pmatrix},\qquad \varepsilon,R>0.$$

The constant path $Y_s=1$ throughout $[0,H]$ has probability $e^{-\varepsilon H}$, independent of $R$. If a deterministic procedure returns a finite upper bound for $q_{21}$ on that path, choose $R$ above it and $\varepsilon$ so small that $e^{-\varepsilon H}>\delta$. Its failure probability then exceeds $\delta$, contradicting coverage uniform over all finite irreducible generators.

The same obstruction holds for procedures whose randomized upper bound is finite almost surely on the constant path: as $R\to\infty$ the conditional failure probability tends to one. Thus the unbounded interval at zero exposure is honest missing information, not a defect to repair by a pseudocount. For $H=10$ and $\varepsilon=0.001$, this common-path probability is approximately $0.990049834$ for every $R$.

Checks, literature and observation scope

The certificate checks $216$ gamma integrals, $162$ interval endpoints, exact absorbing-chain identities and analytic first-departure stopping probabilities. It also checks $504$ tilted Feynman–Kac identities on $8$ chains, including sparse and defective cases. These are algebraic and floating cross-checks, not empirical proof of the anytime theorem or outward-rounded arithmetic.

With $n=2$, $\delta=0.05$, $a=b=1$ and algebraic input $N=S=10^6$, the mixture interval is approximately $[0.995385926,1.004628311]$. The existing finite-tilt construction with $\Theta=\{0.1,0.3,1\}$ has width $0.100109704$ at that input and limiting width $0.1$ when the limiting rate is one. This comparison is not an observed trajectory or a Monte Carlo coverage experiment.

The complete-record likelihood and $N/S$ estimator are classical; see Bladt and Sørensen (2005), equations (2.1)–(2.3). Nonnegative-supermartingale confidence sequences are also classical; see Howard et al. (2020), Section 2.3. This note supplies the explicit gamma-mixture specialization, shrinking comparison and unvisited-row obstruction, without a priority claim for those tools.

Exact jump times and destinations are stronger observations than the window moments in the ordered-window confidence program. This does not justify substituting a fitted mixing rate into that program, nor conditioning its coverage on reporting. Hidden states, discrete snapshots, data-selected mixture parameters, time-varying rates and numerical error require separate control.
