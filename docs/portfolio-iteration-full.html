A certified occupation-ratio solver at every maturity

A tilted occupation ratio maps the interval of regimewise Merton fractions into itself. An explicit contraction bound gives a globally convergent constant-fraction solver, stopping certificates and robustness to numerical error, uniformly over all positive maturities and starting priors at sufficiently fast switching.

Setup and theorem

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, where $Q$ is any irreducible finite row generator and $m>0$. Assume common constant $r$, excess returns $b_i$, variances $s_i\ge s_{\min}>0$, risk aversion $\gamma>0$, $p=1-\gamma$, and Brownian stock noise independent of $Y$.

Hold one deterministic unrestricted fraction throughout $[0,T]$ and maximize the certainty-equivalent rate

$$h_i(w)=r+b_iw-\gamma s_iw^2/2,\quad q_i(w)=b_i-\gamma ws_i,\quad J(w)=\frac1{pT}\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{p\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)dt}.$$

At $p=0$ use $J=\mathbb E_\nu\int_0^T h(Y_t)dt/T$. A global maximizer exists and every one lies in $I=[\min_i b_i/(\gamma s_i),\max_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)]$: the score has strict inward signs outside $I$, and $J(w)\le r+\max_i|b_i||w|-\gamma s_{\min}w^2/2$.

A singleton $I$ is exactly solved; otherwise put $B=\max_I\operatorname{osc}q$, $D=\max_I\operatorname{osc}h$, $\delta=|p|D$. Choose $\theta>0$ and a certified column-ratio bound

$$P=e^{\theta Q},\qquad 0<a_0\le\min_j\frac{\min_iP_{ij}}{\max_iP_{ij}},\quad \tau=\theta/m.$$

Such a positive bound exists even for sparse generators, and nonnegative uniformization terms with a bounded factorial tail can certify it. Define the full-path tilted expectation $\mathbb E_wF=\mathbb E_\nu[Fe^{p\int h(w)}]/\mathbb E_\nu e^{p\int h(w)}$ and

$$\mathcal B=\int_0^T b(Y_t)dt,\quad \mathcal S=\int_0^T s(Y_t)dt,\qquad F(w)=\frac{\mathbb E_w\mathcal B}{\gamma\mathbb E_w\mathcal S}\in I.$$

This ratio uses the exact finite-horizon tilt, not stationary Perron weights or a filtered regime posterior. Write $s_{\max}=\max_i s_i$ and set

$$L=\frac{|p|B^2}{\gamma s_{\min}}\min\left\{\frac T4,\frac{\tau e^{\delta\tau}}{2a_0}\right\},\qquad H=\gamma s_{\max}+\gamma s_{\min}L.$$

Start the iteration from any $w_0\in I$.

$$L<1\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{one global maximizer }w_*,\quad w_{k+1}=F(w_k)\in I,$$ $$|w_k-w_*|\le L^k|w_0-w_*|,\quad |w_k-w_*|\le\frac{|F(w_k)-w_k|}{1-L},\quad 0\le J(w_*)-J(w_k)\le\frac H2\left(\frac{|F(w_k)-w_k|}{1-L}\right)^2.$$

These bounds hold for every $T>0$ and every initial prior for which the displayed $L<1$. A single speed condition sufficient for all horizons and priors is

$$d=\delta\theta,\qquad C=\frac{|p|B^2\theta}{2a_0\gamma s_{\min}},\qquad m>d+C,\qquad L\le\frac{Ce^{d/m}}m\le\frac C{m-d}<1.$$

The constants need not be sharp; the short-horizon part of $L$ can certify convergence below this speed threshold. Convergence is not asserted to be monotone in value, nor is $J$ asserted concave on the whole real line.

One step improves the occupation approximation

$$\rho_c=\frac1c\nu\int_0^c e^{tQ}dt,\quad c=mT,\quad w_0=w_c=\frac{\rho_cb}{\gamma\rho_cs},\quad E=\min\left\{\frac{B\delta T}{4},\frac{B}{2a_0}(e^{\delta\tau}-1)\right\}.$$

The score comparison proved below gives $|w_*-w_c|\le E/(\gamma\rho_cs)$. Consequently the exact ratio iterations satisfy

$$|w_k-w_*|\le\frac{L^kE}{\gamma\rho_cs},\qquad J(w_*)-J(w_k)\le\frac H2\left(\frac{L^kE}{\gamma\rho_cs}\right)^2.$$

At fixed model parameters and for each fixed $k$, $k$ exact ratio evaluations give policy error $O(m^{-k-1})$ and CE-rate loss $O(m^{-2k-2})$, uniformly in all $T>0$ and all priors as $m\to\infty$. In particular one tilted ratio evaluation improves the occupation fraction to $O(m^{-2})$ policy error and $O(m^{-4})$ rate loss; it is a semigroup-based correction, not a closed-form inverse-speed coefficient.

If an oracle has $|\widehat F(w)-F(w)|\le\epsilon$ on $I$, use $\widehat w_{k+1}=\Pi_I\widehat F(\widehat w_k)$. Projection is nonexpansive, so

$$|\widehat w_k-w_*|\le L^k|\widehat w_0-w_*|+\frac{\epsilon(1-L^k)}{1-L},\qquad |w-w_*|\le\frac{|\widehat F(w)-w|+\epsilon}{1-L}\quad(w\in I).$$

The CE-rate bound $H|w-w_*|^2/2$ still applies. Thus a fixed numerical error leaves an error floor; the asymptotic orders require oracle errors of the matching order or smaller.

Proof: covariance, ratio differentiation and contraction

For $0\le\lambda\le1$, let $A_\lambda=mQ+\lambda pD_h$ and $z_\lambda(u)=e^{uA_\lambda}\mathbf1$. The finite-horizon tilted chain has kernel $D_{z_\lambda(T-s)}^{-1}e^{(t-s)A_\lambda}D_{z_\lambda(T-t)}$ and initial law $\nu_i z_{\lambda,i}(T)/(\nu z_\lambda(T))$.

On a complete block of length $\tau$, put $g=z_\lambda(T-s-\tau)$, $v_j=\max_iP_{ij}$ and $\mu_j=v_jg_j/\sum_kv_kg_k$. Entrywise Feynman–Kac comparison bounds every transition row below by $\alpha_\lambda\mu$, where $\alpha_\lambda=a_0e^{-\lambda\delta\tau}$, so oscillations contract by $(1-\alpha_\lambda)^{\lfloor(t-s)/\tau\rfloor}$.

For any two state functions $f,g$, conditioning on the earlier state and using the range covariance bound yields, for either time ordering, $|\operatorname{Cov}_\lambda(f(Y_s),g(Y_t))|\le\operatorname{osc}f\operatorname{osc}g(1-\alpha_\lambda)^{\lfloor|t-s|/\tau\rfloor}/4$. Integration over both time triangles, together with the whole-path range bound, gives

$$\frac{|\operatorname{Cov}_\lambda(\int f,\int g)|}{T}\le \operatorname{osc}f\operatorname{osc}g\min\{T/4,\tau/(2\alpha_\lambda)\}.$$

Differentiate the ratio at a fixed $w$; the quotient rule cancels its mean terms, giving

$$F'(w)=\frac{p\operatorname{Cov}_w(\mathcal B-\gamma F(w)\mathcal S,\int_0^Tq_w(Y_t)dt)}{\gamma\mathbb E_w\mathcal S}.$$

Because $F(w)\in I$, the two covariance observables are integrals of $q_{F(w)}$ and $q_w$, each of oscillation at most $B$. Since $\mathbb E_w\mathcal S\ge Ts_{\min}$, the covariance estimate proves $|F'|\le L$.

The map takes $I$ into itself since its ratio is a positive weighted average of $b_i/(\gamma s_i)$. When $L<1$, the geometric bound on successive differences proves convergence to a unique fixed point, and $J'(w)=\gamma\mathbb E_w\mathcal S(F(w)-w)/T$ identifies every global maximizer with that point.

The derivative identity $J''=-\gamma\mathbb E_w\mathcal S/T+p\operatorname{Var}_w(\int q_w)/T$ gives $|J''|\le H$ on $I$. Integration from $J'(w_*)=0$ gives the quadratic rate-loss bound, and the residual and inexact-oracle bounds follow from the contraction inequality and a geometric sum.

Finally $d\mathbb E_\lambda(\int q_w/T)/d\lambda=p\operatorname{Cov}_\lambda(\int q_w,\int h_w)/T$. Integrating the block estimate over $\lambda$ gives $B(e^{\delta\tau}-1)/(2a_0)$, while the range estimate gives $B\delta T/4$; hence $|J'(w)-\rho_cq_w|\le E$ and the occupation-start bound follows at $w_*$.

A speed condition is genuinely needed

For $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $b=(1,-1)$, $s=(1,1)$, $\gamma=1/2$, $\theta=1$, a valid $a_0=3/4$ gives $B=2$, $D=4$, $d=2$, $C=8/3$. Thus $m>14/3$ suffices for every horizon and prior, with $L\le8/[3(m-2)]$ and $E\le8/[3(m-2)]$.

$$|F(w_c)-w_*|\le\frac{128}{9(m-2)^2},\qquad J(w_*)-J(F(w_c))\le\frac14\left(1+\frac8{3(m-2)}\right)\left(\frac{128}{9(m-2)^2}\right)^2.$$

In contrast, on the same chain with $\gamma=2$, stationary start, $m=1/10$ and $T=100$, strict concavity makes $w_*=0$ unique, but

$$F'(0)=\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma m}\left[1-\frac{1-e^{-2mT}}{2mT}\right] =-\frac{19}{4}-\frac{e^{-20}}4<-1.$$

The fixed point is locally repelling, so uniqueness alone cannot justify this iteration at arbitrary speed. For log utility, on the other hand, $F(w)=w_c$ for every input and one evaluation is exact.

Literature and verification

The finite-horizon Doob kernel is classical: Chetrite and Touchette (2015), Appendix D. Block contraction of Feynman–Kac kernels is developed by Del Moral and Miclo (2002), §§3–4; the ratio derivative and portfolio stopping budgets here are direct consequences, not a novelty claim for contraction mapping.

The certificate checks rational block bounds, independent semigroup ratios and derivatives, global roots, iteration errors, CE-rate losses, adversarial oracle perturbations and the slow-speed negative control. Floating consistency tests are not outward-rounded enclosures; the analytic proof, not a horizon grid, establishes uniformity.

This concerns one constant fraction, not adaptive or filtered trading, and the approximation error is a policy or CE-rate error, not a terminal-wealth guarantee. The sufficient speed threshold and constants are not sharp.
