Translation-hull covariance Gram

Several normalized nonlinear outputs form a Gaussian-function Gram matrix.

The scalar translation-hull theorem extends jointly. Its limiting covariance rank is the number of centered Gaussian functions that remain linearly independent, not the rank of the Green–Kubo matrix.

The joint theorem

Use the fixed-positive-maturity finite-chain setup of the scalar theorem. For $j=1,\ldots,k$, let $r_j$ be tangent to a positive homogeneous gauge $\rho_j$ on the Gaussian support, and assume

$$0<c_{j,-}\le h_j'(x)\le c_{j,+}<\infty.$$

For $n_\ell=m_\ell T\to\infty$, put $L_\ell=\tfrac12\log n_\ell$ and suppose, locally uniformly,

$$h_j(L_\ell+s)-h_j(L_\ell)\longrightarrow q_j(s).$$

Define $A_j(0)=0$ and, for $z\ne0$,

$$A_j(z)=\exp\{-q_j(-\log\rho_j(z))\}.$$

Let $\Psi=(\Psi_1,\ldots,\Psi_k)^\top$, where near zero

$$\Psi_j(w)=\exp\{-h_j(\log(1/r_j(w)))\},$$

and set

$$D_\ell=\operatorname{diag}\{e^{h_1(L_\ell)},\ldots,e^{h_k(L_\ell)}\}.$$

Uniformly over arbitrary initial regime priors, with $G\sim N(0,\Gamma)$ on its support,

$$D_\ell\operatorname{Cov}\{\Psi(W_{m_\ell,T})\}D_\ell \longrightarrow K,\qquad K_{ij}=\operatorname{Cov}\{A_i(G),A_j(G)\}.$$

Proof and exact rank criterion

The scalar argument passes each first moment. Applied to $\Psi_i\Psi_j$, the same compact-window convergence and polynomial envelope pass every mixed second moment, which proves the matrix limit.

The limit is the $L^2$ Gram matrix of $A_j(G)-\mathbb EA_j(G)$. Therefore

$$\ker K=\left\{a\in\mathbb R^k:\sum_j a_jA_j(G) \text{ is constant almost surely}\right\},$$ $$\operatorname{rank}K=\dim\operatorname{span}\bigl\{A_j-\mathbb EA_j:1\le j\le k\bigr\}.$$

In particular, $K\succ0$ exactly when no nonzero linear combination of the hull functions is Gaussian-almost-surely constant. Then the finite-speed covariance is full rank for all sufficiently large speeds.

Arbitrary output rank from one feature

Let $G\sim N(0,1)$, use $\rho_j(z)=|z|$, and take $q_j(s)=c_js$ for distinct $c_j>0$. Then

$$A_j(G)=|G|^{c_j},\qquad K_{ij}=M_{c_i+c_j}-M_{c_i}M_{c_j},$$ $$M_p=\mathbb E|G|^p= \frac{2^{p/2}\Gamma((p+1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi}.$$

The functions $1,x^{c_1},\ldots,x^{c_k}$ are linearly independent on $(0,\infty)$: after $x=e^t$ they are exponentials with distinct exponents. Hence $K\succ0$ for every finite $k$, although $\Gamma$ has rank one.

For $c=(1,2)$,

$$K=\begin{pmatrix}1-2/\pi&\sqrt{2/\pi}\\ \sqrt{2/\pi}&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det K=2-6/\pi=0.090140682897.$$

Error and identification scope

On a joint compact log-radius window, the mixed $(i,j)$ integrand error is bounded by $\exp(\delta_{i,\ell,R}+\delta_{j,\ell,R})-1$, plus the tangent-gauge errors. Truncation gives additive $o(1)$ matrix error; no eigenvalue rate is claimed without quantitative translation, CLT and tail moduli.

This is a componentwise-normalized, fixed-feature, fixed-positive-maturity nonlinear-output Gram theorem. It does not raise the fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank, and it does not integrate different maturity loadings.

Halperin (1962) records the Hilbert-space Gram framework; Ney and Nummelin (1987) supply the Markov-additive limit theory. The translation-hull Gram and arbitrary-rank construction are derived here.
