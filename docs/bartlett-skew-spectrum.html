How weak the extra Bartlett skew modes are

A complete small-horizon spectrum, not just an exact-rank statement.

Research note, 20 October 2026. The limit is $T\downarrow0$ at fixed integer $r$; no error constant is claimed uniform in dimension.

One cycle with two paths

Take states $a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1},b_0,\ldots,b_{r-1},c$, where $r\ge1$. Give the triangle $a_0\to b_0\to c\to a_0$ unit rates, and attach unit-rate reversible paths $a_i\leftrightarrow a_{i+1}$ and $b_i\leftrightarrow b_{i+1}$. The irreducible generator $Q_r$ is balanced, so its stationary law is uniform on $n=2r+1$ states.

$$M_T=\int_0^T(1-t/T)e^{tQ_r}\,dt,\qquad A_T=\operatorname{skew}M_T.$$

Let $\omega_1(T)\ge\cdots\ge\omega_r(T)\ge0$ be the singular values of $A_T$, counting each skew pair once and omitting its stationary zero mode. These are also the skew singular values of the directed Bartlett form for a complete stationary-orthonormal feature basis: if $V^TV=I$, $V^T\mathbf1=0$ and $F=\sqrt nV$, that form is $B_T=V^TM_TV$.

$$\omega_j(T)=a_jT^{2j}\{1+O_r(T)\},\qquad a_j=\sqrt{\frac{2j+1}{2j-1}}\frac{(j-1)!(j+1)!}{2(2j)!(2j+1)!},\quad1\le j\le r.$$

In particular $a_1=\sqrt3/12$. Thus arbitrarily large exact skew rank comes with a hierarchy $T^2,T^4,\ldots,T^{2r}$, not comparably strong modes. This quantifies the one-cycle rank construction; the proof below is self-contained.

From shortest walks to exterior invariants

Let $X(T)=(A_T)_{\{a_i\},\{b_j\}}$ and $u_i=e_{a_i}-e_c$, $v_i=e_{b_i}-e_c$. The within-path diagonal blocks of $M_T$ are symmetric: their resolvent Schur complements are symmetric path matrices minus a scalar root projector. Expanding the resolvent at infinity transfers symmetry to every power of $Q_r$, hence to $M_T$.

Balance gives $A_T\mathbf1=0$, so its two-form is exactly $\alpha_T=\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}(T)u_i\wedge v_j$. The shortest $a_i\to b_j$ walk is unique with length $i+j+1$, while the reverse requires an extra step through $c$. Therefore

$$X_{ij}(T)=T^{i+j+2}W_{ij}(T),\qquad W_{ij}(T)=\frac1{2(i+j+3)!}+O(T).$$

Put $C_j=(1/[2(i+k+3)!])_{i,k=0}^{j-1}$, $\Delta_0=1$ and

$$\Delta_j=\det C_j=(-1)^{j(j-1)/2}2^{-j} \prod_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{k!}{(j+k+2)!}.$$

Multiplying row $i$ by $(i+j+2)!$ gives monic polynomials of decreasing degrees, so column reversal reduces the determinant to Vandermonde. This is a classical polynomial-alternant specialization; see Krattenthaler, Proposition 1.

In $\alpha_T^j/j!$, the coefficient for subsets $I,J$ of size $j$ is, up to sign, $\det X_{I,J}$. Its power is at least $j(j+1)$, with equality only for $I=J=\{0,\ldots,j-1\}$. The Gram matrix of the corresponding $2j$ vectors $u_i,v_i$ is $I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^T$, with determinant $2j+1$.

$$b_j(T):=\|\alpha_T^j/j!\| =\sqrt{e_j(\omega_1^2,\ldots,\omega_r^2)} =\sqrt{2j+1}\,|\Delta_j|T^{j(j+1)}\{1+O_r(T)\}.$$

Here $e_j$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial, as follows directly from orthogonal skew-block form. The product of the largest $j$ squared modes lies between $e_j/\binom{r}{j}$ and $e_j$, yielding $\omega_j=\Theta_r(T^{2j})$ by successive division. The resulting strict separation makes the largest product dominate $e_j$ with relative error $O_r(T^4)$.

Dividing successive leading product constants therefore gives $a_j=\sqrt{(2j+1)/(2j-1)}|\Delta_j/\Delta_{j-1}|$, which simplifies to the displayed factorial formula. This argument applies to every fixed $r$, not just the dimensions checked numerically.

A computable finite-horizon remainder

For $0 $$E_{r,j}=\sqrt{2j+1}\,j!\left\{54j55^{j-1} +\left[\binom{r}{j}^2-1\right]55^j\right\}.$$ $$\left|\frac{b_j(T)}{T^{j(j+1)}}-\sqrt{2j+1}|\Delta_j|\right|\le E_{r,j}T.$$

Leibniz expansion bounds the leading minor's normalized error by $j!j54\,55^{j-1}T$. Each other minor has an extra factor at most $T$, determinant bounded by $j!55^j$, and wedge-vector norm $\sqrt{2j+1}$. Summing these bounds proves the inequality; its conservative constants are explicit and dimension-dependent.

Normalization and deterministic rank loss

On the centered orthonormal coordinates put $S_T=\operatorname{sym}B_T$ and let $\nu_j$ be the positive skew-pair singular values of $S_T^{-1/2}(\operatorname{skew}B_T)S_T^{-1/2}$. Since $S_T=(T/2)\{I+O_r(T)\}$, multiplicative singular-value bounds for congruence, rather than additive perturbation bounds that could swamp weak modes, give

$$\nu_j(T)=2a_jT^{2j-1}\{1+O_r(T)\}.$$

For any real skew matrix $H$ with ordered pair strengths $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_r$,

$$\inf_{Z^T=-Z,\ \operatorname{rank}Z\le2s}\|H-Z\|_2=\eta_{s+1},\qquad0\le sKeeping the largest $s$ canonical skew blocks attains the bound. Conversely, the kernel of any rank-$2s$ approximation intersects the largest $2s+1$ singular subspace, giving the lower bound. This is the usual low-rank approximation principle associated with Eckart and Young, with the skew-constrained operator-norm case proved here directly.

Consequently the directed skew form is within $a_{s+1}T^{2s+2}\{1+O_r(T)\}$ of rank $2s$, or within $2a_{s+1}T^{2s+1}\{1+O_r(T)\}$ after symmetric normalization. These are matrix-level distances, not distances to another realizable Markov model or statistical sample-complexity bounds.

Checks and scope

The certificate checks the factorial and Gram determinants exactly for $r=1,\ldots,6$, then checks the mode hierarchy at 120 decimal digits and repeats the weakest case at 170 digits. It tests the explicit exterior-invariant bound, normalized modes and an independent block exponential. Finite-grid convergence is a control on the all-dimensions proof, not an interval enclosure for individual numerical modes.

For $r=6$, $a_6\approx1.10214780467\times10^{-13}$. At $T=1/64$ the largest mode is about $3.46489829681\times10^{-5}$ and the weakest about $2.29095275904\times10^{-35}$. The weakest normalized mode is about $2.94924254598\times10^{-33}$, despite exact full centered skew rank.

Inverse congruence preserves the generator's rank-two skew part in the infinite-time form, while both symmetric Gram ranks stay full. This note concerns fixed complete stationary features and directed correlations, not ranks created by different maturity loadings or the symmetric occupation covariance itself. No dimension-uniform remainder, all-horizon spectral formula, or noise-robust identification guarantee is claimed.
