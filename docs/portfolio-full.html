When a constant-fraction optimizer has a global certificate

The first-order weight shift on the portfolio page is an asymptotic rule, not a global optimization certificate. The exact objective supplies one for relative risk aversion $\gamma\geq1$, but not in general for $0<\gamma<1$.

An exact tilted-variance identity

Let a finite-state regime chain $Y$ with any initial prior $\nu$ be independent of the Brownian stock noise. Fix a positive horizon $T$, constant interest rate $r$, regime excess returns $b_i=\mu_i-r$, and variances $s_i=\sigma_i^2\geq s_{\min}>0$.

The investor selects a single deterministic fraction $w\in\mathbb R$ and holds it throughout the horizon. For CRRA risk aversion $\gamma>0$ define

$$h_i(w)=r+b_iw-\tfrac12\gamma s_iw^2,\qquad H(w)=\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)\,dt,\qquad C(w)=\frac{\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{(1-\gamma)H(w)}}{(1-\gamma)T}.$$

At $\gamma=1$ use the log-utility limit $C(w)=\mathbb E_\nu H(w)/T$. Write $V=\int_0^T s_{Y_t}\,dt$ and $R(w)=\int_0^T(b_{Y_t}-\gamma w s_{Y_t})\,dt$, and let $\mathbb E_w$ denote expectation under the path tilt proportional to $e^{(1-\gamma)H(w)}$.

$$C'(w)=\frac{\mathbb E_w R(w)}T,\qquad C''(w)=-\frac\gamma T\mathbb E_w V +\frac{1-\gamma}T\operatorname{Var}_w R(w).$$

Differentiate the normalizing log moment twice, using $H'=R$ and $H''=-\gamma V$. Finite regime coefficients and fixed $T$ bound every integrand locally in $w$, justifying differentiation. The same formula at $\gamma=1$ has no variance term and uses the untilted path law.

Strong concavity and residual bounds

For $\gamma\geq1$, the variance term is nonpositive and $V\geq Ts_{\min}$, so

$$C''(w)\leq-\kappa,\qquad \kappa=\gamma s_{\min}>0.$$

This is exact at every switching speed, with no stationary start, reversibility or fast-switching assumption. The certainty equivalent lies between the smallest and largest possible path-average $h$; hence $C(w)\leq r+\max_i|b_i|\,|w|-\gamma s_{\min}w^2/2\to-\infty$.

There is therefore exactly one unconstrained maximizer $w_*$, characterized by $C'(w_*)=0$. Every proposed fraction, including the first-corrected one, obeys

$$|w-w_*|\leq\frac{|C'(w)|}{\kappa},\qquad 0\leq C(w_*)-C(w)\leq\frac{C'(w)^2}{2\kappa}.$$

The distance bound follows by integrating $C''\leq-\kappa$ between $w$ and $w_*$. For the value bound maximize the quadratic majorant $C(v)\leq C(w)+C'(w)(v-w)-\kappa(v-w)^2/2$ over $v$. This is the usual strong-convexity residual argument, with signs reversed; see Boyd and Vandenberghe (2004), §9.1.2, equation (9.9).

If a derivative evaluation has a certified absolute error $\eta$, replace $|C'(w)|$ by $|\widehat C'(w)|+\eta$ in both bounds. A floating-point identity check alone is not a validated bound on that evaluation error.

For the main page's bull/bear parameters at speed one, the first-corrected fraction is $0.6567301154$ and the exact-objective optimum is numerically $0.6607275296$. The residual bounds give fraction error at most $0.0102079501$ and certainty-equivalent rate loss at most $0.0000022508$; these are evaluations of the analytic bounds, not interval-arithmetic certificates.

Below log utility, uniqueness can fail

Take the symmetric unit-rate two-state chain with stationary prior, $r=0$, drifts $(2,-2)$, common volatility one, $T=1$ and $\gamma=1/2$. The averaged Merton fraction is zero, while the exact objective is the even function

$$C(w)=-\frac{w^2}{4} +2\log\left[e^{-1}\left\{\cosh\sqrt{1+w^2} +\frac{\sinh\sqrt{1+w^2}}{\sqrt{1+w^2}}\right\}\right].$$

Since the stationary telegraph integral has variance $(1+e^{-2})/2$,

$$C'(0)=0,\qquad C''(0)=\tfrac12+e^{-2}=0.6353352832>0.$$

Zero is a strict local minimum, not a maximum. The coercive upper bound above still applies, and evenness then forces at least two distinct nonzero global maximizers, one of each sign.

The certificate gives $C(0)=0$ and $C(\pm1)=0.2819124942$. It also finds a positive local maximizer $3.6411106114$, with value $1.3216481724$; no numerical assertion of uniqueness on either half-line is needed for the counterexample.

This does not contradict concavity of utility in terminal wealth: terminal wealth is nonlinear in a constant rebalanced fraction, and mixing two such strategies does not generally remain in that family. Nor is the example a claim about optimal adaptive or filtered trading, or a realistic calibration.

Matrix verification and scope

For $A(w)=mQ+(1-\gamma)\operatorname{diag}h(w)$, the exact transform is $Z(w)=\nu e^{TA(w)}\mathbf1$. If $u'=Au$, then its first two weight derivatives satisfy

$$v'=Av+A'u,\qquad z'=Az+2A'v+A''u,\qquad u(0)=\mathbf1,\quad v(0)=z(0)=0.$$

A separate triangular Feynman–Kac moment system computes the tilted moments of $R$ and $V$. The certificate compares these routes and finite differences across 1,188 prior/chain/speed/horizon/fraction cases, including point priors, directed cycles and a defective generator; its last line is literal PASS.

It also checks optimizer residual bounds for the original example at three speeds and the exact nonconcavity witness above. The claims concern unrestricted constant fractions with strictly positive state variances; they do not bound the full adaptive-control value, or assert that a truncated Green–Kubo objective is globally concave.
