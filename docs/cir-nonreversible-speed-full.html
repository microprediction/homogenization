Stationary variance decreases without reversibility

With common mean reversion and constant vol-of-vol, faster switching strictly reduces stationary CIR variance whenever the regime levels differ, for any finite irreducible chain. Complete monotonicity is stronger and can fail already on a directed three-cycle.

Setup and exact formulas

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, where $m>0$ and $Q$ is a fixed irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ states with invariant row $\pi$. Consider

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta_{Y_t}-v_t)\,dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t,$$

with common $\kappa,\xi>0$, positive $\theta_i$, and $B$ independent of $Y$. Assume $2\kappa\min_i\theta_i\ge\xi^2$ and use the joint invariant law at each speed, with finite second moment. This compares different stationary laws, not paths sharing a fixed initial variance.

Write $f=\theta-\bar\theta\mathbf1$, $\bar\theta=\pi\theta$, $\langle g,h\rangle_\pi=\sum_i\pi_i g_i h_i$, and $H=-Q^\#$, the inverse of $-Q$ on centered functions.

$$\mathcal V(m):=\operatorname{Var}(v) =\frac{\xi^2\bar\theta}{2\kappa} +\kappa\langle f,(\kappa I-mQ)^{-1}f\rangle_\pi,$$ $$\Gamma(m):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\int_0^T v_t\,dt\right) =\frac{\xi^2\bar\theta}{\kappa^2}+\frac2m\langle f,Hf\rangle_\pi.$$

The first formula follows directly from the invariant first- and second-moment equations in the general full note. The second also follows from the affine Poisson solution $\phi(v,i)=v/\kappa+(Hf)_i/m$, since $-\mathcal L\phi=v-\bar\theta$.

Affine conditional means decay exponentially, allowing polynomial factors at resonant eigenvalues. The stationary covariance is therefore integrable, which justifies the Poisson covariance formula for $\Gamma$.

Specifically, the component first moment is $M=(\kappa I-mQ^\top)^{-1}\kappa D_\pi\theta$, and $\mathcal V=\theta^\top M-\bar\theta^2+\xi^2\bar\theta/(2\kappa)$. Substituting it into $\Gamma=2\mathcal V/\kappa+2M^\top Hf/m$ proves the displayed rate formula.

A Loewner order at every switching speed

In an $L^2(\pi)$-orthonormal basis of the centered subspace, write the matrix of $-Q$ as $L=S+A$, where $S=S^\top\succ0$ and $A^\top=-A$. Irreducibility gives strict positivity of $S$ by the Dirichlet form; no diagonalization of $Q$ is needed.

Put $X_m=\kappa I+mS$ and $R_m=\operatorname{sym}(\kappa I+mL)^{-1}$. The standard symmetric-resolvent factorization gives

$$R_m=P_m^{-1},\qquad P_m=X_m+m^2A^\top X_m^{-1}A.$$

This is the finite-matrix version of the factorization in Duncan, Lelièvre and Pavliotis (2016), Lemma 3, equation (28). Here both the symmetric and antisymmetric parts scale with $m$, rather than varying only an antisymmetric perturbation.

Differentiating, using that $S$ commutes with $X_m$, gives

$$P_m'=S+A^\top\!\left[mX_m^{-1}(2\kappa I+mS)X_m^{-1}\right]A\succ0, \qquad R_m'=-R_mP_m'R_m\prec0.$$

Consequently $R_{m_1}\succ R_{m_2}$ whenever $0\le m_1<m_2$. If $\theta$ is nonconstant then $\mathcal V'(m)<0$ for every $m>0$, with limits

$$\mathcal V(0+)=\frac{\xi^2\bar\theta}{2\kappa}+\operatorname{Var}_\pi\theta, \qquad \mathcal V(\infty)=\frac{\xi^2\bar\theta}{2\kappa}.$$

For constant $\theta$ the variance and rate are speed-independent. For nonconstant $\theta$, $\langle f,Hf\rangle_\pi>0$ by the same Dirichlet form, so $\Gamma$ strictly decreases and its excess above the averaged limit is completely monotone.

Complete monotonicity of stationary variance can fail

A completely monotone function obeys $(-1)^k g^{(k)}(m)\ge0$ for every $k\ge0$ and $m>0$. Reversible $Q$ gives a nonnegative sum of reciprocal linear functions in the variance formula, hence this stronger property; the ordinary speed order above does not require it.

Take the unit-rate directed cycle and strictly positive CIR parameters

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\0&-1&1\\1&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \pi=(1,1,1)/3,\qquad\kappa=1,\qquad\xi^2=1/1000,\qquad\theta=(3,4,5)/100.$$

The Feller margins are $(.059,.079,.099)$, all strictly positive. On the centered subspace, $L+L^\top=3I$ and $LL^\top=3I$, so the resolvent gives exactly

$$\mathcal V(m)=\frac1{50000}+\frac1{15000} \frac{1+3m/2}{1+3m+3m^2}.$$

Its fourth derivative has right limit $\mathcal V^{(4)}(0)=-9/1250$. At the strictly positive speed $m=1/100$, exact rational arithmetic gives $\mathcal V^{(4)}(1/100)\approx-0.006182577321<0$, contradicting complete monotonicity. The first derivative nevertheless stays strictly negative by the theorem.

Scope and verification

The certificate checks 260 precision, derivative, invariant-moment and Poisson identities, plus 208 finite Loewner comparisons, including nonuniform stationary laws, a sparse cycle, and a defective generator. The counterexample's rational derivative signs are exact; the matrix regressions use scale-aware floating-point tolerances.

These are stationary one-time variance and long-run integrated-variance-rate results. They do not classify finite-maturity integrated variance, log-return cumulants, leverage or option prices. Allowing vol-of-vol or mean reversion to switch changes the formulas and can destroy this speed order.

No novelty claim is made for the resolvent identity or invariant-moment method. The point here is the all-speed conclusion when both parts of the nonreversible generator scale, and the distinction between monotonicity and alternating signs of every derivative.
