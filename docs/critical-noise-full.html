Critical scores at the vanishing-noise scale

A quadratic path score has inverse-horizon latent-start bias, and can reverse its sign as noise changes.

Setup and result

Let $Y$ jump from $+$ to $-$ at fixed rate $a>0$ and back at fixed rate $b>0$. Put $r=a+b$, $\pi=(b/r,a/r)$, $\bar y=(b-a)/r$, $v=8ab/r^3$, and $A_c=c^{-1}\int_0^cY_sds$. Consider the critical quadratic score

$$S_c=\mu(A_c-\bar y)^2+\sigma_c Z,\qquad \mu>0,\quad Z\sim N(0,1)\text{ independent},\quad \sigma_c={\lambda\mu v\over c},\quad\lambda\ge0.$$

Write $F_+,F_-$ for its fixed-start CDFs, and let $q_c$ be its stationary pooled $p$-quantile for fixed $0<p<1$. If $t_p(\lambda)$ is the $p$-quantile of $N^2+\lambda Z$, for independent standard normals $N,Z$, define

$$h(x)=(\sqrt x-x^{3/2})\phi(\sqrt x)\mathbf1_{\{x>0\}},\qquad H_\lambda(t)=\mathbb E[h(t-\lambda Z)],\qquad C={a-b\over2br}.$$
$$F_+(q_c)-p={C\over c}H_\lambda(t_p(\lambda))+o(c^{-1}),\qquad F_-(q_c)-p=-{b\over a}\{F_+(q_c)-p\}.$$

The first remainder is uniform for $\lambda$ in every fixed compact interval; the second equality is exact whenever the pooled quantile attains $p$. At zero noise this holds for all sufficiently large $c$ because the endpoint atoms move outside the fixed-quantile region. The fixed-positive-noise critical-score theorem has error of order $c^{-2}$; this different noise scaling has order $c^{-1}$ when the displayed coefficient is nonzero.

If $a=b$, reflection makes $(A_c-\bar y)^2$ identically distributed under both starts, so the conditional gaps vanish exactly for every $c$ and noise level. Stationary pooling remains marginal population coverage, not a finite-calibration or distribution-free conditional guarantee.

An exact quadratic CDF profile

For $-1<x<1$ put

$$g_c(x)=e^{-c\{r+(a-b)x\}/2}I_0(c\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{1-x^2}),$$

and set $g_c(x)=0$ at and outside both endpoints. Differentiating the classical telegraph densities gives $f_+-f_-=-g_c'$ on the open interval. For $u\ge0$, define the right-continuous profile

$$J_c(u)={ac\over r}\left[g_c\!\left(\bar y-\sqrt{v/c}\,u\right) -g_c\!\left(\bar y+\sqrt{v/c}\,u\right)\right].$$

The zero endpoint convention incorporates the jumps at $u_-=(1+\bar y)\sqrt{c/v}$ and $u_+=(1-\bar y)\sqrt{c/v}$. Indeed, integrating over the quadratic score's acceptance interval and adding its two endpoint atoms gives, for every real $t$,

$$F_+\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right)-F_\pi\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right) ={1\over c}\mathbb E\!\left[J_c\!\left(\sqrt{t-\lambda Z}\right) \mathbf1_{\{t-\lambda Z\ge0\}}\right].$$

The integrand is defined to be zero for negative arguments before taking a square root. This identity retains atoms even at zero noise; it does not replace their mass by a continuous approximation.

A finite-horizon error budget

Put $\psi(u)=(u-u^3)\phi(u)$ for $u\ge0$, so $h(x)=\psi(\sqrt x)$ on $x\ge0$ and $\psi'(u)=(1-4u^2+u^4)\phi(u)$. A computable exact-arithmetic budget is

$$B_c={1\over2}\left\{\int_0^\infty|J_c'(u)-C\psi'(u)|du +{ac\over r}(e^{-ac}+e^{-bc})\right\}.$$

The derivative excludes the two jump points; their absolute jump sizes are bounded by the displayed exponential terms. Since $J_c-C\psi$ vanishes at zero and infinity, half its total variation bounds its supremum. Convolution with any probability law cannot increase that supremum, so, for every $t$ and every $\lambda\ge0$,

$$\left|F_+\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right)-F_\pi\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right) -{C\over c}H_\lambda(t)\right|\le {B_c\over c}.$$

This bound is centered at the actual threshold $t$, not the limiting quantile $t_p(\lambda)$. At an exact pooled quantile that attains $p$, replace $F_\pi$ by $p$ and take $t=cq_c/(\mu v)$. Numerical quadratures of $B_c$ in the certificate are regression checks, not outward-rounded enclosures.

Proof of the asymptotic coefficient

Normalize the telegraph kernel by $k_c(u)=r\sqrt{vc}\,g_c(\bar y+\sqrt{v/c}\,u)/2$. Taylor expansion of its saddle exponent and the fixed-order Bessel expansion give, locally uniformly with one derivative,

$$k_c(u)=\phi(u)\left[1+{d\over\sqrt c}(u-u^3)\right]+O(c^{-1}),\qquad d={b-a\over2\sqrt{2abr}}.$$

Its exponent is $S(x)=-\{r+(a-b)x\}/2+\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{1-x^2}$, with $S(\bar y)=S'(\bar y)=0$, $S''(\bar y)=-1/v$, and $S''(x)\le-\sqrt{ab}$. Subtracting $k_c(u)$ from $k_c(-u)$ therefore gives $J_c\to C\psi$ and $J_c'\to C\psi'$ locally uniformly.

These limits also control the variation integral defining $B_c$. On $|u|\le\sqrt{K\log c}$, the differentiated Bessel and Taylor remainders after the subtraction are bounded by $c^{-1/2}$ times a polynomial in $\log c$. On the remaining interior region, concavity bounds the kernels and derivatives by a polynomial in $c,u$ times $e^{-\delta u^2}$; choosing fixed $K$ large makes that integral vanish.

Near the endpoints, $I_0(z)\le2e^z/\sqrt{1+z}$ and $I_1(z)\le(z/2)I_0(z)$ remove the apparent denominator singularities and give exponentially small integrals. Thus $B_c\to0$, including the endpoint jumps. No polynomial decay rate for this budget is asserted.

The stationary density of $\sqrt{c/v}(A_c-\bar y)$ converges locally to $\phi$, and its continuous mass tends to one. Scheffé's lemma, including the vanishing atoms, gives total-variation convergence; squaring and adding $\lambda Z$ cannot increase that distance. The reference quantile is continuous in $\lambda$, with positive density there, uniformly on compact $\lambda$ intervals.

Consequently $cq_c/(\mu v)\to t_p(\lambda)$ uniformly on such intervals. Uniform continuity of $h$ and the finite-horizon bound prove the result.

A sign change, not just a slower rate

At zero noise, let $z=\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2)$. The coefficient becomes $C(z-z^3)\phi(z)$, so its sign changes at $p_*=2\Phi(1)-1=0.682689492\ldots$ when $a\ne b$. At $p_*$ only the leading term vanishes; finite-$c$ exact cancellation is not asserted.

Moreover $\int_0^\infty h(x)dx=-2$ and $t_p(\lambda)/\lambda\to z_p$, so $\lambda H_\lambda(t_p(\lambda))\to-2\phi(z_p)$ as $\lambda\to\infty$. For $a>b$ the median coefficient is therefore positive at zero noise and negative at sufficiently large finite $\lambda$, proving at least one sign change by continuity. This is a limit of the coefficient function, not a joint-limit theorem allowing unbounded $\lambda=\lambda_c$.

Literature and scope

The density and atoms are classical: López and Ratanov (2014), equation (2.14), inspected in the publisher PDF. The differentiated asymptotic inputs are DLMF 10.40.1 and 10.40.3; the critical-score profile and error transfer are derived above. The result fixes positive rates and an interior target and treats a quadratic score with noise of order $1/c$, not arbitrary nonlinear scores or all joint noise scalings.
