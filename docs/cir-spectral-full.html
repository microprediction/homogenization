Two reversible modes can produce two turning points

Exact common-reversion CIR formulas separate a sufficient speed-order condition from a three-state counterexample.

Setup and spectral formulas

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, $m>0$, with $Q$ finite, irreducible and reversible under $\pi$. Consider the joint stationary process

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta_{Y_t}-v_t)\,dt+\sqrt{a_{Y_t}v_t}\,dB_t,\qquad V_T=\int_0^T v_t\,dt.$$

Here $\kappa>0$ is common, $a_i=\xi_i^2>0$, $\theta_i>0$, $2\kappa\theta_i\ge a_i$, and $B$ is independent of $Y$. Assume the joint invariant law has finite second moment and use that law at each speed.

For every distinct positive eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $-Q$, let $E_\lambda$ be its $L^2(\pi)$-orthogonal projector. Center $\theta,a$ under $\pi$ and define the basis-independent mode weights

$$S_\lambda=\|E_\lambda\widetilde\theta\|_\pi^2,\quad C_\lambda=\langle E_\lambda\widetilde\theta,E_\lambda\widetilde a\rangle_\pi,\quad W_\lambda=\kappa S_\lambda+C_\lambda/2.$$

Write $\bar\theta=\pi\theta$, $\bar a=\pi a$, $v_\infty=\bar a\bar\theta/(2\kappa)$, and $\Gamma_\infty=\bar a\bar\theta/\kappa^2$.

$$\operatorname{Var}(v)=v_\infty+\sum_\lambda\frac{W_\lambda}{\kappa+m\lambda},$$ $$\Gamma(m):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Var}(V_T)}T =\Gamma_\infty+\frac2m\sum_\lambda\frac{S_\lambda}{\lambda} +\frac1\kappa\sum_\lambda\frac{C_\lambda}{\kappa+m\lambda}.$$

The $S_\lambda$ are nonnegative, but the mixed $C_\lambda$ and $W_\lambda$ need not be. Reversibility alone therefore does not impose monotonicity or a single turning point.

A sufficient monotonicity cone

If $W_\lambda\ge0$ for every distinct mode, both $\operatorname{Var}(v)-v_\infty$ and $\Gamma-\Gamma_\infty$ are completely monotone in $m$: every derivative of order $p\ge0$ has sign $(-1)^p$ or is zero. If $\theta$ is nonconstant, $\Gamma$ strictly decreases; stationary variance strictly decreases unless all $W_\lambda$ vanish.

This is a sufficient condition, not a claimed necessary condition for ordinary monotonicity. Differentiation proves it for stationary variance. For the rate, the modewise alternating derivative divided by $p!$ is

$$\frac{2S_\lambda}{\lambda m^{p+1}} +\frac{C_\lambda\lambda^p}{\kappa(\kappa+m\lambda)^{p+1}}\ge0.$$

If $C_\lambda\ge0$ both terms are nonnegative. Otherwise $W_\lambda\ge0$ implies $-C_\lambda\le2\kappa S_\lambda$, and $(\kappa+m\lambda)^{p+1}>(m\lambda)^{p+1}$ proves strict positivity whenever $S_\lambda>0$.

Affine loading: a single minimum in any number of states

Suppose $a_i=b_0+b\theta_i$, with the positivity and Feller assumptions still satisfied, and $\theta$ nonconstant. Then $C_\lambda=bS_\lambda$ and $W_\lambda=(\kappa+b/2)S_\lambda$.

For $b\ge-2\kappa$ the preceding cone applies; stationary variance is constant precisely at $b=-2\kappa$. For $b<-2\kappa$, stationary variance strictly increases and $\Gamma$ has exactly one minimum.

$$m^2\Gamma'(m)=-2K-\frac b\kappa \sum_\lambda S_\lambda\lambda \left(\frac m{\kappa+m\lambda}\right)^2,\qquad K=\sum_\lambda S_\lambda/\lambda>0.$$

When $b<-2\kappa$, this expression strictly increases from $-2K$ to $(-2-b/\kappa)K>0$. Thus its unique zero proves the one-minimum statement. If $\theta$ is constant, both boxed quantities are speed-independent even when $a$ switches.

A strict-Feller three-state counterexample

$$Q=\frac16\begin{pmatrix}-31&29&2\\29&-31&2\\2&2&-4\end{pmatrix},\quad \pi=(1,1,1)/3,\quad\kappa=1,$$ $$\theta=(128,123,124)/2500,\qquad a=(3,9,3)/100.$$

The Feller margins are $(0.0724,0.0084,0.0692)$, all strictly positive. The two nonzero rates are $\lambda=1,10$, with

$$ (S_1,S_{10})=(1/12500000,1/1500000),\qquad (C_1,C_{10})=(1/250000,-1/50000).$$

Substitution gives the exact rational identity

$$\Gamma'(m)=\frac{P(m)}{37500000\,m^2(1+m)^2(1+10m)^2},$$ $$P(m)=-8600m^4+9580m^3+5799m^2-242m-11.$$

The descending coefficients have two sign changes, so Descartes' rule allows at most two positive roots, counted with multiplicity. Exact rational signs isolate one in $(1/20,1/10)$ and another in $(1,2)$; hence both roots are simple and there are no others.

The rate first decreases, then increases, then decreases. Its local minimum is at $m\approx0.0646002933$, rate $0.0024961473751$; its local maximum is at $m\approx1.5396820254$, rate $0.0025005457672$. The averaged limit is $0.0025$.

Stationary variance itself first increases and then decreases, with its unique maximum at $m=(R-1)/(10-R)\approx1.7261502725$, $R=\sqrt{1750/39}$. Taking the positive square root in its two-term derivative equation gives $(1+10m)/(1+m)=R$; the left side strictly increases from $1$ to $10$.

Proof of the spectral formulas

For a centered eigenfunction $g$ with $Qg=-\lambda g$, stationarity applied to $vg$ gives $\operatorname{Cov}(v,g_Y)=\kappa\langle\widetilde\theta,g\rangle_\pi/(\kappa+m\lambda)$. Applying the generator to $v$ and $v^2$ gives $\mathbb E v=\bar\theta$ and

$$\operatorname{Var}(v)=\operatorname{Cov}(v,\theta_Y)+\mathbb E[a_Yv]/(2\kappa).$$

Summing the eigenfunction identities proves the first boxed formula. On centered state functions let $H=(-Q)^{-1}$; the affine function $\phi(v,i)=v/\kappa+(H\widetilde\theta)_i/m$ solves $-\mathcal L\phi=v-\bar\theta$.

Affine conditional means decay exponentially, with polynomial factors at resonant rates, so the stationary covariance is integrable. The Poisson covariance identity gives

$$\Gamma=2\operatorname{Var}(v)/\kappa +\frac{2\kappa}{m}\sum_\lambda \frac{S_\lambda}{\lambda(\kappa+m\lambda)},$$

which simplifies to the second boxed formula.

Scope and verification

These are exact stationary variance and long-run variance-rate statements, not finite-maturity speed classifications. Reversibility and common $\kappa$ are essential hypotheses of this spectral treatment; no conclusion about log-return cumulants, leverage, option prices or heterogeneous maturity-loading rank is inferred.

The moment/Poisson method is already used in the general full note, and finite polynomial semigroups are classical; see Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012). No novelty claim is made for stationary moment calculations or spectral decomposition.

The certificate compares the mode sums with generic invariant-moment and Poisson solves, including a repeated eigenvalue, and checks alternating derivatives and affine-loading minima. Exact rational identities and signs certify the counterexample's root count; displayed root locations and rates are rounded floating-point calculations.
