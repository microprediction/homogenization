Poisson smoothing removes maturity growth from the error bound

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, where $Q$ is finite and irreducible, stationary row $\pi$ and $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$. Assume $0<a\leq\ell_i\leq b$, with nonconstant $\ell$, and $\|e^{tQ}-\Pi\|_{\infty\to\infty}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}$ for $t\geq0$.

Put $f=\ell-(\pi\ell)\mathbf1$, $F=\|f\|_\infty$, $\beta=m\gamma$, $\Lambda_T=\int_0^T\ell(Y_t)\,dt$, $\mu=\mathbb E_\nu\Lambda_T$, $v=\operatorname{Var}_\nu\Lambda_T$ and $M_j=\mathbb E_\nu(\Lambda_T-\mu)^j$. Conditional on $\Lambda_T$, the Cox count $N_T$ is Poisson with that mean. Fit $G$ with gamma shape $\mu^2/v$ and scale $v/\mu$, and mix a Poisson variable over $G$ to obtain $N_*$.

Explicit bound, uniform in both maturity and prior

Choose any split horizon $\tau>0$. Define $\alpha=2CF^2$, $t_3=18C^2F^3$, $u_3=2C^3F^3$, $t_4=24C^2F^4$, $u_4=60C^3F^4$ and $c=(\log2-\tfrac12)a^2/\alpha$.

$$S_2=\frac{2\tau}{3}\left(t_3+\frac{2\alpha^2}{a}\right) +\tau^2\left(\frac{t_4}{3}+\alpha^2\right),\qquad S_3=\frac{2u_3}{3}+\tau\left(\frac{u_4}{3}+\frac{2\alpha^3}{a^2}\right),$$ $$L_2=\frac{\sqrt6}{12a^{3/2}\sqrt\tau}\left(t_3+\frac{2\alpha^2}{a}\right) +\frac{\sqrt{24}}{48a^2}(t_4+12\alpha^2),\qquad L_3=\frac{\sqrt6u_3}{12(a\tau)^{3/2}} +\frac{\sqrt{24}}{48a^2\tau}\left(u_4+\frac{24\alpha^3}{a^2}\right).$$
$$A_2=\max\{S_2,L_2\},\qquad A_3=\max\{S_3,L_3\},\qquad A_4=\frac{8b^4}{c^4},$$ $$\sup_{T>0,\nu}d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L_\nu(N_T),\mathcal L(N_{*,T,\nu})) \leq\min\{1,A_2/\beta^2+A_3/\beta^3+A_4/\beta^4\}=O(m^{-2}).$$

This holds at every positive speed; the constants are deliberately conservative. All priors and horizons may depend on $m$. Constant intensity is exactly Poisson and is handled separately, without dividing by $F$ or $v$.

Finite-chain moment estimates

Write $J=\Lambda_T-(\pi\ell)T$ and $R_j=\mathbb E_\nu J^j$. Markov conditioning gives $|\mathbb E_\nu J|\leq CF/\beta$ and $0\leq R_2\leq\alpha T/\beta$.

Splitting the propagation across a gap into $\Pi+(P-\Pi)$ gives the following bounds for ordered three- and four-point correlations, with $f_t=f(Y_t)$:

$$|\mathbb E_\nu(f_t f_{t+s}f_{t+s+u})| \leq C^2F^3\{e^{-\beta(t+u)}+e^{-\beta(s+u)}\},$$ $$|\mathbb E_\nu(f_t f_{t+s}f_{t+s+u}f_{t+s+u+w})| \leq C^2F^4\{e^{-\beta(s+w)}+e^{-\beta(u+w)}\}.$$

For three points, condition on the last gap, then split the preceding propagator; the constant part uses $\pi f=0$ and decay from the initial time. For four points, split the middle propagator; its constant part leaves a two-point correlation, and sup-norm contraction bounds the remainder.

Integrating over ordered times gives $|R_3|\leq12C^2F^3T/\beta^2$ and $0\leq R_4\leq24C^2F^4T^2/\beta^2$. Centering these raw moments yields

$$v\leq V=\frac{\alpha T}{\beta},\qquad |M_3|\leq B_3=\frac{t_3T}{\beta^2}+\frac{u_3}{\beta^3},\qquad M_4\leq B_4=\frac{t_4T^2}{\beta^2}+\frac{u_4T}{\beta^3}.$$

Poisson smoothing and the gamma lower tail

For the Poisson probability vector $p(x)$, the classical Charlier orthogonality gives $\|p^{(j)}(x)\|_1\leq\sqrt{j!}\,x^{-j/2}$ for $x>0$. See Zacharovas and Hwang (2010), Lemma 2.1; the identity needed here has a direct proof.

Indeed, $p_k(x+h)/p_k(x)=e^{-h}(1+h/x)^k$. For $K\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(x)$, the expected product at parameters $h,s$ is $e^{hs/x}$. Comparing coefficients gives $\sum_k p_k^{(j)}(x)^2/p_k(x)=j!x^{-j}$, and Cauchy–Schwarz proves the norm bound.

Taylor-expand at $\mu$ through degree three, with matched means and variances. Since $\Lambda_T,\mu\geq aT$, the true intensity remainder uses the smoothed fourth derivative. For $G\geq\mu/2$ its gamma counterpart uses the same bound with an extra factor four.

On $G<\mu/2$, instead use $\|p^{(4)}(x)\|_1\leq16$ and $|G-\mu|\leq\mu$. Gamma Laplace transformation gives $\Pr(G<\mu/2)\leq e^{-(\log2-1/2)\mu^2/v}$. Here $\mu^2/v\geq a^2\beta T/\alpha$ and $\mu\leq bT$.

$$d_{\rm TV}\leq \frac{\sqrt6}{12(aT)^{3/2}}\left(B_3+\frac{2V^2}{aT}\right) +\frac{\sqrt{24}}{48(aT)^2} \left\{B_4+4\left(3V^2+\frac{6V^3}{(aT)^2}\right)\right\} +\frac{b^4T^4}{3}e^{-c\beta T}.$$

For $T\geq\tau$, the first two terms are at most $L_2/\beta^2+L_3/\beta^3$. Since $e^x\geq x^4/24$, the last is at most $A_4/\beta^4$ for every $T>0$. This treats the gamma lower tail rather than assuming it shares the true intensity's positive floor.

For $0<T\leq\tau$, use the unsmoothed derivative bound $\|p^{(j)}(x)\|_1\leq2^j$, which gives $\tfrac23|M_3-2v^2/\mu|+\tfrac13(M_4+3v^2+6v^3/\mu^2)$. Substitution of $\mu\geq aT$ and the moment estimates bounds this by $S_2/\beta^2+S_3/\beta^3$, completing the proof.

Zero-intensity regimes: a uniform extension

The statewise positive floor can be removed, with different constants. Now assume only $\ell\geq0$, nonconstant and not identically zero, so $\bar\ell=\pi\ell>0$. The finite-chain moment estimates above still hold.

Choose $\theta,h>0$ and define the killed semigroup $K_t=e^{t(Q-\theta\operatorname{diag}\ell)}$. Put $r=\|K_h\|_{\infty\to\infty}\in(0,1)$ and $k=-\log r/h$.

Every initial state has positive probability of reaching a positive-intensity state within $h$, so each row sum is strictly below one. Conditioning on an initial time increment gives $u'=m(Q-\theta\operatorname{diag}\ell)u$, $u(0)=\mathbf1$. Thus $\mathbb E_\nu e^{-\theta m\Lambda_T}=\nu K_{mT}\mathbf1$.

Submultiplicativity and $\|K_s\|_\infty\leq1$ give $\nu K_{mT}\mathbf1\leq r^{\lfloor mT/h\rfloor}\leq r^{-1}e^{-kmT}$. Set $a_*=k/(2\theta)$ and $d=k/(2\gamma)$. Chernoff's inequality then yields

$$\sup_\nu\Pr_\nu(\Lambda_T<a_*T)\leq r^{-1}e^{-d\beta T}.$$

Jensen's inequality under stationary initialization gives $r\geq e^{-\theta\bar\ell h}$, hence $a_*\leq\bar\ell/2$. Put $\mathcal H=2CF/\bar\ell$. For $T\geq\mathcal H/\beta$, the mean estimate implies $\mu\geq\bar\ell T/2\geq a_*T$.

On $\Lambda_T\geq a_*T$, use the previous smoothed remainder with $a=a_*$. On its complement, $|\Lambda_T-\mu|\leq\mu\leq bT$ and the unsmoothed derivative bound adds at most $b^4T^4e^{-d\beta T}/(3r)$ to total variation. This is bounded by $8b^4/(rd^4\beta^4)$, uniformly over $T$.

The remaining very short horizons need no positive mean lower bound. Since $0\leq\Lambda_T\leq bT$, one has $v\leq bT\mu$, $|M_3|\leq bTv$ and $M_4\leq(bT)^2v$. The unsmoothed moment comparison therefore gives

$$d_{\rm TV}\leq2b^3T^3+\frac{10}{3}b^4T^4 \quad(0<T\leq\mathcal H/\beta).$$

Evaluate the previously displayed $A_2,A_3,A_4$ at $a=a_*$, not at $\min_i\ell_i$. Adding the short-horizon and true-intensity tail budgets gives an all-speed, all-prior bound:

$$\widehat A_2=A_2,\qquad \widehat A_3=A_3+2b^3\mathcal H^3,\qquad \widehat A_4=A_4+\frac{8b^4}{rd^4}+\frac{10}{3}b^4\mathcal H^4,$$ $$\sup_{T>0,\nu}d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L_\nu(N_T),\mathcal L(N_{*,T,\nu})) \leq\min\{1,\widehat A_2/\beta^2+\widehat A_3/\beta^3+\widehat A_4/\beta^4\} =O(m^{-2}).$$

For $T\geq\mathcal H/\beta$, split again at $\tau$ as in the positive-floor proof. For smaller $T$, the displayed bounded-support estimate supplies the added inverse-speed terms. Thus no minimum speed or minimum horizon is imposed.

The effective $a_*$ is a concentration threshold, not a pathwise intensity floor. Both the true integrated intensity and its gamma surrogate require separate lower-tail budgets. If all intensities are zero, the count is exactly zero and no gamma fit is used.

A first-order obstruction for the standard process extension

Fix $T>0$ and stationary initialization, with nonnegative nonconstant $\ell$. Set $\bar\ell=\pi\ell$, $\theta_m=v/(\bar\ell T)$ and $a_m=\bar\ell/\theta_m$. At each speed, consider a gamma subordinator $\Gamma_t$ of shape $a_mt$ and scale $\theta_m$, independent of a unit-rate Poisson process $P$, and put $M_t=P(\Gamma_t)$.

Then $M_T$ has exactly the fitted negative-binomial law above, but this construction fixes its parameters using the chosen $T$, not all horizons simultaneously. Its independent-increment compound-Poisson representation is classical; see Zhou and Carin (2015), Section 4.2.

$$\log\mathbb E z^{M_t}=-a_mt\log\{1+\theta_m(1-z)\} =t\sum_{j\geq1}\frac{a_mq_m^j}{j}(z^j-1),\qquad q_m=\frac{\theta_m}{1+\theta_m}.$$

Thus jumps of size at least two occur at rate $r_m=a_m\{\log(1+\theta_m)-\theta_m/(1+\theta_m)\}$. The true Cox process has only unit jumps almost surely, since its bounded statewise intensity gives a continuous integrated clock.

$$D_m:=d_{\rm TV}\big(\mathcal L((N_t)_{0\leq t\leq T}), \mathcal L((M_t)_{0\leq t\leq T})\big) \geq B_m:=1-e^{-Tr_m}.$$

This is total variation on the usual measurable space of càdlàg count paths: use the event that some jump has size at least two, whose probabilities are zero and $B_m$. It is not a bound for counts observed only on a fixed time grid.

Let $g=\langle f,(-Q)^\# f\rangle_\pi>0$. Indeed, if $h=(-Q)^\#f$, then $g=\langle(-Q)h,h\rangle_\pi>0$ by the Dirichlet form on centered nonzero vectors, without reversibility.

$$\left|v-\frac{2Tg}{m}\right|\leq\frac{2CF^2}{\beta^2},\qquad 0\leq\frac{\bar\ell\theta_m}{2}-r_m \leq\frac{2\bar\ell\theta_m^2}{3}.$$

The first inequality integrates the stationary covariance envelope, including its tail beyond $T$. The second follows from $\log(1+\theta)-\theta/(1+\theta)=\int_0^\theta s/(1+s)^2\,ds$ and $0\leq1-(1+s)^{-2}\leq2s$.

$$\left|B_m-\frac{Tg}{m}\right|\leq \frac{CF^2}{\beta^2}+\frac{2T\bar\ell\theta_m^2}{3} +\frac{T^2\bar\ell^2\theta_m^2}{8}=O(m^{-2}).$$

Here $0\leq x-(1-e^{-x})\leq x^2/2$ and $r_m\leq\bar\ell\theta_m/2$ give the last term. Hence $\liminf_{m\to\infty}mD_m\geq Tg>0$ for this standard process extension, even though its terminal count has the proved $O(m^{-2})$ error.

For symmetric switching with intensities $(3,1)$, $T=1$ and speed 80, the terminal TV error is $0.0000077919$, while the path lower bound is $0.0061409272$. This obstruction concerns the independent-increment gamma clock; it does not rule out simple temporally dependent surrogates or second-order approximation on a fixed observation grid.

Certificates and scope

verify_uniform_positive_counts.py independently checks Poisson derivative norms, the Charlier squared-norm identity and gamma tail bounds. It compares 168 count laws at horizons $0.02,0.2,1,5,20,100$, using exact triangular intensity moments and matrix generating functions; both pure and stationary priors are included.

For symmetric switching with intensities $(3,1)$, $C=1$, $\gamma=2$, $\tau=1$ and speed 80, the all-horizon bound is $0.0125223590$. Pure first-state TV errors at $T=1,20,100$ are $0.0000077200$, $0.0000015441$ and $0.0000006892$. The defective-chain example at speed 80 has bound $0.0115323596$ and error $0.0000000703$ at $T=100$.

The largest omitted-tail TV budget is $3.773\times10^{-22}$, separate from roundoff. Matrix-exponential checks scale the base tolerance $1.455\times10^{-11}$ by the generator-horizon norm. The final certificate line is literal PASS.

The symmetric cases also cross-check the matrix generating function against an independently evaluated, overflow-safe closed-form two-state transform. The maximum probability-vector $\ell^1$ discrepancy is $7.340\times10^{-12}$.

verify_nonnegative_uniform_counts.py checks the zero-intensity extension separately, including starts in an inactive regime, a defective chain, very short horizons and the all-zero exact case. It compares 168 count laws, including 40 short-layer cases, and checks 18 killed-kernel bounds.

For symmetric switching with intensities $(3,0)$, $\theta=0.1$, $h=5$, $\tau=1$ and speed 320, the all-horizon bound is $0.0550543576$; the pure zero-regime start error at $T=1$ is $0.0000050773$. The zero-intensity defective example at speed 1280 has common bound $0.0305855767$. The new certificate ends in literal PASS.

verify_count_path_obstruction.py checks the compound-Poisson representation and batch rate, and 45 stationary count comparisons including a defective chain and zero intensities. It checks the explicit finite-rate remainder for $B_m$, independently of the terminal-count error; its final line is literal PASS.

Uniformity concerns individual count marginals at all horizons, not a single negative-binomial arrival process with consistent independent increments. Zero-intensity regimes are covered by the extension, while a statewise positive floor gives the shorter bound. Constants depend on fixed mixing data and a positive stationary mean; vanishing stationary means and closing spectral gaps are outside the uniform claim.
