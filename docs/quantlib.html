Fast regimes in QuantLib's models

Which of QuantLib's models and processes take regime-switching parameters, and which method handles each.

Any model in QuantLib can have some of its parameters driven by a fast hidden chain. How far the expansion reaches depends on where the switched parameters enter the pricing equation.

The test

Many QuantLib models are exponential-affine: a price or characteristic function has the form

$$u_i(t, x) = e^{-B(t)\cdot x}\,a_i(t),$$

where $B$ solves a Riccati equation. If the switched parameters do not appear in that Riccati equation, $B$ is the same in every regime. The regime then enters only through a forcing $g_i(t)$, and

$$a' = \big(Q + \operatorname{diag} g\big)\,a .$$

The engine expands this to all orders, with the initial layer.

When the switched parameters do enter the Riccati equation, or the model is not affine, the first-order rule still applies to any Markov pricing equation. It adds the Green–Kubo term $\sum_{jk} K_{jk} A_j A_k$ to the averaged operator.

Three families lie outside the framework. Rough Heston is not Markov. GJR-GARCH is a discrete-time model, which would need the discrete-time analogue of the expansion. The credit loss models (Gaussian large-pool and other latent-factor models) are static one-period factor models; dynamic credit with switching hazards is on the credit page.

Products

  • European options priced through characteristic functions use the engine directly, as on the Black–Scholes and Heston pages.
  • Bond options and swaptions in affine short-rate models need the regime at expiry, which starts the initial layer at order zero, as on the bond options page.
  • Asian and lookback options add a state variable. The pricing equation stays Markov, so the first-order rule applies to the enlarged generator.
  • Barrier and American options add a boundary or a free boundary. The rule applies to the operator, and the boundary adds layers that are not treated here.

The new checks

Merton's jump diffusion, variance gamma and Bates are Lévy or affine with constant or regime-free Riccati parts. For each, the regime-dependent forcing is the switched part of the characteristic exponent:

$$\begin{aligned} g_i &= \psi_i(u) &&\text{Merton, variance gamma}, \\ g_i(t) &= \kappa\,\theta_i\,D(t) + \lambda_i\,\big(\phi_J(u) - 1 - iu\bar k\big) &&\text{Bates}, \end{aligned}$$

where $\psi_i$ is the Lévy exponent in regime $i$, $D$ the Heston Riccati solution, $\phi_J$ the jump characteristic function and $\bar k$ the mean jump size. The certificate compares the expansion with the numerical solution as the switching speeds up. Every order converges at its expected rate, from one to five, until the error reaches rounding.
