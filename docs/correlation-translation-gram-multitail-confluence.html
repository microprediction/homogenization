Coalescing rare tails create a derivative Gram ladder

For $s$ merging slopes, the $s-1$ lost directions return at successive even powers of the separation.

Distinct rare-tail slopes promote several eigenvalues at one scale. When all slopes merge, weighted label polynomials and exponential derivatives determine a complete smaller-scale ladder.

Multi-tail confluence theorem

Fix $c,\tau>0$, distinct nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, integers $s\ge2$ and $1\le r\le k-s-1$, positive weights $q_1,\ldots,q_s$, and distinct real offsets $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_s$ satisfying $\sum_\ell q_\ell\gamma_\ell=0$.

For $\varepsilon_n,\delta_n\downarrow0$, set

$$\tau_{\ell n}=\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n,\qquad A_{\ell n}=\exp(\tau_{\ell n}/\varepsilon_n),\qquad p_{\ell n}=q_\ell\varepsilon_n^{2r}A_{\ell n}^{-2c}.$$

Let $X_n$ have law $(1-\sum_\ell p_{\ell n})\mathcal L(G)+ \sum_\ell p_{\ell n}\delta_{A_{\ell n}}$, where $G\sim N(0,1)$, and let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_i}$. Assume

$$\frac{\varepsilon_n}{\delta_n^{s-1}}\longrightarrow0.$$

Give $\mathbb R^s$ the weighted inner product $\langle a,b\rangle_q=\sum_\ell q_\ell a_\ell b_\ell$. Put $\zeta_j=(\gamma_\ell^j)_\ell$, let $\widehat\zeta_j$ be its residual after projection onto $\zeta_0,\ldots,\zeta_{j-1}$, and set $\eta_j=\|\widehat\zeta_j\|_q^2$.

In output space write $\phi_a=(x_i^a/a!)_i$ and $v_j=(x_i^je^{\tau x_i}/j!)_i$. Let $R_j$ project orthogonally away from

$$L_j=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_r,v_0,\ldots,v_{j-1}\}.$$

The first $r$ Gaussian eigenvalues and the two order-$\varepsilon_n^{2r}$ primary eigenvalues are those of the common-slope theorem, with total tail weight $Q=\sum_\ell q_\ell$. For every $1\le j\le s-1$,

$$\lambda_{r+2+j}(K_n)= \eta_j\|R_jv_j\|_2^2\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2j} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2j}).$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2(s-1)})$.

Why the ladder is complete

Weighted Gram–Schmidt applied to the label Taylor frame $e^{\gamma_\ell\delta_nx}$ produces the successive latent coordinates $\delta_n^j\widehat\zeta_j$. Output Gram–Schmidt simultaneously leaves $R_jv_j$.

The label coefficient is positive because the distinct $\gamma_\ell$ give a nonsingular Vandermonde matrix. The output coefficient is positive because

$$1,x,\ldots,x^r,e^{\tau x},xe^{\tau x},\ldots,x^je^{\tau x}$$

is an extended Chebyshev system at the $k\ge r+s+1\ge r+j+2$ distinct nodes. Successive Schur complements and finite-dimensional spectral perturbation therefore give the displayed coefficients.

The next Gaussian feature has size $\varepsilon_n^{r+1}$, whereas the last tail feature has size $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^{s-1}$. Their ratio is exactly the stated separation parameter; higher tail terms are smaller by another factor $\delta_n$.

Three-tail certificate

Take $s=3$, $k=6$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3)$, centered offsets $(-4/3,-1/3,2/3)$, nodes $(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2)$, and $\delta_n=\varepsilon_n^{1/3}$.

The weighted label residuals have squared norms $(6,10/3,6/5)$. The two derivative-ladder coefficients are

$$(3.038546555413267,\ 0.049977993617021).$$

At $n=1048576$, the exact normalized ratios are $(0.975091876995,0.982401555213)$, while the next Gaussian eigenvalue divided by the last tail scale is $0.000000009128$. The 260-digit certificate also checks the primary pair, full confluent rank, monotone convergence, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for the displayed triangular laws at one fixed positive maturity, with fixed $s,k,r,q,$ and $\gamma$. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not a finite-$n$ bound or uniformity over moving slope arrays, dimensions, or separation rates.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
