A global error bound for the call correction

The infinite damped Fourier integral, not just a fixed frequency cutoff.

Let $B,W$ be independent Brownian motions and $Y$ an independent symmetric two-state chain flipping at rate $m$, initially stationary. Fix $T,\kappa,\xi,\theta_+,\theta_->0$, $v_0\ge0$, $X_0=0$, and $|\rho|<1$.

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta_{Y_t}-v_t)dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t,\qquad dX_t=-\tfrac12v_tdt+\sqrt{v_t}(\rho\,dB_t+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,dW_t).$$

Write $\bar\theta=(\theta_++\theta_-)/2$, $\delta=(\theta_+-\theta_-)/2$, $\Phi_m(s)=\mathbb E e^{sX_T}$, and let $\Phi_{\rm av}$ replace $\theta_Y$ by $\bar\theta$. Choose $\gamma>1$, $p>1$, $r=p\gamma$, $q=p/(p-1)$ satisfying the explicit sufficient conditions

$$a_r:=\kappa-\rho\xi r>0,\qquad d_r:=\sqrt{a_r^2-\xi^2r(r-1)}>0.$$

For $s=\gamma+iu$, let $B_s(0)=0$ solve $B_s'=\xi^2B_s^2/2-(\kappa-\rho\xi s)B_s+(s^2-s)/2$ and define

$$h_T(s)={\kappa^2\delta^2\over2}\int_0^T B_s(t)^2dt,\quad D_\alpha(u)=(\alpha+iu)(\gamma+iu),\quad \alpha=\gamma-1,$$ $$c_1(k)={e^{-\alpha k}\over\pi}\int_0^\infty \Re{e^{-iuk}\Phi_{\rm av}(\gamma+iu)h_T(\gamma+iu)\over D_\alpha(u)}du.$$

All these integrals converge absolutely, and the constants below give, for $m\ge m_0$ and any compact $K$,

$$\sup_{k\in K}|C_m(k)-C_{\rm av}(k)-c_1(k)/m|\le A_K/m^2, \qquad C_m(k)=\mathbb E(e^{X_T}-e^k)_+.$$

Uniform polynomial Riccati bounds

Put $\eta=1-\rho^2$, $a=\kappa-\rho\xi\gamma$, $d_0=\sqrt{a^2-\xi^2\gamma(\gamma-1)}$, $c=\xi\sqrt\eta$, and $\ell=-2\rho\xi a-\xi^2(2\gamma-1)$. The assumptions at $r$ imply $a,d_0>0$: $a_s$ is linear and $d_s^2$ is concave on $[0,r]$.

$$H_0=1+{|\ell|\over2d_0c},\quad R_0={a\over d_0}+{|\rho|\over\sqrt\eta},\quad z={2\over1+R_0},\quad \nu=\min\{z/2,1-e^{-z/(2\sqrt{H_0})}\},$$ $$C_B={2\max\{a+\sqrt{H_0}d_0,\ |\rho|\xi+\sqrt{H_0}c\}\over\xi^2\nu},\qquad C_D={\xi^2 C_B^2\over2}+\max\{a,|\rho|\xi\}C_B+{\gamma^2\over2}.$$

Then, simultaneously for every $t\ge0$ and $u\ge0$,

$$|B_{\gamma+iu}(t)|\le C_B(1+u),\qquad |B_{\gamma+iu}'(t)|\le C_D(1+u)^2.$$

To prove this, set $b=a-i\rho\xi u$, $d=\sqrt{b^2-\xi^2s(s-1)}$ with $\Re d>0$, and $g_*=(b-d)/(b+d)$. The closed solution is $B_s=(b-d)(1-e^{-dt})/[\xi^2(1-g_*e^{-dt})]$.

Writing $d=x+iy$, we have $x^2\ge d_0^2+c^2u^2$ and $|d|^2\le H_0(d_0^2+c^2u^2)$. Moreover,

$$\Re(b\bar d)={ax^2-\rho\xi\ell u^2/2\over x} \ge {ad_0^2+\xi^2(\kappa-\rho\xi/2)u^2\over x}>0.$$

Thus $|g_*|<1$, $|1-g_*|\ge z$ and $x/|d|\ge H_0^{-1/2}$. For $t\le z/(2|d|)$ use $|1-e^{-dt}|\le |d|t$; for larger $t$ use $|1-g_*e^{-dt}|\ge1-e^{-xt}$. The denominator is at least $\nu$ in both cases, proving the $B_s$ bound; its ODE gives the derivative bound.

A speed-independent exponential envelope

Let $a_* =\kappa\min\theta_i$, $a^*=\kappa\max\theta_i$, $\beta_*=(a_r-d_r)/\xi^2$ and define

$$M=e^{(a^*T+v_0)\beta_*},\quad C_0=2^{2a_*/\xi^2}e^{a_*\kappa T/\xi^2},\qquad C=M^{1/p}C_0^{1/q},\quad b_0={a_*T\sqrt\eta\over\xi\sqrt q}.$$

Both the switched and averaged models satisfy $|\Phi(\gamma+iu)|\le Ce^{-b_0|u|}$, for every switching speed. The following supersolution proof is specialized to common $\kappa,\xi,\rho$.

The nonnegative Riccati solution $\beta'=\xi^2\beta^2/2-a_r\beta+r(r-1)/2$ stays below $\beta_*$. The generator supersolution $e^{rX+A(T-t)+\beta(T-t)v}$, $A'=a^*\beta$, gives $\mathbb E e^{rX_T}\le M$ by localization and Fatou's lemma.

For $V=\int_0^T v_tdt$, the scalar CIR Laplace supersolution with immigration $a_*$ gives $\mathbb E e^{-sV}\le C_0e^{-a_*T\sqrt{2s}/\xi}$. Indeed its negative Riccati solution has discriminant $d=\sqrt{\kappa^2+2\xi^2s}$ and log-amplitude at most $(2a_*/\xi^2)\log2+a_*(\kappa-d)T/\xi^2$.

Conditional on $B,Y$, the independent Gaussian part of $X_T$ has variance $J=\eta V$. With $H=\mathbb E(e^{\gamma X_T}\mid B,Y)$, Jensen gives $\mathbb E H^p\le M$; Hölder applied to $H e^{-u^2J/2}$ proves the stated envelope. The same supersolutions apply to $\bar\theta$.

Growing the frequency interval

Set $G=\kappa|\delta|C_B$, $L=\kappa|\delta|C_D$ and

$$H=TG^2/2,\quad R_3=TGL/4,\quad R_4=TG^4/2,\quad \varepsilon=(1+H)^{-1/2},$$ $$m_0=\left\lceil\max\{4/\varepsilon^2,(G\varepsilon)^2, 4R_3^2\varepsilon^6,2R_4\varepsilon^4\}\right\rceil,\qquad U_m=\varepsilon\sqrt m-1.$$

On $0\le u\le U_m$ the amplitude ratio $r_s$ solves $r_s'=g-2mr_s-gr_s^2$, $r_s(0)=0$, where $g=\kappa\delta B_s$. The bootstrap $|r_s|\le\sup|g|/m$ and $g(0)=0$ give

$$\Phi_m=\Phi_{\rm av}\exp(h_T/m+R_m),\qquad |h_T|\le H(1+u)^2,\quad |R_m|\le {R_3(1+u)^3\over m^2}+{R_4(1+u)^4\over m^3}.$$

For completeness, $e=r_s-g/(2m)$ obeys $e'+2me=-gr_s^2-g'/(2m)$ and $e(0)=0$. Integrate its decaying kernel and then $g e$ to obtain the remainder bound. Reconstructing the mean amplitude as an exponential proves it stays nonzero on this interval, avoiding an assumed zero-free ratio.

Our choice gives $m\ge\sup|g|$, $|h_T|/m\le1$ and $|R_m|\le1$ there. Taylor's inequality therefore bounds the transform error by

$$ {Ce^{-b_0u}\over m^2} \{e^2 R_3(1+u)^3+(e^2R_4+eH^2/2)(1+u)^4\}.$$

Since $|D_\alpha|\ge\alpha\gamma$, integration over the inner interval is at most $A_{\rm in}/m^2$, where

$$J_n=\sum_{j=0}^n{n\choose j}{j!\over b_0^{j+1}},\qquad A_{\rm in}={C\over\pi\alpha\gamma} \{e^2R_3J_3+(e^2R_4+eH^2/2)J_4\}.$$

On the outer interval use the two exact envelopes and $|h_T|\le H(1+u)^2$ directly. Integrating these polynomials, using $1+U_m=\varepsilon\sqrt m$ and $m\ge1$, gives an outer bound $Ce^{b_0}E e^{-b_0\varepsilon\sqrt m}/(\pi\alpha\gamma)$, with

$$E={2+H\varepsilon^2\over b_0}+{2H\varepsilon\over b_0^2}+{2H\over b_0^3},\qquad A_{\rm out}={Ce^{b_0}E\over\pi\alpha\gamma} \left({4\over e b_0\varepsilon}\right)^4,\qquad A_K=e^{-\alpha\inf K}(A_{\rm in}+A_{\rm out}).$$

The elementary bound $\sup_{x\ge0}x^4e^{-\lambda x}=(4/(e\lambda))^4$ finishes the proof. The damped payoff transform is $\Phi(\gamma+iu)/D_\alpha(u)$, by integrating over $k<X_T$; absolute convergence justifies all inversions and subtractions.

Checks and scope

The certificate checks closed Riccati solutions against independent ODEs, the polynomial envelopes, inner log remainders, and exact switched-amplitude call integrals with doubled quadrature order. It prints the conservative threshold and global budget as well as the observed inverse-square residuals.

At $\kappa\theta=(.2,.5)$, $\kappa=1.4$, $\xi=.8$, $\rho=-.6$, $T=1$, $v_0=.04$, $\gamma=p=2$, the explicit threshold is $m_0=123$ and $A_K=1.57337627\times10^{11}$ for $|k|\le.2$. This proves the asymptotic order but is not a useful practical pricing tolerance.

Over $k=-.2,0,.2$, the maximum call residual drops from $3.45455317\times10^{-6}$ at $m=16$ to $5.06216752\times10^{-8}$ at $m=128$. At cutoff $240$, the combined analytic tail budget for $m\ge16$ is $2.705\times10^{-14}$; doubling quadrature order changes the prices and coefficient by at most $6.400\times10^{-13}$. These floating checks are not outward-rounded enclosures.

This is a fixed-maturity theorem for symmetric mean-level switching and a stationary regime start. It is not uniform in maturity, the correlation boundary, or the moment-strip boundary, and makes no implied-volatility claim without a lower Vega bound. The analytic constants are intentionally loose; numerical quadrature and roundoff are separate errors.

The affine transform framework is classical: Duffie, Pan and Singleton (2000), Proposition 1. The existing cumulant note supplies the two-state amplitude reduction; the polynomial contour estimates and growing-cutoff error proof above complete the global step in issue 197.
