Critical noise in a finite regime chain

Even-power path scores combine boundary covariance with initial-mean memory times stationary skewness, uniformly over Gaussian noise levels.

Coefficient and scope

Fix an irreducible finite row generator $Q$, stationary row $\pi$, and real observable $f$ centered so $\pi f=0$. With pointwise products, define

$$h=Q^\#f,\qquad B=2Q^\#(fh),\qquad \Gamma=-2\pi(fh)>0,\qquad \kappa_3=3\pi(fB).$$

Here $Q^\#$ satisfies $QQ^\#=I-\mathbf1\pi$, $\pi Q^\#=0$ and $Q^\#\mathbf1=0$. The scalars $\Gamma,\kappa_3$ are the stationary second and third cumulant rates of the integrated observable. The vector $B$ is the initial-state second raw-moment boundary term around the stationary mean, not a variance contrast after recentering each start.

$$A_c={1\over c}\int_0^c f(Y_s)ds,\qquad S_c=\mu A_c^2+\sigma_c Z,\qquad \sigma_c={\lambda\mu\Gamma\over c},\quad \mu>0,$$

Take independent standard Gaussian $Z$ and let $q_c$ be the exact stationary pooled $p$-quantile, for fixed $0<p<1$. Let $t_p(\lambda)$ be the $p$-quantile of $N^2+\lambda Z$, with independent standard normals $N,Z$. For $x>0$, set $u=\sqrt x$ and define

$$P_i(x)=\phi(u)\left[-{B_i\over\Gamma}u +{h_i\kappa_3\over3\Gamma^2}(u^3-3u)\right],\qquad K_{i,\lambda}(t)=\mathbb E[P_i(t-\lambda Z)],$$

Set $P_i=0$ on $x\le0$. The expansion is uniform over every nonnegative noise level, including zero:

$$\Pr_i(S_c\le q_c)-p={1\over c}K_{i,\lambda}(t_p(\lambda))+r_{i,c}(\lambda), \qquad\sup_{i,\lambda\ge0}c(1+\lambda)|r_{i,c}(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0.$$

The chain and nonconstant feature are fixed; no uniformity as their spectral or variance scales degenerate is claimed. No extra nonlattice hypothesis or global density expansion is needed. At zero noise, a fixed interior pooled quantile eventually avoids all atoms, so its CDF attains $p$.

Both coefficient vectors have stationary weighted sum zero because $\pi h=\pi B=0$. Population pooling is exactly marginal; this remains latent-start model-conditional bias, not a finite-calibration or feature-conditional guarantee.

Proof by the even part of the Fourier transform

Put $D=\operatorname{diag}f$. The simple eigenvalue $\Lambda(z)$ of $Q+zD$ near zero and its analytic spectral projector $\Pi(z)$ obey

$$\Lambda(z)={\Gamma z^2\over2}+{\kappa_3z^3\over6}+O(z^4),\qquad (e_i-\pi)\Pi(z)\mathbf1=-h_i z+{B_i z^2\over2}+O(z^3).$$

For completeness, normalize the right eigenvector $r(z)$ by $\pi r(z)=1$. Differentiating its eigen-equation gives $r'(0)=-h$, $r''(0)=B$ and the two displayed eigenvalue derivatives. Normalize the left eigenvector by $\ell(z)r(z)=1$; then $(\ell(z)\mathbf1)'_{z=0}=0$, giving the projector expansion.

For $X_c=\sqrt{c/\Gamma}A_c$, write $\delta\chi_i(u)=(e_i-\pi)e^{c(Q+iuD/\sqrt{c\Gamma})}\mathbf1$. Multiplying the eigenvalue and projector expansions and taking real parts cancels the odd $c^{-1/2}$ term:

$$\operatorname{Re}\delta\chi_i(u)={e^{-u^2/2}\over c} \left[-{B_i\over2\Gamma}u^2-{h_i\kappa_3\over6\Gamma^2}u^4\right]+o(c^{-1}).$$

Inverting the two Gaussian polynomials gives the signed correction density $\phi(x)\{B_iH_2(x)/(2\Gamma)-h_i\kappa_3H_4(x)/(6\Gamma^2)\}$, with $H_2=x^2-1$ and $H_4=x^4-6x^2+3$. Its integral over $[-\sqrt x,\sqrt x]$ is $P_i(x)$. The following global estimate justifies this calculation without smoothing.

Why high frequencies do not obstruct the zero-noise limit

In $L^2(\pi)$, $A(\theta)=Q+i\theta D$ has a Hermitian part $H=(Q+Q^*)/2$ independent of $\theta$. The identity $\operatorname{Re}\langle v,Qv\rangle_\pi=-\tfrac12\sum_{j,k}\pi_jq_{jk}|v_k-v_j|^2$ shows that an eigenvalue on the imaginary axis at $\theta\ne0$ would force $v$ constant and then $f$ constant. Thus every such eigenvalue has strictly negative real part.

As $|\theta|\to\infty$, the eigenvalues cluster at $i\theta a+\operatorname{spec}(Q_{C_aC_a})+o(1)$, where $C_a=\{j:f_j=a\}$. This follows by the Schur complement between distinct diagonal blocks of $D$; the off-block inverses are $O(|\theta|^{-1})$. Every level set is proper, and each killed generator $Q_{C_aC_a}$ is transient by irreducibility.

Continuity on the remaining compact frequency set gives, for each $\delta>0$, a number $\gamma_\delta>0$ with spectral abscissa at most $-\gamma_\delta$ for all $|\theta|\ge\delta$. This includes repeated feature values and defective eigenvalues. A unitary Schur form has strict upper entries bounded by $2\|H\|$, since its Hermitian part is a unitary conjugate of $H$.

$$\sup_{|\theta|\ge\delta}\|e^{cA(\theta)}\|_{L^2(\pi)} \le e^{-\gamma_\delta c}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{(Kc)^k\over k!},\qquad K=2n\|H\|.$$

Indeed the triangular Duhamel series terminates after $n-1$ strict-upper factors; diagonal propagators contribute at most $e^{-\gamma_\delta c}$. This polynomial-times-exponential bound is uniform even at arbitrarily large frequencies.

Unsmoothed CDF and integrable profile error

Let $b_i=[\Pi''(0)\mathbf1]_i$, $\kappa_4=\Lambda^{(4)}(0)$, and $\chi_i(u)=\mathbb E_i e^{iuX_c}$. The even characteristic Edgeworth approximant is

$$\mathcal E_{i,c}(u)=e^{-u^2/2}\left[1+{1\over c}\left\{ {b_i(iu)^2\over2\Gamma}+\left({\kappa_4\over24\Gamma^2} -{h_i\kappa_3\over6\Gamma^2}\right)(iu)^4 +{\kappa_3^2(iu)^6\over72\Gamma^3}\right\}\right].$$

Its inverse transform defines a smooth signed CDF $G_{i,c}$ with uniformly bounded derivative; also $b_i-\pi b=B_i$. The signed-approximant smoothing inequality used by Hervé–Pène, page 450, gives the finite bound

$$\|\widetilde F_{i,c}-G_{i,c}\|_\infty\le\epsilon_{i,c}(T):= {2\over\pi}\int_0^T {|\operatorname{Re}\chi_i(u)-\mathcal E_{i,c}(u)|\over u}du +{24\|G'_{i,c}\|_\infty\over\pi T},$$

where $\widetilde F$ is the CDF of the symmetrized law of $X_c$. The remainder divided by $u$ is integrable at zero, as both characteristic functions have mass one and are even. On $|u|\le c^{1/20}$ analytic expansion gives an integrable Gaussian-times-polynomial $O(c^{-3/2})$ remainder, with an exponentially small remainder vanishing at zero.

From $c^{1/20}$ to $\delta\sqrt{c\Gamma}$, $\operatorname{Re}\Lambda(iu/\sqrt{c\Gamma})\le-d u^2/c$ controls the principal part. Beyond this range the high-frequency bound applies; take $T=e^{\gamma_\delta c/2}$, so its $du/u$ integral is exponentially small times a polynomial. Therefore the unsmoothed CDF error is $O(c^{-3/2})$ uniformly on the line.

Define $J_{i,c}(x)=c\{\Pr_i(X_c^2\le x)-\Pr_\pi(X_c^2\le x)\}$, zero for $x<0$, and

$$M_{i,c}=\|J_{i,c}-P_i\|_\infty=O(c^{-1/2}),\qquad L_{i,c}=\int_0^\infty|J_{i,c}(x)-P_i(x)|dx\longrightarrow0.$$

Folding the two CDFs proves the supremum estimate, with the explicit bound $M_{i,c}\le2c\{\epsilon_{i,c}(T)+\sum_j\pi_j\epsilon_{j,c}(T)\}$ for any $T>0$. For integrability, local real-parameter spectral bounds give $\mathbb E_i e^{z\int_0^c f(Y_s)ds}\le C e^{Cc z^2}$ for small real $z$.

Chernoff's inequality thus gives Gaussian tails for $|X_c|\le d\sqrt c$, and exponentially small tails beyond that range up to the bounded support of $X_c$. Integrating on $x\le R_c^2$ costs $O(c^{-1/2}\log c)$ for $R_c=\sqrt{K_0\log c}$. On the complement, $c\int_{R_c^2}^\infty\Pr_i(X_c^2>x)dx$ and the normal-polynomial profile tail vanish when fixed $K_0$ is large enough, proving $L_{i,c}\to0$.

All-noise transfer and quantiles

$$\left|F_i(\mu\Gamma t/c)-F_\pi(\mu\Gamma t/c)-{K_{i,\lambda}(t)\over c}\right| \le {1\over c}\min\left\{M_{i,c},{L_{i,c}\over\lambda\sqrt{2\pi}}\right\}.$$

The second bound is omitted at zero noise; the first is supremum contraction and the second uses the maximum of the Gaussian density. Multiplication by $c(1+\lambda)$ bounds the error by $2\max\{M_{i,c},L_{i,c}/\sqrt{2\pi}\}\to0$ at every actual threshold.

Set $\rho=\lambda/(1+\lambda)$: the normalized reference law is $(1-\rho)N^2+\rho Z$, for $0\le\rho\le1$. The pooled unsmoothed CDF converges to the chi-square CDF, and convolution contracts Kolmogorov distance. Compactness and strict increase at the reference $p$-quantiles imply $\sup_\lambda|cq_c/(\mu\Gamma)-t_p(\lambda)|/(1+\lambda)\to0$.

The family $(1+\lambda)K_{i,\lambda}((1+\lambda)s)$ extends continuously to $P_i(s)$ at $\rho=0$ and $-(B_i/\Gamma)\phi(s)$ at $\rho=1$, since $P_i$ is uniformly continuous and integrable with integral $-B_i/\Gamma$. Uniform equicontinuity on compact $s$ sets completes the quantile transfer. At zero noise all atoms correspond to paths staying in one feature level, with locations $cf_j^2/\Gamma$ and exponentially decaying killed-block masses; these are eventually outside the interior quantile region or at zero.

Every fixed even power

Fix an integer $k\ge1$ and instead take $S_c^{(k)}=\mu A_c^{2k}+\sigma_c Z$, with $\lambda=c^k\sigma_c/(\mu\Gamma^k)$; let $q_c^{(k)}$ be its stationary pooled $p$-quantile and $t_p^{(k)}(\lambda)$ the reference quantile of $N^{2k}+\lambda Z$. Set $P_i^{(k)}(x)=P_i(x^{1/k})$ for $x>0$, zero otherwise, and $K_{i,\lambda}^{(k)}(t)=\mathbb E P_i^{(k)}(t-\lambda Z)$.

$$\Pr_i(S_c^{(k)}\le q_c^{(k)})-p ={K_{i,\lambda}^{(k)}(t_p^{(k)}(\lambda))\over c}+r_{i,c}^{(k)}(\lambda),\qquad \sup_{i,\lambda\ge0}c(1+\lambda)|r_{i,c}^{(k)}(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0.$$

The unsmoothed profile is exactly $J_{i,c}^{(k)}(x)=J_{i,c}(x^{1/k})$. Its supremum error is the same $M_{i,c}$, but the needed integrable error has the weight

$$L_{i,c}^{(k)}=\int_0^\infty|J_{i,c}^{(k)}(x)-P_i^{(k)}(x)|dx =k\int_0^\infty y^{k-1}|J_{i,c}(y)-P_i(y)|dy\longrightarrow0.$$

To prove this stronger estimate, truncate at $y=R_c^2$, where $R_c^2=K_0\log c$. The central contribution is $O(c^{-1/2}(\log c)^k)$; the preceding Chernoff bounds give a tail at most $Cc R_c^{2k}e^{-aR_c^2}+Cc^{k+1}e^{-bc}$, plus a vanishing normal-polynomial profile tail.

Choose fixed $K_0$ large enough; $k$ remains fixed. Gaussian convolution now gives the same actual-threshold bound $c^{-1}\min\{M_{i,c},L_{i,c}^{(k)}/(\lambda\sqrt{2\pi})\}$, omitting the second term at zero noise.

For a finite-$c$ bound, any $R>0$ and integer $\ell\ge1$ give

$$L_{i,c}^{(k)}\le R^{2k}M_{i,c} +{c\over R^{2\ell}}\left\{\mathbb E_i|X_c|^{2(k+\ell)} +\mathbb E_\pi|X_c|^{2(k+\ell)}\right\} +k\int_{R^2}^\infty y^{k-1}|P_i(y)|dy.$$

This follows from $|J_{i,c}(y)|\le c\{\Pr_i(X_c^2>y)+\Pr_\pi(X_c^2>y)\}$ and a moment tail bound. The integrated moments follow from $v_j'=Qv_j+jDv_{j-1}$, $v_0=\mathbf1$ and $v_j(0)=0$ for $j>0$, so this bound and the signed-Esseen bound on $M$ involve only finite matrices and one-dimensional integrals.

$$\int_0^\infty P_i^{(k)}(x)dx=-{D_{k,i}\over\Gamma^k},\qquad D_{k,i}=k(2k-1)!!\left[B_i\Gamma^{k-1} -{2(k-1)\over3}h_i\kappa_3\Gamma^{k-2}\right].$$

Indeed substitution $y=u^2$ reduces the integral to Gaussian even moments and $\mathbb E N^{2k+2}=(2k+1)\mathbb E N^{2k}$. Compactify the reference laws to $(1-\rho)N^{2k}+\rho Z$ as before; they have unique interior quantiles, and the normalized coefficient family extends continuously to $P_i^{(k)}(s)$ and $-(D_{k,i}/\Gamma^k)\phi(s)$ at the two endpoints.

This proves the uniform quantile transfer; transformed atoms again lie at zero or diverge. If $c^k\sigma_c\to\infty$, the theorem therefore gives

$$c^{k+1}\sigma_c\{\Pr_i(S_c^{(k)}\le q_c^{(k)})-p\} \longrightarrow-\mu D_{k,i}\phi(\Phi^{-1}(p)).$$

In particular a quartic score with fixed positive noise has the $c^{-3}$ coefficient $6\Gamma B_i-4\kappa_3h_i$, whereas at zero noise every even power has the same quantile event $\{|A_c|\le r\}$ and the same $c^{-1}$ profile evaluated at the corresponding quantile.

All constants fix $Q,f,k,p$; this is not uniform over unbounded power or degenerating chains. This supplies the higher-even-power extension requested in issue 195, subject to review.

An actual-threshold Fourier error budget

For $\lambda>0$ there is also a finite-$c$ bound without substituting a limiting quantile; write $F_i^{(k)}$ for the CDF of $S_c^{(k)}$. For the chosen power $k$, put $g_{t,\lambda}(x)=\Phi((t-x^{2k})/\lambda)$ and $\widehat g(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-iux}g_{t,\lambda}(x)dx$; let $a_i=B_i/(2\Gamma)$, $d_i=-h_i\kappa_3/(6\Gamma^2)$, and $R_i(u)=e^{-u^2/2}(-a_i u^2+d_i u^4)/c$. For any $U>0$ the error $|F_i^{(k)}(\mu\Gamma^k t/c^k)-F_\pi^{(k)}(\mu\Gamma^k t/c^k)-K_{i,\lambda}^{(k)}(t)/c|$ is at most

$$ {1\over\pi}\int_0^U|\widehat g(u)|\, |\operatorname{Re}\delta\chi_i(u)-R_i(u)|du +{\|g^{(4)}\|_1\over\pi}\left[ {2\over3U^3}+{e^{-U^2/2}\over cU} \left({|a_i|\over U^2}+|d_i|\right)\right].$$

Indeed $|\widehat g(u)|\le\|g^{(4)}\|_1/u^4$; use $|\delta\chi_i|\le2$ and the Gaussian tail bound on the remaining integral. All quantities are determined by a finite matrix exponential and one-dimensional integrals. Floating quadratures in the certificate, including their refinement checks, are not outward-rounded enclosures.

Benchmark and relation to fixed noise

The certificate uses the nonreversible generator and feature

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1.8&1.4&.4\\ .3&-1.2&.9\\1.1&.2&-1.3\end{pmatrix}, \qquad f_{\rm raw}=(1,-.7,.2)^\top,$$

centered under its computed stationary law. It gives $\Gamma=0.385937945$ and $\kappa_3=-0.046285861$. At $p=.9,\lambda=1,c=2048$, the $c$-scaled gaps are $(-.032473882,-.036965545,.066119823)$, against limits $(-.032472792,-.036942314,.066093592)$.

The largest scaled quantile discrepancy over six noise/target pairs falls from $0.002173971$ at $c=32$ to $0.000032646$ at $c=2048$. Omitting stationary skewness leaves a nonvanishing coefficient error $0.001381965$ in that benchmark. Independent Bessel-CDF comparisons check the two-state specialization; eigenprojector derivatives independently check the Poisson coefficients.

Across $\lambda=0,.02,c^{-1/2}$ and targets $.5,.9$, the largest scaled discrepancy falls from $0.002325935$ at $c=32$ to $0.000034840$ at $c=2048$. Across $\lambda=\sqrt c,.5c/\Gamma,c^2$ at target $.9$, the largest $c(1+\lambda)$-weighted discrepancy falls from $0.000731350$ to $0.000004105$. At fixed physical noise $.5$, the $c^2$-coefficient error falls from $0.004144202$ to $0.000067222$.

The numerical routine retains killed-block atoms when evaluating the unsmoothed folded CDF, with an independent repeated-feature jump check. Its high-frequency matrix-norm comparisons are grid regressions, not numerical proofs of a continuum spectral gap.

There are also 128 quartic/sextic quantile cases, covering zero, shrinking, bounded and growing normalized noise, with 16 Fourier budgets and 16 independent finite-moment Taylor checks. Their largest $c(1+\lambda)$-weighted discrepancy falls from $0.877089823$ at $c=32$ to $0.014101738$ at $c=2048$.

Sixteen independent two-state quartic/sextic Bessel-CDF vectors agree within $1.989\times10^{-11}$. These short-horizon checks use a larger Fourier cutoff because steep maps with weak smoothing are not resolved by the default cutoff; large-noise gap integrals use fixed Gaussian quadrature to avoid adapting to the weighted Fourier roundoff floor.

The identity $\int_0^\infty P_i(x)dx=-B_i/\Gamma$ gives $\lambda K_{i,\lambda}(t_p(\lambda))\to-(B_i/\Gamma)\phi(\Phi^{-1}(p))$ as $\lambda\to\infty$. The all-noise remainder now justifies joint limits too: if $c\sigma_c\to\infty$, then $c^2\sigma_c\{\Pr_i(S_c\le q_c)-p\}\to-\mu B_i\phi(\Phi^{-1}(p))$. Fixed positive noise therefore recovers the existing $c^{-2}$ coefficient.

The spectral mechanism is classical; compare Hervé and Pène (2010), Theorem 8.1 and Lemma 8.3, for discrete-time initial bias and stationary skewness. Their signed-approximant smoothing inequality on page 450 is used above; the continuous-time high-frequency estimate and weighted profile transfer are proved here. This supplies the all-noise extension requested in issue 193, subject to review.
