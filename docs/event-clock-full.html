Anytime generator confidence from a known event clock

A classical resolvent inverse, with a computable confidence radius and no matrix-logarithm alias gate.

Fix an irreducible finite row generator $Q$ on $n\ge2$ known labeled states and a known positive diagonal matrix $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)$, specified in physical-time units before observing data. Conditional on the entire regime path $Y$, observations arrive as a Poisson process of intensity $\lambda_{Y_t}$, without feedback on $Y$. Set $T_0=0$, let $T_k$ be the successive arrival times, and record $Z_k=Y_{T_k}$, including repeated equal labels.

The ordered labels suffice here: neither physical regime-jump counts nor observation timestamps enter the estimator. Constant $\lambda_i=\rho$ gives an independent rate-$\rho$ Poisson observation clock. Unknown or estimated intensities, adaptive clocks and noisy regime labels are outside the theorem.

Population identification and sampling bias

$$R=\int_0^\infty e^{t(Q-\Lambda)}\Lambda\,dt =(\Lambda-Q)^{-1}\Lambda,\qquad Q=\Lambda(I-R^{-1}).$$

The killed semigroup gives the density of the next observation and its state; its row mass is at most $e^{-t\min_i\lambda_i}$. Integration proves the identity, and $\Lambda-Q$ is strictly row-diagonally dominant, hence invertible. Thus known $\Lambda$ identifies every finite $Q$, without reversibility, diagonalizability or a spectral branch choice.

This is the finite-state form of classical random-time identification, not a new identification principle: see Duffie and Glynn (2004), Sections 3–4 and Proposition 2. If $\pi Q=0$, the stationary event law is $\nu=\pi\Lambda/(\pi\Lambda\mathbf1)$ because $\pi\Lambda R^{-1}=\pi\Lambda$. Consequently $\pi_i\propto\nu_i/\lambda_i$; event frequencies are generally not physical occupation frequencies.

The multiplication order matters: $\Lambda$ premultiplies $I-R^{-1}$. If only ordered labels are retained and the clock is unknown, $(Q,\Lambda)$ and $(cQ,c\Lambda)$ have the same $R$ for every $c>0$. Identification using timestamps with unknown intensities is a different problem.

Deterministic reconstruction bound

Use the maximum absolute row-sum norm $\|\cdot\|_\infty$. Suppose $\widehat R$ is stochastic, $\|\widehat R-R\|_\infty\le\eta$, and $\widehat R$ is invertible. Define $\kappa=\|\widehat R^{-1}\|_\infty$ and report only when $\kappa\eta<1$.

$$\widehat Q=\Lambda(I-\widehat R^{-1}),\qquad \|\widehat Q-Q\|_\infty\le \varepsilon_Q:=\frac{\lambda_{\max}\kappa^2\eta}{1-\kappa\eta}.$$

Indeed, $R=\widehat R[I+\widehat R^{-1}(R-\widehat R)]$ gives $\|R^{-1}\|_\infty\le\kappa/(1-\kappa\eta)$ by a Neumann series. Apply the inverse-difference identity and submultiplicativity to obtain the displayed radius.

Although $\widehat Q\mathbf1=0$, estimated off-diagonal entries may be negative. Clip those entries to zero and reset the diagonal to obtain a generator $\overline Q$; its radius is $\varepsilon_Q+\|\overline Q-\widehat Q\|_\infty$, not $\varepsilon_Q$ alone. Singular estimates or a failed gate give abstention, not a model-validity test.

An explicit anytime input budget

Fix $0<\delta<1$ before sampling and count $N_{ij}(K)=\sum_{k0$ let $\widehat R_{ij}=N_{ij}/V_i$; otherwise set the row to the $i$th unit row and $\eta_i=2$. The uniform Dirichlet row mixture, for a candidate probability row $p$, is

$$M_{i,K}(p)=\frac{\Gamma(n)\prod_j\Gamma(N_{ij}+1)}{\Gamma(V_i+n)} \prod_jp_j^{-N_{ij}},\qquad H_i=\log(n/\delta)-\log M_{i,K}(\widehat R_i),$$ $$\eta_i=\min\!\left\{2,\sqrt{\frac{2H_i}{V_i}}\right\}\quad(V_i>0), \qquad\eta=\max_i\eta_i.$$

Use $0^0=1$ for empty cells; compute the log mixture with log-gamma functions. Since the empirical row maximizes the multinomial likelihood, $M_{i,K}(\widehat R_i)\le1$ and $H_i>0$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, conditional on each initial state, this $\eta$ bounds $\|\widehat R-R\|_\infty$ simultaneously for every integer $K\ge0$.

To prove this, fix a probability row $v$ and multiply $v_{Z_{k+1}}/R_{i,Z_{k+1}}$ only when $Z_k=i$. Given all labels and timestamps through event $k$, the next label has row law $R_{Z_k}$, so each factor has conditional mean one. Integrating over the uniform Dirichlet law gives $M_{i,K}(R_i)$, a nonnegative mean-one martingale.

Ville's inequality and a union bound over the $n$ rows give $M_{i,K}(R_i)\le n/\delta$ simultaneously, outside an event of probability at most $\delta$. On that event, $\log M_{i,K}(R_i)-\log M_{i,K}(\widehat R_i)=V_iD_{\rm KL}(\widehat R_i\|R_i)\le H_i$. Pinsker's inequality then gives the stated row radius; no independence between rows or binomial law conditional on random $V_i$ is assumed.

For completeness, aggregate coordinates where $\widehat R_{ij}\ge R_{ij}$ and apply the log-sum inequality to reduce Pinsker to binary relative entropy. Its curvature in the first argument is $1/[x(1-x)]\ge4$, proving $D_{\rm KL}(a\|b)\ge\tfrac12\|a-b\|_1^2$. This is the standard mixture-martingale route to confidence sequences; see Howard et al. (2021), Section 3.2.

What the guarantee permits

The same probability event covers every reported generator radius, including an event count selected using past labels and timestamps. This is simultaneous initial-state-conditional model confidence, not coverage conditional on reporting or on the entire realized clock schedule. It is not a conformal feature-conditional guarantee or a test of the assumed Cox observation mechanism.

For each fixed model, $R$ is strictly positive, all $V_i\to\infty$, and $\widehat R\to R$ almost surely. Stirling's formula gives $\log M_{i,K}(\widehat R_i)=-(n-1)\log V_i/2+O(1)$, so $\eta\to0$ and the gate eventually passes almost surely. Both the unprojected radius and the clipping cost tend to zero, but this is pointwise in $Q,\Lambda$, not a uniform event-count or calendar-time complexity bound.

The certificate checks 126 exact rational martingale means and 42 first-crossing laws, with maximum failure $0.013369900$ at $\delta=0.2$. It checks 28 killed kernels independently by quadrature and 84 perturbations, including 27 nonzero clipping costs and a defective generator. These finite checks audit the implementation, not the infinite-time proof.

For balanced transition counts with each row a cyclic permutation of $(900000,80000,20000)$, $\delta=0.05$ and $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,4)$, the budget is $\eta=0.006107436$, $\kappa=1.229453944$, the gate product is $0.007508811$, and $\varepsilon_Q=0.037206323$. The empirical inverse is already a generator; its physical stationary law is $(4/7,2/7,1/7)$ despite uniform event frequencies. These are prescribed feasible balanced counts, not a simulated coverage experiment.

For the directed three-state cyclic permutation matrix $C$, the opposite generators $Q_\pm=(4\pi/\sqrt3)(C^{\pm1}-I)$ have identical unit-spacing snapshot kernels: their nonzero eigenvalues are $-2\pi\sqrt3\pm2\pi i$, so both exponentials are scalar on the centered subspace. Their unit-rate Poisson kernels instead differ in row-sum norm by $0.080310240$. Randomizing a known clock removes that alias, but very fast chains can still make $R$ nearly singular and the finite-data inverse unstable.
