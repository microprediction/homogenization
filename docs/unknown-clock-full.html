Calibrating an unknown observation clock

Labeled states and one bounded waiting-time transform identify both the generator and the clock, with anytime confidence bounds.

Fix an irreducible row generator $Q$ on $n\ge2$ complete known labeled states. Conditional on the entire regime path $Y$, observations form a Poisson process of unknown fixed state-dependent intensity $\lambda_{Y_t}$, where $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)>0$, without feedback on $Y$. Observe $Z_k=Y_{T_k}$ and $\tau_{k+1}=T_{k+1}-T_k$ in physical-time units, starting with $T_0=0$ and any initial state.

Keep repeated equal labels and choose a discount $s>0$ before observing data. No clock bounds, generator-speed cap, stationary start or mixing constants are supplied. The Cox mechanism is assumed, not tested; adaptive intensities, noisy labels, rounded timestamps and incomplete state spaces are outside this result.

A constructive inverse using bounded moments

Let $R_{ij}=\Pr(Z_{k+1}=j\mid Z_k=i)$ and $f_i=\mathbb E[e^{-s\tau_{k+1}}\mid Z_k=i]$. The next-arrival density is $e^{t(Q-\Lambda)}\Lambda$, giving

$$R=(\Lambda-Q)^{-1}\Lambda,\qquad f=(sI+\Lambda-Q)^{-1}\Lambda\mathbf1,\qquad (R^{-1}+s\Lambda^{-1})f=\mathbf1.$$ $$d=\mathbf1-R^{-1}f,\qquad \lambda_i=\frac{s f_i}{d_i},\qquad Q=\Lambda(I-R^{-1}).$$

Strict diagonal dominance makes both resolvents invertible; also $00$. Thus one label-transition matrix and one bounded waiting-time-transform column identify $Q$ and $\Lambda$ globally, including defective and nonreversible cases. Without timestamps, replacing $(Q,\Lambda)$ by $(DQ,D\Lambda)$ for any positive diagonal $D$ preserves $R$, so labels alone leave rowwise speeds unidentified.

Identification with observable random sampling times is classical: Duffie and Glynn (2004), Section 4, Proposition 5 treats a more general setting. Here the inverse and its confidence propagation are derived explicitly for finite states, without a novelty claim for random-time identification. Bounded $e^{-s\tau}$ replaces a raw waiting-time mean, avoiding an assumed known waiting-time tail bound.

Deterministic clock and generator radii

Use the maximum absolute row-sum matrix norm and the maximum absolute coordinate vector norm. Suppose $\widehat R$ is stochastic, $\widehat f\in[0,1]^n$, and $\|\widehat R-R\|_\infty\le\eta$, $\|\widehat f-f\|_\infty\le\zeta$. If $\widehat R$ is singular, abstain; otherwise put

$$\widehat G=\widehat R^{-1},\quad \kappa=\|\widehat G\|_\infty,\quad E=\frac{\kappa^2\eta}{1-\kappa\eta},\quad \widehat d=\mathbf1-\widehat G\widehat f,\quad b=E+\kappa\zeta.$$

Require $\kappa\eta<1$, $\widehat d_i>b$ and $\widehat f_i>\zeta$ for every state, or abstain. A Neumann series gives $\|R^{-1}-\widehat G\|_\infty\le E$; since $\|f\|_\infty\le1$, $\|d-\widehat d\|_\infty\le E+\kappa\zeta=b$. Hence, defining the following coordinatewise intervals and point estimates,

$$L_i=\frac{s\max(0,\widehat f_i-\zeta)}{\widehat d_i+b},\quad U_i=\frac{s\min(1,\widehat f_i+\zeta)}{\widehat d_i-b},\quad \widehat\lambda_i=\frac{s\widehat f_i}{\widehat d_i},\qquad L_i\le\lambda_i\le U_i.$$ $$e_\Lambda=\max_i\max(\widehat\lambda_i-L_i,U_i-\widehat\lambda_i),\quad \widehat Q=\operatorname{diag}(\widehat\lambda)(I-\widehat G),\quad \|\widehat Q-Q\|_\infty\le e_\Lambda\|I-\widehat G\|_\infty+(\max_iU_i)E=:\varepsilon_Q.$$

The last bound follows by adding and subtracting $\Lambda(I-\widehat G)$. Clip negative off-diagonal entries of $\widehat Q$ and reset its diagonal to obtain a generator $\overline Q$; its radius is $\varepsilon_Q+\|\overline Q-\widehat Q\|_\infty$. No small off-diagonal inverse residual is treated as evidence that the observation model is correct.

The row selection is predictable in the filtration containing labels and timestamps through the previous event. Rewards from the next event have the same conditional means $R_{ij},f_i$ by the joint Markov property, but need not be independent across statistics or rows. The Gaussian-mixture method is standard; see Howard et al. (2021), Section 3.2.

The same event covers every reported clock interval and generator radius, including data-selected event counts based on past timestamps and labels. This is initial-state-conditional simultaneous model confidence, not coverage conditional on reporting, on the entire realized clock schedule, or on conformal features. For each fixed model, positivity and irreducibility imply consistent bounded row averages, $V_i\to\infty$, shrinking budgets and eventual gate passage almost surely; no uniform sample-count or calendar-time rate is claimed.

Focused certificate

The certificate enumerates exact finite Markov path laws and numerically evaluates 504 bounded-reward mixture means and 42 first-crossing probabilities. The largest first-crossing probability is $0.000013577$ at $\delta=0.2$; these generic bounded-reward checks audit the concentration mechanism, not the continuous waiting-time law. Independently, 84 continuous-time inverse/quadrature cases include a defective chain, with 283 perturbations certified, 53 abstentions and 97 nonzero clipping costs.

For prescribed balanced label-transition counts with each row a cyclic permutation of $(900000,80000,20000)$, $s=1$, $\delta=0.05$, and row transform means $(0.510124518,0.671894456,0.777527424)$, the input budgets are $\zeta=0.002418191$, $\eta=0.007254573$, and the inverse gate product is $0.008919164$. Clock intervals are $[0.968605827,1.033170853]$, $[1.912873121,2.094725519]$, and $[3.718989779,4.324756119]$; the generator radius is $0.122367343$. The point inverse recovers clock $(1,2,4)$; these are prescribed sufficient statistics, not a Monte Carlo coverage claim.
