Gauge-flat covariance

Directional asymmetry changes the Green–Kubo constant, not the logarithmic power.

Norm symmetry is unnecessary for the flat-map variance law. A continuous positive homogeneous gauge can penalize positive and negative excursions differently, and its easiest moderate-deviation direction still determines the leading constant.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain setup of the norm theorem, with centered $g:\mathsf S\to\mathbb R^d$, fixed $T>0$, occupation average $W_{m,T}$, and nonzero symmetric Green–Kubo matrix $\Gamma$. Let $\rho$ be continuous, positive away from zero, and satisfy $\rho(cw)=c\rho(w)$ for $c>0$.

Define

$$\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma) =\max_{\|z\|_2=1}\rho(\Gamma^{1/2}z)^2>0,$$

and, for $\sigma>1$, $b>0$ and $w\ne0$, put

$$\Phi_{\sigma,b,\rho}(w)=\exp\{-\exp(b\rho(w)^{-1/(\sigma-1)})\}, \qquad \Phi_{\sigma,b,\rho}(0)=0.$$

At every fixed $T>0$, uniformly over arbitrary initial regime priors,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Phi_{\sigma,b,\rho}(W_{m,T})) \sim\frac{b^{2(\sigma-1)}}{2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)} \frac{mT}{[\log(mT)]^{2(\sigma-1)}}.$$

Why symmetry is absent

Put $\beta=\sigma-1$ and $H=\operatorname{range}\Gamma$. The moderate-deviation rate is $J(y)=b^{2\beta}y^\top\Gamma^+y/2$ on $H$.

Positive homogeneity alone gives

$$\min_{y\in H:\,\rho(y)=1}J(y) =\frac{b^{2\beta}}2 \min_{z\in H:\,\rho(\Gamma^{1/2}z)=1}\|z\|_2^2 =\frac{b^{2\beta}}{2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)}.$$

Compactness supplies the maximizing direction. The shrinking-boundary and variance-separation arguments use only this contraction, so neither convexity nor the identity $\rho(-w)=\rho(w)$ is required.

Convex gauges and the polar formula

If $\rho$ is convex, let $\rho^\circ(\ell)=\sup_{\rho(w)\le1}\ell^\top w$. Bipolar duality gives $\rho(w)=\sup_{\rho^\circ(\ell)\le1}\ell^\top w$, hence

$$\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma) =\sup_{\rho^\circ(\ell)\le1}\ell^\top\Gamma\ell.$$

For $\rho(w)=\sum_i(a_i^+w_i^++a_i^-w_i^-)$, the polar unit ball is the asymmetric box $\prod_i[-a_i^-,a_i^+]$. The maximum of the positive-semidefinite quadratic occurs at a box vertex.

Coordinate and rank scope

Under $w'=Rw$, transport the gauge as $\rho'(w')=\rho(R^{-1}w')$ and take $\Gamma'=R\Gamma R^\top$. Then $\Lambda_{\rho'}(\Gamma')=\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)$.

This is a fixed-feature directional magnitude. It neither identifies a post-averaging nonlinear covariance rank nor combines maturity-dependent loadings; $\operatorname{rank}\Gamma$ remains only the active linear feature dimension.

Numerical certificate

For the nonreversible three-state two-feature chain, the certificate uses $a^+=(1,.7)$ and $a^-=(2,1.4)$. It finds $\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)=4.604379006$ at the polar vertex $(-2,-1.4)$ and predicted constant $.244332623$.

The exact two-dimensional Legendre rate is contracted over the asymmetric gauge boundary. At gauge radius $.005$, its quadratic normalization is $1.000143409$, while a non-orthogonal coordinate transport preserves the polar maximum to the printed precision.

Literature boundary

Gärtner (1977) supplies the spectral large-deviation principle, and Ney and Nummelin (1987) treat Markov additive processes. The asymmetric-gauge contraction and polar specialization are derived above.
