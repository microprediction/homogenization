Sparse six-state Green–Kubo cycle mixtures

Sharp repeated-mode bounds for sparse mixtures and reversible attachments.

Research note, 21 October 2026. This is a restricted architecture theorem, not a solution of the unrestricted minimum-state problem.

Result

Let a balanced stationary flow on six states be either a positive mixture of at most two simple directed cycles; an equal-weight mixture of three simple directed cycles; an arbitrary positive mixture of the three four-cycles displayed below; the sum of at most two genuinely directed simple cycles and an arbitrary nonnegative reversible stationary flow; or the sum of one directed simple cycle and an arbitrary nonnegative reversible stationary flow. Only the final support, not the directed base, is assumed connected.

Let $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge0$ be its two intrinsic energy-normalized skew frequencies. Then

$$\sigma_2\le\sqrt{\frac35}.$$

For two cycles the bound is attained at equal weights by, for example,

$$c_A=(0,5,2,3,4,1),\qquad c_B=(0,3,4,5,2,1),$$

whose union is neither a cactus nor translation invariant. Within this cone equality holds exactly when $a=b>0$, and then both frequencies equal $\sqrt{3/5}$.

The following genuinely three-cycle architecture permits arbitrary positive weights:

$$c_A=(0,1,2,3),\qquad c_B=(0,1,4,5),\qquad c_C=(2,3,4,5).$$

Equality within this weight cone occurs exactly when the three weights agree; its normalized pencil is then $t(t^2+3/5)^2$. A one-cycle irreducible flow must itself be Hamiltonian and also satisfies the bound.

The two-directed-cycle attachment class obeys the same sharp bound without counting or restricting its reversible edges. In particular, every arbitrary-weight three-cycle mixture containing a two-cycle is covered. The final, one-cycle attachment class obeys the stronger bound $\sigma_2\le1/\sqrt3<\sqrt{3/5}$.

Consequently no four-feature compression from any stated class realizes $I_4+\cot(\pi/5)\operatorname{diag}(J,J)$, where $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The target needs two frequencies $\cot(\pi/5)=\sqrt{1+2/\sqrt5}>\sqrt{3/5}$.

Stationary-flow coordinates

Any prescribed positive stationary law $\pi$ gives a generator through $q_{ij}=W_{ij}/\pi_i$ for $i\ne j$. Thus varying holding times or $\pi$ changes the generator but not the stationary-flow Laplacian

$$C=\operatorname{diag}(W{\bf1})-W.$$

Delete one row and column and write the grounded form as $\bar C=H+D$, with $H=(\bar C+\bar C^\top)/2$ and $D=(\bar C-\bar C^\top)/2$. Irreducibility makes $H$ positive definite.

The eigenvalues of $H^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2}$ are $\{\pm i\sigma_1,\pm i\sigma_2,0\}$. Hence

$$\frac{\det(tH-D)}{\det H}=t(t^2+\sigma_1^2)(t^2+\sigma_2^2).$$

Exact pencil calculation

Direct expansion of the five-dimensional determinant gives

$$\frac{\det(tH-D)}{\det H}=t\{t^4+S(a,b)t^2+P(a,b)\},$$ $$S(a,b)=\frac{2(5a^4+12a^3b+11a^2b^2+12ab^3+5b^4)}{3(a^2+3ab+b^2)^2},$$ $$P(a,b)=\left(\frac{a^2+ab+b^2}{a^2+3ab+b^2}\right)^2.$$

Put $x=ab/(a+b)^2\in[0,1/4]$. Then

$$S(x)=\frac{2(5-8x-3x^2)}{3(1+x)^2},\qquad P(x)=\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^2.$$

The squared frequencies are the roots of $f_x(z)=z^2-S(x)z+P(x)$. At the proposed sharp value,

$$f_x(3/5)=\frac{16(4x-1)}{25(1+x)}\le0.$$

Since $f_x$ is monic with two nonnegative real roots, $3/5$ lies between them. Therefore $\sigma_2^2\le3/5$.

Equality requires $x=1/4$, equivalently $a=b>0$. Substitution gives $t(t^2+3/5)^2$, proving both equality and multiplicity without a floating search.

Arbitrary weights on two cycles

There are 409 simple directed cycles on six labeled states, counted up to cyclic rotation. Relabel the first cycle canonically according to its length, from two through six; retaining connected unions leaves 1,372 relative pairs and 133 distinct exact coefficient triples.

For each pencil write $r=b/a=s/(1-s)$ with $0\le s\le1$, and let $f(z)=z^2-Sz+P$ be its squared-frequency polynomial. Exact rational arithmetic covers $[0,1]$ by at most six dyadic intervals, on each of which Bernstein coefficients certify either

$$S\le\frac65\qquad\text{or}\qquad f(3/5)\le0.$$

The first inequality gives $\sigma_2^2\le S/2\le3/5$. In the second case $3/5$ lies between the two nonnegative roots, so again $\sigma_2^2\le3/5$.

Equal weights on three cycles

If a cycle is repeated, its copies merge and the preceding arbitrary-weight result applies. For three distinct cycles, fixing the first cycle canonically by its length and leaving the other two unordered gives 382,466 connected representatives.

For each representative, integer arithmetic evaluates $\det(t(2H)-2D)$ at $t=1,2,3$ and recovers its coefficients $a_5t^5+a_3t^3+a_1t$. The cases reduce to 3,207 normalized pairs $(S,P)=(a_3/a_5,a_1/a_5)$.

Every pair satisfies at least one of $S\le6/5$ or $P-(3/5)S+9/25\le0$. The same root argument proves the bound; four first-cycle-normalized representatives attain equality, including the displayed triple.

One full three-cycle weight cone

Give the displayed cycles weights $a,b,c>0$, and put

$$e_1=a+b+c,\qquad e_2=ab+ac+bc,\qquad e_3=abc.$$

Writing the exact pencil as $\det(tH-D)=a_5t^5+a_3t^3+a_1t$, direct polynomial expansion gives

$$8a_5=e_2(3e_1e_2-2e_3),\qquad 4a_3=e_2(2e_1e_2-3e_3),\qquad 8a_1=e_1e_2^2.$$

For $f(z)=z^2-(a_3/a_5)z+a_1/a_5$, the root barrier at $3/5$ therefore factors as

$$f(3/5)=-\frac{e_2(e_1e_2-9e_3)}{25a_5}\le0.$$

Indeed $e_1\ge3e_3^{1/3}$ and $e_2\ge3e_3^{2/3}$ by AM–GM, so $e_1e_2\ge9e_3$. Equality holds only when $a=b=c$. Thus $3/5$ lies between the two nonnegative squared frequencies, proving the bound throughout this two-dimensional positive weight cone and identifying its unique weight ratio for equality.

Reversible attachments cannot amplify skew

This extension is not limited by the number of reversible two-cycles. Let $H_0,D$ be the grounded symmetric and skew forms of any balanced directed component, and add an arbitrary reversible stationary flow with grounded form $R\succeq0$. Then $D$ is unchanged and $H=H_0+R$.

Assume $H$ is positive definite and $\ker H_0\subseteq\ker D$, as holds for a directed cycle on any subset of the states.

Using the Moore–Penrose inverse on the range of $H_0$, put

$$K_0=H_0^{\dagger/2}DH_0^{\dagger/2},\qquad C=H^{-1/2}H_0^{1/2}.$$

The kernel condition gives $D=H_0^{1/2}K_0H_0^{1/2}$, while $H_0\preceq H$ gives $CC^\top\preceq I$. Therefore

$$H^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2}=CK_0C^\top,\qquad s_j(CK_0C^\top)\le\lVert C\rVert^2s_j(K_0)\le s_j(K_0).$$

Thus every intrinsic skew frequency contracts under reversible attachments. A directed cycle of length $\ell$ has positive Fourier frequencies

$$\cot\frac{\pi}{\ell},\ \cot\frac{2\pi}{\ell},\ldots, \cot\frac{\lfloor(\ell-1)/2\rfloor\pi}{\ell}.$$

No connectivity assumption on the directed component is needed. If its active support is connected and has six states, the exact classification above gives $s_2(K_0)\le\sqrt{3/5}$.

On five active states the certificate repeats the exact pencil argument over 84 simple cycles, 232 connected pairs and 41 distinct pencils. On at most four active states the centered space has dimension at most three, so there is at most one positive skew frequency.

If the two directed-cycle supports are disconnected, they are disjoint. The six-state budget leaves only the potentially two-mode split $3+3$, whose second frequency is $\cot(\pi/3)=1/\sqrt3$; a $2+4$ split has only one positive frequency.

Thus every two-cycle base has $s_2(K_0)\le\sqrt{3/5}$, and contraction proves the bound after adding an arbitrary reversible stationary flow. Reversible two-cycles do not consume the two genuinely directed-cycle slots.

There is a stronger specialization when the directed component is just one cycle. For $\ell\le6$, its second frequency is zero for $\ell\le4$, equals $\cot(2\pi/5)$ for $\ell=5$, and equals $\cot(\pi/3)=1/\sqrt3$ for $\ell=6$. Hence any connected six-state flow with only one genuinely directed cycle, plus any number and arrangement of reversible edges, satisfies the claimed stronger bound, even when those edges are needed to connect a shorter cycle to the remaining states.

Why feature choice cannot evade the bound

Let $U$ collect any four independent Poisson potentials and normalize their symmetric energy. The resulting feature skew matrix is the compression

$$X^\top(iH^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2})X,\qquad X^\top X=I_4.$$

Hermitian compression interlacing, in the form proved by Fan and Pall (1957), bounds its second positive eigenvalue by $\sigma_2$. This is a necessary condition for a fixed-parameter Green–Kubo block; it is not a rank claim after integrating different maturity loadings.

The target has symmetric part $I_4$ and two positive skew frequencies equal to $\cot(\pi/5)$. The strict comparison above therefore excludes every generator in any stated sparse-cycle class, for every positive stationary law and every four-feature embedding.

Certificate and scope

The certificate enumerates all 409 simple cycles, 1,372 first-cycle-normalized connected pairs and 382,466 connected distinct equal-weight triples. It constructs 133 exact two-cycle coefficient triples and 3,207 normalized three-cycle coefficient pairs, then proves the alternatives above by rational Bernstein bounds and integer determinants. It also expands the displayed unequal-weight three-cycle pencil symbolically and verifies all three coefficient identities as exact bivariate polynomials.

It separately verifies the displayed two-cycle extremal formula and checks 300 independently chosen rates and stationary laws against the full whitened skew spectrum. Four directed-cycle Fourier spectra, 240 one-cycle attachments, 240 connected two-cycle attachments and 120 disconnected-base attachments check the contraction theorem's implementation.

The portable assertion ceilings are $2\times10^{-11}$ for the spectrum formula and $2\times10^{-10}$ for stationarity. These floating checks test the implementation; the architecture-wide inequality is the analytic determinant argument above.

This result does not prove that $\sqrt{3/5}$ is the largest repeated frequency among all six-state balanced flows. Unequal positive weights on other architectures with three genuinely directed cycles, general flows with four or more genuinely directed cycles, and unrestricted state counts six through nine remain open.
