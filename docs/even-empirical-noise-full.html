Smooth even-critical empirical calibration at every noise scale

Only two Taylor coefficients survive: remote score branches are exponentially invisible, while the first odd term couples to latent-start memory.

Model and coefficient

Fix a finite irreducible row generator $Q$, stationary row $\pi$, nonconstant real $f$ with $\pi f=0$, and a fixed even degree $d=2k\ge2$. Define, using pointwise products,

$$h=Q^\#f,\quad B=2Q^\#(fh),\quad\Gamma=-2\pi(fh)>0,\quad\kappa_3=3\pi(fB),\qquad a_i=-B_i/\Gamma,\quad e_i=h_i\kappa_3/(3\Gamma^2).$$

Here $B$ is a raw second-moment boundary correction around the common stationary mean, not a variance contrast after recentering each state. Let $\psi$ be $C^{d+2}$ on the range of $f$, with

$$\psi(0)=0,\qquad\psi(a)=\mu a^d+\nu a^{d+1}+O(a^{d+2}),\qquad \mu>0,\qquad \psi(a)>0\quad(a\ne0).$$

The last condition only isolates zero as the unique minimum on the compact occupation range. The score may have additional turning points away from zero; the Taylor expansion already makes its two local branches monotone near zero.

$$A_c={1\over c}\int_0^c f(Y_t)dt,\quad X_c=\sqrt{c/\Gamma}A_c,\quad S_c=\psi(A_c)+\sigma Z,\quad\lambda={c^k\sigma\over\mu\Gamma^k},\qquad\sigma\ge0.$$

Use $N$ independent stationary calibration paths and an independent test path starting in state $i$, with independent standard Gaussian noises. For $1\le j\le N$, let $C_i(c,N,j,\lambda)$ be test coverage below the $j$th calibration score, averaged over panels. At zero noise, order pairs $(S,V)$ lexicographically using an independent uniform tie variable for every calibration and test observation.

Then $p_N=j/(N+1)$ is the exact marginal baseline: $\sum_i\pi_i C_i=p_N$. Define $\tau_i=2\nu h_i/(d\mu)$, $\widetilde a_i=a_i+3\tau_i$ and $\widetilde e_i=e_i+\tau_i$.

For $x>0$, set $r=x^{1/d}$ and define

$$P_i^{(d)}(x)=\phi(r)[\widetilde a_i r+\widetilde e_i(r^3-3r)],\quad P_i^{(d)}(x)=0\ (x\le0),\qquad K_{i,\lambda}^{(d)}(t)=\mathbb E P_i^{(d)}(t-\lambda Z).$$

With $U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(j,N+1-j)$ and $t_u^{(d)}(\lambda)$ the reference quantile of $G^d+\lambda Z$, put $\mathcal K_i^{N,j,d}(\lambda)=\mathbb E_U K_{i,\lambda}^{(d)}(t_U^{(d)}(\lambda))$. Here $G,Z$ are independent standard Gaussians.

$$\boxed{C_i-p_N={\mathcal K_i^{N,j,d}(\lambda)\over c}+R_{i,c,N,j}^{(d)}(\lambda),\qquad \sup_{N\ge1,\,1\le j\le N,\,i,\,\lambda\ge0}c(1+\lambda)|R_{i,c,N,j}^{(d)}(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0.}$$

This is an absolute remainder theorem for fixed $Q,f,d$, allowing arbitrarily fast $N=N_c$ growth and extreme ranks. It is not relative accuracy when the coefficient vanishes, nor coverage conditional on a realized panel or on test features. It extends the population even-power theorem to empirical calibration and smooth even-critical score maps.

Finite error bound

Set $q_c(x)=c^k\psi(\sqrt{\Gamma/c}\,x)/(\mu\Gamma^k)$ and write $F_{i,c}^{(d)}(x)=\Pr_i(q_c(X_c)\le x)$ and $F_{\pi,c}^{(d)}=\sum_i\pi_i F_{i,c}^{(d)}$. The pooled reference error and signed errors are

$$E_c=\|F_{\pi,c}^{(d)}-F_{G^d}\|_\infty,\quad J_{i,c}^{(d)}=c(F_{i,c}^{(d)}-F_{\pi,c}^{(d)}),\quad U_{i,c}=\|J_{i,c}^{(d)}-P_i^{(d)}\|_\infty,\quad L_{i,d,c}=\int_0^\infty|J_{i,c}^{(d)}-P_i^{(d)}|dx.$$

Let $m_{2r}=\mathbb E G^{2r}=(2r-1)!!$, including $m_0=1$, and set

$$V_{i,d}(g)=(\widetilde a_i-3\widetilde e_i)g^d+\widetilde e_i g^{d+2},\quad M_{i,d}=\max\{\sqrt{2\widetilde a_i^2+24\widetilde e_i^2},\ d\sqrt{\mathbb E V_{i,d}(G)^2}\},$$ $$\mathbb E V_{i,d}(G)^2=(\widetilde a_i-3\widetilde e_i)^2m_{2d}+2\widetilde e_i(\widetilde a_i-3\widetilde e_i)m_{2d+2}+\widetilde e_i^2m_{2d+4}.$$

At every finite $c,N$ and every noise level,

$$\boxed{c(1+\lambda)|R_{i,c,N,j}^{(d)}(\lambda)|\le (1+\lambda)\min\left(U_{i,c},{L_{i,d,c}\over\lambda\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)+M_{i,d}\sqrt{E_c}.}$$

Omit the second minimum entry at $\lambda=0$. The first term controls the signed error at the actual threshold; the second transfers the reference coefficient without any $N$ or $j$ factor.

Hermite probability-coordinate lemma

Put $\rho=\lambda/(1+\lambda)$, $s=1-\rho$, and let $F_\rho$ be the CDF of $Y_\rho=sG^d+\rho Z$. Define $H_{i,\rho}(t)=s^{-1}K_{i,\lambda}^{(d)}(t/s)$ for $\rho<1$, and let $W_i(g)=\widetilde a_i(g^2-1)+\widetilde e_i(g^4-6g^2+3)$.

The elementary identity $(\phi H_n)'=-\phi H_{n+1}$, for probabilists' Hermite polynomials, gives

$$H_{i,\rho}(t)=-{1\over2s}\mathbb E[W_i(G)\mathbf1_{\{Y_\rho\le t\}}].$$

For $\rho\le1/2$ and $D=\{x<Y_\rho\le y\}$, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the increment by $\|W_i(G)\|_2\sqrt{\Pr(D)}$. Hermite orthogonality gives $\|W_i(G)\|_2^2=2\widetilde a_i^2+24\widetilde e_i^2$.

For $\rho\ge1/2$, integration by parts in $G$ instead gives

$$H_{i,\rho}(t)={d\over2\rho}\mathbb E[V_{i,d}(G)\phi((t-sG^d)/\rho)],\quad H_{i,\rho}(y)-H_{i,\rho}(x)=-{d\over2\rho}\mathbb E[V_{i,d}(G)Z\mathbf1_D].$$

These formulas extend to $\rho=1$, with $H_{i,1}(t)=(d/2)\mathbb E V_{i,d}(G)\phi(t)$. Independence and Cauchy–Schwarz therefore prove the global bound

$$|H_{i,\rho}(y)-H_{i,\rho}(x)|\le M_{i,d}\sqrt{|F_\rho(y)-F_\rho(x)|},\qquad0\le\rho\le1.$$

At $\rho=0$, the reference CDF is flat on the negative half-line, but the profile is identically zero there too. Thus no inverse-density assumption is needed at the folded endpoint or in the tails.

Empirical threshold transfer, including atoms

Let $\widehat F_c$ be the pooled CDF of $s q_c(X_c)+\rho Z$. Conditioning on $Z$ proves $\|\widehat F_c-F_\rho\|_\infty\le E_c$. At the actual augmented $j$th calibration pair $(q,V)$,

$$U=\widehat F_c(q-)+V[\widehat F_c(q)-\widehat F_c(q-)]\sim\operatorname{Beta}(j,N+1-j),\qquad |F_\rho(q)-U|\le E_c.$$

Apply the profile lemma pointwise to $q$ and $F_\rho^{-1}(U)$ and then average over $U$. This costs at most $M_{i,d}\sqrt{E_c}$ regardless of the beta parameters. Zero-noise atoms at zero or in the tails therefore require no separate rank-overshoot allowance for the specified randomized rule.

Convolving the signed CDF error gives the first term of the finite bound by supremum contraction and the Gaussian density maximum. At a tied threshold, the augmented contrast is a convex combination of left and right CDF contrasts; continuity of $P_i^{(d)}$ preserves the same bound. Independence of the test path lets us subtract the exact pooled rank baseline before averaging.

Finite-chain input and endpoints

For this fixed model and score, $E_c=O(c^{-1/2})$, $U_{i,c}=o(1)$ and $L_{i,d,c}\to0$. Here is the spectral mechanism, with full Fourier and moment budgets in the population note.

$$\Lambda(z)=\Gamma z^2/2+\kappa_3z^3/6+O(z^4),\qquad (\delta_i-\pi)\Pi(z)\mathbf1=-h_i z+B_i z^2/2+O(z^3),$$

where $\delta_i$ is the point-mass row at state $i$.

For the pure even power, the real part of the Fourier transform cancels the order-$c^{-1/2}$ odd term. Its next signed density folds to the profile with coefficients $(a_i,e_i)$.

Taylor expansion gives

$$q_c(x)=x^d+{\nu\sqrt\Gamma\over\mu\sqrt c}x^{d+1}+{x^{d+2}\over c}R_c(x),$$

where $R_c$ is uniformly bounded on the normalized support of $X_c$. On the central window $|x|\le\sqrt{K\log c}$, the two roots of $q_c(x)=r^d$ therefore satisfy

$$x_+(r)=r-{\nu\sqrt\Gamma\over d\mu\sqrt c}r^2+O(c^{-1}r^3),\qquad x_-(r)=-r-{\nu\sqrt\Gamma\over d\mu\sqrt c}r^2+O(c^{-1}r^3).$$

The one-sided signed CDF has first term $(h_i/\sqrt\Gamma)\phi(x)/\sqrt c$. Evaluating it at these displaced roots adds $\tau_i r^3\phi(r)/c$, exactly the change $(a_i,e_i)\mapsto(\widetilde a_i,\widetilde e_i)$.

The bounded $R_c$ term moves each local root only by $O(c^{-1}r^3)$, so its interaction with the one-sided contrast is $o(c^{-1})$ after central truncation. This proves that no Taylor coefficient beyond $\nu$ enters the displayed profile near the minimum.

Choose $\delta>0$ so that these are the only score branches on $|a|<\delta$. Compactness and the unique-minimum condition give $\inf_{|a|\ge\delta}\psi(a)>0$, while a tilted-generator Chernoff bound gives $\max_i\Pr_i(|A_c|\ge\delta)\le C e^{-\eta c}$.

Discarding every extra branch therefore changes $E_c$ and $U_{i,c}$ exponentially, and changes the weighted norm $L_{i,d,c}$ by at most $O(c^{k+1}e^{-\eta c})$ because $0\le q_c\le O(c^k)$. This localization is uniform in the empirical threshold, so even arbitrarily large panels and extreme ranks cannot revive the remote branches.

Signed Fourier smoothing gives a uniform $O(c^{-3/2})$ symmetrized CDF remainder. For nonzero frequencies the Dirichlet form excludes imaginary-axis eigenvalues; at large frequencies proper transient feature-level killed blocks and unitary Schur bounds give uniform polynomial-times-exponential decay, including defective generators.

Real tilted-semigroup Chernoff bounds and truncation at $R_c=\sqrt{K\log c}$ prove the weighted $L^1$ limit for each fixed $d$. No discrete-time nonlattice assumption is imposed. Since the first term of the finite bound is at most $2\max(U_{i,c},L_{i,d,c}/\sqrt{2\pi})$, the uniform theorem follows.

In the pure-power case $\nu=0$, a finite moment-tail budget for any $R>0$ and integer $\ell\ge1$ is

$$L_{i,d,c}\le R^dU_{i,c}+{c\over R^{2\ell}}[\mathbb E_i|X_c|^{d+2\ell}+\mathbb E_\pi|X_c|^{d+2\ell}] +\int_R^\infty d r^{d-1}|P_i^{(d)}(r^d)|dr.$$

Moments come from $v_m'=Qv_m+m\operatorname{diag}(f)v_{m-1}$ with $v_0=\mathbf1$, $v_m(0)=0$ for $m>0$.

$$J_{N,j}=\mathbb E\phi(\Phi^{-1}(U)),\quad \widetilde\Theta_{i,d}={d\over2}m_d[\widetilde a_i+(d-2)\widetilde e_i] =\Theta_{i,d}+(d+1)m_d{\nu h_i\over\mu},\quad \lambda\mathcal K_i^{N,j,d}(\lambda)\longrightarrow\widetilde\Theta_{i,d}J_{N,j}\quad(\lambda\to\infty).$$

This endpoint statement fixes $N,j$. In particular, for fixed positive $\sigma$, $c^{k+1}\sigma(C_i-p_N)\to\mu\Gamma^k\widetilde\Theta_{i,d}J_{N,j}$.

The asymmetric increment is $(d+1)!!\nu h_i\Gamma^k$, which independently equals minus the leading start contrast in $\nu\mathbb E A_c^{d+1}$ and fixes the sign. When $\nu=0$, zero-noise ranks reduce exactly to ranks of $|A_c|$ and are independent of the even degree; that finite-sample identity is not claimed when $\nu\ne0$.

For the large-noise endpoint, normalized reference quantiles converge to $\Phi^{-1}(u)$ for every $0<u<1$. The integration-by-parts representation bounds $H_{i,\rho}$ uniformly, so dominated convergence justifies beta averaging without discarding its tails.

Checks, literature and limits

The certificate checks 36 nonreversible three-state empirical-rank cases for $\psi(a)=a^d[(1-1.2a)^2+.02]$ at $c=32,128,512$, degrees $2,4,6$, four noise levels and $N=j=9$. This score stays positive away from zero but has two additional turning points for every tested degree; the inversion sums all monotonicity intervals rather than assuming two global branches.

The worst weighted error falls from $1.09848274$ to $.0615449815$; the worst score Kolmogorov error falls from $.0184408825$ to $.00119445801$. Joint Fourier/spatial/beta-order refinement is $1.49\times10^{-4}$, within a portable $3\times10^{-4}$ allowance; 18 Hermite representations, six probability-modulus inequalities and 300 exact pure-power rank patterns also pass.

These Fourier and quadrature calculations are numerical consistency checks, not interval enclosures or a proof of continuum uniformity.

The Hermite derivative identity is classical DLMF 18.9.28, and the randomized probability transform is classical Rüschendorf (2009). The occupation-tail localization uses the additive-functional Feynman–Kac formula of Fitzsimmons and Pitman (1999).
