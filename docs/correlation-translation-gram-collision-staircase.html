Rare-tail Gram collisions form an integer staircase

At $\delta\asymp\varepsilon^m$, the tail contrast meets the $m$th post-tail Gaussian mode.

The first two tail/Gaussian collisions are not isolated coincidences. Every integer power of the exponent spacing is a boundary, with separated Gaussian modes above it and one explicit rank-two block at it.

Collision-staircase theorem

Fix an integer $m\ge1$, $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, $1\le r\le k-m-3$, a slope $\tau>0$, and weights $q_1,q_2>0$. Put $Q=q_1+q_2$, $\gamma_1=-q_2/Q$, and $\gamma_2=q_1/Q$.

Let $\varepsilon_n,\delta_n\downarrow0$ satisfy $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n^m\to\rho\in(0,\infty)$. Use the Gaussian-plus-two-atom triangular law

$$\tau_{\ell n}=\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n,\qquad A_{\ell n}=\exp(\tau_{\ell n}/\varepsilon_n),\qquad p_{\ell n}=q_\ell\varepsilon_n^{2r}A_{\ell n}^{-2c},$$

and let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$.

Write $\phi_a=(x_j^a/a!)_j$, $v=(e^{\tau x_j})_j$, and $\dot v=(x_je^{\tau x_j})_j$. For $s\ge0$, let $P_s$ project orthogonally away from

$$L_s=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+s},v\}.$$

For $G\sim N(0,1)$, center $Z_j=|G|^c(\log|G|)^j$ and let $h_j$ be the residual variance of $Z_j$ after projection onto $Z_0,\ldots,Z_{j-1}$.

Each of the $m-1$ intervening Gaussian modes stays separated:

$$\lambda_{r+2+a}(K_n) =h_{r+a}\|P_{a-1}\phi_{r+a}\|^2\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)}),\qquad 1\le a<m.$$

At the boundary, put $g_m=P_{m-1}\phi_{r+m}$, $b_m=P_{m-1}\dot v$, and

$$M_{m,\rho}=h_{r+m}g_mg_m^\top+\frac{q_1q_2}{Q}\rho^2b_mb_m^\top.$$

This matrix has rank two. If $\nu_1\ge\nu_2>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+m+1+j}(K_n) =\nu_j\varepsilon_n^{2(r+m)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+m)}),\qquad j=1,2.$$

The first $r$ Gaussian coefficients and two primary order-$\varepsilon_n^{2r}$ coefficients are unchanged. Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+m)})$.

Proof

Successive Schur complements remove $L_0,L_1,\ldots,L_{m-1}$. At step $a<m$, Gaussian Gram–Schmidt leaves $\varepsilon_n^{r+a}\sqrt{h_{r+a}}\,P_{a-1}\phi_{r+a}$.

The tail contrast has feature size $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n$. Relative to the step-$a$ Gaussian feature its size is asymptotic to $\rho\varepsilon_n^{m-a}$, so it is negligible for every $a<m$.

At step $m$, Gaussian Gram–Schmidt leaves $\varepsilon_n^{r+m}\sqrt{h_{r+m}}\,g_m$, while weighted label centering leaves $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n\sqrt{q_1q_2/Q}\,b_m$. Their orthogonal latent coordinates give the two summands of $M_{m,\rho}$.

Higher Gaussian features are smaller by powers of $\varepsilon_n$, and higher tail-confluence terms are smaller by powers of $\delta_n$. Finite-dimensional spectral perturbation therefore gives all displayed limits and the remainder statement.

The vectors $g_m,b_m$ are independent. Otherwise a nonzero combination of $1,x,\ldots,x^{r+m},e^{\tau x},xe^{\tau x}$ would vanish at $k\ge r+m+3$ distinct nodes, contradicting the extended Chebyshev property.

Third-collision certificate

Take $m=3$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=q_1=1$, $q_2=2$, $x=(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3)$, $\rho=3/2$, and $\varepsilon_n=1/n$. The separated fourth- and sixth-order coefficients are

$$(0.158456045596511,0.030054217288597),$$

and the two critical eighth-order coefficients are

$$(\nu_1,\nu_2)=(0.098789647380103,0.000079884359623).$$

At $n=2048$, the exact critical ratios are $(0.999479318458,1.002655554111)$. The 240-digit certificate checks every displayed scale, monotone critical convergence, positivity, and full confluent rank.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for the displayed triangular laws and nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It treats each fixed integer $m$ and fixed $\rho$, not a finite-$n$ error bound or a statement uniform in $m$ or moving $\rho$.

The result concerns the output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies the classical column-confluence geometry, Kato supplies finite-dimensional self-adjoint perturbation theory, and Karlin and Studden supply the zero-count argument.
