Two ordered window covariances identify any finite generator

With known stationary law and complete state features, two ordered lag-covariance matrices of equal-window occupation totals recover every finite irreducible generator exactly. Any fixed positive window length works; no long-window limit, reversibility or diagonalizability is required.

Data from three consecutive windows

Let $Q$ be an irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ states, with known positive stationary law $\pi$ and time unit. Put $D_\pi=\operatorname{diag}\pi$ and choose a known complete centered basis $U\in\mathbb R^{n\times(n-1)}$ with $\pi U=0$ and $U^TD_\pi U=I$.

Start the chain in $\pi$ and, for fixed $T>0$, define the column vectors

$$X_j=\int_{jT}^{(j+1)T}U^Te_{Y_t}\,dt,\quad j=0,1,2,\qquad J_0=\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_1),\quad J_1=\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_2).$$

Rows refer to the earlier window, columns to the later window. Neither matrix should be symmetrized. A different known complete feature basis can be converted using its stationary feature Gram.

Exact inversion without a matrix logarithm

All matrices in this section act on the centered coordinates. Write $L=-U^TD_\pi QU$, $H=L^{-1}$, and $E=e^{-TL}$.

$$J_0=H^2(I-E)^2,\qquad J_1=J_0E.$$
$$E=J_0^{-1}J_1,\qquad Z=(I-E)^2J_0^{-1}=L^2,\qquad \boxed{Q=-U\,Z_{\rm pr}^{1/2}\,U^TD_\pi.}$$

Here $Z_{\rm pr}^{1/2}$ is the principal matrix square root, not a symmetric positive-semidefinite root or Cholesky factor. The data determine the generator itself, including cycle orientation, not merely its reversal class.

Proof and branch selection

For $u\ge s$, stationarity gives the ordered feature covariance $U^TD_\pi e^{(u-s)Q}U=e^{-(u-s)L}$. Changing variables $v=T-s$ and $w=u-T$ in the adjacent-window double integral gives

$$J_0=\int_0^T\int_0^T e^{-(v+w)L}\,dv\,dw =\left(\int_0^T e^{-vL}\,dv\right)^2=H^2(I-E)^2.$$

Separating the windows by one additional interval inserts the factor $e^{-TL}$, proving $J_1=J_0E$. These are exact finite-window identities, not mixing approximations.

Every eigenvalue of $L$ has positive real part, so $H$ and $I-E$ are invertible and $J_0$ is nonsingular. Moreover $L^2$ has no eigenvalue on the nonpositive real axis, and $L$ is its unique square root with spectrum in the open right half-plane. Thus the principal root equals $L$, including for defective generators.

Since $Q\mathbf1=0$ and $\pi Q=0$, lifting its centered restriction gives $Q=-ULU^TD_\pi$. No choice among matrix-logarithm branches remains: the window-covariance amplitude determines $L^2$ in addition to the sampled semigroup $E$.

An exact snapshot alias that ordered windows resolve

For uniform $\pi$ on three states, put $P=I-\mathbf1\pi$ and

$$C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q_\pm=-\frac32P\pm\frac12C,\quad T=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3},\quad \epsilon=e^{-\pi\sqrt3}.$$

These are the unit directed three-cycle and its reversal. Both have exactly the same snapshot transition matrix $e^{TQ_\pm}=\mathbf1\pi-\epsilon P$, and hence identical snapshot laws at every integer multiple of $T$.

Nevertheless their adjacent-window covariance operators in raw state coordinates differ by

$$U(J_{0,+}-J_{0,-})U^TD_\pi=\frac{(1+\epsilon)^2}{3}C.$$

Indeed $H_\pm=P/2\pm C/6$, so $H_+^2-H_-^2=C/3$. Numerically $T=3.6275987285$, $\epsilon=0.0043334205$, and the operator's $(1,2)$ difference is $0.3362285399$. The two-matrix inversion recovers the correct orientation for both chains.

Verification, literature and scope

The certificate uses independent piecewise Feynman–Kac mixed derivatives to construct the observed matrices. It checks 120 reconstructions across 30 chains on two to seven states, including nonuniform laws, sparse directed cycles, a defective generator and a five-state occupation-law-equivalent pair.

Three independent double-quadrature calculations cross-check the ordered integral identities. These floating-point checks are not interval certificates or uniform numerical-error bounds.

The principal-root theorem and its branch condition are classical; see Higham (2008), Theorem 1.29 and Chapter 6. The reconstruction is a direct combination of stationary semigroup integration and that theorem.

This result assumes exact population covariances, known stationary law, known state labels, time unit and complete fixed features. It does not identify an unknown hidden representation or apply to a matrix formed by integrating different maturity loadings.

No finite-sample estimation guarantee is claimed: $J_0^{-1}$, the subtraction $I-E$ and the matrix square root can be ill-conditioned. Noisy matrices need not admit this model or the required square-root branch. Symmetrized covariances and one-horizon occupation totals discard ordering and are different data.
