An occupation-prior fraction accurate at every horizon

The stationary averaged fraction can fail inside the initial switching layer. Replacing its stationary weights by the expected occupation weights gives a constant fraction with an explicit $O(1/m)$ optimizer error and $O(1/m^2)$ certainty-equivalent-rate loss, uniformly over every positive horizon and every starting prior.

Setup and result

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, $m>0$, with $Q$ an irreducible finite row generator. Assume common constant $r$, excess returns $b_i$, variances $s_i\ge s_{\min}>0$, positive risk aversion $\gamma$, $p=1-\gamma$, and Brownian stock noise independent of $Y$.

Hold one deterministic unrestricted fraction $w$ throughout the horizon and maximize

$$h_i(w)=r+b_iw-\gamma s_iw^2/2,\quad q_i(w)=b_i-\gamma ws_i,\quad J_{m,T,\nu}(w)=\frac{\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{p\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)dt}}{pT}.$$

At $\gamma=1$, use the expected log-wealth rate $J=\mathbb E_\nu\int_0^T h(Y_t)dt/T$. Every global maximizer lies in $I=[\min_i b_i/(\gamma s_i),\max_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)]$: $J'$ has the strict inward signs outside $I$, and $J(w)\le r+\max_i|b_i||w|-\gamma s_{\min}w^2/2$ gives existence.

If $I$ is a singleton, its fraction is exactly optimal; otherwise define

$$B=\max_{w\in I}\operatorname{osc}q(w),\qquad D=\max_{w\in I}\operatorname{osc}h(w),\qquad\delta=|p|D.$$

These finite maxima use endpoints for $B$ and endpoints plus interior critical points of the quadratic differences for $D$. Choose any fixed unscaled block duration $\theta>0$ and any rigorously valid $a_0>0$ satisfying

$$P=e^{\theta Q},\qquad 0<a_0\le\min_j\frac{\min_iP_{ij}}{\max_iP_{ij}}.$$

Irreducibility makes $P$ strictly positive even if some direct rates vanish. Nonnegative uniformization terms and a bounded factorial tail can certify $a_0$; a floating matrix exponential alone does not.

Write $c=mT$ and compute the unweighted expected occupation prior and its quadratic optimum

$$\rho_c=\frac1c\nu\int_0^c e^{tQ}dt,\qquad w_c=\frac{\rho_cb}{\gamma\rho_cs}\in I,\qquad E_{m,T}=\min\left\{\frac{B\delta T}{4},\frac{B}{2a_0}(e^{\delta\theta/m}-1)\right\}.$$
$$|J'(w)-\rho_cq(w)|\le E_{m,T}\quad(w\in I),$$ $$|w_*-w_c|\le\frac{E_{m,T}}{\gamma\rho_cs},\qquad 0\le J(w_*)-J(w_c)\le\frac{E_{m,T}^2}{2\gamma\rho_cs}.$$

These are exact finite-speed bounds for every $m,T>0$, every prior and every global maximizer $w_*$; uniqueness is not assumed. For $m>d:=\delta\theta$, the horizon-independent score budget is at most $Bd/[2a_0(m-d)]$, proving the uniform orders using $\rho_cs\ge s_{\min}$.

For a stationary start, $\rho_c=\pi$ at every horizon, so the stationary averaged fraction itself has this uniform guarantee. For an arbitrary prior, $\rho_c$ retains the initial layer; for log utility $\delta=0$, the occupation fraction is exactly optimal at every speed and horizon.

Every interpolated tilted chain mixes in blocks

Fix $w\in I$ and interpolate the full path tilt by $e^{\lambda p\int_0^T h(Y_t)dt}$, $0\le\lambda\le1$. Put $A_\lambda=mQ+\lambda pD_h$, $z_\lambda(u)=e^{uA_\lambda}\mathbf1$ and $\tau=\theta/m$.

The exact finite-horizon kernel is $K^{\lambda,T}_{s,t}=D_{z_\lambda(T-s)}^{-1}e^{(t-s)A_\lambda}D_{z_\lambda(T-t)}$. Its initial law is $\nu_i z_{\lambda,i}(T)/(\nu z_\lambda(T))$, not generally $\nu$.

For a complete block, let $H=e^{\tau A_\lambda}$, $g=z_\lambda(T-s-\tau)$, $v_j=\max_iP_{ij}$ and $\mu_j=v_jg_j/\sum_kv_kg_k$. Entrywise Feynman–Kac comparison gives the common row minorization

$$\frac{H_{ij}g_j}{(Hg)_i}\ge a_0e^{-\lambda\delta\tau}\mu_j =:\alpha_\lambda\mu_j.$$

The reference law changes with the remaining horizon, but the mass does not. Subtracting this row part and composing complete blocks yields

$$\operatorname{osc}(K^{\lambda,T}_{s,t}f)\le (1-\alpha_\lambda)^{\lfloor(t-s)/\tau\rfloor}\operatorname{osc}f.$$

Interpolate the score and integrate the covariance

Write $R=\int_0^Tq(Y_t)dt$, $H_0=\int_0^Th(Y_t)dt$, and $\mathbb E_\lambda$ for the interpolated tilted law. Direct differentiation gives $d\mathbb E_\lambda(R/T)/d\lambda=\operatorname{Cov}_\lambda(R,pH_0)/T$.

For either time ordering, conditioning on the earlier state and bounding variance by a quarter of the squared range gives $|\operatorname{Cov}_\lambda(q(Y_s),h(Y_t))|\le BD(1-\alpha_\lambda)^{\lfloor|t-s|/\tau\rfloor}/4$. Integrating both time triangles therefore gives

$$\frac{|\operatorname{Cov}_\lambda(R,pH_0)|}{T}\le \frac{B\delta}{2}\int_0^\infty(1-\alpha_\lambda)^{\lfloor u/\tau\rfloor}du =\frac{B\delta\tau}{2a_0}e^{\lambda\delta\tau}.$$

Integrate over $\lambda$ to get $B(e^{\delta\tau}-1)/(2a_0)$. The direct whole-path range bound also gives $B\delta T/4$, while the two endpoints of the interpolation are $\rho_cq(w)$ and $J'(w)$; this proves the score budget, including its zero limit when $\delta=0$.

Since $\rho_cq(w)=\gamma\rho_cs(w_c-w)$, evaluating at an optimum proves its distance bound. Integrating the score inequality from $w_c$ to $w_*$ gives $J(w_*)-J(w_c)\le-a\Delta^2/2+E_{m,T}|\Delta|\le E_{m,T}^2/(2a)$, with $a=\gamma\rho_cs$ and $\Delta=w_*-w_c$.

A two-state example

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad b=(1,-1),\quad s=(1,1),\quad \gamma=1/2,\quad\theta=1,\quad a_0=3/4,\quad B=2,\quad D=4,\quad\delta=2.$$

Here the transition-column ratio is $\tanh(1)>3/4$, since the positive Taylor series gives $e^2>7$. For a pure first-state start, $w_c=(1-e^{-2mT})/(mT)$.

$$E_{m,T}=\min\{T,\tfrac43(e^{2/m}-1)\},\qquad |w_*-w_c|\le2E_{m,T},\qquad J(w_*)-J(w_c)\le E_{m,T}^2.$$

In particular, for $m>2$, uniformly in $T$ and in the starting prior,

$$|w_*-w_c|\le\frac{16}{3(m-2)},\qquad J(w_*)-J(w_c)\le\frac{64}{9(m-2)^2}.$$

The pure-start fraction approaches a nonzero profile when $T=1/m$, whereas the stationary averaged fraction is zero. The occupation approximation remains accurate there and at fixed or arbitrarily long horizons; these constants are conservative, not a market calibration.

Validation, literature and scope

The certificate uses exact rational uniformization lower bounds, independent score calculations and root/value-gap checks on sparse and defective generators. Floating roots and matrix exponentials are consistency tests, not interval enclosures or a sampled proof of uniformity.

The time-dependent finite-horizon kernel is classical; see Chetrite and Touchette (2015), Appendix D. Block contraction for Feynman–Kac kernels is developed in Del Moral and Miclo (2002), §§3–4; the portfolio constants and loss bound are derived directly, without a priority claim for the stability mechanism.

This is an approximation for a single constant fraction, not adaptive or filtered trading, and $\rho_c$ is an unconditional expected occupation law under the supplied prior. The loss is a CE-rate loss, not a terminal-wealth or option-price error; no uniqueness or sharp-constant claim is made.
