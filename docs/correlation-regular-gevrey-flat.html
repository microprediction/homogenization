Regular Gevrey covariance

A slowly varying factor changes the scale, not the gauge contraction.

Pure Gevrey powers are not essential to fractional-power flat covariance. A regularly varying penalty has a sharp inverse scale and the same local Green–Kubo geometry.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain and tangent-gauge setup of the preceding theorem. Let $F:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be continuous, strictly increasing and regularly varying with index $q>0$, and define

$$\Upsilon_{F,r}(w)=\exp\{-F(1/r(w))\},\qquad w\ne0, \qquad \Upsilon_{F,r}(0)=0.$$

Write $B$ for the inverse of $x\mapsto x^2F(x)$. For $n=mT\to\infty$, uniformly over arbitrary initial priors,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Upsilon_{F,r}(W_{m,T})) \sim C_{q,2}\,F(B(n)),$$ $$C_{q,p}=\frac{q+2}{2},p^{2/(q+2)} [q\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)]^{-q/(q+2)}.$$

Here $\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)=\max_{\|z\|_2=1}\rho(\Gamma^{1/2}z)^2$. For $F(x)=bx^q$, the formula reduces exactly to the fractional-power theorem.

Inverse-scale contraction

Put $a_n=B(n)$ and $v_n=n/a_n^2=F(a_n)$. Because $B$ is regularly varying with index $1/(q+2)$, $a_n\to\infty$, $a_n=o(\sqrt n)$ and the spectral moderate-deviation principle applies at speed $v_n$.

For fixed $y\ne0$, regular variation and the tangent relation give

$$\frac{F(1/r(y/a_n))}{F(a_n)}\longrightarrow\rho(y)^{-q}.$$

After truncating the singularity at zero and the coercive tails, the $p$th moment contracts to

$$\inf_y\left\{\frac{p}{\rho(y)^q} +\frac12y^\top\Gamma^+y\right\}=C_{q,p}.$$

Since $C_{q,2}<2C_{q,1}$, the squared mean is exponentially negligible and the second moment supplies the variance constant.

Non-power certificate

The certificate takes $F(x)=x^2\log(1+x)$ and uses the nonreversible three-state chain with the asymmetric gauge constant $\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)=4.604379006$. The limiting variance constant is $C_{2,2}=0.932061272$.

Literature and scope

Karamata (1933) supplies the regular-variation framework, Varadhan (1966) the Laplace principle, and Gärtner (1977) the spectral Markov large-deviation principle.

This remains a fixed-feature, fixed-maturity scalar variance statement. It makes no nonlinear-rank or maturity-integrated identification claim. At index zero, the slow-profile theorem moves gauge geometry to second order.
