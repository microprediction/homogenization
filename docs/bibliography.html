Bibliography

The classical theory of averaging and homogenization, the literature around the regime-switching example, and the wider literature collected on the survey page, grouped by role. Every DOI was checked against Crossref. The literature map draws a selection of them: the regime-switching homogenization papers and the strands nearest to them.

Regime-switching bond prices as linear ODE systems

Because the mean-reversion speed does not switch, the regime-switching model is Markov-modulated exponential-affine, and its bond price reduces to a linear ODE system.

  • Cotton, P. (2001). An Analytic Approach to Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Processes with Fluctuating Parameters and Applications in the Modeling of Fixed Income Securities. PhD thesis, Stanford University. pdf · ProQuest.
    Treats Ornstein-Uhlenbeck short rates and hazard rates whose parameters are driven by a Markov chain. It reduces bond prices and survival probabilities to a linear system of ordinary differential equations and expands them for fast switching. Its second half is developed to all orders in the regime-switching pages.
  • Elliott, R. J. and Mamon, R. S. (2002). An interest rate model with a Markovian mean reverting level. Quantitative Finance 2(6):454-458. doi:10.1088/1469-7688/2/6/304.
    Takes a Vasicek short rate whose mean-reversion level is driven by a finite-state Markov chain and gives the bond price analytically through a fundamental matrix. It covers the regime-switching Vasicek model in the case of equal volatilities.
  • Elliott, R. J. and Siu, T. K. (2009). On Markov-modulated Exponential-affine Bond Price Formulae. Applied Mathematical Finance 16(1):1-15. doi:10.1080/13504860802015744.
    Proves Markov-modulated exponential-affine bond price formulae for regime-switching Hull-White and CIR short rates, with coefficients given by fundamental matrix solutions of linear matrix differential equations, which contains the reduction used in the examples as a special case.
  • Landén, C. (2000). Bond pricing in a hidden Markov model of the short rate. Finance and Stochastics 4(4):371-389. doi:10.1007/pl00013526.
    Studies a short rate whose drift and diffusion parameters are modulated by a possibly unobserved Markov process, introduces semi-affine term structures, gives a closed-form bond price when the chain has two states, and studies a Vasicek extension numerically.
  • van Beek, M., Mandjes, M., Spreij, P. and Winands, E. (2020). Regime switching affine processes with applications to finance. Finance and Stochastics 24(2):309-333. doi:10.1007/s00780-020-00419-2.
    Shows that an affine process with Markov-switching parameters is affine on the enlarged state space, so transforms reduce to ODE systems, unifying earlier semi-analytic regime-switching bond formulas.
  • Rodrigo, M. R. and Mamon, R. S. (2021). Bond pricing formulas for Markov-modulated affine term structure models. Journal of Industrial and Management Optimization 17(5):2685ff. doi:10.3934/jimo.2020089.
    Gives exact and approximate zero-coupon bond formulas for Markov-modulated affine short rates with time-dependent coefficients and validates them on a regime-switching Vasicek model.

Fast-switching expansions

The nearest published expansions in the inverse switching rate. None treats the regime-switching Vasicek bond price or survival probability.

  • Yin, G. (2009). Asymptotic expansions of option price under regime-switching diffusions with a fast-varying switching process. Asymptotic Analysis 65(3-4):203-222. doi:10.3233/asy-2009-0953.
    Develops uniform asymptotic expansions, with averaged Black-Scholes leading term and full series, for European option prices when the regime chain switches fast, the closest published analogue of the regime-switching expansion but for equity options rather than bond prices.
  • Basu, A. and Ghosh, M. K. (2009). Asymptotic analysis of option pricing in a Markov modulated market. Operations Research Letters 37(6):415-419. doi:10.1016/j.orl.2009.06.005.
    Analyzes Markov-modulated option prices in the limits where the chain moves very fast or very slowly relative to the asset.
  • Il'in, A. M., Khasminskii, R. Z. and Yin, G. (1999). Asymptotic Expansions of Solutions of Integro-Differential Equations for Transition Densities of Singularly Perturbed Switching Diffusions: Rapid Switchings. Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications 238(2):516-539. doi:10.1006/jmaa.1998.6532.
    Obtains asymptotic expansions of the transition densities of diffusions whose coefficients are modulated by a rapidly switching Markov chain, the density-level analogue of the expansion for a Markov-modulated Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.
  • Yin, G. G. and Zhang, Q. (1998). Continuous-Time Markov Chains and Applications: A Singular Perturbation Approach. Springer, Applications of Mathematics 37 (2nd ed. 2013, doi 10.1007/978-1-4614-4346-9). doi:10.1007/978-1-4612-0627-9.
    Constructs asymptotic expansions, with outer and initial-layer terms, for probability distributions of Markov chains with generators of the form Q/epsilon, the general machinery behind expanding in the inverse switching rate.
  • Khasminskii, R. Z. and Yin, G. (1996). Asymptotic Series for Singularly Perturbed Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck Equations. SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 56(6):1766-1793. doi:10.1137/s0036139994270085.
    Derives full asymptotic series with error bounds for Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck equations of diffusions with fast and slow components.
  • Huang, G., Mandjes, M. and Spreij, P. (2014). Weak convergence of Markov-modulated diffusion processes with rapid switching. Statistics and Probability Letters 86:74-79. doi:10.1016/j.spl.2013.12.013.
    Proves that a diffusion whose drift and diffusion coefficients are modulated by a rapidly switching Markov chain converges weakly to the diffusion with averaged coefficients, which is the leading-order statement underlying the averaged Vasicek limit.
  • Huang, G., Jansen, H. M., Mandjes, M., Spreij, P. and Turck, K. D. (2016). Markov-modulated Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes. Advances in Applied Probability 48(1):235-254. doi:10.1017/apr.2015.15.
    Computes moments and Laplace-transform PDEs of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with Markov-modulated parameters and proves functional central limit theorems when the modulating chain is accelerated, which is the regime-switching state process studied as a process rather than through its exponential functional.
  • Huang, G., Mandjes, M. and Spreij, P. (2016). Large deviations for Markov-modulated diffusion processes with rapid switching. Stochastic Processes and their Applications 126(6):1785-1818. doi:10.1016/j.spa.2015.12.005.
    Proves a joint sample-path large deviations principle for a Markov-modulated diffusion and the chain's occupation measure under rapid switching and small noise.

The classics of averaging and homogenization

  • Bensoussan, A., Lions, J.-L. and Papanicolaou, G. (1978). Asymptotic Analysis for Periodic Structures. North-Holland, Studies in Mathematics and its Applications 5 (reprinted AMS Chelsea Publishing, 2011). doi:10.1090/chel/374.
    The standard reference for two-scale expansions and correctors in elliptic and parabolic equations with rapidly varying coefficients, including the probabilistic treatment of diffusions in periodic and random media on which the corrector calculation is modelled; the DOI is for the 2011 AMS Chelsea reprint.
  • Papanicolaou, G. C., Stroock, D. W. and Varadhan, S. R. S. (1977). Martingale approach to some limit theorems. Papers from the Duke Turbulence Conference (Durham, NC, 1976), Duke University Mathematics Series III, Duke University. link.
    Proves diffusion limits for processes driven by fast ergodic Markov noise via the martingale problem, supplying the Poisson-equation and perturbed test function arguments that justify replacing the fast regime by its average.
  • Khas'minskii, R. Z. (1966). A Limit Theorem for the Solutions of Differential Equations with Random Right-Hand Sides. Theory of Probability and Its Applications 11(3):390-406. doi:10.1137/1111038.
    Establishes the averaging and diffusion-approximation limit for differential equations driven by rapidly mixing random coefficients, the prototype for letting the regime chain switch at rate 1/epsilon.
  • Kurtz, T. G. (1973). A limit theorem for perturbed operator semigroups with applications to random evolutions. Journal of Functional Analysis 12(1):55-67. doi:10.1016/0022-1236(73)90089-x.
    Gives an operator-semigroup limit theorem for singularly perturbed generators of the form (1/epsilon)Q + L, which covers random evolutions such as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process modulated by a fast Markov chain.
  • Pavliotis, G. A. and Stuart, A. M. (2008). Multiscale Methods: Averaging and Homogenization. Springer, Texts in Applied Mathematics 53. doi:10.1007/978-0-387-73829-1.
    A textbook treatment of formal multiscale expansions for Markov chains, ODEs, SDEs and PDEs, including a chapter on averaging for Markov chains that matches the two-state switching setting.
  • Freidlin, M. I. and Wentzell, A. D. (1984). Random Perturbations of Dynamical Systems. Springer, Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften 260. doi:10.1007/978-1-4684-0176-9.
    Develops the averaging principle and associated large deviations for systems with fast and slow components, which describe the leading-order behaviour and rare-event corrections beyond a power-series expansion in epsilon.

Fast mean-reverting stochastic volatility

  • Cotton, P., Fouque, J.-P., Papanicolaou, G. and Sircar, R. (2004). Stochastic Volatility Corrections for Interest Rate Derivatives. Mathematical Finance 14(2):173-200. doi:10.1111/j.0960-1627.2004.00188.x.
    Derives the corrections to Vasicek and CIR bond prices when the volatility of the short rate is driven by a fast mean-reverting factor. The regime-switching expansion is the same calculation with a two-state chain in place of the diffusion.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Papanicolaou, G. and Sircar, K. R. (2000). Derivatives in Financial Markets with Stochastic Volatility. Cambridge University Press, ISBN 0-521-79163-4. link.
    Introduces fast mean-reverting stochastic volatility asymptotics in which prices are expanded around an effective constant-volatility model with a first-order correction, the template for the fast regime chain.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Papanicolaou, G., Sircar, R. and Sølna, K. (2011). Multiscale Stochastic Volatility for Equity, Interest Rate, and Credit Derivatives. Cambridge University Press. doi:10.1017/cbo9781139020534.
    Extends the fast and slow scale expansions to interest rate and credit models, including Vasicek and CIR bond prices and defaultable bonds with stochastic intensity. It is the closest book-length treatment of the regime-switching problem, with a diffusive fast factor instead of a Markov chain.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Papanicolaou, G., Sircar, R. and Sølna, K. (2003). Singular Perturbations in Option Pricing. SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 63(5):1648-1665. doi:10.1137/s0036139902401550.
    Gives the error analysis that justifies the first-order fast-scale correction for option prices, including nonsmooth payoffs.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Papanicolaou, G., Sircar, R. and Sølna, K. (2003). Multiscale Stochastic Volatility Asymptotics. Multiscale Modeling and Simulation 2(1):22-42. doi:10.1137/030600291.
    Combines a fast and a slow volatility factor in a joint singular and regular perturbation expansion of option prices.
  • DeSantiago, R., Fouque, J.-P. and Sølna, K. (2008). Bond markets with stochastic volatility. Advances in Econometrics 22 (Econometrics and Risk Management):215-242. doi:10.1016/s0731-9053(08)22009-8.
    Computes fast and slow stochastic volatility corrections to Vasicek and CIR bond prices, again with a diffusive volatility factor where the regime-switching example uses a two-state chain.

Short rates, affine models and credit

  • Vasicek, O. (1977). An equilibrium characterization of the term structure. Journal of Financial Economics 5(2):177-188. doi:10.1016/0304-405x(77)90016-2.
    Introduces the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck short rate and its exponential-affine bond price, which is the model obtained in each regime and in the averaged limit.
  • Cox, J. C., Ingersoll Jr., J. E. and Ross, S. A. (1985). A Theory of the Term Structure of Interest Rates. Econometrica 53(2):385-407. doi:10.2307/1911242.
    Introduces the square-root short rate model with exponential-affine bond prices, the natural alternative regime dynamics when positivity of the hazard rate is required.
  • Duffie, D. and Kan, R. (1996). A Yield-Factor Model of Interest Rates. Mathematical Finance 6(4):379-406. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9965.1996.tb00123.x.
    Characterizes the multifactor affine term structure models in which bond prices are exponential-affine with coefficients solving Riccati equations.
  • Duffie, D., Pan, J. and Singleton, K. (2000). Transform Analysis and Asset Pricing for Affine Jump-Diffusions. Econometrica 68(6):1343-1376. doi:10.1111/1468-0262.00164.
    Gives the general exponential-affine transform formula for affine jump-diffusions via Riccati ODEs, the framework within which regime switching is accommodated by enlarging the state space.
  • Lando, D. (1998). On Cox Processes and Credit Risky Securities. Review of Derivatives Research 2(2-3):99-120. doi:10.1007/bf01531332.
    Models default as the first jump of a Cox process so that survival probabilities take the form E[exp(-integral of the intensity)], the quantity computed for a regime-switching intensity.
  • Duffie, D. and Singleton, K. J. (1999). Modeling Term Structures of Defaultable Bonds. Review of Financial Studies 12(4):687-720. doi:10.1093/rfs/12.4.687.
    Shows that defaultable bonds can be priced by discounting at a default-adjusted short rate, so survival probabilities and bond prices are the same computation.

Regime switching in econometrics and pricing

  • Hamilton, J. D. (1989). A New Approach to the Economic Analysis of Nonstationary Time Series and the Business Cycle. Econometrica 57(2):357-384. doi:10.2307/1912559.
    Introduces the Markov-switching time series model and its filter, the econometric origin of regime-switching interest rate and credit models.
  • Hansen, A. T. and Poulsen, R. (2000). A simple regime switching term structure model. Finance and Stochastics 4(4):409-429. doi:10.1007/pl00013523.
    Proposes a short-rate model with Markov regime switching in the parameters and studies its term structure, appearing in the same issue as Landén (2000).
  • Ang, A. and Bekaert, G. (2002). Regime Switches in Interest Rates. Journal of Business and Economic Statistics 20(2):163-182. doi:10.1198/073500102317351930.
    Estimates regime-switching short-rate models on US, UK and German data and documents that regimes capture nonlinearities in drift and volatility.
  • Bansal, R. and Zhou, H. (2002). Term Structure of Interest Rates with Regime Shifts. Journal of Finance 57(5):1997-2043. doi:10.1111/0022-1082.00487.
    Builds a discrete-time affine term structure model with regime shifts using log-linear approximations and finds that regime shifts improve the fit to yields.
  • Wu, S. and Zeng, Y. (2005). A General Equilibrium Model of the Term Structure of Interest Rates under Regime-Switching Risk. International Journal of Theoretical and Applied Finance 8(7):839-869. doi:10.1142/s0219024905003323.
    Develops an equilibrium term structure model with priced regime-shift risk and obtains closed-form affine yields under a log-linear approximation.
  • Yao, D. D., Zhang, Q. and Zhou, X. Y. (2006). A Regime-Switching Model for European Options. in Stochastic Processes, Optimization, and Control Theory (H. Yan, G. Yin, Q. Zhang, eds.), Springer, International Series in Operations Research and Management Science, pp. 281-300. doi:10.1007/0-387-33815-2_14.
    Prices European options in a Black-Scholes model with Markov-switching parameters by successive approximation of the coupled pricing system.
  • Papanicolaou, A. and Sircar, R. (2014). A regime-switching Heston model for VIX and S&P 500 implied volatilities. Quantitative Finance 14(10):1811-1827. doi:10.1080/14697688.2013.814923.
    Adds Markov regime switching to Heston stochastic volatility to fit VIX and S&P 500 option smiles jointly, an equity counterpart of combining a regime chain with an affine factor.

Models in the examples

  • Heston, S. L. (1993). A Closed-Form Solution for Options with Stochastic Volatility with Applications to Bond and Currency Options. Review of Financial Studies 6(2):327-343. doi:10.1093/rfs/6.2.327.
    Introduces the square-root stochastic variance model and its characteristic function; the Heston example lets the long-run variance switch.
  • Lewis, A. L. (2001). A Simple Option Formula for General Jump-Diffusion and Other Exponential Levy Processes. SSRN working paper 282110. doi:10.2139/ssrn.282110.
    Gives the single-integral option price in terms of the characteristic function that the Heston example uses.
  • Fischer, W. and Meier-Hellstern, K. (1993). The Markov-modulated Poisson process (MMPP) cookbook. Performance Evaluation 18(2):149-171. doi:10.1016/0166-5316(93)90035-s.
    Collects the generating functions, moments and fitting methods of the Markov-modulated Poisson process, the model in the Poisson counts example.

The wider program

Works behind the sister sites listed on the home page.

  • Luce, R. D. (1959). Individual Choice Behavior: A Theoretical Analysis. Wiley, New York (Dover reprint 2005). doi:10.1037/14396-000.
    Axiomatizes probabilistic choice through the choice axiom, yielding the ratio-scale choice model used elsewhere in the author's program.
  • Vovk, V., Gammerman, A. and Shafer, G. (2005). Algorithmic Learning in a Random World. Springer (2nd ed. 2022, doi 10.1007/978-3-031-06649-8). doi:10.1007/b106715.

Diffusion approximation, random evolutions and cumulant expansions

The general theory of equations switched by fast random processes. The first-order rule on the any-equation page appears here as the diffusion approximation, as the limit of a random evolution, and as the second cumulant.

  • Kubo, R. (1962). Generalized Cumulant Expansion Method. Journal of the Physical Society of Japan 17(7):1100-1120. doi:10.1143/jpsj.17.1100.
    Generalizes cumulant expansions to non-commuting operators with ordered exponentials, the tool behind stochastic line-shape and relaxation theory.
  • Griego, R. J. and Hersh, R. (1969). Random Evolutions, Markov Chains, and Systems of Partial Differential Equations. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 62(2):305-308. doi:10.1073/pnas.62.2.305.
    Introduces random evolutions: operators switched by a Markov chain, whose expectations solve coupled systems of partial differential equations. This is the structure of every pricing equation in the examples.
  • Papanicolaou, G. C. and Varadhan, S. R. S. (1973). A limit theorem with strong mixing in Banach space and two applications to stochastic differential equations. Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 26(4):497-524. doi:10.1002/cpa.3160260405.
    A limit theorem for evolutions driven by strongly mixing noise in a Banach space, applied to stochastic differential equations; an early operator form of the diffusion approximation.
  • Papanicolaou, G. C. and Kohler, W. (1974). Asymptotic theory of mixing stochastic ordinary differential equations. Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 27(5):641-668. doi:10.1002/cpa.3160270503.
    The asymptotic theory of ordinary differential equations with fast mixing random coefficients, in which the limit is a diffusion whose coefficients are integrated correlation functions.
  • Hersh, R. (1974). Random evolutions: A survey of results and problems. Rocky Mountain Journal of Mathematics 4(3):443-477. doi:10.1216/rmj-1974-4-3-443.
    A survey of random evolutions and their limit theorems under rescaling.
  • van Kampen, N. G. (1974). A cumulant expansion for stochastic linear differential equations. I. Physica 74(2):215-238. doi:10.1016/0031-8914(74)90121-9.
    A cumulant expansion for linear differential equations with random coefficients. Its second-order term is the time-ordered, correlation-weighted product of the random operators, the physicists' form of the first-order rule for switched operators.
  • Phillips, R. G. and Kokotovic, P. V. (1981). A singular perturbation approach to modeling and control of Markov chains. IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 26(5):1087-1094. doi:10.1109/tac.1981.1102780.
    Singular perturbation for Markov chains with weak and strong interactions, the control-theory origin of aggregating fast states.
  • Ethier, S. N. and Kurtz, T. G. (1986). Markov Processes: Characterization and Convergence. Wiley. doi:10.1002/9780470316658.
    The standard reference for convergence of Markov processes through generators and martingale problems, with a chapter on random evolutions and diffusion approximations.
  • Pinsky, M. A. (1991). Lectures on Random Evolution. World Scientific. doi:10.1142/1328.
    Lectures on random evolutions, including their averaging and diffusion-approximation limits.
  • Pardoux, E. and Veretennikov, A. Yu. (2001). On the Poisson Equation and Diffusion Approximation. I. Annals of Probability 29(3):1061-1085. doi:10.1214/aop/1015345596.
    Solves the Poisson equation for the generator of an ergodic diffusion on the whole space and uses it to prove diffusion approximations; the continuous-state version of solving Q w = f.
  • Skorokhod, A. V., Hoppensteadt, F. C. and Salehi, H. (2002). Random Perturbation Methods with Applications in Science and Engineering. Springer, Applied Mathematical Sciences 150. doi:10.1007/b98905.
    Averaging and diffusion approximation for systems perturbed by fast random processes, with applications across science and engineering.
  • Kushner, H. J. and Yin, G. G. (2003). Stochastic Approximation and Recursive Algorithms and Applications. Springer, Stochastic Modelling and Applied Probability 35, 2nd ed.. doi:10.1007/b97441.
    Stochastic approximation with correlated and Markovian noise, where the averaged ordinary differential equation and the fluctuation limit play the roles of the averaged law and the Green-Kubo correction.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Garnier, J., Papanicolaou, G. and Sølna, K. (2007). Wave Propagation and Time Reversal in Randomly Layered Media. Springer, Stochastic Modelling and Applied Probability 56. doi:10.1007/978-0-387-49808-9.
    Waves in randomly layered media treated by the diffusion approximation for fast random coefficients, including the effective medium and the first corrections.
  • Yin, G. G. and Zhang, Q. (2013). Continuous-Time Markov Chains and Applications: A Two-Time-Scale Approach. Springer, Stochastic Modelling and Applied Probability 37, 2nd ed.. doi:10.1007/978-1-4614-4346-9.
    The second edition of the two-time-scale Markov chain monograph, with outer expansions, initial layers and applications to control and finance.
  • Kipnis, C. and Varadhan, S. R. S. (1986). Central limit theorem for additive functionals of reversible Markov processes and applications to simple exclusions. Communications in Mathematical Physics 104(1):1-19. doi:10.1007/bf01210789.
    Proves the central limit theorem for time integrals of a function of a reversible Markov process, with the long-run variance given through the resolvent; the reversibility assumption is essential to the proof.

Multiscale and regime-switching option pricing and credit

  • Hull, J. and White, A. (1987). The Pricing of Options on Assets with Stochastic Volatilities. Journal of Finance 42(2):281-300. doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.1987.tb02568.x.
    Prices an option under uncorrelated stochastic volatility as the Black-Scholes price averaged over integrated variance. Its expansion to second order in the variance of integrated variance is the smile formula on the any-equation page.
  • Naik, V. (1993). Option Valuation and Hedging Strategies with Jumps in the Volatility of Asset Returns. Journal of Finance 48(5):1969-1984. doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb05137.x.
    Option valuation when volatility jumps between two levels following a Markov chain, one of the first regime-switching option pricing models.
  • Di Masi, G. B., Kabanov, Yu. M. and Runggaldier, W. J. (1995). Mean-Variance Hedging of Options on Stocks with Markov Volatilities. Theory of Probability and Its Applications 39(1):172-182. doi:10.1137/1139008.
    Mean-variance hedging of options on a stock whose volatility is driven by a Markov chain.
  • Sircar, K. R. and Papanicolaou, G. C. (1999). Stochastic volatility, smile & asymptotics. Applied Mathematical Finance 6(2):107-145. doi:10.1080/135048699334573.
    Stochastic volatility, the smile and asymptotics: an early account of how fast volatility fluctuations produce a smile around an effective constant volatility.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Papanicolaou, G. and Sircar, K. R. (2000). Mean-Reverting Stochastic Volatility. International Journal of Theoretical and Applied Finance 3(1):101-142. doi:10.1142/s0219024900000061.
    The paper that introduced fast mean-reverting stochastic volatility asymptotics for option pricing, with the first correction expressed through a few group parameters that can be fitted to the implied volatility skew.
  • Guo, X. (2001). Information and option pricings. Quantitative Finance 1(1):38-44. doi:10.1080/713665550.
    Option pricing with a two-state regime-switching volatility and the role of information about the regime.
  • Buffington, J. and Elliott, R. J. (2002). American Options with Regime Switching. International Journal of Theoretical and Applied Finance 5(5):497-514. doi:10.1142/s0219024902001523.
    European and American options when the parameters of the underlying switch with a Markov chain.
  • Elliott, R. J., Chan, L. and Siu, T. K. (2005). Option pricing and Esscher transform under regime switching. Annals of Finance 1(4):423-432. doi:10.1007/s10436-005-0013-z.
    Regime-switching option pricing with the equivalent martingale measure chosen by the Esscher transform.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Sircar, R. and Sølna, K. (2006). Stochastic Volatility Effects on Defaultable Bonds. Applied Mathematical Finance 13(3):215-244. doi:10.1080/13504860600563127.
    Fast and slow stochastic volatility corrections for defaultable bonds and credit spreads.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Wignall, B. C. and Zhou, X. (2008). Modeling correlated defaults: first passage model under stochastic volatility. Journal of Computational Finance 11(3):43-78. doi:10.21314/jcf.2008.192.
    Correlated defaults in a first-passage model under stochastic volatility, with the joint default probabilities corrected by multiscale asymptotics.
  • Duffie, D., Eckner, A., Horel, G. and Saita, L. (2009). Frailty Correlated Default. Journal of Finance 64(5):2089-2123. doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01495.x.
    Evidence that US corporate defaults cluster beyond what observable covariates explain, modelled with a common latent frailty process driving all default intensities.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Lorig, M. and Sircar, R. (2016). Second order multiscale stochastic volatility asymptotics: stochastic terminal layer analysis and calibration. Finance and Stochastics 20(3):543-588. doi:10.1007/s00780-016-0298-y.
    Second-order multiscale stochastic volatility asymptotics, including a stochastic terminal layer, with calibration to implied volatilities.
  • Fouque, J.-P., Sircar, R. and Zariphopoulou, T. (2017). Portfolio Optimization and Stochastic Volatility Asymptotics. Mathematical Finance 27(3):704-745. doi:10.1111/mafi.12109.
    Applies fast and slow volatility asymptotics to Merton's portfolio problem, showing the first correction to the value function and the optimal strategy.
  • Cuchiero, C., Keller-Ressel, M. and Teichmann, J. (2012). Polynomial processes and their applications to mathematical finance. Finance and Stochastics 16(4):711-740. doi:10.1007/s00780-012-0188-x.
    Markov processes whose generator maps polynomials of degree n to polynomials of degree at most n, so that moments follow from a finite matrix exponential; the square-root process and its integral are examples, used here for exact cumulants under a switching level.

Likelihood and tests for switching

  • Carrasco, M., Hu, L. and Ploberger, W. (2014). Optimal Test for Markov Switching Parameters. Econometrica 82(2):765-784. doi:10.3982/ecta8609.
    An information-matrix-type test for Markov switching that uses only the model under the null and is asymptotically equivalent to the likelihood ratio test.
  • White, H. (1982). Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Misspecified Models. Econometrica 50(1):1-25. doi:10.2307/1912526.
    Introduces the information-matrix test, based on the difference between the two forms of the information matrix.
  • Chesher, A. (1984). Testing for Neglected Heterogeneity. Econometrica 52(4):865-872. doi:10.2307/1911188.
    Shows that the score test for random coefficients is close to the information-matrix test.

  • Colquhoun, D. and Hawkes, A. G. (1982). On the stochastic properties of bursts of single ion channel openings and of clusters of bursts. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London B 300(1098):1-59. doi:10.1098/rstb.1982.0156.
    Extends the analysis to bursts of openings and clusters of bursts, whose structure shows how the hidden states connect.

Spatial and nonlinear homogenization

  • Tartar, L. (2010). The General Theory of Homogenization: A Personalized Introduction. Springer, Lecture Notes of the Unione Matematica Italiana 7. doi:10.1007/978-3-642-05195-1.

  • Allaire, G. (1992). Homogenization and Two-Scale Convergence. SIAM Journal on Mathematical Analysis 23(6):1482-1518. doi:10.1137/0523084.

  • Armstrong, S., Kuusi, T. and Mourrat, J.-C. (2019). Quantitative Stochastic Homogenization and Large-Scale Regularity. Springer, Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften 352. doi:10.1007/978-3-030-15545-2.

  • Evans, L. C. (1989). The perturbed test function method for viscosity solutions of nonlinear PDE. Proceedings of the Royal Society of Edinburgh, Section A 111(3-4):359-375. doi:10.1017/s0308210500018631.

  • Ziliotto, B. (2017). Stochastic Homogenization of Nonconvex Hamilton-Jacobi Equations: A Counterexample. Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 70(9):1798-1809. doi:10.1002/cpa.21674.
    Constructs a stationary ergodic nonconvex Hamilton-Jacobi equation in two dimensions that does not homogenize.
  • Cioranescu, D. and Murat, F. (1997). A Strange Term Coming from Nowhere. In Topics in the Mathematical Modelling of Composite Materials, Birkhauser, 45-93. doi:10.1007/978-1-4612-2032-9_4.
    Shows that a Dirichlet problem in a domain with many small holes has a limit with an extra zero-order term that no averaging of the coefficients produces.
  • Dal Maso, G. (1993). An Introduction to Gamma-Convergence. Birkhauser, Progress in Nonlinear Differential Equations and Their Applications 8. doi:10.1007/978-1-4612-0327-8.
    The textbook on Gamma-convergence, the notion of convergence of energy functionals under which minimizers converge.
  • Hou, T. Y. and Wu, X.-H. (1997). A Multiscale Finite Element Method for Elliptic Problems in Composite Materials and Porous Media. Journal of Computational Physics 134(1):169-189. doi:10.1006/jcph.1997.5682.
    Builds finite-element basis functions from local solutions of the oscillating equation, so that a coarse grid carries the effective behaviour.

Fluid dynamics and turbulent transport

  • Taylor, G. I. (1922). Diffusion by Continuous Movements. Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society s2-20(1):196-212. doi:10.1112/plms/s2-20.1.196.
    Shows that a particle carried by a turbulent velocity spreads, at long times, with a diffusivity equal to the time integral of the velocity autocorrelation. This is the first Green-Kubo formula.
  • Kraichnan, R. H. (1968). Small-Scale Structure of a Scalar Field Convected by Turbulence. Physics of Fluids 11(5):945-953. doi:10.1063/1.1692063.
    A scalar advected by a random velocity that is delta-correlated in time, the model in which the eddy diffusivity is exact.
  • Hasselmann, K. (1976). Stochastic climate models Part I. Theory. Tellus 28(6):473-485. doi:10.1111/j.2153-3490.1976.tb00696.x.
    Slow climate variables driven by fast weather treated as a diffusion approximation, with the fast variables entering as noise.
  • McLaughlin, D. W., Papanicolaou, G. C. and Pironneau, O. R. (1985). Convection of Microstructure and Related Problems. SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 45(5):780-797. doi:10.1137/0145046.
    Homogenization of a flow with small-scale structure: the effective large-scale equations acquire an eddy term from a cell problem.
  • Frisch, U., She, Z. S. and Sulem, P. L. (1987). Large-scale flow driven by the anisotropic kinetic alpha effect. Physica D 28(3):382-392. doi:10.1016/0167-2789(87)90026-1.
    Multiscale expansion of the Navier-Stokes equations around a small-scale flow that lacks parity invariance, giving a large-scale instability at first order.
  • Avellaneda, M. and Majda, A. J. (1991). An integral representation and bounds on the effective diffusivity in passive advection by laminar and turbulent flows. Communications in Mathematical Physics 138(2):339-391. doi:10.1007/bf02099496.
    An integral representation and bounds for the effective diffusivity of a passive scalar in periodic and random flows.
  • Dubrulle, B. and Frisch, U. (1991). Eddy viscosity of parity-invariant flow. Physical Review A 43(10):5355-5364. doi:10.1103/physreva.43.5355.
    Derives the eddy viscosity of the Navier-Stokes equations by multiscale expansion around a parity-invariant small-scale flow.
  • Fannjiang, A. and Papanicolaou, G. (1994). Convection Enhanced Diffusion for Periodic Flows. SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 54(2):333-408. doi:10.1137/s0036139992236785.
    Effective diffusivity of a passive scalar in periodic cellular flows, and how it scales with the molecular diffusivity.
  • Frisch, U. (1995). Turbulence: The Legacy of A. N. Kolmogorov. Cambridge University Press. doi:10.1017/cbo9781139170666.
    A book on turbulence with chapters on multiscale methods, eddy viscosity and the closure problem.
  • Majda, A. J. and Kramer, P. R. (1999). Simplified models for turbulent diffusion: Theory, numerical modelling, and physical phenomena. Physics Reports 314(4-5):237-574. doi:10.1016/s0370-1573(98)00083-0.
    A review of turbulent diffusion in simplified random and periodic flows, including homogenization, eddy diffusivity and anomalous regimes.
  • Majda, A. J., Timofeyev, I. and Vanden-Eijnden, E. (2001). A mathematical framework for stochastic climate models. Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 54(8):891-974. doi:10.1002/cpa.1014.
    Eliminates fast modes of fluid and climate equations by the diffusion approximation, giving reduced stochastic equations for the slow modes.
  • Tao, T. (2016). Finite time blowup for an averaged three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equation. Journal of the American Mathematical Society 29(3):601-674. doi:10.1090/jams/838.
    Replaces the bilinear term of the Navier-Stokes equations by an averaged operator that keeps the energy identity, and proves finite-time blowup for the modified equation; the result does not transfer to the original equations.
  • OpenAI (2026). Finite time blowup for Navier-Stokes. Preprint, September 2026. link.
    Claims that a smooth, compactly supported force and zero initial velocity give a three-dimensional Navier-Stokes solution with bounded energy whose velocity becomes unbounded in finite time, the forced alternatives of the Clay statement; under evaluation.

Green-Kubo, motional narrowing, relaxation and transport

The same integrated correlations in statistical physics, spectroscopy, chemistry and fluid dynamics.

  • Onsager, L. (1931). Reciprocal Relations in Irreversible Processes. I.. Physical Review 37(4):405-426. doi:10.1103/physrev.37.405.
    The reciprocal relations: transport coefficients are symmetric under microscopic reversibility. The same argument makes the Green-Kubo matrix symmetric for a reversible chain.
  • Bloembergen, N., Purcell, E. M. and Pound, R. V. (1948). Relaxation Effects in Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Absorption. Physical Review 73(7):679-712. doi:10.1103/physrev.73.679.
    Nuclear magnetic relaxation in liquids, with relaxation rates set by spectral densities of fluctuating local fields.
  • Taylor, G. I. (1953). Dispersion of soluble matter in solvent flowing slowly through a tube. Proceedings of the Royal Society A 219(1137):186-203. doi:10.1098/rspa.1953.0139.
    Dispersion of a solute in flow through a tube: shear and cross-stream diffusion combine into an enhanced effective diffusion along the tube.
  • Green, M. S. (1954). Markoff Random Processes and the Statistical Mechanics of Time-Dependent Phenomena. II. Irreversible Processes in Fluids. Journal of Chemical Physics 22(3):398-413. doi:10.1063/1.1740082.
    Expresses transport coefficients in fluids as time integrals of equilibrium correlation functions, one half of the Green-Kubo formula.
  • Anderson, P. W. (1954). A Mathematical Model for the Narrowing of Spectral Lines by Exchange or Motion. Journal of the Physical Society of Japan 9(3):316-339. doi:10.1143/jpsj.9.316.
    Exchange narrowing of spectral lines: a frequency that hops between values produces a line whose width falls as the hopping speeds up. The two-site case is the two-state eigenvalue on the yield-curve page with an imaginary forcing.
  • Kubo, R. (1954). Note on the Stochastic Theory of Resonance Absorption. Journal of the Physical Society of Japan 9(6):935-944. doi:10.1143/jpsj.9.935.
    The stochastic theory of resonance absorption and motional narrowing, with the line width given by the integrated correlation of the frequency fluctuation.
  • Giddings, J. C. and Eyring, H. (1955). A Molecular Dynamic Theory of Chromatography. Journal of Physical Chemistry 59(5):416-421. doi:10.1021/j150527a009.
    A molecule alternating at random between moving and adsorbed phases in chromatography; the band's speed is the time-averaged velocity and its spreading comes from the fluctuations.
  • Aris, R. (1956). On the dispersion of a solute in a fluid flowing through a tube. Proceedings of the Royal Society A 235(1200):67-77. doi:10.1098/rspa.1956.0065.
    Method of moments for Taylor dispersion, adding molecular diffusion and giving the effective dispersion coefficient in general geometries.
  • Kubo, R. (1957). Statistical-Mechanical Theory of Irreversible Processes. I. General Theory and Simple Applications to Magnetic and Conduction Problems. Journal of the Physical Society of Japan 12(6):570-586. doi:10.1143/jpsj.12.570.
    Linear response theory: transport coefficients as integrated correlation functions, the other half of the Green-Kubo formula.
  • Redfield, A. G. (1957). On the Theory of Relaxation Processes. IBM Journal of Research and Development 1(1):19-31. doi:10.1147/rd.11.0019.
    Relaxation theory for spins coupled to fast fluctuations. The operators do not commute, and the rates and frequency shifts come from integrated correlations, as in the any-equation page with commutators.
  • Blume, M. (1968). Stochastic Theory of Line Shape: Generalization of the Kubo-Anderson Model. Physical Review 174(2):351-358. doi:10.1103/physrev.174.351.
    Generalizes the Kubo-Anderson stochastic line-shape model to fluctuating Hamiltonians that do not commute at different times.
  • Szabo, A., Shoup, D., Northrup, S. H. and McCammon, J. A. (1982). Stochastically gated diffusion-influenced reactions. Journal of Chemical Physics 77(9):4484-4493. doi:10.1063/1.444397.
    Diffusion-influenced reactions whose reactivity is switched on and off by a stochastic gate.
  • Zwanzig, R. (1990). Rate processes with dynamical disorder. Accounts of Chemical Research 23(5):148-152. doi:10.1021/ar00173a005.
    Rate processes with dynamical disorder: reaction rates that fluctuate in time, with fast and slow fluctuation limits.
  • Jülicher, F., Ajdari, A. and Prost, J. (1997). Modeling molecular motors. Reviews of Modern Physics 69(4):1269-1282. doi:10.1103/revmodphys.69.1269.
    Molecular motors as particles switching between potentials under a chemical cycle that breaks detailed balance.
  • Reimann, P. (2002). Brownian motors: noisy transport far from equilibrium. Physics Reports 361(2-4):57-265. doi:10.1016/s0370-1573(01)00081-3.
    A review of Brownian motors, where directed transport needs broken detailed balance and broken symmetry.
  • Lawley, S. D. and Keener, J. P. (2015). A New Derivation of Robin Boundary Conditions through Homogenization of a Stochastically Switching Boundary. SIAM Journal on Applied Dynamical Systems 14(4):1845-1867. doi:10.1137/15m1015182.
    Derives Robin boundary conditions by homogenizing a boundary that switches at random between absorbing and reflecting.
