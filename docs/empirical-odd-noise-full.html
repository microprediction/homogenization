Empirical odd-score calibration at every noise scale

Couple the complete calibration sample, rather than inserting random extreme quantiles into an interior-target theorem.

Model, rank and conditioning

Fix a finite irreducible row generator $Q$, stationary row $\pi$, and nonconstant real feature $f$ with $\pi f=0$. Put $h=Q^\#f$, $\Gamma=-2\pi(fh)>0$, $b_i=h_i/\sqrt\Gamma$, and

$$A_c=c^{-1}\int_0^c f(Y_t)dt,\quad X_c=\sqrt{c/\Gamma}A_c,\quad S_c=\mu A_c^d+\sigma Z,\quad \lambda={c^{d/2}\sigma\over\mu\Gamma^{d/2}}.$$

Here $d\ge1$ is a fixed odd integer, $\mu>0$, $\sigma\ge0$, and $Z$ is standard Gaussian. Take $N$ independent stationary calibration paths, independent noises, and an independent test path starting in regime $i$. For $1\le k\le N$, let $C_i(c,N,k,\lambda)$ be the probability that the test score is below the $k$th calibration score, averaged over the panel.

At zero noise order score pairs $(S,V)$ lexicographically, with an independent uniform $V$ for every calibration and test score. This is a specified randomized prediction rule, not an assertion about deterministic ties. Then $p_N=k/(N+1)$ is the exact marginal rank baseline:

$$\sum_i\pi_i C_i=p_N.$$

The empirical all-noise profile

$$r_d(t)=\operatorname{sgn}(t)|t|^{1/d},\quad P_i(t)=b_i\phi(r_d(t)),\quad K_{i,\lambda}(t)=\mathbb E P_i(t-\lambda Z).$$

Let $T_{N,k}(\lambda)$ be the $k$th order statistic of $N$ iid copies of $G^d+\lambda Z$, with independent standard Gaussians. Equivalently, for $U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,N+1-k)$ and reference quantile $t_u(\lambda)$, define

$$\mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)=\mathbb E K_{i,\lambda}(T_{N,k}(\lambda)) =\mathbb E_U K_{i,\lambda}(t_U(\lambda)).$$

For every fixed model, degree and finite $N$, uniformly over states, ranks and all noise levels,

$$\boxed{C_i-p_N={\mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)\over\sqrt c}+R_{i,c,N,k}(\lambda),\qquad \sup_{i,k,\lambda\ge0}\sqrt c(1+\lambda)|R_{i,c,N,k}(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0}.$$

The statement is latent-start model-conditional and averages over calibration samples. It is not coverage conditional on a realized panel, features or observed history. The finite bound below also gives an explicit sufficient condition for growing $N$.

A finite bound without conditional quantile errors

Let $F_{i,c}$ be the CDF of $X_c$ and $F_{\pi,c}=\sum_i\pi_i F_{i,c}$. The signed CDF and transport errors are

$$U_{i,c}=\|\sqrt c(F_{i,c}-F_{\pi,c})-b_i\phi\|_\infty,\qquad L_{i,d,c}=d\int |x|^{d-1}|\sqrt c(F_{i,c}-F_{\pi,c})-b_i\phi(x)|dx,$$ $$\Delta_{d,c}=d\int |x|^{d-1}|F_{\pi,c}(x)-\Phi(x)|dx =W_1(\mathcal L_\pi(X_c^d),\mathcal L(G^d)),\qquad \alpha_d=\min(1,2/d).$$

For every finite $c,N$ and every noise level,

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned} \sqrt c(1+\lambda)\left|C_i-p_N-{\mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)\over\sqrt c}\right| &\le(1+\lambda)\min\left(U_{i,c},{L_{i,d,c}\over\lambda\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)\\ &\quad+4|b_i|d!!\left({N\Delta_{d,c}\over1+\lambda}\right)^{\alpha_d}. \end{aligned}}$$

At $\lambda=0$ omit the second entry of the minimum. Here $d!!=1\cdot3\cdots d$. The first line is an actual-threshold signed-CDF convolution bound; the second transfers the complete empirical threshold to its reference law.

Proof: couple samples, not beta tail quantiles

Use common uniforms to couple $X_{c,j}=F_{\pi,c}^{-1}(V_j)$ and $G_j=\Phi^{-1}(V_j)$, and use the same independent $Z_j$ in both score samples. Monotonicity of the odd power makes this a one-dimensional optimal coupling, so $\mathbb E|X_{c,j}^d-G_j^d|=\Delta_{d,c}$.

The $k$th order-statistic value is 1-Lipschitz in the maximum coordinate difference, including when values tie. If $\widehat T$ is the actual scaled calibration threshold,

$$\mathbb E\left|{\widehat T\over1+\lambda}-{T_{N,k}(\lambda)\over1+\lambda}\right| \le{N\Delta_{d,c}\over1+\lambda}.$$

Put $H_{i,\lambda}(u)=(1+\lambda)K_{i,\lambda}((1+\lambda)u)$. This family has a global noise-uniform Hölder bound:

$$|H_{i,\lambda}(u)-H_{i,\lambda}(v)|\le4|b_i|d!!|u-v|^{\alpha_d}.$$

For $\lambda\le1$, use the Lipschitz bound on $\phi$ when $d=1$, or $P_i(t)=b_i\phi(0)e^{-|t|^{2/d}/2}$ and the $2/d$-Hölder power inequality when $d\ge3$. Scaling and convolution cost at most $2^{1+\alpha_d}\le4$.

For $\lambda\ge1$, write the convolution with a Gaussian density and use $\|P_i\|_1=|b_i|d!!$, $\|\phi'\|_\infty\le1$. This gives Lipschitz constant at most $4|b_i|d!!$ for distances below one; for larger distances use $2\|H_{i,\lambda}\|_\infty\le4|b_i|d!!$.

Jensen's inequality and the order-statistic coupling prove the second line of the finite bound. The first follows by convolving the signed CDF error: its supremum is $U_{i,c}$, and its weighted $L^1$ norm after the odd-root substitution is $L_{i,d,c}$. Taking expectations over actual calibration thresholds proves the inequality, without any interior-quantile truncation.

The finite-chain input and computable tails

For this fixed finite irreducible model, $U_{i,c}=O(c^{-1/2})$, $L_{i,d,c}\to0$, and $\Delta_{d,c}\to0$. Here is the spectral argument, also detailed in the population odd-score note.

For $D=\operatorname{diag}f$, the dominant eigenvalue of $Q+zD$ is $\Gamma z^2/2+\kappa_3z^3/6+O(z^4)$ and its projector applied to $\mathbf1$ is $\mathbf1-zh+O(z^2)$. Signed Fourier smoothing gives an order-$c^{-1}$ CDF remainder; the stationary skewness cancels in the state-minus-stationary contrast, yielding the asserted $U$ rate.

No discrete-time nonlattice assumption is imported. The Dirichlet form excludes imaginary-axis eigenvalues of $Q+i\theta D$ for $\theta\ne0$; at high frequencies, distinct feature levels separate into proper transient killed blocks. A unitary Schur expansion bounds the semigroup uniformly by an exponentially decaying term times a fixed-degree polynomial in $c$, including defective generators.

The real tilted semigroup gives $\mathbb E_i e^{z\int_0^cf(Y_s)ds}\le C e^{Cc z^2}$ for small real $z$. Chernoff tails and truncation at a sufficiently large multiple of $\sqrt{\log c}$ turn the CDF estimates into the weighted $L^1$ limits above. The finite inequality therefore proves the all-noise theorem, since its first line is at most $2\max(U_{i,c},L_{i,d,c}/\sqrt{2\pi})$.

For explicit moment-tail budgets, let $D_c=\|F_{\pi,c}-\Phi\|_\infty$ and take $R>0$, even $2m>d$.

$$\Delta_{d,c}\le2R^dD_c+R^{d-2m}[\mathbb E_\pi|X_c|^{2m}+\mathbb E|G|^{2m}],$$ $$L_{i,d,c}\le2R^dU_{i,c}+\sqrt c R^{d-2m}[\mathbb E_i|X_c|^{2m}+\mathbb E_\pi|X_c|^{2m}] +d|b_i|\int_{|x|>R}|x|^{d-1}\phi(x)dx.$$

Moments follow from the finite hierarchy $v_j'=Qv_j+jDv_{j-1}$. For a signed-smoothing budget, use the characteristic function $\chi_i(u)=e_i e^{c(Q+iuD/\sqrt{c\Gamma})}\mathbf1$ and signed approximant

$$\widehat B_i(u)=e^{-u^2/2}\left[1+{ -iub_i+\kappa_3(iu)^3/(6\Gamma^{3/2})\over\sqrt c}\right],\quad \kappa_3=6\pi[fQ^\#(fh)],\quad B_i(x)=\Phi(x)+{\phi(x)\over\sqrt c}\left[b_i-{\kappa_3(x^2-1)\over6\Gamma^{3/2}}\right].$$ $$\epsilon_i(T)={2\over\pi}\int_0^T{|\chi_i(u)-\widehat B_i(u)|\over u}du +{24\|B_i'\|_\infty\over\pi T},\quad U_{i,c}\le\sqrt c\left(\epsilon_i+\sum_j\pi_j\epsilon_j\right),\quad D_c\le\sum_j\pi_j\epsilon_j+{|\kappa_3|\|(x^2-1)\phi(x)\|_\infty\over6\Gamma^{3/2}\sqrt c}.$$

These inequalities and the moment tails provide finite budgets, not asserted sharp. The theorem also applies to varying $N_c$ whenever $N_c\Delta_{d,c}\to0$, uniformly in ranks; no unrestricted calibration-size/noise uniformity is claimed.

Atoms and the two endpoints

At a tied value $x$ with tie uniform $v$, the augmented CDF contrast is the convex combination of the left and right ordinary CDF contrasts. The profile is continuous, so both limits and their combination satisfy the same $U_{i,c}$ bound. Randomized ranks therefore need no additional marginal atom-overshoot term.

$$J_{N,k}=\mathbb E\phi(\Phi^{-1}(U)),\qquad \mathcal K_i^{N,k}(0)=b_iJ_{N,k},\qquad \lambda\mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)\longrightarrow b_i d!!J_{N,k}\quad(\lambda\to\infty).$$

For the last limit, use $\int P_i=b_i d!!$, the Gaussian convolution formula and the same sample coupling as noise dominates. Bounded convergence applies to the globally bounded profile family, including the beta tails. Hence zero noise gives a $c^{-1/2}$ gap for every odd degree; fixed positive $\sigma$ gives

$$c^{(d+1)/2}\sigma(C_i-p_N)\longrightarrow \mu d!!h_i\Gamma^{(d-1)/2}J_{N,k}.$$

Noiseless augmented rank events are in fact identical sample by sample for every increasing odd power, not only asymptotically.

Certificate, literature and limits

For $N=k=9$, the certificate checks 36 nonreversible three-state cases with $c=32,128,512$, degrees $1,3,5$ and $\lambda=0,.5,2,10^5$. The worst weighted error falls from $.070864855$ to $.00453871677$; $\Gamma=.385937945$ and $J_{9,9}=.1538752731$. Joint Fourier/spatial/beta-order refinement changes weighted values by at most $1.75\times10^{-5}$, within a portable $3\times10^{-5}$ allowance.

There are also 300 exact odd-rank patterns, 120 Hölder and 27,000 order-statistic comparisons, and nine independent transport identities. An auxiliary iid binary-score rank check has randomized conditional coverages $3/4,1/4$ and marginal $1/2$; it tests the tie rule, not the irreducible CTMC limit. Fourier, interpolation, spatial and beta-order refinement are numerical consistency checks, not rigorous interval enclosures or proofs of continuum uniformity.

The one-dimensional transport identity is classical Vallender (1974). For spectral initial-bias and Edgeworth background see Hervé and Pène (2010), Theorem 8.1 and Lemma 8.3; the continuous-time high-frequency and calibration-transfer steps are supplied here.

Independent calibration paths, fixed $Q,f,d$ and the stated tie rule are essential. This does not cover overlapping panels, same-panel predictor fitting, feature-conditional coverage or degenerating mixing gaps. The result addresses issue 248 without changing its status.
