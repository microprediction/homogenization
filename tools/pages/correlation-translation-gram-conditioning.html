Conditioning of the translation-hull Gram

Distinct power outputs have full rank, but coalescing exponents lose identification at exact rates.

The translation-hull Gram can be positive definite and still nearly singular. For powers of one Gaussian feature, a confluent-Vandermonde expansion gives the determinant and every eigenvalue scale.

Coalescing-exponent theorem

Let $G\sim N(0,1)$, fix $c>0$, and take distinct real numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_k$. For sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, define

$$F_{\varepsilon,j}=|G|^{c+\varepsilon x_j}-M_{c+\varepsilon x_j}, \qquad M_p=\mathbb E|G|^p.$$

Let $K_\varepsilon$ be their covariance Gram matrix and put

$$Y_r=|G|^c(\log|G|)^r-M_c^{(r)},\qquad H_c=(\mathbb E[Y_rY_s])_{r,s=0}^{k-1},$$ $$(H_c)_{rs}=M^{(r+s)}(2c)-M^{(r)}(c)M^{(s)}(c),$$ $$\Delta(x)=\prod_{i<j}(x_j-x_i).$$

Then $H_c\succ0$ and

$$\det K_\varepsilon= \varepsilon^{k(k-1)} \left\{\frac{\Delta(x)}{\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}r!}\right\}^{\!2} \det H_c\{1+O(\varepsilon)\}.$$

If the eigenvalues are ordered decreasingly, then

$$\lambda_j(K_\varepsilon)=\Theta(\varepsilon^{2(j-1)}), \qquad 1\le j\le k.$$

Proof

The centered map $f(a)=|G|^a-M_a$ is analytic into $L^2$ near $c$, with $f^{(r)}(c)=Y_r$. Thus its $k$ sampled columns have the $L^2$ expansion

$$f(c+\varepsilon x_j)= \sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon^rx_j^r}{r!}Y_r+O_{L^2}(\varepsilon^k).$$

The leading exterior product is $\varepsilon^{k(k-1)/2}\Delta(x)(\prod r!)^{-1}Y_0\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_{k-1}$. Squaring its norm gives the determinant formula.

No nonzero combination of the $Y_r$ is almost surely zero: it would make $z^cP(\log z)$ constant on $(0,\infty)$, and the limit at zero first makes the constant zero and then forces $P=0$. Hence $H_c\succ0$.

Finally, right multiplication by the fixed inverse Vandermonde matrix reduces the sampled-column operator to an invertible derivative frame times $\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon,\ldots,\varepsilon^{k-1})$, plus a smaller remainder. Singular-value comparison gives the displayed eigenvalue hierarchy.

Sharp two-output constants

For the symmetric pair $c-\delta/2,c+\delta/2$, write $V_c=\operatorname{Var}(|G|^c)$ and

$$D_c=\det\operatorname{Gram} \left(|G|^c-M_c, |G|^c\log|G|-M_c'\right)>0.$$

Symmetry removes the odd remainder terms, so

$$\det K_\delta=D_c\delta^2+O(\delta^4),\qquad \lambda_{\min}(K_\delta)=\frac{D_c}{2V_c}\delta^2+O(\delta^4),$$ $$\operatorname{cond}_2K_\delta=\frac{4V_c^2}{D_c}\delta^{-2}\{1+O(\delta^2)\}.$$

At $c=1$, the certificate gives

$$V_1=0.363380227632,\qquad D_1=0.024978898995,$$ $$\frac{D_1}{2V_1}=0.034370195591,\qquad \frac{4V_1^2}{D_1}=21.145077668713.$$

At $\delta=0.025$, the exact determinant, smallest eigenvalue and scaled condition number are respectively $0.999854950$, $0.999683519$ and $1.000488120$ times these asymptotic predictions.

Four-output certificate

For $c=1$ and $x=(0,1,2,3)$, the Vandermonde-to-factorial ratio is one. The derivative-Gram determinant, hence the leading coefficient of $\det K_\varepsilon/\varepsilon^{12}$, is $0.000054298718305$.

At $\varepsilon=0.003125$, the exact determinant ratio is $1.005256943$. Between $\varepsilon=0.00625$ and $0.003125$, the four measured eigenvalue exponents are $(0.012231,2.005936,3.998635,5.990805)$, approaching $(0,2,4,6)$.

Scope

This is conditioning of the Gaussian-function Gram after the fixed-positive-maturity fast-switching limit. It neither changes fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor integrates distinct maturity loadings.

The expansion holds for fixed $c$, fixed distinct nodes and $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. It gives no joint finite-speed/coalescing-exponent error bound: that would require the quantitative translation, central-limit and tail moduli deliberately left open by the preceding theorem.

Halperin (1962) supplies the Hilbert-space Gram identity, while Gautschi (1978) treats inverse conditioning of ordinary and confluent Vandermonde matrices. The covariance determinant and eigenvalue hierarchy above are derived here.
