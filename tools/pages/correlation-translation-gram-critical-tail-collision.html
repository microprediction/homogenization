Tail and Gaussian Gram modes collide at one critical scale

When tail separation is proportional to exponent spacing, two mechanisms form one rank-two block.

A coalescing rare-tail mode dominates the next Gaussian mode when its slope separation is much larger than the exponent spacing. At comparable scales neither coefficient survives alone: the two projected derivative directions combine in an explicit positive matrix.

Critical-collision theorem

Fix $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, $1\le r\le k-4$, a slope $\tau>0$, and weights $q_1,q_2>0$. Put $Q=q_1+q_2$, $\gamma_1=-q_2/Q$, and $\gamma_2=q_1/Q$.

For $\varepsilon_n,\delta_n\downarrow0$ with $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n\to\rho\in(0,\infty)$, define

$$\tau_{\ell n}=\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n,\qquad A_{\ell n}=\exp(\tau_{\ell n}/\varepsilon_n),\qquad p_{\ell n}=q_\ell\varepsilon_n^{2r}A_{\ell n}^{-2c},$$

and use the Gaussian-plus-two-atom law from the separated-scale theorem. Let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$.

Write $\phi_a=(x_j^a/a!)_j$, $v=(e^{\tau x_j})_j$, and $\dot v=(x_je^{\tau x_j})_j$. Let $P$ project orthogonally away from

$$L=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_r,v\},\qquad g=P\phi_{r+1},\qquad b=P\dot v.$$

For $G\sim N(0,1)$, center $Z_a=|G|^c(\log|G|)^a$ and define the positive Gaussian residual variance

$$h_{r+1}=\left\|Z_{r+1}-\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}\{Z_0,\ldots,Z_r\}}Z_{r+1}\right\|_{L^2}^2.$$

The critical matrix is

$$N_\rho=h_{r+1}gg^\top+\frac{q_1q_2}{Q}\rho^2bb^\top.$$

It has rank two. If $\nu_1\ge\nu_2>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+2+j}(K_n)=\nu_j\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)}),\qquad j=1,2.$$

The first $r$ Gaussian coefficients and the two primary order-$\varepsilon_n^{2r}$ coefficients remain those of the preceding theorem. Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)})$.

Proof

Gram–Schmidt expansion of the centered Gaussian powers leaves, after the leading space $L$ is removed, the feature $\varepsilon_n^{r+1}\sqrt{h_{r+1}},g$. Its covariance contribution is the first term of $N_\rho$.

Weighted centering cancels the common tail derivative. The orthogonal label contrast leaves $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n\sqrt{q_1q_2/Q},b$, whose covariance converges after division by $\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)}$ to the second term of $N_\rho$.

The two latent features are orthogonal in the asymptotic Gram factorization, so their covariance matrices add. The vectors $g$ and $b$ are independent: otherwise a combination of $1,x,\ldots,x^{r+1},e^{\tau x},xe^{\tau x}$ would vanish at $k\ge r+4$ nodes, contradicting the extended Chebyshev property.

Six-output certificate

Take $c=\tau=q_1=1$, $q_2=2$, $x=(-5,-3,-1,1,3,5)/2$, $r=2$, $\rho=3/2$, and $\varepsilon_n=1/n$. The two critical sixth-order coefficients are

$$(\nu_1,\nu_2)=(0.263605382159052,0.000140525839569).$$

At $n=2048$, the exact coefficient ratios are $(0.997043033331,1.000968037032)$. The certificate also checks both primary fourth-order modes and full rank of the concrete confluent evaluation matrix.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for the displayed triangular laws at one fixed positive maturity. It gives a limiting critical block, not a finite-$n$ error bound or a uniform statement over moving $\rho$.

The regimes $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n\to\infty$ and $\rho\in(0,\infty)$ are now separated. The theorem does not address $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n\to0$, and it changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank after integrating distinct maturity loadings.

Gautschi treats confluent-Vandermonde geometry, and Kato supplies the finite-dimensional self-adjoint perturbation framework. Karlin and Studden supply the zero-count argument.
