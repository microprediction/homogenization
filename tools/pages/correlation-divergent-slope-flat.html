Divergent-slope covariance

A logarithmically stable slope is enough for the Green–Kubo correction.

Regular variation of the full profile is unnecessary in the steep slow-profile phase. The same second-order covariance law holds whenever the derivative diverges but changes negligibly across its logarithmic saddle window, including the previously missing index-one profiles.

The theorem

Use the fixed-maturity finite-chain and tangent-gauge setup of the slow-profile theorem. Let $h$ be eventually increasing and $C^2$, put $d=h'$, and assume

$$d(x)\to\infty,\qquad \log d(x)=o(x),$$

together with the logarithmic saddle-window condition: for every fixed $C>0$,

$$[\log d(x)]^2\sup_{|u-x|\le C\log d(x)} \left|\frac{h''(u)}{h'(u)}\right|\longrightarrow0.$$

Put $n=mT$, $L_n=\tfrac12\log n$ and $d_n=d(L_n)$. For every fixed $p>0$, uniformly over initial priors,

$$-\log\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T})^p =p h(L_n)-\frac p2d_n\{\log[p\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)d_n]-1\}+o(d_n).$$

Consequently,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T})) =2h(L_n)-d_n\{\log[2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)d_n]-1\}+o(d_n),$$
$$-\log\frac{(\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r})^2}{\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r}^2} =d_n\log2+o(d_n).$$

Why the condition matches the saddle

At the moment saddle, write $W_{m,T}=\sqrt{d_n/n}\,y$. The finite-chain moderate-deviation principle has speed $d_n$, while the tangent gauge gives

$$\log\frac1{r(W_{m,T})} =L_n-\frac12\log d_n-\log\rho(y)+o(1).$$

The shift from $L_n$ is therefore $O(\log d_n)$. The saddle-window condition makes the Taylor remainder $o(d_n)$ uniformly on the compact sets used in the contraction, so

$$h(\log(1/r(W_{m,T}))) =h(L_n)-d_n\{\tfrac12\log d_n+\log\rho(y)\}+o(d_n).$$

Contracting against $y^\top\Gamma^+y/2$ on $\operatorname{Ran}\Gamma$ gives the stated moment formula. The separation $d_n\log2\to\infty$ then makes the squared mean negligible relative to the second moment.

The missing index-one family

The assumptions include the regular-variation profiles of the preceding theorem, but also $h(x)=x\ell(x)$ when $\ell$ is a sufficiently smooth, slowly varying function with $\ell(x)\to\infty$. Thus arbitrary finite chains and gauges inherit the scalar telegraph leading laws from the prior note.

For the concrete profile $h(x)=x\log(1+x)$,

$$d(x)=\log(1+x)+\frac{x}{1+x},$$

and the variance expansion becomes

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}=2L_n\log(1+L_n) -d_n\{\log[2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)d_n]-1\}+o(d_n).$$

This gives the Green–Kubo correction that a leading equivalence $-\log\operatorname{Var}\sim2L_n\log(1+L_n)$ cannot identify.

Literature and scope

Karamata (1933) and de Bruijn (1959) supply the slow-variation and Laplace context. Gärtner (1977) and Ney and Nummelin (1987) supply the spectral Markov framework.

This remains a fixed-feature, fixed-positive-maturity scalar variance result. Singular $\Gamma$ is restricted to its range, and no rank after integrating distinct maturity loadings is claimed.
