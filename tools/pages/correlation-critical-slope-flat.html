Critical-slope covariance

At finite logarithmic slope, a Gaussian functional replaces the moderate-deviation saddle.

The second-order formula for steep slow profiles cannot be continued to bounded slope. First and second moments then remain on the central-limit scale, and their difference must be retained exactly. This is the regularly varying, generally Hölder boundary rather than a nontrivial smooth-flat phase.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain and tangent-gauge setup of the preceding theorem. Suppose $h$ is eventually increasing and continuously differentiable with

$$h'(x)\longrightarrow c\in(0,\infty).$$

Let $G\sim N(0,\Gamma)$, including its possibly singular law on $\operatorname{Ran}\Gamma$. For every fixed $p>0$, uniformly over arbitrary initial regime priors,

$$e^{p h(L_n)}\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T})^p \longrightarrow\mathbb E\rho(G)^{pc},\qquad L_n=\tfrac12\log(mT).$$

Consequently, with $V_{c,\rho,\Gamma}=\operatorname{Var}(\rho(G)^c)>0$,

$$e^{2h(L_n)}\operatorname{Var}(\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T}))\longrightarrow V_{c,\rho,\Gamma},$$ $$-\log\operatorname{Var}=2h(L_n)-\log V_{c,\rho,\Gamma}+o(1).$$

For the exactly linear profile $h(t)=ct$, this says $(mT)^c\operatorname{Var}(r(W_{m,T})^c)\to V_{c,\rho,\Gamma}$.

Why the phase changes

The finite-chain central limit theorem gives $\sqrt{mT}W_{m,T}\Rightarrow G$. On every fixed compact set, the tangent relation and the mean-value theorem give

$$e^{h(L_n)}\Psi_{h,r}(z/\sqrt{mT})\longrightarrow\rho(z)^c.$$

Spectral-gap concentration controls the complement and gives convergence of the first two moments. Subtracting them yields the variance constant without an exponential moment-separation argument.

This also explains the boundary of the preceding theorem: its separation scale is $h'(L_n)\log2$. When $h'(L_n)\to c$, that scale stays finite and cannot justify discarding the squared mean.

An explicit counterexample

For the scalar symmetric telegraph chain, $\Gamma=1$, $\rho(w)=|w|$ and $c=1$. The exact constant is

$$V_{1,|\cdot|,1}=\operatorname{Var}|N(0,1)|=1-\frac2\pi=0.363380228.$$

Thus the additive constant in $-\log\operatorname{Var}$ is $1.012305534$. Naively substituting bounded $h'=1$ into the steep-profile correction gives $1-\log2=0.306852819$, an error of $0.705452714$.

Literature and scope

Ney and Nummelin (1987, Part I) develop eigenvalue-based limit theory for Markov additive processes. Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir (2010) derive the Bessel occupation densities used by the certificate.

This is a fixed-feature, fixed-maturity scalar variance theorem. The rank of the fixed $\Gamma$ only specifies the support of $G$; no covariance rank after integrating distinct maturity loadings is claimed.

When $h'(L_n)\to0$, the normalized Gaussian functional itself becomes deterministic. The vanishing-slope theorem identifies its log-gauge fluctuation.
