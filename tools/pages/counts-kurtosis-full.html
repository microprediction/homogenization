A fourth cumulant that changes sign exactly once

Let a stationary two-state chain have rates $\alpha>0$ from state 1 to state 2 and $\beta>0$ in the reverse direction. Put $r=\alpha+\beta$, $p=\beta/r$, $w=p(1-p)$, $x=rT$ and $\Delta=\ell_1-\ell_2\neq0$.

For nonnegative intensities, $N_T\mid\Lambda_T\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda_T)$ with $\Lambda_T=\int_0^T\ell(Y_t)\,dt$. The result below concerns $\kappa_4(\Lambda_T)=\kappa_4^{\rm F}(N_T)$, the fourth factorial cumulant of the count, not its ordinary fourth cumulant.

Exact formula and complete sign classification

$$B(x)=x-3+\left(\frac{x^2}{2}+2x+3\right)e^{-x},\qquad C(x)=x-\frac52+2(x+1)e^{-x}+\frac12e^{-2x},$$
$$\kappa_4(\Lambda_T)=\frac{24\Delta^4w}{r^4} \{B(x)-w[4B(x)+C(x)]\},\qquad q(x)=\frac{B(x)}{4B(x)+C(x)}.$$ $$q'(x)>0,\qquad q(0+)=\frac16,\qquad q(\infty)=\frac15.$$

Both $B$ and $C$ are positive for $x>0$, so the sign is exactly that of $q(x)-w$.

In the middle range, $T_*=q^{-1}(w)/r$ is the unique crossing. Equal switching rates have $p=1/2$, $w=1/4$ and recover the strictly negative symmetric result. Boundedness alone determines none of these signs.

How the crossing meets the two thresholds

Let $x_*=rT_*$ and first approach the lower threshold with $\delta=w-1/6\downarrow0$. Direct Taylor expansion and series inversion give

$$q(x)=\frac16+\frac{x}{90}-\frac{x^2}{540}+\frac{x^3}{6300}+O(x^4),$$ $$x_*=90\delta+1350\delta^2+\frac{210600}{7}\delta^3+O(\delta^4).$$

The crossing therefore emerges linearly from zero maturity.

At the upper threshold, let $\eta=1/5-w\downarrow0$. Removing the exponentially small terms from $B$ and $C$ gives

$$q_0(x)=\frac{x-3}{5x-29/2},\qquad \frac15-q_0(x)=\frac{1}{50x-145},\qquad q(x)-q_0(x)=O(xe^{-x}).$$

Inverting this relation yields the sharp divergent scale

$$x_*=\frac{1}{50\eta}+\frac{29}{10} +O\!\left(\eta^{-3}e^{-1/(50\eta)}\right),\qquad T_*=\frac{x_*}{r}.$$

Thus the single crossing moves to infinity reciprocally as $w$ approaches $1/5$ from below. These are endpoint statements for the stationary two-state crossing, not maturity-uniform approximations for other initial laws or larger chains.

Derivation from ordered correlations

Write $f_t=\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=1\}}-p$ and $J_T=\int_0^T f_t\,dt$. Then $\mathbb E(f_{t+s}\mid Y_t)=e^{-rs}f_t$ and $f_t^2=(1-2p)f_t+w$.

For $t_1<t_2<t_3<t_4$, repeated conditioning gives

$$\mathbb E(f_{t_1}f_{t_2}f_{t_3}f_{t_4}) =w^2e^{-r(t_2-t_1+t_4-t_3)} +w(1-4w)e^{-r(t_4-t_1)}.$$

Let $H(x)=x-1+e^{-x}$ and $A(x)=x^2/2-2x+3-(x+3)e^{-x}$. Integrating the two ordered-gap terms, with their $4!$ orderings, yields

$$\mathbb E J_T^2=\frac{2w}{r^2}H(x),\qquad \mathbb E J_T^4=\frac{24}{r^4}\{w^2A(x)+w(1-4w)B(x)\}.$$

For example, the gap integrals defining $A$ and $B$ have Laplace transforms $1/[z^3(z+1)^2]$ and $1/[z^2(z+1)^3]$, respectively. Subtracting $3(\mathbb EJ_T^2)^2$, using $C=H^2/2-A$, gives the displayed cumulant. A deterministic shift $\ell_2T$ contributes nothing to this fourth cumulant.

Why the crossing is unique

The initial values and first derivatives of $B,C$ vanish at zero, and

$$B''(x)=\frac{x^2}{2}e^{-x}>0,\qquad C''(x)=2e^{-x}(x-1+e^{-x})>0,$$ $$\frac{C''(x)}{B''(x)} =4\frac{x-1+e^{-x}}{x^2} =4\int_0^1(1-u)e^{-xu}\,du.$$

The last integral strictly decreases with $x$. Since $C'/B'$ is a weighted average of $C''/B''$, its derivative is strictly negative; applying the same argument once more makes $C/B$ strictly decreasing.

Taylor expansion gives $B(x)\sim x^4/24$ and $C(x)\sim x^4/12$ at zero. At infinity both functions are asymptotic to $x$. Thus $C/B$ decreases strictly from two to one, proving the asserted behavior of $q=1/(4+C/B)$.

As checks on the two endpoints,

$$\lim_{T\downarrow0}\frac{\kappa_4(\Lambda_T)}{T^4} =\Delta^4w(1-6w),\qquad \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\kappa_4(\Lambda_T)}{T} =\frac{24\Delta^4w(1-5w)}{r^3}.$$

The endpoint limits alone would not establish uniqueness of a crossing. The monotone-ratio argument does.

Certificate, literature and scope

For $p=0.25$, $r=1$, $\Delta=1$, the crossing is $T_*=3.0464755667$. The integrated-rate fourth cumulants at $T=1$ and $T=5$ are $-0.0068086443$ and $0.2656789881$.

Two-state occupation-time laws are classical; see Pedler (1971). The moment identities and sign classification are proved directly here, without a priority claim.

Stationary initialization and two states are assumptions of the classification. No analogous sign assertion is made for arbitrary initial priors or larger chains. If $\Delta=0$, the integrated intensity is deterministic and every fourth factorial cumulant is zero.
