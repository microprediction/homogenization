Second-order grid bias, and a negative-rate trap

Uniform-grid approximations to stationary ordered occupation moments have quadratic population bias, not the first-order error of individual path integrals. Ignoring that bias can nevertheless produce negative reconstructed transition rates with no sampling noise. An explicit moment budget permits honest finite-sample use; a branch condition also permits exact population correction.

Aligned left-endpoint windows

Let $Y$ be a stationary finite irreducible row-generator chain with known state labels and time unit. Suppose a justified bound $a\ge\max_x(-Q_{xx})$ is available, and fix $h>0$, an integer $m\ge1$, and $T=mh$. Define exact and gridded state occupations by

$$O_j=\int_{jT}^{(j+1)T}e_{Y_t}dt,\qquad \widetilde O_j=h\sum_{b=0}^{m-1}e_{Y_{jT+bh}}.$$

Write $A_i=\mathbb E[O_0O_{i+1}^T]$ and $G_i=\mathbb E[\widetilde O_0\widetilde O_{i+1}^T]$, $i=0,1$. These are ordered raw moments, not centered covariances; rows refer to the earlier window. With $D=\operatorname{diag}\pi$, $P=e^{hQ}$ and $S_m=\sum_{b=0}^{m-1}P^b$,

$$G_0=Dh^2PS_m^2,\qquad G_1=G_0P^m,\qquad G_i\mathbf1=T^2\pi^T.$$

A deterministic population mesh budget

$$\boxed{\|G_i-A_i\|_F\le\eta_{\rm mesh}:=T^2h^2a^2/3,\qquad i=0,1.}$$

This holds at every $h,m$, without a spectral branch assumption or knowing $\pi$. It is a population-moment bound, not a pathwise quadrature bound.

For each pair of mesh cells the exact transition time is $\ell+v-u$, where $\ell=(i+1)T+(b-c)h\ge h$ and $u,v\in[0,h]$. Taylor expansion at $\ell$ has first-order integral zero, since $\int_0^h\int_0^h(v-u)dudv=0$.

All intermediate times are nonnegative, so $\|e^{tQ}\|_\infty=1$ bounds the remainder by $\tfrac12\|Q\|_\infty^2(v-u)^2$. Since $\int_0^h\int_0^h(v-u)^2dudv=h^4/6$, summing the $m^2$ cell pairs gives $\|D^{-1}(G_i-A_i)\|_\infty\le T^2h^2\|Q\|_\infty^2/12$.

Rowwise $\ell^2\le\ell^1$ and $\|\pi\|_2\le1$ convert this to the Frobenius bound, using $\|Q\|_\infty\le2a$. The symmetric two-state chain flipping at rate $1/2$, with $T=1$, has $h^{-2}\|G_0-A_0\|_F\to(1-e^{-1})^2/24\approx0.016649017$, so quadratic order cannot generally be improved.

What the uncorrected population inverse actually returns

The raw-moment inverse recovers $\pi^T=T^{-2}G_0\mathbf1$ and $E=G_0^{-1}G_1=e^{TQ}$. If these grid moments are wrongly treated as exact continuous occupations, then for any shift $\lambda>0$ it forms

$$Z_h=(I-E)^2G_0^{-1}D+\lambda^2\Pi =\frac4{h^2}\sinh^2(hQ/2)+\lambda^2\Pi,\qquad \Pi=\mathbf1\pi.$$

The identity follows from $(I-P)S_m=I-P^m$, $G_0^{-1}D=(h^2PS_m^2)^{-1}$, and commutation of these functions of $Q$. The matrix $S_m$ is invertible because the nonstationary eigenvalues of $P$ have modulus less than one.

If $ha<\pi$, Gershgorin's disks give $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le a$ for every eigenvalue $z$ of $Q$. Therefore $-2\sinh(hQ/2)/h+\lambda\Pi$ has spectrum strictly in the open right half-plane and is the principal square root of $Z_h$. The naive inverse is exactly

$$Q_h=\lambda\Pi-(Z_h)^{1/2}_{\rm principal}=\frac2h\sinh(hQ/2),$$ $$\|Q_h-Q\|_\infty\le\frac2h\{\sinh(ha)-ha\} \le\frac{h^2a^3}{3}\cosh(ha).$$

The bound follows termwise from the odd power series and is independent of the window length $T$. For the symmetric two-state chain flipping at rate $a$, $Q=-2a(I-\Pi)$ and $\|I-\Pi\|_\infty=1$, making the first inequality an equality. Neither this formula nor its branch choice is asserted on arbitrarily coarse grids.

An exact negative-rate counterexample

Take the unit directed three-cycle $Q=C-I$, $C_{12}=C_{23}=C_{31}=1$, and $h=1/2$. Then $Q^3=3C-3C^2$, so a missing reverse-edge entry of $Q_h$ equals $-h^2/8$ plus a remainder bounded by $h^4\cosh(h)/60$, using the odd power series and $\|Q\|_\infty=2$.

The elementary series bound $\cosh(1/2)\le8/7$ yields the exact enclosure

$$-\frac{109}{3360}\le(Q_h)_{13}\le-\frac{101}{3360}<-0.03.$$

The computed entry is approximately $-0.0315429653$, regardless of the integer window size $m$. More samples at this fixed grid spacing do not remove the population bias, and the uncorrected answer is not a Markov generator.

Population correction and finite-sample use

Under the same sufficient condition $ha<\pi$, analytic matrix functional calculus gives

$$Q=\frac2h\operatorname{arsinh}(hQ_h/2),$$

because the principal scalar inverse satisfies $\operatorname{arsinh}(\sinh z)=z$ for $|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi/2$. This identity extends to Jordan blocks; it is a population correction, not an unproved finite-sample error bound for a nonlinear inverse.

Alternatively, retain the continuous-occupation target and inflate its moment budgets. For a fixed number $N\ge1$ of consecutive triple-window blocks, the grid products still lie entrywise in $[0,T^2]$, so the raw block-reward confidence proof applies unchanged around $G_i$.

Given a fixed certified Dobrushin bound $0\le\alpha<1$ for $e^{3TQ}$, put $c_0=0$, $c_{j+1}=1+\alpha c_j$ and $H_N=c_N^2+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1+c_j)^2$. For a fixed $0<\delta<1$, valid simultaneous budgets for the exact continuous targets at confidence $1-\delta$ are

$$\eta_0=\eta_1=\frac{nT^2}{N}\sqrt{\frac{H_N}{2}\log\frac{4n^2}{\delta}} +\frac{T^2h^2a^2}{3}.$$

The triangle inequality adds population mesh bias to sampling error; no pathwise jump-count estimate is needed. These budgets can feed the residual-aware reconstruction test, including its stationary-law uncertainty, with possible abstention. The probability of reporting an incorrect certified radius is at most $\delta$, not a guarantee conditional on reporting.

Checks, literature and scope

The certificate checks $171$ grid cases on $19$ chains, including nonuniform, sparse, reversible and defective examples. Independent continuous-time Feynman–Kac moments, direct discrete lag sums, compact matrix identities, generator bias bounds and the analytic correction agree. The negative entry has an exact rational Taylor-remainder enclosure; floating comparisons are not interval proofs.

The principal-root tool is classical; see Higham (2008), Chapter 6. Fixed-spacing embedding nonuniqueness is classical too; see Davies (2010), Section 2. Since the grid laws depend only on $P$ and $\pi$, aggregation alone cannot universally resolve snapshot aliasing without additional restrictions.

The cancellation uses stationary starts and identical, aligned uniform left-endpoint grids in both windows. No arbitrary-prior, irregular-grid, noisy-label, hidden-state, optimal sampling-rate, or uniform-in-generator conditioning claim is made. The grid and record length are fixed before observation; exit-rate and mixing bounds need independent justification, and arithmetic errors need their own enclosures.
