Multiple rare tails promote multiple Gram modes

The number of distinct tail slopes is the number of extra eigenvalues lifted at one scale.

A single rare atom adds one direction to the coalescing Gram staircase. Several atoms with distinct logarithmic slopes add independent directions, so the rank promoted at one scale can be counted exactly.

Multi-tail rank theorem

Fix $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, $1\le r\le k-2$, and $1\le s\le k-r-1$. Let

$$P_n=\left(1-\sum_{\ell=1}^s p_{\ell n}\right)\mathcal L(G) +\sum_{\ell=1}^s p_{\ell n}\delta_{A_{\ell n}},\qquad G\sim N(0,1).$$

Suppose $\varepsilon_n\downarrow0$, every $p_{\ell n}\to0$, and, for distinct $0<\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_s$ and positive $q_\ell$,

$$\varepsilon_n\log A_{\ell n}\longrightarrow\tau_\ell,\qquad \frac{p_{\ell n}A_{\ell n}^{2c}}{\varepsilon_n^{2r}}\longrightarrow q_\ell.$$

Let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$. With $\phi_a=(x_j^a/a!)_j$, define

$$E_r=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r-1}\},\quad N_r=E_r^\perp,\quad a_r=\frac{P_r\phi_r}{\|P_r\phi_r\|_2},$$

where $P_r$ projects onto $N_r$. For $v_\tau=(e^{\tau x_j})_j$, set $w_\ell=P_rv_{\tau_\ell}$ and

$$M_r=c_{r+1}a_ra_r^\top+\sum_{\ell=1}^s q_\ell w_\ell w_\ell^\top,$$

where $c_{r+1}$ is the Gaussian coefficient from the exact coalescing-Gram theorem. If $\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_{s+1}>0$ are the nonzero eigenvalues of $M_r$, then

$$\frac{\lambda_j(K_n)}{\varepsilon_n^{2(j-1)}}\longrightarrow c_j\quad(1\le j\le r),$$ $$\lambda_{r+j}(K_n)=\mu_j\varepsilon_n^{2r} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}),\qquad 1\le j\le s+1.$$

Thus $s$ distinct rare-tail slopes promote exactly $s$ additional eigenvalues to order $\varepsilon_n^{2r}$. Any remaining trailing eigenvalues are $o(\varepsilon_n^{2r})$.

Proof

Schur-complement elimination of $E_r$ leaves the Gaussian block $c_{r+1}a_ra_r^\top$ after division by $\varepsilon_n^{2r}$. The mixture covariance contributes $\sum_\ell q_\ell w_\ell w_\ell^\top$.

Terms involving two rare-component weights vanish at this scale. After division by $\varepsilon_n^{2r}$, their self and cross pieces are bounded by a constant times $p_{\ell n}+p_{mn}+\sqrt{p_{\ell n}p_{mn}}$, which tends to zero.

The vectors $a_r,w_1,\ldots,w_s$ are independent. Otherwise a nonzero combination of $1,x,\ldots,x^r,e^{\tau_1x},\ldots,e^{\tau_sx}$ would vanish at the $k\ge r+s+1$ distinct nodes, contradicting the extended Chebyshev property of exponential polynomials.

Hence $M_r$ has rank $s+1$. Weyl's inequality preserves the first $r$ coefficients, while spectral separation identifies the next $s+1$ eigenvalues with the positive spectrum of $M_r$.

Six-output, two-tail certificate

Take $c=1$, $x=(-5,-3,-1,1,3,5)/2$, $r=2$, $(\tau_1,\tau_2)=(1/2,1)$, $q_1=q_2=1$, and $\varepsilon_n=1/n$. The three predicted fourth-order coefficients are

$$(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3) =(32.259604734011018,0.072074279055711,0.000326948154050).$$

At $n=1024$, the exact coefficient ratios are $(0.997059926274,0.998576960229,0.996738769007)$. The certificate also checks the rank-three limiting block, the two untouched leading coefficients, and full rank of the concrete Chebyshev evaluation matrix.

Scope and literature

This is a triangular-law theorem for nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained by integrating distinct maturity loadings.

The remainders are pointwise along the stated sequences; no finite-$n$ rate is claimed without quantitative convergence rates. Coincident tail slopes merge at this scale, while their missing mode splits quadratically at a smaller scale.

Karlin and Studden develop the extended Chebyshev theory used for independence. Kato supplies the self-adjoint perturbation framework.
