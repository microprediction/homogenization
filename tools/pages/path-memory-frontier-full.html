The exact variance cost of suppressing start memory

For a two-state chain, the minimum stationary path variance at a prescribed mean-memory budget is explicit at every rate and horizon. Tight budgets remove the start of the path, not just its endpoint weight.

Setup: positive weights, including endpoint observations

Rescale time by the relaxation rate: $R=r\Delta>0$, and let $X_x\in\{0,1\}$ have row generator $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-p&p\\1-p&-(1-p)\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $p\in(0,1)$. For a deterministic probability measure $\nu$ on $[0,R]$, define

$$A_\nu=\int(X_x-p)\,\nu(dx),\qquad L(\nu)=\int e^{-x}\nu(dx),\qquad E(\nu)=\iint e^{-|x-y|}\nu(dx)\nu(dy).$$

The measure can combine a density with point observations; $\delta_t$ denotes unit mass at time $t$. Exact conditional means and stationary covariance give

$$\mathbb E_i A_\nu=(i-p)L(\nu),\qquad \operatorname{Var}_\pi A_\nu=p(1-p)E(\nu).$$

For $S=\mu A_\nu+\sigma Z$, independent $Z\sim N(0,1)$ and $\sigma>0$, write $F_i$ for its start-$i$ CDF and $F_\pi=(1-p)F_0+pF_1$. A common rate-one Bernoulli refresh clock couples any start to stationarity at its first refresh $\tau$.

Before $\tau$ the state difference is $i-J$, where $J\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$; afterwards it is zero. Thus $\mathbb E|A_i-A_\pi|=|i-p|L(\nu)$, and the Gaussian CDF Lipschitz bound proves

$$\sup_q|F_i(q)-F_\pi(q)|\le \frac{|\mu|\,|i-p|}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}L(\nu).$$

This includes the unique pooled population quantile, which has exact stationary marginal coverage. The optimization below constrains exact mean memory and this coverage bound, not the actual coverage gap.

The finite-rate frontier

The unconstrained endpoint-plus-density design already appears in the calibration design note. Here the added constraint is an explicit budget on latent-start memory.

For $0\le\ell\le1$, minimize $E(\nu)$ over probability measures with $L(\nu)\le\ell$. Budgets below $e^{-R}$ are infeasible; at $\ell=e^{-R}$ the only feasible measure is $\delta_R$, with energy one. Put $\ell_0=2/(R+2)$ and $\ell_1=1/(R+1)$.

$$E_*(\ell)=\begin{cases} 2/(R+2),&\ell\ge\ell_0,\\ \ell^2+2(1-\ell)^2/R,&\ell_1\le\ell\le\ell_0,\\ (2d+1)/(d+1)^2,&e^{-R}\le\ell\le\ell_1, \end{cases}$$

In the last line, $d=R-a$ and $a\in[0,R]$ is the unique solution of $\ell=e^{-a}/(R-a+1)$.

For $\ell\ge\ell_0$, the unique optimizer is $\nu_*=(dx+\delta_0+\delta_R)/(R+2)$ on $[0,R]$. The constraint is inactive above $\ell_0$, where $L(\nu_*)=\ell_0$.

For $\ell_1\le\ell\le\ell_0$, set $c=(1-\ell)/R$. The optimizer is $\nu_*=c\,dx+(\ell-c)\delta_0+c\delta_R$; its starting mass decreases to zero at $\ell_1$.

For $e^{-R}\le\ell\le\ell_1$, set $c=1/(R-a+1)$. The optimizer is $\nu_*=c\mathbf1_{[a,R]}dx+c\delta_R$, with no starting atom. Its memory decreases strictly as $a$ increases from zero to $R$, and the formulas agree at both transitions.

A global optimality certificate

Let $u_*(x)=\int e^{-|x-y|}\nu_*(dy)$. In every case there are constants $\lambda\ge0$, $\eta$ such that $u_*+\lambda e^{-x}\ge\eta$ everywhere, with equality on the support of $\nu_*$, and $\lambda(L(\nu_*)-\ell)=0$.

For the first case take $\eta=2/(R+2)$ and $\lambda=0$. For the middle case, $u_*=2c-(2c-\ell)e^{-x}$, so take $\eta=2c$, $\lambda=2c-\ell\ge0$.

For the last case take $\eta=2c$, $\lambda=ce^a$. On $[a,R]$, $u_*(x)=c(2-e^{-(x-a)})$; on $[0,a)$, $u_*(x)+\lambda e^{-x}=2c\cosh(a-x)\ge2c$.

For any feasible competitor $\nu$, the mass and memory constraints imply $\int u_*d\nu\ge\eta-\lambda\ell=E(\nu_*)$. Writing $\rho=\nu-\nu_*$ yields the quantitative certificate

$$E(\nu)-E(\nu_*)=E(\rho)+2\int u_*d\rho\ge E(\rho)\ge0.$$

The exponential kernel is strictly positive definite on finite signed measures: $E(\rho)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}2|\widehat\rho(\omega)|^2/(1+\omega^2)\,d\omega$. Equality forces $\rho=0$, proving uniqueness; integrating the displayed potentials gives the frontier.

Exponential forgetting at a finite variance factor

Fix $0<\beta<1$ and use the last optimizer with $a=\beta R$, $d=(1-\beta)R$. Then exactly

$$L(\nu_*)=\frac{e^{-\beta R}}{(1-\beta)R+1},\qquad E(\nu_*)=\frac{2(1-\beta)R+1}{((1-\beta)R+1)^2},\qquad \frac{E(\nu_*)}{2/(R+2)}\longrightarrow\frac1{1-\beta}.$$

Thus a fixed early-time cutoff allows exponentially small latent-start coverage bounds with a finite relative cost in the path component of stationary variance. This is not the fixed polynomial-ramp expansion; the weights here depend on the chosen rate and budget.

Every optimizer has positive mass at $R$, so if weights must be $L^2$ densities with no atoms, the same frontier is an unattained infimum for every $\ell>e^{-R}$. Approximate an optimizer at a slightly tighter budget by positive densities; continuity of the memory and bounded continuous covariance kernel preserves feasibility and convergence of energy. The budget $e^{-R}$ itself admits no density.

For a finite observation grid containing both endpoints with largest gap $h$, the optimal grid energy satisfies $0\le E_{\rm grid}-E_*\le h$. Move each point of $\nu_*$ to the next grid point: this can only decrease memory, while each displacement is in $[0,h]$, so pairwise distances and hence kernel values change by at most $h$.

Checks, literature and scope

At $R=8$, the transition budgets are $0.2$ and $0.1111111111$. The minimum normalized energies at budgets $0.05$ and $0.005$ are $0.2317899808$ and $0.3368964877$.

The certificate checks mass, memory, potentials and energy on 32 frontier cases, independently computes means and variances with 96 regime-resolved moment semigroups, and compares four constrained grid optimizations. Four Gaussian-smoothed pooled-quantile calculations check the coverage bound under Fourier refinement; these numerical checks are not interval enclosures.

The endpoint-atom/density structure and potential criterion belong to classical correlated-observation design; see Dette, Pepelyshev and Zhigljavsky (2016), Theorems 2.1 and 2.3 and Appendix A.4. The unconstrained formula is the exponential-kernel location-model specialization, not a novelty claim.

The added memory constraint is treated self-containedly above with nonnegative measures. This is an exact stationary variance frontier, not an optimization of quantile width, predictive accuracy, calibration dependence, or distribution-free feature-conditional coverage. Weights and the score map are deterministic and frozen; finite calibration and estimated rates require separate analysis.
