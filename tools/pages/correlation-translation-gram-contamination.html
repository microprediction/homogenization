Rare contamination and translation-Gram limits

Weak convergence and one fixed moment do not control a coalescing nonlinear-output Gram.

A vanishing atom can disappear from the limiting law and from the covariance at one exponent, yet survive exactly on the smallest-eigenvalue scale when two exponents coalesce. This gives a sharp obstruction to replacing local uniform integrability by fixed-exponent convergence.

Contamination theorem

Fix $c>0$. Let $G$ be standard Gaussian and let $X_n$ have law

$$P_n=(1-p_n)\mathcal L(G)+p_n\delta_{A_n},\qquad A_n\longrightarrow\infty,\quad p_n\longrightarrow0.$$

Take $\varepsilon_n\downarrow0$ and suppose, for finite $\tau,q\ge0$, that

$$\varepsilon_n\log A_n\longrightarrow\tau,\qquad \frac{p_nA_n^{2c}}{\varepsilon_n^2}\longrightarrow q.$$

Let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of the symmetric power pair $|X_n|^{c-\varepsilon_n/2}$ and $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_n/2}$. Define

$$V_c=\operatorname{Var}(|G|^c),\qquad D_c=\det\operatorname{Gram}\left(|G|^c-M_c,\ |G|^c\log|G|-M_c'\right).$$

Then

$$\frac{\lambda_{\min}(K_n)}{\varepsilon_n^2}\longrightarrow \frac{D_c}{2V_c}+q\{\cosh(\tau)-1\}.$$

The Gaussian coefficient is recovered only when $q=0$ or $\tau=0$. Total variation convergence $\|P_n-\mathcal L(G)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le p_n\to0$ therefore does not determine the joint coalescing limit.

A fixed-moment counterexample

Set $\varepsilon_n=1/n$, $A_n=e^n$, and $p_n=e^{-2cn}/n^2$. Then $\tau=q=1$ and

$$X_n\Rightarrow G,\qquad \mathbb E|X_n|^{2c}-\mathbb E|G|^{2c}=n^{-2}+o(n^{-2}).$$

Thus the fixed-$c$ covariance converges, even with its second moment, while the coalescing coefficient is not Gaussian. At $c=1$ the Gaussian term, contamination increment, and resulting coefficient are

$$(0.034370195591,\ 0.543080634815,\ 0.577450830406).$$

The exact mixture certificate gives $0.576932644247$ at $n=2048$, which is $0.999102631546$ of the contaminated limit but $16.7858411721$ times the Gaussian prediction.

Proof

For arbitrary exponents $a,b$, the mixture covariance has the exact decomposition

$$\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(|X_n|^a,|X_n|^b) =(1-p_n)K_G(a,b)+p_n(1-p_n)(A_n^a-M_a)(A_n^b-M_b).$$

At $a_i=c+\varepsilon_nx_i$, $x=(-1/2,1/2)$, the second term divided by $\varepsilon_n^2$ converges to $qvv^\top$, where $v=(e^{-\tau/2},e^{\tau/2})^\top$. Terms containing a Gaussian moment vanish because $A_n^c\to\infty$.

The leading Gaussian Gram is $V_c\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$, whose null unit vector is $u=(1,-1)^\top/\sqrt2$. The Gaussian second-order eigenvalue coefficient is $D_c/(2V_c)$, and the added coefficient is

$$q\,u^\top vv^\top u=\frac q2(e^{\tau/2}-e^{-\tau/2})^2=q\{\cosh(\tau)-1\}.$$

This proves the theorem by the two-dimensional symmetric eigenvalue formula, or equivalently by continuous perturbation of its simple zero eigenvalue.

Which hypothesis fails

For the concrete sequence, the rare-atom contributions to the derivative Gram of the centered map $a\mapsto |X_n|^a-\mathbb E|X_n|^a$ have orders $n^{-2}$, $n^{-1}$, and $1$ in entries $(0,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(1,1)$. Hence the value covariance converges but the derivative Gram does not.

The uniform $L^2$ Taylor bound also fails: every fixed neighborhood to the right of $c$ sees exponentially growing rare-atom derivatives. The joint theorem's local derivative-Gram and Taylor assumptions exclude exactly this boundary-layer contamination.

Scope

This is a triangular-law counterexample for nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It neither changes fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor creates rank by integrating different maturity loadings.

Huber (1964) introduced the classical Gaussian contamination model. The coalescing spectral boundary and its exact coefficient are derived here.
