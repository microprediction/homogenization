Two boundary defects give a second-order independence bound

Let $Q$ be a finite irreducible row generator with stationary row $\pi$, $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$ and stationary reversal $Q^*=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)^{-1}Q^T\operatorname{diag}(\pi)$. A Cox process $N$ has nonnegative statewise intensities $\ell_i$ and hidden generator $mQ$, initialized in any probability row $\nu$.

Choose an envelope $\|e^{tQ}-\Pi\|_\infty\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}$; the stationary specialization also uses $\|e^{tQ^*}-\Pi\|_\infty\leq C_*e^{-\gamma_*t}$ for $t\geq0$. Put $\bar\ell=\pi\ell$, $F=\|\ell-\bar\ell\mathbf1\|_\infty$ and

$$d(L)=\min\left\{2,\frac{2CF}{m\gamma}(1-e^{-m\gamma L})\right\},\qquad d_*(L)=\min\left\{2,\frac{2C_*F}{m\gamma_*}(1-e^{-m\gamma_*L})\right\}.$$

For a partition $0=s_0<\cdots<s_r$ write $T_j=s_j-s_{j-1}$, $X_j=N_{s_j}-N_{s_{j-1}}$, $\sigma_j=\nu e^{ms_{j-1}Q}$ and $p_{\nu,j}=\mathcal L_{\sigma_j}(N_{T_j})$. Put $e(L)=\min\{2,4CF(1-e^{-m\gamma L})/(m\gamma)\}$.

$$d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathcal L_\nu(X_1,\ldots,X_r),\bigotimes_{j=1}^r p_{\nu,j}\right) \leq\min\left\{1,\frac12\sum_{j=2}^r e(s_{j-1})d(T_j)\right\} \leq\min\left\{1,\frac{4(r-1)C^2F^2}{m^2\gamma^2}\right\}.$$

This holds at every positive speed, uniformly over all initial priors and positive bin lengths, including bins shrinking with speed and unbounded total horizon. The second-order rate concerns a bounded number of bins; the displayed constant grows with $r$.

The product uses each bin's own unconditional transient marginal, not a filtered conditional law. For $\nu=\pi$, one can replace $e(s_{j-1})$ by $d_*(s_{j-1})$, recovering the sharper bound

$$d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathcal L_\pi(X_1,\ldots,X_r),\bigotimes_jp_{\pi,j}\right) \leq\min\left\{1,\frac{2(r-1)CC_*F^2}{m^2\gamma\gamma_*}\right\}.$$

Computable envelopes need no diagonalization: for $P_h=e^{hQ}$ set $\rho=\tfrac12\max_{i,j}\sum_k|(P_h)_{ik}-(P_h)_{jk}|$. For a nontrivial irreducible chain, $0<\rho<1$, and row contraction gives $\|e^{tQ}-\Pi\|_\infty\leq2\rho^{\lfloor t/h\rfloor}$. Thus $C=2/\rho$, $\gamma=-\log\rho/h$ work; apply the same construction separately to $Q^*$.

Initial-state memory of an entire count vector

For any finite bin schedule in a window of length $L$, let $u_i$ be its full count-vector law conditional on initial state $i$. We claim $\max_i\|u_i-\pi u\|_1\leq d(L)$, independently of the number of bins.

Build the window backwards from its right endpoint and let $B(t)=S_{j(t)}-I$, where $S_j$ shifts count coordinate $j$ up by one. On $\ell^1(\mathbb N^r)$, $\|B(t)\|\leq2$, and the backward count equation is

$$u'=mQu+\operatorname{diag}(\ell)B(t)u,\qquad u(0)=\mathbf1\delta_0.$$

Each row of $u(t)$ is a probability measure, so its norm in $\ell^\infty(\text{regimes};\ell^1(\text{counts}))$ is one. Write $w=(I-\Pi)u$ and $f=\ell-\bar\ell\mathbf1$.

$$w'=(mQ+\bar\ell B(t))w+(I-\Pi)\operatorname{diag}(f)B(t)u,\qquad w(0)=0.$$

The regime-independent propagator $\mathcal P(t,s)$ generated by $\bar\ell B(t)$ is a Poisson-convolution contraction on count measures and commutes with regime matrices. Duhamel's formula therefore gives

$$w(L)=\int_0^L(e^{mQ(L-t)}-\Pi)\mathcal P(L,t) \operatorname{diag}(f)B(t)u(t)\,dt,$$ $$\|w(L)\|_{\infty,1}\leq2CF\int_0^L e^{-m\gamma(L-t)}dt =\frac{2CF}{m\gamma}(1-e^{-m\gamma L}).$$

The trivial distance bound between probability measures is two, proving the claim. The same proof with $Q^*$ gives $d_*(L)$; no eigenvector expansion or positive intensity floor is used.

A stationary cut multiplies the two defects

At a cut, let $A$ and $B$ denote any count vectors on the past and future sides. Given the boundary regime, they are independent, and that regime has law $\pi$. Write their conditional laws as $a_i,b_i$ and their marginals as $a=\sum_i\pi_i a_i$, $b=\sum_i\pi_i b_i$.

$$\mathcal L(A,B)-a\otimes b =\sum_i\pi_i(a_i-a)\otimes(b_i-b).$$

Under stationarity the conditional past count law is the law for a chain started at $i$ with generator $mQ^*$ and the bin order reversed. The two memory bounds and the product rule for $\ell^1$ norms give

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(A,B),a\otimes b) \leq\frac12d_*(L_A)d(L_B).$$

For the first $j$ bins, separate bin $j$ from its prefix and apply this cut bound. Induction and invariance of TV under taking a product with a common probability law give the displayed sum over $j$, hence the theorem.

Forward endpoint memory for arbitrary priors

The general bound avoids conditioning on a possibly rare boundary state. For any bin schedule up to time $t$, let $\mathsf F_t$ be the row of count measures $\mathsf F_t(a,i)=\Pr_\nu(A_t=a,Y_t=i)$, with $A_t$ the accumulated count vector.

Write $\sigma_t=\nu e^{mtQ}$, $a_t=\mathsf F_t\mathbf1$, $\mathsf R_t=\mathsf F_t-a_t\sigma_t$ and $D_f=\operatorname{diag}(f)$. The count shifts now follow the forward bin schedule; the forward equation is

$$\mathsf F_t'=m\mathsf F_tQ+B(t)\{\mathsf F_t\operatorname{diag}(\ell)\},\qquad \mathsf F_0=\delta_0\nu.$$

Apply Duhamel around the constant intensity $\bar\ell$, using the same count-convolution contraction $\mathcal P$ as before. Subtract the marginal equation multiplied by $\sigma_L$, using $\sigma_L=\sigma_t e^{m(L-t)Q}$, to obtain exactly

$$\mathsf R_L=\int_0^L\mathcal P(L,t)B(t) \{\mathsf F_tD_f-(\mathsf F_tf)\sigma_t\} (e^{m(L-t)Q}-\Pi)\,dt.$$

The brace has zero regime sum for every count vector, justifying subtraction of $\Pi$. Its total $\ell^1$ norm over counts and regimes is at most $2F$, because $\mathsf F_t$ is a probability measure. Hence, uniformly over $\nu$,

$$\|\mathsf R_L\|_{1,1}\leq\min\left\{2,4CF\int_0^Le^{-m\gamma(L-t)}dt\right\} =e(L).$$

The last inequality also uses the trivial norm bound two for a difference of probability measures. At a cut with future conditional count laws $u_i$, the joint-minus-product measure is $\sum_i(\mathsf R_L)_i\otimes(u_i-\pi u)$, since $\mathsf R_L\mathbf1=0$.

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L_\nu(A,B),\mathcal L_\nu(A)\otimes\mathcal L_\nu(B)) \leq\tfrac12 e(L_A)d(L_B).$$

The initial-state memory lemma supplies $d(L_B)$ even though the actual future start is $\sigma_{L_A}$, not $\pi$: the stationary mixture is only a convenient reference canceled by the zero regime sum. Telescoping over cuts proves the all-prior theorem without any inverse prior mass or burn-in penalty.

The second-order rate is sharp

Take the symmetric base generator $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, intensities $(\bar\ell+\delta,\bar\ell-\delta)$ with $\bar\ell\geq|\delta|>0$, and stationary start. Observe two bins of lengths $A,B>0$, separated by an unobserved gap $g\geq0$, and let $D_m(g)$ be TV between their joint law and the product of their exact marginals.

Write $p_x$ for the Poisson probability sequence of mean $x$ and $M(x)=\max_k p_x(k)=p_x(\lfloor x\rfloor)$. Then the following finite-speed bound holds, including a zero intensity in one state:

$$\left|D_m(g)-\frac{e^{-2mg}\delta^2}{2m^2}M(\bar\ell A)M(\bar\ell B)\right| \leq e^{-2mg}\left\{\frac{\delta^2}{2m^2}(e^{-2mA}+e^{-2mB}) +\frac{\delta^4(A+B)}{m^3}\right\}.$$

Thus for fixed adjacent bins, $m^2D_m(0)\longrightarrow\delta^2M(\bar\ell A)M(\bar\ell B)/2>0$: the general $m^{-2}$ order cannot be improved. A fixed positive gap adds exponential suppression; the limiting coefficient is only asserted for fixed positive bin lengths.

For the proof let $u_\pm(T)$ be the bin-count laws conditional on the starting regime, $s_T=(u_+(T)+u_-(T))/2$ and $h_T=(u_+(T)-u_-(T))/2$. Reversal at the first endpoint and the gap transition $e^{mgQ}=\Pi+e^{-2mg}(I-\Pi)$ give the exact signed-measure identity

$$\mathcal L(X_1,X_2)-s_A\otimes s_B=e^{-2mg}h_A\otimes h_B, \qquad D_m(g)=\tfrac12e^{-2mg}\|h_A\|_1\|h_B\|_1.$$

On count measures write $B_0=S-I$, $\|B_0\|\leq2$, and $P_T=e^{\bar\ell T B_0}$ for the Poisson-convolution contraction. The backward equations reduce to

$$s'=\bar\ell B_0s+\delta B_0h,\qquad h'=(\bar\ell B_0-2m)h+\delta B_0s,\qquad s_0=\delta_0,\quad h_0=0.$$

Duhamel and $\|s_t\|_1=1$ give $\|h_T\|_1\leq|\delta|/m$ and $\|s_T-p_{\bar\ell T}\|_1\leq2\delta^2T/m$. Substituting the latter estimate in $h_T=\delta\int_0^Te^{-2m(T-t)}P_{T-t}B_0s_t\,dt$ yields

$$\left\|h_T-\frac{\delta}{2m}B_0p_{\bar\ell T}\right\|_1 \leq R_m(T):=\frac{|\delta|}{m}e^{-2mT}+\frac{2|\delta|^3T}{m^2}.$$

Indeed the Poisson part integrates exactly, since $P_{T-t}B_0p_{\bar\ell t}=B_0p_{\bar\ell T}$, and the remaining integral is at most $4|\delta|^3m^{-1}\int_0^Te^{-2m(T-t)}t\,dt\leq2|\delta|^3T/m^2$. Poisson unimodality gives $\|B_0p_x\|_1=2M(x)$ by telescoping successive differences.

Both $\|h_T\|_1$ and $\|\delta B_0p_{\bar\ell T}/(2m)\|_1$ are at most $|\delta|/m$. The reverse triangle inequality and the exact product formula therefore bound the TV error by $e^{-2mg}|\delta|\{R_m(A)+R_m(B)\}/(2m)$, proving the display.

For intensities $(3,1)$ and two unit bins the coefficient is $0.0366312778$. At speed 80 the leading term is $0.0000057236$, while the exact error is $0.0000057234$; the explicit absolute remainder bound is $0.0000039063$.

Replacing the marginals

If $\widetilde p_j$ are any probability laws with $d_{\rm TV}(p_{\nu,j},\widetilde p_j)\leq\eta_j$, product telescoping gives the explicit joint bound

$$d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathcal L_\nu(X_1,\ldots,X_r),\bigotimes_j\widetilde p_j\right) \leq\min\left\{1,\frac12\sum_{j=2}^r e(s_{j-1})d(T_j)+\sum_j\eta_j\right\}.$$

Consequently any uniform $O(m^{-2})$ marginal approximation gives a uniform $O(rm^{-2})$ grid approximation. The exact-moment negative-binomial marginal theorem is developed separately in companion PR #118; this note supplies the decoupling step, not another proof of that theorem.

Each bin's moment fit is made at its own length; the resulting grid surrogates are not asserted consistent under arbitrary refinements. This is not a full arrival-path TV theorem or a bound uniform over unbounded numbers of bins, and the original stationary specialization retains its sharper constant.

Certificate and background
