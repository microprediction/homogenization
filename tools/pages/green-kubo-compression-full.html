Which directed blocks fit a given chain?

When the feature embedding is unknown, the complete energy-normalized skew spectrum of a candidate chain gives an exact feasibility test. Its largest mode alone loses information; for one-way cycles the full test gives a closed minimum-state formula.

Fixed-chain realization theorem

Fix an irreducible finite row generator $Q$ on $N\ge2$ states and its stationary law $\pi$. Let $C=-D_\pi Q$, $m=N-1$, and choose any real orthonormal basis $T$ of $\mathbf1^\perp$.

$$M=T^\top C^\top T,\quad H=(M+M^\top)/2\succ0,\quad D=(M-M^\top)/2, \quad B_Q=H^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2}.$$

Indeed $u^\top Cu=\tfrac12\sum_{v\ne w}\pi_vq_{vw}(u_v-u_w)^2$; irreducibility makes this strictly positive on nonconstant potentials. Thus the whitening is nonsingular on the quotient by constants.

Write $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_m$ for the eigenvalues of the Hermitian matrix $iB_Q$. They occur in opposite pairs, with zeros included; they do not depend on the choice of $T$.

For a real target $K=G+A\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ with $G=G^\top\succ0$ and $A^\top=-A$, let $\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_d$ be the eigenvalues of $iG^{-1/2}AG^{-1/2}$. There are real centered features $F$ such that $K=F^\top D_\pi(-Q)^{-1}_0F$ if and only if

$$d\le m,\qquad \lambda_j\ge\mu_j\ge\lambda_{j+m-d},\qquad 1\le j\le d.$$

The inverse is on centered functions. This is feasibility for the specified generator with freely chosen real features, not reconstruction of a generator or a test for a fixed known feature matrix.

Reduction to a real skew compression

Every centered feature matrix can be written as $F=-QU$, uniquely modulo constant potential columns. Its directed block is $U^\top C^\top U$, so after subtracting constants write $U=TX$.

$$G=X^\top HX,\quad A=X^\top DX,\quad W=H^{1/2}XG^{-1/2}, \qquad W^\top W=I_d,\quad W^\top B_QW=G^{-1/2}AG^{-1/2}.$$

Hermitian interlacing proves necessity. For sufficiency one needs a real isometry $W$: the complex Hermitian converse alone does not establish real features.

Here is a real converse. For a codimension-one compression of a real skew matrix with distinct positive canonical frequencies $\sigma_j$, let $p(t)$ and $q(t)$ be the ambient and desired characteristic polynomials.

$$\frac{q(t)}{p(t)}=\sum_j w_j\frac{t}{t^2+\sigma_j^2}+\frac{w_0}{t}, \qquad w_j\ge0,\quad \sum_jw_j+w_0=1.$$

The $w_0/t$ term occurs only in odd ambient dimension. Partial fractions and interlacing of the squared positive frequencies give the nonnegative weights; explicitly,

$$w_j=\begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{\prod_k(\tau_k^2-\sigma_j^2)}{\prod_{\ell\ne j}(\sigma_\ell^2-\sigma_j^2)},&m\text{ even},\\[6pt] \displaystyle\frac{\prod_k(\tau_k^2-\sigma_j^2)}{-\sigma_j^2\prod_{\ell\ne j}(\sigma_\ell^2-\sigma_j^2)},&m\text{ odd}, \end{cases}\qquad w_0=\frac{\prod_k\tau_k^2}{\prod_j\sigma_j^2}\quad(m\text{ odd}).$$

Choose a real unit normal $x$ with squared projection $w_j$ on each canonical plane and $w_0$ on the kernel. The cofactor identity $\det(tI-B|_{x^\perp})=p(t)x^\top(tI-B)^{-1}x=q(t)$ produces the desired real compression.

For arbitrary codimension, the interlacing inequalities admit consecutive one-step interlacing rows, as in the classical Hermitian embedding proof. Average that array with its negative reversed array: linear interlacing inequalities are preserved, every row becomes sign-symmetric, and the two endpoint rows are unchanged.

Apply the real one-step construction successively, then use real orthogonal canonical forms to match the target skew matrix, not just its spectrum. Repeated roots and boundary equalities follow by limits and compactness of real isometries; the equal-dimension case is ordinary real orthogonal equivalence.

Finally $U=TH^{-1/2}WG^{1/2}$ and $F=-QU$ realize $K$ exactly. This proves both directions, including repeated skew modes and reversible targets.

An exact count for one-way cycles

Now restrict the architecture to a single directed cycle, with arbitrary positive holding rates. Its balanced stationary flow is constant around the cycle; Fourier diagonalization of $C$ therefore gives, independently of holding times,

$$\lambda_j=\cot(j\pi/N),\qquad j=1,\ldots,N-1.$$

Define $\operatorname{arccot}x\in(0,\pi)$, including for negative $x$. Opposite-pair symmetry makes the lower interlacing inequalities equivalent to the upper ones, so the exact minimum within this architecture is

$$N_{\mathrm{cycle}}(K)=\max\left\{d+1, \left\lceil\max_{1\le j\le d}\frac{j\pi}{\operatorname{arccot}\mu_j}\right\rceil\right\}.$$

All signed modes are needed, not just the positive ones. The ceiling is an exact mathematical operation; a floating value near an integer is not a certified boundary decision.

For $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $K_*=I_4+\kappa\operatorname{diag}(J,J)$ with $\kappa=\cot(\pi/5)$, the minimum is exactly ten. Rank excludes fewer than five states, and cycles with five through nine fail because their second mode $\cot(2\pi/N)$ is strictly below $\kappa$; ten passes every inequality.

An explicit witness uses the energy-orthonormal cosine/sine pairs of Fourier modes one and two on the ten-cycle. Replace the first cosine by $a$ times that cosine plus $\sqrt{1-a^2}$ times the energy-normalized alternating mode, where $a=\kappa/\cot(\pi/10)$; keep the other three potentials and set $F=-QU$.

The two skew pairs then both equal $\kappa$, and the symmetric block is $I_4$, for any prescribed positive ten-state law. A shared-hub realization in the separate constructive note uses nine states, so ten is not the unrestricted minimum; the six-to-nine gap remains open.

How much irreversibility can features hide?

If $\operatorname{rank}B_Q=2r$, the largest possible rank of a reversible-looking block $A=0$, $G\succ0$, is $m-r$. Set every $\mu_j=0$ in the criterion; equivalently choose one real direction from each canonical plane and every kernel direction.

For a one-way cycle this maximum is $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$. Thus a high-rank symmetric observed block need not certify reversibility of its driver; conversely, sufficiently many independent symmetric features rule out that candidate architecture.

Scope, literature and checks

Changing holding times at fixed stationary flows leaves $C$ unchanged up to an irrelevant scalar, hence preserves the entire embedding class; time reversal preserves it too because the signed spectrum is symmetric. None of this preserves finite-time path laws or a fixed feature embedding, and no rank after integrating maturity-varying loadings enters the test.

Hermitian interlacing and its converse are classical: Fan and Pall (1957), Theorem 1. The phase-compression counterpart is Qiu, Wang, Mao and Chen (2022), Lemma 4.3; the real residue argument above supplies the required real-feature sufficiency rather than attributing it to a complex-isometry theorem.

The certificate checks real residue reconstructions, independent Poisson and Green–Kubo calculations, holding-time invariance, cycle counts and an independent correlation quadrature. Exact angle ordering proves the ten-state exclusion threshold; floating matrix checks are consistency tests, not outward-rounded enclosures or an infeasibility proof for other architectures.
