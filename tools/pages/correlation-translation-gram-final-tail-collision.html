The final tail derivative collides with the next Gaussian mode

At the sharp boundary of the multi-tail ladder, two independent directions form a rank-two block.

The derivative ladder stays wholly above the next Gaussian feature only while $\varepsilon_n/\delta_n^{s-1}\to0$. At a finite nonzero boundary ratio, its last mode and that Gaussian feature interact.

Final-tail collision theorem

Use the $s$-tail triangular law, weights, centered distinct offsets, and notation of the multi-tail confluence theorem. Assume $s\ge2$, $1\le r\le k-s-2$, and

$$\frac{\delta_n^{s-1}}{\varepsilon_n}\longrightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty).$$

Let $\zeta_j=(\gamma_\ell^j)_\ell$ and let $\eta_j$ be the squared weighted norm of its residual after projection onto $\zeta_0,\ldots,\zeta_{j-1}$. Write

$$\phi_a=(x_i^a/a!)_i,\qquad v_j=(x_i^je^{\tau x_i}/j!)_i.$$

The first $r$ Gaussian modes, the two primary modes, and the first $s-2$ tail modes retain their separated coefficients. Thus, for $1\le j\le s-2$,

$$\lambda_{r+2+j}(K_n)= \eta_j\|R_jv_j\|_2^2\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2j} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^{2j}).$$

Let $P$ project orthogonally away from

$$L=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_r,v_0,\ldots,v_{s-2}\},$$

and put $g=P\phi_{r+1}$ and $b=Pv_{s-1}$. For $Z_a=|G|^c(\log|G|)^a$, let $h_{r+1}$ be the residual variance of $Z_{r+1}$ after projection onto $Z_0,\ldots,Z_r$, and define

$$M_\rho=h_{r+1}gg^\top+\eta_{s-1}\rho^2bb^\top.$$

This matrix has rank two. If $\nu_1\ge\nu_2>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+s+j}(K_n)= \nu_j\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)}),\qquad j=1,2.$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+1)})$.

Why the block has rank two

After the earlier Schur complements, Gaussian Gram–Schmidt leaves $\varepsilon_n^{r+1}\sqrt{h_{r+1}}\,g$. Weighted label Gram–Schmidt leaves $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^{s-1}\sqrt{\eta_{s-1}}\,b$.

Their ratio tends to $\rho$, and their latent coordinates are orthogonal, so their covariance contributions add. The vectors $g$ and $b$ are independent because

$$1,x,\ldots,x^{r+1},e^{\tau x},xe^{\tau x},\ldots,x^{s-1}e^{\tau x}$$

is an extended Chebyshev system at the $k\ge r+s+2$ distinct nodes. Higher Gaussian and tail Taylor features are smaller by $\varepsilon_n$ and $\delta_n$, respectively.

Three-tail certificate

Take $s=3$, $k=6$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3)$, centered offsets $(-4/3,-1/3,2/3)$, nodes $(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2)$, $\rho=3/2$, and $\delta_n=\sqrt{\rho\varepsilon_n}$.

The separated first-tail coefficient is $3.038546555413267$. The two critical coefficients are

$$(0.115976752655168,\ 0.000092873239250).$$

At $n=65536$, their exact normalized ratios are $(0.991633879726,0.991897573456)$. The 260-digit certificate also checks the primary block, first tail scale, full confluent rank, monotone convergence, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for fixed $s,k,r,q,\gamma,$ and $\rho$ at one fixed positive maturity. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not a finite-$n$ bound or uniformity over moving dimensions, offsets, or boundary ratios.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
