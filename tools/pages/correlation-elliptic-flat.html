Ellipsoidal flat covariance

The coordinate-covariant constant is a generalized Green–Kubo eigenvalue.

A positive quadratic metric turns the radial flat-map theorem into a coordinate-covariant result. The selected direction maximizes metric size per unit Green–Kubo moderate-deviation cost.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain setup of the radial theorem, with centered $g:\mathsf S\to\mathbb R^d$, fixed $T>0$, occupation average $W_{m,T}$, and nonzero symmetric Green–Kubo matrix $\Gamma$. Fix a symmetric positive-definite matrix $A$ and write $\|w\|_A=(w^\top A w)^{1/2}$.

For $\sigma>1$, $b>0$ and $w\ne0$, put

$$\Phi_{\sigma,b,A}(w)=\exp\{-\exp(b\|w\|_A^{-1/(\sigma-1)})\}, \qquad \Phi_{\sigma,b,A}(0)=0.$$

Let

$$\lambda_A=\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma^{1/2}A\Gamma^{1/2})>0.$$

At every fixed $T>0$, uniformly over arbitrary initial regime priors,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Phi_{\sigma,b,A}(W_{m,T})) \sim\frac{b^{2(\sigma-1)}}{2\lambda_A} \frac{mT}{[\log(mT)]^{2(\sigma-1)}}.$$

Boundary contraction

Put $\beta=\sigma-1$ and $H=\operatorname{range}\Gamma$. The moderate-deviation rate on $H$ is

$$J(y)=\frac{b^{2\beta}}2y^\top\Gamma^+y.$$

The flat penalty changes at the ellipsoid $y^\top A y=1$. Writing $y=\Gamma^{1/2}z$ on $H$ reduces the boundary contraction to the Rayleigh problem

$$\min_{y\in H:\,y^\top Ay=1}J(y) =\frac{b^{2\beta}}2 \min_{z\in H:\,z^\top\Gamma^{1/2}A\Gamma^{1/2}z=1}\|z\|^2 =\frac{b^{2\beta}}{2\lambda_A}.$$

The shrinking-boundary argument and variance separation are otherwise unchanged from the radial proof. Every fixed positive moment power has this leading cost, so the squared mean is exponentially negligible relative to the second moment.

Coordinate and rank scope

For an invertible coordinate change $w'=Rw$, transform $\Gamma'=R\Gamma R^\top$ and $A'=R^{-\top}AR^{-1}$. Then $\|w'\|_{A'}=\|w\|_A$, while $\Gamma'A'=R\Gamma A R^{-1}$, so $\lambda_A$ is unchanged.

Keeping $A=I$ after a non-orthogonal coordinate change instead changes the map; that is the coordinate dependence noted in the radial theorem. The ellipsoidal result supplies the metric transformation needed for covariance.

The constant uses one generalized spectral magnitude at fixed features. It neither identifies nonlinear transformed-covariance rank nor integrates different maturity loadings; $\operatorname{rank}\Gamma$ remains only the dimension of the active linear feature space.

Numerical certificate

For the same nonreversible three-state chain as the radial check, take $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&.8\\.8&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The certificate finds $\lambda_A=2.417014149$ and, at $\sigma=2$, $b=1.5$, the predicted constant $0.465450316$.

The exact two-dimensional Legendre rate is contracted over each metric ellipse and then used in the full double-exponential saddle. At metric radius $.005$, its quadratic normalization is $1.000083063$.

Literature boundary

Gärtner (1977) supplies the spectral large-deviation principle, and Ney and Nummelin (1987) treat Markov additive functionals. The ellipsoidal contraction and its coordinate-covariant constant are derived above.
