Papers

Our write-ups on fast hidden variables and their correctors. The bibliography places them among the literature.

Published

  • Cotton, P. (2001). An Analytic Approach to Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Processes with Fluctuating Parameters and Applications in the Modeling of Fixed Income Securities. PhD thesis, Stanford University. PDF · ProQuest · errata. Vasicek short rates and hazard rates whose mean level and volatility are driven by a fast process, continuous or two-state; the reduction to linear systems and the fast-switching expansions.
  • Cotton, P., Fouque, J.-P., Papanicolaou, G. and Sircar, R. (2004). Stochastic Volatility Corrections for Interest Rate Derivatives. Mathematical Finance 14(2):173–200. doi:10.1111/j.0960-1627.2004.00188.x · PDF. Vasicek and CIR short rates with fast mean-reverting stochastic volatility, and the first-order terms that change the shape of the yield curve.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). Got Milk? On Homogenization and Survival Probability. Article · code: homogenize. An expository account of regime-switching survival probabilities.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). A Fundamental Theorem for Epidemiology. Article, 27 May 2020. Doubling times under a week against peak infection near 20%: de Finetti's theorem reduces the question to mixtures of independent neighborhoods, and the susceptible fraction at the peak to a harmonic mean.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). Addressing the Herd Immunity Paradox Using Symmetry, Convexity Adjustments and Bond Prices. Preprint. arXiv:2006.07341. Epidemics that turn over below the herd-immunity threshold implied by their early growth, explained by a growth convexity from Vasicek's bond formula and a harmonic-mean convexity at the peak.
  • Cotton, P. (2020). Repeat Contacts and the Spread of Disease: An Agent Model with Compartmental Solution. Preprint. arXiv:2005.10311. SIR with transmission attenuated by the probability that a contact is new; the speed at which acquaintances go stale changes final sizes by an order of magnitude.

  • Cotton, P. (2026). The Likelihood of a Fast-Switching Short Rate. Note in preparation. Summary · certificate.   • Cotton, P. (2026). Solvability Beyond the Centered Corrector. Note in preparation. Summary · certificate. The group inverse determines only the centered shape of each corrector; the next Fredholm condition fixes its null-space amplitude. When the fast generator varies slowly, differentiating the moving centering condition adds a signed geometric transport coefficient distinct from the instantaneous Green–Kubo term; for a two-state periodic protocol it is exactly the oriented line integral $\oint (g_0-g_1)(\alpha+\beta)^{-1}\,d\pi_0$, invariant to traversal speed and odd under loop reversal. For a general smooth finite-state family this term is a parameter-space one-form $\mathcal A_a=\pi(\partial_aQ)(Q^{\#})^2f$; its exterior derivative gives an explicit curvature, and Stokes' theorem converts a closed-loop correction into a curvature-area integral. Changing the particular Poisson corrector sends $\mathcal A$ to $\mathcal A-dc$, so curvature and closed-loop holonomy are gauge invariant even though the open-path connection is not. For constant scalar forcing an all-orders recursion yields the Green–Kubo variance coefficient and signed three- and four-time cumulant coefficients. A resolvent-contour certificate gives computable Cauchy remainders for both the slow eigenvalue and the spectral projector selecting the initial-data amplitude; a separate fast-spectrum contour retains nonnormal transient amplification while proving exponential layer decay. Combining all three gives an explicit all-time semigroup bound that exposes the maturity loss from eigenvalue truncation and remains second order on a certified $t=O(\varepsilon^{-2})$ window. An explicit initial layer gives a uniform second-order remainder on fixed time intervals.
  • Cotton, P. (2026). Cumulants of Integrated Variance Under a Fast Regime. Note in preparation. Summary · certificate. The fourth cumulant of a return under a switched variance is three times the variance of integrated variance only for independent Brownian noise, and that variance is its Green–Kubo term up to a remainder bounded uniformly in maturity; for a CIR variance with a switching level the same holds with the maturity replaced by an integral of the reversion weight, and the first-order rule gives every cumulant at fixed maturity.
  • Cotton, P. (2026). The Direction of a Regime Cycle in the Heston Smile. Note in preparation. Summary · chain certificate · circle certificate. A chain cycling through variance level and vol-of-vol moves the smile through an antisymmetric Green–Kubo commutator. For every stationary Markov driver, time reversal transposes the Green–Kubo form. More strongly, under a stationary start the complete occupation-measure law is exactly reversal-blind at every switching rate; a time-dependent loading instead reverses its schedule, so asymmetric schedules can recover direction. An energy-normalized resolvent factorization gives sharp variance-reduction and directional-response bounds, while intrinsic normalization by the actual symmetric Green–Kubo form gives a sharp sector constant $\lVert C\rVert$. Symmetry on all centered observables characterizes reversibility under a resolvent-invertibility hypothesis; one selected feature is always blind, while two independent Gaussian feature probes detect every finite-state irreversible chain almost surely and have mean-square signal equal to the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the skew resolvent. A fourth-moment bound turns this into a finite certificate: 47 independent pairs have a universal miss probability below one percent at half the root-mean-square signal scale. Bounded Rademacher probes obey the same finite guarantee and a smaller exact fourth moment, with the sharper dimension-$d$ constant $6-8/d$; the four-state certificate needs 25 rather than 47 pairs. Median block means turn the corresponding finite-variance energy estimator into an explicit $O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ relative-error confidence certificate for the population skew-resolvent energy. When the full action $Jx$ is available, quadratic trace probes reduce the worst-case relative-variance constant from five to one and the half-scale one-percent miss certificate from 47 probes to 14; this stronger result does not apply to scalar-only bilinear queries. Normalizing Gaussian directions to the sphere of radius $\sqrt d$ removes radial noise: the exact relative variance is at most $(d-2)/(d+2)$, and the three-dimensional certificate needs eight probes for one-percent half-scale detection and 100 actions for the displayed median-of-means guarantee. Haar-orthogonal blocks add a finite-population factor $(d-k)/(d-1)$ to the $k$-action variance; a full frame is exact, and in the three-dimensional certificate a two-action frame has exact relative variance $1/20$ and deterministically estimates the energy within 50%. This orthogonal-block result still requires a full action oracle. Given a supplied operator-norm error radius for an estimated generator, an estimator-centered fixed-point group-inverse theorem propagates that error into a fully computable interval for the population energy, retaining both the observed stationary-law norm and coordinatewise group-inverse sensitivities; for a continuously and fully observed finite-state path, simultaneous exponential counting-martingale bounds supply entrywise rate intervals from holding times and transition counts without a known rate cap. For small chains, exact vertex propagation gives the sharp spectral-norm radius of this rectangular confidence set; analytic Frobenius and induced-norm bounds remain the large-state fallback. This finite-path guarantee does not cover hidden or discretely observed regimes. The dimension-free constant $6$ is asymptotically sharp, but sign probes can return zero with positive probability and therefore are not almost-sure identifiers.
