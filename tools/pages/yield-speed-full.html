Clock normalization at the long end

The three-number coefficients $c_j$ use the actual fast generator $Q$. The eigenvalue coefficients $\mu_k$ instead use a chosen base generator $Q_0$ in $Q=Q_0/\varepsilon$. Keeping these two conventions distinct is necessary even when the matrix calculations are correct.

The corrected identity

For fixed loadings and $\kappa$, write $c_j(Q)$ to expose their generator dependence. Positive clock scaling leaves the stationary law unchanged and gives $Q^\#=\varepsilon Q_0^\#$, since this matrix satisfies the defining group-inverse identities on the centered subspace. Therefore $c_j(Q)=\varepsilon c_j(Q_0)$ and

$$\varepsilon\mu_2 =\frac{c_2(Q)}{\kappa^2}+\frac{c_3(Q)}{\kappa^3}+\frac{c_4(Q)}{\kappa^4}, \qquad \mu_2=\frac{c_2(Q_0)}{\kappa^2}+\frac{c_3(Q_0)}{\kappa^3}+\frac{c_4(Q_0)}{\kappa^4}.$$

Indeed, the centered limiting forcing is $\widetilde D=-(\theta-\pi\theta)+(\sigma^2-\pi\sigma^2)/(2\kappa^2)$. Its base-generator Green–Kubo quadratic form is $\mu_2=-\pi\cdot(\widetilde D\,Q_0^\#\widetilde D)$; expanding this quadratic form gives the identity. The physical contribution to the growth rate is $\varepsilon\mu_2$, not the unscaled coefficient $\mu_2$.

Every term is independent of clock normalization

For fixed forcing $D$, the group-inverse eigenvalue recursion has the exact homogeneity law

$$v_k(cQ_0,D)=c^{-k}v_k(Q_0,D),\qquad \mu_k(cQ_0,D)=c^{1-k}\mu_k(Q_0,D),\qquad c>0.$$

Induct from $v_0=\mathbf1$: the formula for $\mu_k=\pi\cdot(Dv_{k-1})$ supplies $c^{1-k}$. Every term in the recursion's centered right-hand side has the same factor $c^{1-k}$, and the group inverse supplies one more factor $c^{-1}$. The proof requires no eigenbasis or reversibility.

Changing $(Q_0,\varepsilon)$ to $(cQ_0,c\varepsilon)$ leaves the physical generator and every physical series term unchanged:

$$(c\varepsilon)^{k-1}\mu_k(cQ_0,D)=\varepsilon^{k-1}\mu_k(Q_0,D).$$

This is a coefficient identity, not a claim that the series converges at every tested speed. It changes neither the physical growth rate nor the maturity range of an approximation.

A non-unit-speed witness and regression checks

Take $Q_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $\theta=(1,-1)$, $\sigma^2=0$, $\kappa=1$ and $\varepsilon=1/10$. Then $\mu_2=1/2$, whereas $c_2(Q)=1/20$ and $c_3(Q)=c_4(Q)=0$. The Perron growth rate is $\sqrt{\varepsilon^{-2}+1}-\varepsilon^{-1}$, whose first correction is $\varepsilon/2$.

The certificate verifies this witness, non-unit-speed Green–Kubo scaling, and normalization invariance through order six on two-, three- and five-state chains, including a defective generator. It uses scaled portable tolerances and ends with literal PASS.

Group inverses in finite-chain analysis are classical (Meyer 1975). The publisher's metadata and abstract were inspected; the scaling proof above is elementary and does not import a theorem from its restricted full text.
