Vanishing-slope covariance

Below the power boundary, the Gaussian log-radius supplies the first random term.

When the logarithmic profile has slope tending to zero, its normalized value converges to one. The covariance is smaller by the square of that slope, and the exact constant is the variance of a Gaussian log-gauge rather than a moderate-deviation contraction.

The theorem

Use the fixed-maturity finite-chain and tangent-gauge setup of the critical-slope theorem. Put $n=mT$, $L_n=\tfrac12\log n$, $Z_n=\sqrt nW_{m,T}$, and let $G\sim N(0,\Gamma)$ on $\operatorname{Ran}\Gamma$, where $\Gamma\ne0$.

Assume the central limit theorem is uniform over initial priors. Also assume a uniform logarithmic small-ball bound: for some $a,C,\kappa>0$ and $0<u<1$,

$$\sup_\nu\Pr_\nu\{0<\rho(Z_n)\le u\}\le Cu^a+Ce^{-\kappa n},\qquad \sup_\nu\Pr_\nu\{Z_n=0\}=o(d_n^2).$$

The first condition is the local-limit input needed at the logarithmic singularity; it is not implied by a weak central limit theorem alone. Standard spectral concentration supplies the corresponding upper-tail uniform integrability for bounded finite-state features.

Let $h(x)\to\infty$ be eventually $C^2$, with

$$d(x)=h'(x)>0,\qquad d(x)\to0,\qquad \frac{h''(x)}{h'(x)}\to0,$$

and write $d_n=d(L_n)$. For $\Psi_{h,r}(w)=\exp\{-h(\log(1/r(w)))\}$ near zero, with $\Psi_{h,r}(0)=0$,

$$\frac{e^{h(L_n)}\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T})-1}{d_n} \longrightarrow\log\rho(G)\qquad\text{in }L^2,$$

uniformly over initial priors. Therefore, with $V_{\log,\rho,\Gamma}=\operatorname{Var}\{\log\rho(G)\}>0$,

$$e^{2h(L_n)}d_n^{-2}\operatorname{Var}(\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T})) \longrightarrow V_{\log,\rho,\Gamma},$$ $$-\log\operatorname{Var}=2h(L_n)-2\log d_n -\log V_{\log,\rho,\Gamma}+o(1).$$

Proof mechanism

For fixed $y$, the derivative condition gives $h'(L_n-s)/h'(L_n)\to1$ uniformly on bounded $s$. The tangent relation $r/\rho\to1$ then yields

$$\frac{\exp\{h(L_n)-h(L_n-\log\rho(y)+o(1))\}-1}{d_n} \longrightarrow\log\rho(y).$$

The uniform central limit theorem gives convergence in distribution. The small-ball estimate makes $|\log\rho(Z_n)|^2$ uniformly integrable near zero, while spectral concentration handles large radii.

The atom condition removes the value $-1/d_n$ created by $Z_n=0$. Truncation followed by the displayed local expansion proves $L^2$ convergence, and taking centered second moments proves the variance formula.

Subpower specialization

For $h(t)=t^\gamma$ with $0<\gamma<1$, the exact expansion is

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}=2L_n^\gamma+2(1-\gamma)\log L_n -2\log\gamma-\log V_{\log,\rho,\Gamma}+o(1).$$

These maps are continuous at zero but decay more slowly than every positive power: this is a sub-Hölder boundary, not another smooth-flat phase. Multiplying $\rho$ or $\Gamma^{1/2}$ by a scalar only adds a constant to $\log\rho(G)$, so $V_{\log,\rho,\Gamma}$ sees shape and angular geometry but not overall scale.

Numerical certificate

For $h(t)=\sqrt t$, the symmetric telegraph chain and $\rho(x)=|x|$,

$$\mathbb E\log|G|=-0.635181422731,\qquad V_{\log,|\cdot|,1}=\frac{\pi^2}{8}=1.233700550136.$$

At $L_n=32,512,8192$, the normalized Gaussian-profile variance is respectively $.761918509$, $.935328501$, and $.983619760$. The exact telegraph variance divided by the finite-profile Gaussian prediction is $.991478755$ at speed $64$ and $.999994874$ at speed $65{,}536$.

The two comparisons separate profile linearization from the finite-chain central limit approximation. The certificate evaluates the exact atom-plus-Bessel law rather than simulating paths.

Literature and scope

Ney and Nummelin (1987, Part I) provide spectral limit theory for Markov additive processes. Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir (2010) give the telegraph occupation density used by the certificate.

This is a fixed-feature, fixed-positive-maturity scalar variance theorem. Singular $\Gamma$ is allowed through its Gaussian support, but the result makes no claim about rank after integrating distinct maturity loadings.
