Hull–White with a switching volatility

A short rate fitted to today's curve whose volatility follows a hidden regime, and how far the regime moves the curve.

The model

In the Hull–White model the short rate is a Gaussian factor plus a deterministic shift chosen to reproduce today's discount curve. Here the factor's volatility is set by a hidden regime $y_t \in \{1, 2\}$:

$$r_t = x_t + \varphi(t), \qquad dx_t = -a\,x_t\,dt + \sigma_{y_t}\,dW_t, \qquad x_0 = 0 .$$

The regime switches at rate $\lambda$ in each direction. The factor reverts over $1/a$ and the regime changes every $1/\lambda$. The market supplies the discount curve $P^M(0,T)$ and its instantaneous forward rate $f^M(0,t)$.

Parameters: $a = 0.3$, $\sigma = (0.02, 0.008)$, a flat curve at 3%, and bonds of maturity $T = 5$ unless stated.

The quantity

The zero-coupon bond seen from each starting regime,

$$P_i(0,T) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{-\int_0^T r_s\,ds} \mid y_0 = i\big] = e^{-\int_0^T\varphi}\;\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-\int_0^T x_s\,ds} \mid x_0 = 0,\ y_0 = i\big],$$

and its ratio to the market bond $P^M(0,T)$.

Averaging

When the regime switches infinitely fast the limit is Hull–White with the average variance $\bar\sigma^2 = \tfrac12(\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)$. That model reproduces the curve when the shift is

$$\varphi(t) = f^M(0,t) + \tfrac12\bar\sigma^2\,B(t)^2, \qquad B(t) = \frac{1 - e^{-at}}{a},$$

and this is the shift used throughout: the averaged model fits the curve exactly, and the switching then moves each regime's bond away from it by an amount the expansion computes.

Reduction to a linear system

By the Feynman–Kac formula the functions $u_i(t,x) = \mathbb{E}[e^{-\int_0^t x_s\,ds} \mid x_0 = x,\ y_0 = i]$ solve the coupled equations

$$\partial_t u_i = -a\,x\,\partial_x u_i + \tfrac12\sigma_i^2\,\partial_{xx}u_i - x\,u_i + \sum_j Q_{ij}\,u_j, \qquad u_i(0,x) = 1.$$

Try the Vasicek form $u_i = e^{-B(t)x}a_i(t)$. Then $\partial_x u_i = -B\,u_i$ and $\partial_{xx}u_i = B^2u_i$, and after dividing by $e^{-Bx}$ the terms proportional to $x$ are

$$-B'\,x = a B\,x - x .$$

They cancel when $B' = 1 - aB$, that is $B(t) = (1 - e^{-at})/a$, the same $B$ as in the shift. The reversion speed does not switch, so one $B$ works for both regimes. What remains involves $t$ only: $a' = (Q + \operatorname{diag} g)a$, $a(0) = \mathbf 1$ and

$$g_i(t) = \tfrac12\sigma_i^2\,B(t)^2 .$$

The factor starts at $x_0 = 0$, so the prefactor $e^{-Bx_0}$ is one. Integrating the shift, $\int_0^T\varphi = -\log P^M(0,T) + \tfrac12\bar\sigma^2\int_0^TB^2$, and the bond is

$$P_i(0,T) = P^M(0,T)\;e^{-\frac12\bar\sigma^2\int_0^TB^2}\;a_i(T) .$$

The middle factor is $e^{-\int\bar g}$: it cancels the averaged part of $a_i$, which is how the averaged model returns the curve. Everything left over is the effect of the switching.

The expansion

Let the regime switch at rate $\lambda$ in each direction, and write

$$\varepsilon = \frac1\lambda, \qquad \bar g = \tfrac12(g_1 + g_2), \qquad \tilde g = \tfrac12(g_1 - g_2),$$

Here

$$\tilde g = \tfrac12\,\tilde s\,B^2, \qquad \tilde s = \tfrac12(\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_2^2) .$$

The average $\bar g$ is what the averaged model sees. The half-difference $\tilde g$ measures how much the two regimes disagree.

First order

To first order in $\varepsilon$, at fixed $t > 0$,

$$a_i(t) = \exp\Big(\int_0^t \bar g + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^t \tilde g^{\,2}\Big)\Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde g(t)\Big) + O(\varepsilon^2),$$

with the upper sign for a start in regime 1. The three factors have separate meanings, explained on the idea page.

  • The factor below is the averaged model: $$e^{\int_0^t \bar g} .$$
  • The term below is half the variance of the fluctuating integral of $g$: $$\frac\varepsilon2\int_0^t\tilde g^{\,2} .$$ It is the same for both starting regimes, and it grows with $t$.
  • The bracket is the memory of the starting regime. The switch stays near its starting state for about $1/(2\lambda)$, which shifts the integral by $\pm\tilde g/(2\lambda)$.

Every order

Add and subtract the two equations for $a_1$ and $a_2$. The mean $m = \tfrac12(a_1 + a_2)$ and the normalized difference $\omega = (a_1 - a_2)/(a_1 + a_2)$ then satisfy

$$\omega' = \tilde g\,(1 - \omega^2) - 2\lambda\,\omega, \qquad \omega(0) = 0,$$ $$\log m(t) = \int_0^t \big(\bar g + \tilde g\,\omega\big), \qquad a_{1,2} = m\,(1 \pm \omega).$$

The first equation has a large restoring term $-2\lambda\omega$: the switch pulls the two regimes together at rate $2\lambda$, while $\tilde g$ pushes them apart. So $\omega$ stays close to the value that balances the two, and expanding in $\varepsilon$ finds that value order by order. Write

$$\omega = \sum_n \varepsilon^n\omega_n$$

and match powers of $\varepsilon$:

$$\omega_1 = \frac{\tilde g}{2}, \qquad \omega_{n+1} = -\frac12\Big(\omega_n' + \tilde g\sum_{i+j=n}\omega_i\,\omega_j\Big).$$

This is the two-state case of the solvability argument: at each order the restoring term can be inverted, which here is a division by $2\lambda$.

The series does not satisfy $\omega(0) = 0$.

An initial layer of width about $1/(2\lambda)$ repairs the start and is solved order by order in the same way. Its leading term adds $\mp\tfrac12\varepsilon\,\tilde g(0)\,e^{-2\lambda t}$ to the first-order bracket, which returns the bracket to $1$ at $t = 0$; when $\tilde g(0) \ne 0$ the first-order formula above is therefore accurate to $O(\varepsilon^2)$ only at fixed $t > 0$. The engine page gives the version for any number of regimes.

The bond in closed form

Second order

Two more steps give the next order. The recursion gives $\omega_2 = -\omega_1'/2 = -\tilde g'/4$, which adds $-\tfrac14\varepsilon^2\int_0^T\tilde g\,\tilde g' = -\tfrac18\varepsilon^2\big(\tilde g(T)^2 - \tilde g(0)^2\big)$ to the exponent. The initial layer subtracts $\tfrac12\varepsilon\,\tilde g(0)\,e^{-2\lambda t}$ from $\omega$, and through $\int\tilde g\,\omega$ that adds $-\tfrac14\varepsilon^2\,\tilde g(0)^2$. Together, up to terms of size $e^{-2\lambda T}$,

$$a_{1,2}(T) = \exp\Big(\int_0^T \bar g + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^T \tilde g^{\,2} - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\big(\tilde g(T)^2 + \tilde g(0)^2\big)\Big) \Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde g(T) \mp \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\,\tilde g'(T)\Big) + O(\varepsilon^3).$$

What remains is to evaluate $\int\bar g$, $\int\tilde g^{\,2}$, $\tilde g$ and $\tilde g'$ for this model.

Step 1: the forcing

With $\bar s, \tilde s = \tfrac12(\sigma_1^2 \pm \sigma_2^2)$,

$$\bar g = \tfrac12\bar s\,B^2, \qquad \tilde g = \tfrac12\tilde s\,B^2, \qquad \tilde g(0) = 0, \qquad \tilde g' = \tilde s\,B\,B' = \tilde s\,B\,e^{-at} .$$

Step 2: the integrals

Powers of $B$ are sums of exponentials, so with $I_n = \int_0^TB^n$,

$$\int_0^T\bar g = \tfrac12\bar s\,I_2, \qquad \int_0^T\tilde g^{\,2} = \tfrac14\tilde s^2\,I_4 .$$

The first is cancelled by the shift. The second is the Green–Kubo term.

Step 3: the result

$$\frac{P_{1,2}(0,T)}{P^M(0,T)} = \exp\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\,\tilde s^2\,I_4 - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{32}\,\tilde s^2\,B^4\Big) \Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{4}\,\tilde s\,B^2 \mp \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\,\tilde s\,B\,E\Big) + O(\varepsilon^3),$$ $$\begin{aligned} B &= \frac{1 - E}{a}, \qquad E = e^{-aT}, \\ I_1 &= \frac{1}{a}\Big(T - \frac{1 - E}{a}\Big), \\ I_2 &= \frac{1}{a^2}\Big(T - \frac{2(1 - E)}{a} + \frac{1 - E^2}{2a}\Big), \\ I_3 &= \frac{1}{a^3}\Big(T - \frac{3(1 - E)}{a} + \frac{3(1 - E^2)}{2a} - \frac{1 - E^3}{3a}\Big), \\ I_4 &= \frac{1}{a^4}\Big(T - \frac{4(1 - E)}{a} + \frac{3(1 - E^2)}{a} - \frac{4(1 - E^3)}{3a} + \frac{1 - E^4}{4a}\Big) . \end{aligned}$$

The upper sign is for a start in regime 1, the volatile one. Three things can be read from the card.

  • At order zero the ratio is one: every regime sees the market curve.
  • At first order the two regimes move in opposite directions by $\tfrac14\varepsilon\tilde sB^2$. A start in the volatile regime means more variance in $\int r$ over the next $1/(2\lambda)$, more convexity, and a higher bond price.
  • The Green–Kubo term raises both bonds, by $\tfrac18\varepsilon\tilde s^2I_4$. It is quadratic in the small number $\tilde s$, and at these parameters it is a thousand times smaller than the memory term.

Fitting the curve from the starting regime

The shift above fits the averaged model. If the starting regime $i$ is known, the fit can be made exact for that regime instead, because $a_i$ is known: choose

$$\varphi_i(t) = f^M(0,t) + \frac{d}{dt}\log a_i(t), \qquad\text{so that}\qquad e^{-\int_0^T\varphi_i}\,a_i(T) = P^M(0,T) \text{ for every } T.$$

To first order this is the averaged shift plus two corrections,

$$\varphi_{1,2}(t) = f^M(0,t) + \tfrac12\bar s\,B^2 + \frac{\varepsilon}{8}\,\tilde s^2B^4 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde s\,B\,e^{-at} + O(\varepsilon^2),$$

the derivative of the exponent and of the bracket in the card. regimelib's SwitchingHullWhite uses the averaged shift, so that the model is QuantLib's when the regimes coincide.
