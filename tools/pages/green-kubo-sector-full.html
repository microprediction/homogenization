Directional response can count more regimes than rank

Two fixed features have symmetric Green–Kubo rank at most two, however many regimes drive them. Their antisymmetric response carries an additional sharp regime-count constraint: a sufficiently large response relative to the actual symmetric form rules out every small chain.

Fixed-generator, fixed-feature theorem

Let $Q$ be an irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ states, with stationary law $\pi$, and let $f_1,\ldots,f_d$ be real centered features. With $R=(-Q)^{-1}$ on the centered space, define

$$K_{ab}=\langle f_a,Rf_b\rangle_\pi,\qquad G=\tfrac12(K+K^\top),\qquad A=\tfrac12(K-K^\top).$$

The symmetric form has $\ker G=\ker[f_1\ \cdots\ f_d]$ and rank at most $n-1$. The same kernel is annihilated by $A$, and the inverse square roots below act only on its orthogonal complement.

$$\left\|G^{-1/2}AG^{-1/2}\right\|_2\le\cot\frac\pi n,$$ $$|A_{12}|\le\cot\frac\pi n\sqrt{G_{11}G_{22}-G_{12}^2}.$$

The second inequality also holds for singular two-feature blocks, with both sides zero when the determinant is zero. For $G\succ0$ of size two put $\chi=|A_{12}|/\sqrt{\det G}$; then the least possible number of states realizing the entire directed block $K$ is exactly

$$n_{\min}(K)=\min\{n\in\mathbb N:n\ge3,\ \chi\le\cot(\pi/n)\}.$$

This is an exact population realization statement with unrestricted real feature values and unrestricted finite irreducible generators. It does not identify the actual generator or actual state count, impose a prescribed stationary law, or by itself give a finite-sample confidence statement.

Proof of the sector bound

Write $L=-Q$, $S=(L+L^*)/2$ and $T=(L-L^*)/2$, using the $L^2(\pi)$ adjoint. The stationary off-diagonal flows $c_{ij}=\pi_iq_{ij}$ balance at every vertex and decompose into nonnegative flows around simple directed cycles.

To see this directly, follow positive edges until a vertex repeats and subtract the smallest flow on that cycle. Balance is preserved and at least one edge disappears, so the procedure terminates; every cycle has length at most $n$.

For a cycle of length $\ell$ carrying flow $w$, its contribution to $\langle z,Lz\rangle_\pi$ is $w\sum_j\overline z_j(z_j-z_{j+1})$. Discrete Fourier modes diagonalize this expression with eigenvalues $1-e^{2\pi i k/\ell}$, so each mode has imaginary-to-real ratio at most $\cot(\pi/\ell)\le\cot(\pi/n)$.

Summing the cycle inequalities proves $|\operatorname{Im}\langle z,Lz\rangle_\pi|\le\cot(\pi/n)\langle z,Sz\rangle_\pi$ for every complex vector $z$. Substituting $z=u+itv$ and minimizing over $t>0$ gives

$$|\langle u,Tv\rangle_\pi|\le\cot(\pi/n) \sqrt{\langle u,Su\rangle_\pi\langle v,Sv\rangle_\pi}.$$

For $u=Rf$ and $v=Rg$, the symmetric Green–Kubo pairing is $G(f,g)=\langle u,Sv\rangle_\pi$, and the antisymmetric pairing is $A(f,g)=-\langle u,Tv\rangle_\pi$. This proves the normalized operator bound; replacing $g$ by $g-G(f,g)f/G(f,f)$ gives the sharper two-feature determinant form.

If $G(f,f)=0$, the identity $\langle u,Su\rangle_\pi=\frac12\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}(u_j-u_i)^2$ and irreducibility force $f=0$, which handles the degenerate case. This proof controls the numerical range, not only eigenvalues: an eigenvalue sector alone is insufficient to bound a nonnormal generator's feature response.

Every admissible two-feature block is realized

Fix $n\ge3$, a signed $\kappa$ with $|\kappa|\le\cot(\pi/n)$, and set $\vartheta=2\pi/n$. On the cyclic states $j=0,\ldots,n-1$, use constant forward and backward rates

$$a+b=\frac1{1-\cos\vartheta},\qquad a-b=\frac\kappa{\sin\vartheta}.$$

The sector condition makes $a,b\ge0$ and at least one positive, so this chain is irreducible and has uniform stationary law. For the orthonormal feature pair $e_j=\sqrt2(\cos j\vartheta,\sin j\vartheta)$, direct inversion on the first Fourier mode gives

$$K(e,e)=\frac1{1+\kappa^2}\begin{pmatrix}1&\kappa\\-\kappa&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

For a target $K=G+A$ with $G\succ0$, take $\kappa=A_{12}/\sqrt{\det G}$ and any real matrix $C$ with $C^\top C=G$ and $\det C>0$. The feature matrix $\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}\,eC$ realizes exactly $G+A$, because $C^\top\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)C=(\det C)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.

This proves sufficiency in the minimum-state formula, including equality, where one cyclic direction has zero rate. Rank two itself excludes one or two states, and the sector inequality excludes every remaining smaller $n$.

A rank-two block that requires seven regimes

$$K=\begin{pmatrix}1/10&1/5\\-1/5&1/10\end{pmatrix},\qquad G=I/10,\quad\chi=2.$$

This is the circle-diffusion block already appearing in the cycle note at $D=1,c=2$. Its symmetric rank only forces three regimes, but $2>\cot(\pi/6)=\sqrt3$ rules out every chain with six or fewer states.

Seven states suffice by the preceding cyclic construction: take $\kappa=2$, $n=7$ and features $(\cos j\vartheta,\sin j\vartheta)$. The rates are nonnegative because $\tan^2(\pi/7)<1/4$.

For an exact check, $x=\tan^2(\pi/7)\in(0,1/3)$ solves $p(x)=x^3-21x^2+35x-7=0$. On that interval $p'\ge64/3>0$, while $p(1/4)=29/64>0$; hence $x<1/4$ without a floating trigonometric comparison.

Coefficient error and interpretation

Suppose a supplied bound $\|\widehat K-K\|_2\le\epsilon$ holds, and the true two-feature $G$ is positive definite. Put $\widehat G=\operatorname{Sym}\widehat K$, $\widehat A=\operatorname{Anti}\widehat K$; then $\widehat G+\epsilon I\succeq G\succ0$ and

$$\chi\ge\chi_{\rm low}:= \frac{(|\widehat A_{12}|-\epsilon)_+}{\sqrt{\det(\widehat G+\epsilon I)}}.$$

Thus $\chi_{\rm low}>\cot(\pi/N)$ excludes every chain with at most $N$ states. If $\widehat G-\epsilon I\succ0$, the true positive-definiteness assumption is itself certified by the same error bound.

For the displayed block used as $\widehat K$, $\epsilon=1/1000$ gives $\chi_{\rm low}=199/101>\sqrt3$, so the seven-state lower bound survives that error budget. This is deterministic error propagation, not a claim that an estimated block has that accuracy.

Speeding up $Q$ by $m$ divides both $G$ and $A$ by $m$, leaving $\chi$ unchanged. A scalar yield curve that observes only symmetric contractions cannot supply $A$.

Unlike rank, the sector bound survives common loading aggregation

For real measurable loadings $M(t)$ and a nonnegative measure $\mu$, suppose $\overline K=\int M(t)K M(t)^\top\mu(dt)$ exists. With $c_n=\cot(\pi/n)$, the operator sector inequality is equivalently $-c_nG\preceq iA\preceq c_nG$ on the complexified feature space.

Congruence and positive integration preserve these inequalities, so $-c_n\overline G\preceq i\overline A\preceq c_n\overline G$ still holds. Thus any two-feature block of this aggregate retains the sector-based lower bound, although $\operatorname{rank}\overline G$ can exceed $n-1$ and cannot count regimes.

For example, a three-state cycle's two-dimensional block can be placed in two disjoint coordinate pairs on two loading intervals. The aggregate has symmetric rank four but still satisfies the three-state sector bound.

This requires the same congruence loadings and nonnegative weights for the symmetric and antisymmetric parts. It does not cover arbitrary cross-maturity statistics, entrywise unrelated loading schemes or signed combinations; the minimum-state realization formula above remains a fixed two-feature theorem.

The minimum-state converse is only for two independent features. Higher-dimensional simultaneous realization, constrained physical features and continuous-state models are not classified here.

Literature and checks

Cycle decomposition is classical; Landim and Xu (2016), Lemma 4.1, gives the stationary generator decomposition used above. Davies (2010), proof of Theorem 9, equations (5)–(6), records the classical generator eigenvalue sector and its earlier origins; the numerical-range proof and Green–Kubo realization calculation are supplied explicitly here, without a priority claim for the cotangent constant.

The cotangent sector also follows by dropping the affinity information in Ohga, Ito and Kolchinsky (2023), equation (3), and applying their short-time correlation bound to the Poisson solutions $Rf,Rg$. Their thermodynamic inequality is stronger when cycle affinities are known; the focus here is the integrated-response minimum-state realization and its aggregation and coefficient-error consequences, not a new universal asymmetry inequality.

The certificate checks nonnormal and sparse generators, exact rational cycle-flow decompositions, cyclic sharpness, two-feature realizations, reversals, speed invariance and deterministic perturbation budgets. Floating computations are consistency tests, not outward-rounded enclosures or a proof by random sampling.
