Infinitely many maturity reversals, with six regimes

Stable complex spectrum defeats a global maturity-zero bound; six states are necessary and sufficient in the stated class.

A nonzero share-weighted log-return mean gap can change sign infinitely often in maturity, even with strictly positive CIR variance and a stable share-moment matrix. Six regimes suffice; a nonzero gap with five or fewer cannot have infinitely many positive zeros when the share-moment matrix is stable.

This shows why the real-spectrum maturity bound needs its spectral assumption. These are zeros of one exact scalar observable as maturity varies, not call-price crossings across strikes.

Stationary-start model and exact rational transform

Let $G$ be a finite irreducible row generator, started at its invariant law $\pi$, independent of the original Brownian motions. Compare $G$ with $G^*=D_\pi^{-1}G^\top D_\pi$, using identical fixed $v_0>0$, $X_0=0$, and coefficients in

$$dv=(a_Y-\kappa_Yv)dt+\xi_Y\sqrt v\,dB,\qquad dX=-vdt/2+\sqrt v(\rho_YdB+\sqrt{1-\rho_Y^2}\,dW).$$

Assume nonnegative variance, a true stock martingale, and finite share-weighted log-return means at every finite maturity. Set $A=G-\operatorname{diag}(\kappa-\rho\xi)$ and $\langle x,y\rangle_\pi=x^\top D_\pi y$.

$$D(T)=\mathbb E_G[e^{X_T}X_T]-\mathbb E_{G^*}[e^{X_T}X_T] =\tfrac12\int_0^T(T-u)\mathcal L_a(e^{uA})du,\quad \mathcal L_a(M)=\langle a,M\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,Ma\rangle_\pi.$$

Indeed the stock numéraire leaves regime intensities unchanged, changes variance reversion to $\kappa-\rho\xi$, and changes the log-return drift to $v/2$. The first-moment masses solve $z'=(G^\top-\operatorname{diag}(\kappa-\rho\xi))z+\operatorname{diag}(a)\pi^\top$. The common initial-variance term cancels by weighted adjointness, giving the formula.

$$p(z)=\det(zI-A),\qquad \widehat D(z)={N(z)\over2z^2p(z)},\quad N(z)=\mathcal L_a(\operatorname{adj}(zI-A)),\quad \deg N\le n-3.$$

The degree reduction follows from $\mathcal L_a(I)=\mathcal L_a(A)=0$, both consequences of stationarity. From now on assume $A$ is stable: every eigenvalue has strictly negative real part; positive $k_i=\kappa_i-\rho_i\xi_i$ is sufficient.

Why five or fewer states cannot oscillate forever

Partial fractions give $D(T)=b_1T+b_0+R(T)$, where $R$ is a finite sum of decaying exponential polynomials, including possible Jordan terms. Infinitely many positive zeros of a nonzero $D$ require $b_1=b_0=0$, equivalently $N(0)=N'(0)=0$.

For $n\le4$ these two conditions force $N\equiv0$. For $n=5$ they give $N(z)=cz^2$, so $D(T)=ch(T)/2$, where $h$ is the inverse Laplace transform of $1/p$. The following positivity argument shows that this nonzero signal has no positive zeros, even with complex spectrum.

For any real Metzler matrix $A$, choose $\theta$ such that $B=A+\theta I$ is entrywise nonnegative, and write

$$\det(I-wB)^{-1}=\exp\left(\sum_{j\ge1}{\operatorname{tr}(B^j)\over j}w^j\right) =\sum_{\ell\ge0}c_\ell w^\ell,\qquad c_0=1,\quad c_\ell\ge0.$$

If $M=\|B\|_\infty$, coefficientwise comparison with $(1-Mw)^{-n}$ gives $c_\ell\le\binom{n+\ell-1}{\ell}M^\ell$. Consequently the following series converges for every finite $T$ and has Laplace transform $1/p$ for sufficiently large real $z$:

$$h(T)=e^{-\theta T}\sum_{\ell\ge0}c_\ell{T^{n+\ell-1}\over(n+\ell-1)!} \ge e^{-\theta T}{T^{n-1}\over(n-1)!}>0\quad(T>0).$$

Thus every nonzero signal with $n\le5$ has a fixed sign eventually, or has the displayed strictly positive-kernel form. Analyticity excludes infinitely many zeros on a bounded interval, including accumulation at the origin. Identically zero signals are excluded from this minimum-state assertion.

A rational six-state positive-CIR example

Take $G=P-I$, where $P_{i,i+1}=1$ cyclically on $i=0,\ldots,5$, and use uniform $\pi$ and $v_0=.04$.

$$r=(99/100,100/99,1,1,1,1),\quad \kappa_i=2,\quad \rho_i=-1/2,\quad \xi_i=2r_i,\quad k_i=2+r_i,$$ $$a=10\mathbf1+(0,338101/494010,3383/4990,-1577/4990,1,0).$$

The strict Feller margin is $\min_i(2a_i-\xi_i^2)=38343/2495>0$. The exponential moment supersolution coefficient $\xi_i^2b^2/2+(\rho_i\xi_i s-2)b+(s^2-s)/2$ has maxima $-4900/9801$ and $-224429/1960200$ at $(s,b)=(2,1/2)$ and $(-1/10,1/10)$.

With $C_s=\max_i a_i b$, localization bounds $\mathbb E e^{sX_t+bv_t}$ by $e^{bv_0+C_st}$ at every finite horizon. The moment above one makes the localized stocks uniformly integrable; the negative moment supplies the other side of the strip. The variance is strictly positive, and both orientations satisfy the required true-martingale and moment conditions.

$$p(z)=\prod_{i=0}^5(z+3+r_i)-1=(z+3)q_5(z),\qquad N(z)=\alpha z^2(z+3),\quad \alpha={199\over296406000}>0,$$ $$\widehat D(z)={\alpha\over2q_5(z)}.$$

All cancellations are exact rational identities, checked using matrix powers and the characteristic polynomial. The $z^2$ factor removes the affine maturity terms; the $z+3$ factor removes the leading real eigenmode.

A rigorous spectral enclosure and an explicit oscillation bound

Write $A=A_0+E$, with $A_0=P-4I$ normal and $E=-\operatorname{diag}(r-\mathbf1)$. Then $\|E\|_2=1/99<1/50$. Around the six eigenvalues $-4+e^{2\pi i j/6}$ of $A_0$, take disjoint disks of radius $1/50$.

On each disk boundary the normal resolvent has norm $50$, so $(zI-A_0)^{-1}tE$ has norm below one for every $0\le t\le1$. No eigenvalue crosses a boundary along this homotopy; hence each disk contains exactly one simple eigenvalue of $A$. Since $\prod r_i=1$, the eigenvalue in the rightmost disk is exactly $-3$.

The dominant pair among the five remaining roots is $\lambda=\sigma+i\omega$ and its conjugate, near $-7/2\pm i\sqrt3/2$. Its real part satisfies $-88/25<\sigma<-87/25$, while the other three roots have real parts below $-112/25$. All distinct roots are separated by more than $24/25$ and lie less than $51/25$ apart.

$$c={\alpha\over2q_5'(\lambda)}\ne0,\qquad \left|D(T)-2\operatorname{Re}(ce^{\lambda T})\right| \le{3\alpha\over2}\left({25\over24}\right)^4e^{-112T/25},\qquad 2|c|\ge\alpha\left({25\over51}\right)^4.$$

These bounds follow directly by summing the three remaining simple-pole residues and bounding the four factors in each $q_5'$. At the phase extrema $T_j=(j\pi-\arg c)/\omega$, the leading cosine is $(-1)^j$, with envelope $2|c|e^{\sigma T_j}$. At such a time $T$, the ratio of the error bound to the envelope is at most

$${250563\over8192}e^{-24T/25}<1\qquad(T\ge5),$$

since $e^4>\sum_{j=0}^4 4^j/j!=103/3>250563/8192$. The leading cosine has infinitely many alternating extrema beyond this horizon, with $\omega\ne0$. Therefore $D$ changes sign infinitely often and has infinitely many positive maturity zeros.

Rational sign certificates without underflow

The cofactor identity $(zI-A)^{-1}_{0,5}=1/p(z)$ gives $D(T)=\alpha[(e^{TA})_{1,5}-r_0(e^{TA})_{0,5}]/2$. The certificate removes the positive exponential scale and encloses $H(T)=2e^{5T}D(T)/\alpha$ using $A+5I$.

$$H(4)\in[126,127],\quad H(6)\in[-1267,-1266],\quad H(10)\in[786335,786336],$$ $$H(14)\in[-397276026,-397276025],\quad H(18)\in[176052884169,176052884170].$$

These five signs alone certify at least four distinct positive zeros; the spectral argument proves infinitely many. Degree-300 rational Taylor actions have a uniform error below $1.05\times10^{-127}$ over these five calculations. With $L=\lceil\|T(A+5I)\|_\infty\rceil$, each error is bounded by $2\cdot3^L L^{301}/301!$.

Independent semigroup and physical polynomial-moment comparisons use tolerances scaled to the quantities being subtracted, not to their small difference. The rational sign intervals, rather than floating tail subtraction, certify the late reversals.

Literature and limits

Finite polynomial moment closure is classical; see Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012). The normal-resolvent enclosure is a standard perturbation argument; see Embree and Trefethen (2001), the pseudospectral definition and Theorems 4 and 7.

The minimum of six applies to nonzero stationary-start signals with a stable share-moment matrix, not to arbitrary initial laws or unstable tilted systems. Infinite oscillation requires the exact cancellations exhibited here; arbitrary coefficient perturbations need not preserve it.

This is an exact fixed-parameter existence result, not an asymptotic approximation or a count of call crossings. The global strike-crossing problem in issue 204 remains separate.
