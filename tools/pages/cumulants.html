Integrated-variance cumulants

Exact fourth-cumulant benchmarks and an explicit European option correction for a positive switched CIR variance.

The smile produced by uncorrelated stochastic volatility is often described through the variance of integrated variance. There are two points to keep separate. The Brownian return has a fourth cumulant exactly equal to three times that variance. The risk-neutral log return does not: its drift contributes the third and fourth cumulants of integrated variance as well. With leverage, four joint cumulants replace the conditional-normal shortcut.

The exact normal-mixture identities

Let $V_T$ be any non-negative random variable and let $Z$ be a standard normal independent of it. Define

$$M_T = \sqrt{V_T}\,Z, \qquad X_T = -\tfrac12 V_T + \sqrt{V_T}\,Z.$$

In a stochastic-volatility model, $V_T=\int_0^T v_s\,ds$, $M_T$ is the Brownian return, and $X_T$ is the risk-neutral log return when the price Brownian motion is independent of the volatility process. Conditional normality gives the cumulant-generating functions

$$K_M(z)=K_V(z^2/2), \qquad K_X(z)=K_V((z^2-z)/2).$$

Comparing coefficients proves the exact identities

$$\kappa_{2r}(M_T)=(2r-1)!!\,\kappa_r(V_T), \qquad \kappa_{2r+1}(M_T)=0,$$ $$\kappa_4(M_T)=3\,\operatorname{Var}(V_T),$$ $$\kappa_4(X_T)=3\kappa_2(V_T)+\tfrac32\kappa_3(V_T)+\tfrac1{16}\kappa_4(V_T).$$

The last two formulas are not interchangeable. The common expression $3\operatorname{Var}(V_T)$ is exact for the martingale return $M_T$ but only the leading part of the log-return cumulant when the higher cumulants of $V_T$ are negligible.

A positive switched CIR variance

Now let a stationary finite-state chain $Y_t$ switch the long-run level of a CIR variance:

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta(Y_t)-v_t)\,dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t, \qquad v_0>0.$$

The Brownian motion $B$ is independent of $Y$ and of the price Brownian motion. We keep $\kappa$ and $\xi$ fixed and assume $2\kappa\min_i\theta_i\ge\xi^2$. Thus, from every $v_0>0$, the model stays strictly positive; equality is allowed. Write

$$B_\kappa(t)=\frac{1-e^{-\kappa t}}{\kappa}, \qquad \bar\theta=\mathbb E_\pi\theta(Y_0),$$ $$C_m(u)=\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(\theta(Y_0),\theta(Y_u)), \qquad K_m=\int_0^\infty C_m(u)\,du.$$

Integrating the CIR equation and then integrating over time gives

$$V_T=B_\kappa(T)v_0+\kappa\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-s)\theta(Y_s)\,ds +\xi\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-s)\sqrt{v_s}\,dB_s.$$

The regime integral and the martingale are uncorrelated. Since

$$\mathbb E[v_s]=\bar\theta+(v_0-\bar\theta)e^{-\kappa s},$$

Itô isometry gives the exact decomposition

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(V_T)={}&\xi^2\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-s)^2 \big(\bar\theta+(v_0-\bar\theta)e^{-\kappa s}\big)\,ds\\ &+2\kappa^2\int_0^T C_m(u)H_T(u)\,du, \end{aligned}$$ $$H_T(u)=\int_0^{T-u}B_\kappa(x)B_\kappa(x+u)\,dx.$$

The first line is the integrated-variance variance of the averaged CIR model. The second is exactly the extra variance created by the hidden regimes. This separation does not require an asymptotic expansion.

The Green–Kubo correction and a uniform bound

Put $I_2(T)=\int_0^T B_\kappa(s)^2\,ds$. Replacing the narrow correlation kernel by its mass $K_m$ gives

$$\operatorname{Var}(V_T)=\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T) +2\kappa^2 K_m I_2(T)+R_V(T,m).$$

The remainder can be bounded without fixing the maturity. Monotonicity and $0\le B_\kappa\le1/\kappa$ imply

$$|H_T(u)-H_T(0)|\le\frac{2u}{\kappa^2}.$$

Splitting the covariance integral at $T$ then gives

$$\sup_{T\ge0}|R_V(T,m)|\le4\int_0^\infty u\,|C_m(u)|\,du.$$

If the generator is $mQ_0$ and $|C_m(u)|\le A e^{-m\rho u}$, the right side is at most $4A/(m^2\rho^2)$. Consequently

$$\kappa_4(M_T)=3\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T) +6\kappa^2K_m I_2(T)+R_4(T,m),$$ $$\sup_{T\ge0}|R_4(T,m)|\le12\int_0^\infty u\,|C_m(u)|\,du=O(m^{-2}).$$

This is a finite-rate statement, uniform over all maturities. It is stronger than merely observing a first-order slope at one fixed expiry.

When drift and vol-of-vol switch together

The correct drift coordinates are not $\kappa$ and $\theta$ separately. Write $c_i=\kappa_i\theta_i$. If vol-of-vol also switches, put $a_i=\xi_i^2$. The regime-dependent part of the CIR variance generator is

$$c_i A_c+\kappa_i A_\kappa+a_i A_a,\qquad A_c=\partial_v,\quad A_\kappa=-v\partial_v,\quad A_a=\tfrac12v\partial_{vv}.$$

Thus the averaged drift is $\bar c-\bar\kappa v$, whose effective mean level is $\bar c/\bar\kappa$, generally not $\bar\theta$.

The Feller test is statewise, including vol-of-vol

Suppose $Y$ is independent of $B$, every $c_i,\kappa_i,\xi_i>0$, $v_0>0$, and

$$dv_t=(c_{Y_t}-\kappa_{Y_t}v_t)dt+\xi_{Y_t}\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t.$$

Statewise Feller theorem. Zero is inaccessible from every positive initial value and every initial regime if

$$2c_i\ge\xi_i^2\qquad\text{for every }i.$$

For an irreducible finite chain this condition is also necessary.

The result allows both mean reversion and vol-of-vol to switch. Put $K_t=\int_0^t\kappa_{Y_u}du$, $X_t=e^{K_t}v_t$, and introduce the strictly increasing clock

$$s(t)=\frac14\int_0^t e^{K_u}\xi_{Y_u}^2du.$$

Itô's formula followed by the quadratic-variation time change gives exactly

$$dZ_s=\delta_s\,ds+2\sqrt{Z_s}\,dW_s,\qquad Z_{s(t)}=X_t,\qquad \delta_{s(t)}={4c_{Y_t}\over\xi_{Y_t}^2}.$$

This is a squared-Bessel equation with an adapted, piecewise-constant dimension. Before a possible hit of zero,

$$d\log Z_s={\delta_s-2\over Z_s}\,ds+{2\over\sqrt{Z_s}}\,dW_s.$$

If every $\delta_s\ge2$, the finite-variation term is nonnegative; McKean's stopped-log argument rules out $\log Z_s\to-\infty$ in finite time. Equality is included. Unlike the common-$\xi$ proof, this argument does not apply a comparison theorem across unequal diffusion coefficients.

For necessity, if some regime $i$ has $2c_i<\xi_i^2$, let $q_i=-Q_{ii}$ and let $\tau_0$ be the zero-hitting time of the CIR process frozen in that regime. Conditional on starting in $i$, the probability of reaching zero before the first switch is

$$\Pr_{v_0,i}(\tau_00,$$ $$\alpha=\frac{q_i}{\kappa_i},\qquad \beta=\frac{2c_i}{\xi_i^2}<1,\qquad z=\frac{2\kappa_i v_0}{\xi_i^2},$$

where $U$ is Tricomi's confluent hypergeometric function. The formula follows by solving $\tfrac12\xi_i^2v u''+(c_i-\kappa_i v)u'=q_i u$ with $u(0)=1$ and $u(\infty)=0$. Accessibility of $i$ then gives positive boundary-hitting probability from every communicating regime. Consequently the Feller test cannot be applied only to the averaged model.

A two-state counterexample makes the distinction concrete. Take switching rate $q=1.5$, $(c_1,c_2)=(0.04,0.16)$, $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2)=(0.8,2)$, and $(\xi_1,\xi_2)=(0.4,0.3)$. For the symmetric fast chain the correctly averaged squared vol-of-vol is $\overline{\xi^2}=0.125$, so the averaged model passes with $2\bar c/\overline{\xi^2}=1.6$. Regime 1 nevertheless has $2c_1/\xi_1^2=0.5$. Starting from $v_0=0.04$ there, the exact probability of hitting zero before the next switch is $0.2341927683$. The boundary certificate independently evaluates the integral representation of $U$ and agrees below $9\times10^{-16}$.

Now let $K^{(0)}$ be the Green–Kubo matrix of the centered feature triple $(c,\kappa,a)$ for the unscaled generator $Q_0$. For a stationary-start chain with generator $mQ_0$, a regime-free polynomial payoff $f$, and fixed $T$, the semigroup expansion on every invariant finite-degree polynomial space is

$$P_m(T)f=e^{T\bar L}f+\frac1m\int_0^T e^{(T-s)\bar L}\mathcal D e^{s\bar L}f\,ds+O_T(m^{-2}),$$

where the remainder is in the finite-dimensional matrix norm and

$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal D={}&(K^{(0)}_{\kappa\kappa}v-K^{(0)}_{c\kappa})\partial_v\\ &+\left[K^{(0)}_{cc}+\tfrac12K^{(0)}_{ca} -\{K^{(0)}_{c\kappa}+K^{(0)}_{\kappa c} +\tfrac12K^{(0)}_{\kappa a}+K^{(0)}_{a\kappa}\}v +K^{(0)}_{\kappa\kappa}v^2\right]\partial_{vv}\\ &+\tfrac12\left[\{K^{(0)}_{ca}+K^{(0)}_{ac}+K^{(0)}_{aa}\}v -\{K^{(0)}_{\kappa a}+K^{(0)}_{a\kappa}\}v^2\right]\partial_{vvv}\\ &+\tfrac14K^{(0)}_{aa}v^2\partial_{vvvv}. \end{aligned}$$

Indeed, centering the three coefficient vectors and solving the fast Poisson equations gives $\mathcal D=\sum_{r,s\in\{c,\kappa,a\}}K^{(0)}_{rs}A_rA_s$; multiplying the differential operators yields the displayed formula. Standard finite-dimensional semigroup perturbation then gives the Duhamel term and its $O_T(m^{-2})$ remainder. In particular,

$$[A_c,A_\kappa]=-\partial_v,\qquad [A_c,A_a]=\tfrac12\partial_{vv},\qquad [A_\kappa,A_a]=\tfrac12v\partial_{vv}.$$

Thus reversing a nonreversible regime cycle transposes $K^{(0)}$ and flips the entire antisymmetric contribution

$$-K^{(0),\rm anti}_{c\kappa}\partial_v +\tfrac12K^{(0),\rm anti}_{ca}\partial_{vv} +\tfrac12K^{(0),\rm anti}_{\kappa a}v\partial_{vv},$$

while preserving every symmetric term. A reversible two-state chain has no directional term.

The same theorem supplies a cumulant correction without a common Riccati coefficient. Augment the state by $z_t=\int_0^t v_sds$; the averaged generator gains $v\partial_z$, while $\mathcal D$ is unchanged because the integrated coordinate itself is not switched. Define

$$a_r=[e^{T\bar L}z^r](v_0,0),\qquad b_r=\left[\int_0^T e^{(T-s)\bar L}\mathcal D e^{s\bar L}z^rds\right](v_0,0).$$

Then $\mathbb E[V_T^r]=a_r+b_r/m+O_T(m^{-2})$ for $r\le4$. Let $\bar\kappa_r$ be the cumulant formed from $(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$, and let $\dot\kappa_r$ be the directional derivative of that moment–cumulant polynomial in direction $(b_1,\ldots,b_r)$. The needed coefficients are

$$\begin{aligned} \dot\kappa_2={}&b_2-2a_1b_1,\\ \dot\kappa_3={}&b_3-3(a_1b_2+a_2b_1)+6a_1^2b_1,\\ \dot\kappa_4={}&b_4-4(a_1b_3+a_3b_1)-6a_2b_2\\ &+12(a_1^2b_2+2a_1a_2b_1)-24a_1^3b_1. \end{aligned}$$

For price Brownian noise independent of the variance driver, this gives the explicit fixed-maturity corrections

$$\kappa_4(M_T)=3\bar\kappa_2(V_T)+\frac{3\dot\kappa_2}{m}+O_T(m^{-2}),$$ $$\kappa_4(X_T)=3\bar\kappa_2+\frac32\bar\kappa_3+\frac1{16}\bar\kappa_4 +\frac1m\left(3\dot\kappa_2+\frac32\dot\kappa_3+\frac1{16}\dot\kappa_4\right)+O_T(m^{-2}).$$

Why the simultaneous-switching bound is not maturity-uniform

The dependence of the remainder on $T$ is necessary, already for the first integrated-variance moment. This is consistent with the fixed-time perturbed-semigroup framework of Kurtz (1973); the calculation below identifies the precise obstruction to strengthening the present expansion. Consider the symmetric two-state generator $Q_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, stationary law $\pi=(1/2,1/2)$, and coefficient vectors $c=(0.04,0.16)$ and $\kappa=(0.8,2)$. One may take $\xi=(0.2,0.2)$, so the statewise Feller inequalities are strict. Vol-of-vol does not enter the following mean calculation.

If $r_m$ is the stationary row vector with components $\mathbb E[v_t\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=i\}}]$, then

$$r_m\{mQ_0-\operatorname{diag}(\kappa)\} =-\pi\operatorname{diag}(c),\qquad \mu_m:=r_m\mathbf1=\frac{25m+13}{50(7m+4)}.$$

Consequently

$$\mu_m=\frac1{14}-\frac9{2450m}+\frac{18}{8575m^2}+O(m^{-3}),$$ $$\mu_m-\frac1{14}+\frac9{2450m} =\frac{18}{1225m(7m+4)}>0.$$

With $Y_0\sim\pi$ and any fixed finite initial variance mean, the stable two-state first-moment system gives $\mathbb E[V_T]=\mu_mT+O_m(1)$ as $T\to\infty$. Thus even after the averaged and first-order long-run slopes have been matched, the residual grows linearly in maturity for every fixed $m$. An absolute $O(m^{-2})$ remainder uniform over all $T\ge0$ is therefore impossible without retaining second-order long-run information.

The obstruction also survives a joint limit. Starting from $v_0=\mu_0=1/14$ and taking $T=m^2$, the exact matrix-exponential solution satisfies

$$\mathbb E[V_{m^2}]-\mu_0m^2-\mu_1m\longrightarrow\frac{18}{8575} =0.0020991254\ldots,\qquad \mu_1=-\frac9{2450}.$$

For $m=8,16,32,64,128$, the certificate obtains respectively $0.0022948980$, $0.0021929979$, $0.0021449064$, $0.0021217062$, and $0.0021103356$. This counterexample concerns simultaneous switching. It does not weaken the separate fixed-$\kappa$ covariance result above, whose special structure yields a maturity-uniform bound.

Uniformity recovered by retaining the exact slow mode

The secular obstruction can be removed at the first-moment level without returning to the full switched diffusion. Let $Q_0$ be any irreducible finite-state generator with stationary row vector $\pi$, put $K=\operatorname{diag}(\kappa)$ with $\min_i\kappa_i>0$, and assume $Y_0\sim\pi$. If

$$r_m(t)_i=\mathbb E[v_t\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=i\}}],\qquad A_m=mQ_0-K,\qquad b=\pi\operatorname{diag}(c),$$

then $r_m'=r_mA_m+b$. Its stationary row vector and long-run mean are

$$r_{m,\infty}A_m=-b,\qquad \mu_m=r_{m,\infty}\mathbf1.$$

Let $\lambda_m<0$ be the simple principal eigenvalue of the irreducible Metzler matrix $A_m$, and let $P_m$ be its Riesz projection. For $d_m=r_m(0)-r_{m,\infty}$ define the slope-and-mode composite

$$\mathcal M_m(T)=\mu_mT+ d_mP_m\mathbf1\,\frac{e^{\lambda_mT}-1}{\lambda_m}.$$

The exact remainder is

$$\mathbb E[V_T]-\mathcal M_m(T) =d_m(I-P_m)A_m^{-1}(e^{A_mT}-I)\mathbf1.$$

Analytic spectral perturbation gives $P_m=\mathbf1\pi+O(m^{-1})$, a complementary inverse of order $m^{-1}$, and a complementary semigroup bounded by $Ce^{-cmT}$. In particular $(I-P_m)\mathbf1=O(m^{-1})$, and therefore

$$\sup_{T\ge0}|\mathbb E[V_T]-\mathcal M_m(T)|=O(m^{-2}).$$

If the initial variance is independent of the stationary regime, so $r_m(0)=v_0\pi$, the left complementary amplitude is also $O(m^{-1})$ and the rate improves to

$$\sup_{T\ge0}|\mathbb E[V_T]-\mathcal M_m(T)|=O(m^{-3}).$$

This is the affine first-moment analogue of retaining an exact Perron exponent at the long end. The spectral step follows the isolated-eigenprojection perturbation framework of Kato (1949). It does not assert a uniform approximation for higher integrated-variance cumulants, leveraged returns, option prices, or the full transition semigroup.

For the preceding strictly positive two-state model, the certificate maximizes the exact error over $0\le T\le m^2$. At $m=8,16,32,64,128$, independent initial variance gives errors $3.834\times10^{-6}$, $5.130\times10^{-7}$, $6.635\times10^{-8}$, $8.437\times10^{-9}$, and $1.064\times10^{-9}$, with measured rate $2.987$. Allowing the conditional initial means to differ by regime gives $6.056\times10^{-5}$, $1.631\times10^{-5}$, $4.233\times10^{-6}$, $1.078\times10^{-6}$, and $2.721\times10^{-7}$, with measured rate $1.986$.

An explicit Green–Kubo slow mode

The eigenvectors in the preceding composite are not necessary at the target $O(m^{-2})$ accuracy. Let $Q_0^{\#}$ be the group inverse, normalized by $Q_0Q_0^{\#}=Q_0^{\#}Q_0=I-\mathbf1\pi$, and define

$$\bar\kappa=\pi\kappa,\qquad \widetilde\kappa=\kappa-\bar\kappa\mathbf1,\qquad h=Q_0^{\#}\widetilde\kappa,$$ $$K_{\kappa\kappa}=-\pi\!\left(\widetilde\kappa\,h\right).$$

Ordinary isolated-eigenvalue perturbation gives the two explicit expansions

$$\lambda_m=-\bar\kappa+\frac{K_{\kappa\kappa}}m+O(m^{-2}),\qquad P_m\mathbf1=\mathbf1+\frac hm+O(m^{-2}).$$

The second coefficient is the Poisson corrector for the switched mean-reversion rate; the first is its Green–Kubo quadratic form. Retain the exact stationary slope $\mu_m$, but replace the spectral data by

$$\widehat\lambda_m=-\bar\kappa+\frac{K_{\kappa\kappa}}m,$$ $$\widehat{\mathcal M}_m(T)=\mu_mT+ d_m\left(\mathbf1+\frac hm\right) \frac{e^{\widehat\lambda_mT}-1}{\widehat\lambda_m}.$$

Because $(e^{\lambda T}-1)/\lambda$ and its first derivative in $\lambda$ are bounded uniformly over $T\ge0$ when $\lambda$ stays to the left of a fixed negative constant, replacing $(\lambda_m,P_m\mathbf1)$ by these first-order expressions costs only $O(m^{-2})$. Combining this with the fast-complement estimate proves

$$\sup_{T\ge0}|\mathbb E[V_T]-\widehat{\mathcal M}_m(T)|=O(m^{-2}).$$

The exact slope is essential: any nonzero error in its coefficient is multiplied by $T$ and therefore cannot be bounded uniformly over all maturities. The explicit composite needs one linear solve for $\mu_m$ and one group inverse of $Q_0$, but no $m$-dependent eigenvectors.

For the two-state example, the uniform errors at $m=8,16,32,64,128$ are $1.779\times10^{-5}$, $4.888\times10^{-6}$, $1.282\times10^{-6}$, $3.283\times10^{-7}$, and $8.307\times10^{-8}$ for an independent initial variance, with rate $1.983$. For regime-dependent conditional initial means they are $8.448\times10^{-5}$, $2.200\times10^{-5}$, $5.617\times10^{-6}$, $1.419\times10^{-6}$, and $3.568\times10^{-7}$, with rate $1.992$. A nonreversible three-state check gives rates $1.954$ and $1.987$, respectively.

Removing the exact stationary solve on growing maturity windows

The exact slope is necessary on the whole half-line, but it can be replaced to any prescribed algebraic order on a correspondingly growing maturity window. Write $D_\kappa=\operatorname{diag}(\kappa)$, $D_c=\operatorname{diag}(c)$, $S=Q_0^{\#}$, and seek

$$r_{m,\infty}=\sum_{j=0}^p m^{-j}\rho_j+O(m^{-p-1}),\qquad \mu_m=\sum_{j=0}^p m^{-j}\mu_j+O(m^{-p-1}),\quad \mu_j=\rho_j\mathbf1.$$

All coefficients follow from one group inverse and a Poisson recursion. First set

$$\mu_0=\frac{\pi c}{\bar\kappa},\qquad \rho_0=\mu_0\pi,$$ $$z_1=(\rho_0D_\kappa-\pi D_c)S,\qquad \mu_1=-\frac{z_1\kappa}{\bar\kappa},\qquad \rho_1=z_1+\mu_1\pi.$$

For every $j\ge1$, continue with

$$z_{j+1}=\rho_jD_\kappa S,\qquad \mu_{j+1}=-\frac{z_{j+1}\kappa}{\bar\kappa},\qquad \rho_{j+1}=z_{j+1}+\mu_{j+1}\pi.$$

The normalization makes $z_j\mathbf1=0$ and the choice of $\mu_j$ enforces the next solvability condition $\rho_j\kappa=0$. Substitution into $r_{m,\infty}(mQ_0-D_\kappa)=-\pi D_c$ proves the expansion recursively; finite-dimensional resolvent analyticity, in the sense of Kato (1949), supplies its remainder. In particular,

$$\mu_1=\frac{\pi\!\left[(c-\mu_0\kappa) Q_0^{\#}(\kappa-\bar\kappa\mathbf1)\right]}{\bar\kappa}.$$

Replace $r_{m,\infty}$ and $\mu_m$ in the explicit Green–Kubo slow-mode composite by their order-$p$ truncations. For each fixed $p\ge1$ and any deterministic horizon $T_m$, the same fast-mode estimate gives

$$\sup_{0\le T\le T_m}\left|\mathbb E[V_T]- \widehat{\mathcal M}^{[p]}_m(T)\right| \le C_p\left(m^{-2}+T_m m^{-p-1}\right).$$

Consequently $T_m=O(m^{p-1})$ preserves the $O(m^{-2})$ accuracy without any $m$-dependent linear solve: one slope correction covers bounded maturities, two cover maturities of order $m$, and three cover maturities of order $m^2$. This is a joint switching-speed/maturity theorem, not an all-maturity approximation. Uniformity over every $T\ge0$ still requires the exact slope.

For the positive two-state benchmark, the recursion gives $(\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)=(0.0714285714,-0.0036734694, 0.0020991254,-0.0011995002)$. The measured slope-remainder orders for $p=1,2,3$ are $1.994$, $2.994$, and $3.994$; the full composite orders on $T\le m^{p-1}$ are $1.990$, $1.986$, and $1.992$. A nonreversible three-state check gives slope orders $1.995$, $2.995$, and $3.997$.

The exact long-run second-cumulant slope and intercept

The higher-cumulant expansion above is fixed-maturity, but its first secular coefficient can still be obtained exactly when all three variance coefficients switch. Let the joint process $(Y_t,v_t)$ start in its invariant law $\Pi_m$, put $a_i=\xi_i^2$, and define the row vectors

$$r_i=\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[v\mathbf1_{\{Y=i\}}],\qquad s_i=\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[v^2\mathbf1_{\{Y=i\}}].$$

With $A_m=mQ_0-D_\kappa$ and $B_m=mQ_0-2D_\kappa$, stationarity of the first two polynomial moments gives the finite linear systems

$$rA_m=-\pi D_c,\qquad sB_m=-rD_{2c+a},\qquad \mu_m=r\mathbf1.$$

The centered Poisson equation for the joint generator,

$$-\mathcal L_m\phi(i,v)=v-\mu_m,$$

has an affine solution $\phi_i(v)=\alpha_i v+\beta_i$. Its coefficients require only two more finite solves:

$$A_m\alpha=-\mathbf1,qquad mQ_0\beta=\mu_m\mathbf1-D_c\alpha,qquad \pi\beta=0.$$

The second equation is solvable because $\pi D_c\alpha=-\pi D_cA_m^{-1}\mathbf1=r\mathbf1=\mu_m$. The stationary covariance identity, or direct integration of the affine conditional mean, therefore proves the exact formula

$$\lim_{T\to\infty}{\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_m}(V_T)\over T} =:\sigma_m^2 =2\left\{(s-\mu_m r)\alpha+ (r-\mu_m\pi)\beta\right\}.$$

No reversibility is used. The affine conditional-mean system is exponentially stable, so the stronger fixed-rate statement is $\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_m}(V_T)=\sigma_m^2T+O_m(1)$ as $T\to\infty$. This is the finite-dimensional switched-CIR specialization of the Poisson-equation method developed for ergodic diffusions by Pardoux and Veretennikov (2001).

The bounded term is also explicit. Put $\bar\phi_m=\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[\phi]=r\alpha+\pi\beta$ and solve the second affine Poisson equation $-\mathcal L_m\psi=\phi-\bar\phi_m$, with $\psi_i(v)=p_iv+q_i$. Its coefficients are

$$A_mp=-\alpha,\qquad mQ_0q=-\beta+\bar\phi_m\mathbf1-D_cp,\qquad \pi q=0.$$

Solvability follows from $\pi D_cp=-\pi D_cA_m^{-1}\alpha=r\alpha=\bar\phi_m$; the last equality uses $\pi\beta=0$. Integrating the stationary covariance twice gives the exact finite-maturity decomposition

$$\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_m}(V_T)=\sigma_m^2T+\chi_m+\rho_m(T),$$ $$\chi_m=-2\left\{(s-\mu_mr)p+(r-\mu_m\pi)q\right\}, \qquad \rho_m(T)=2\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}\!\left[(v_0-\mu_m)(P_T\psi)(Y_0,v_0)\right].$$

The decay can in fact be made uniform over every half-line of switching rates. Assume $\kappa_*:=\min_i\kappa_i>0$ and fix $m_0>0$. If $(P_T\psi)(i,v)=a_m(T)_i+b_m(T)_iv$, then

$$b_m(T)=e^{A_mT}p,\qquad a_m(T)=e^{mQ_0T}q+\int_0^T e^{mQ_0(T-u)}D_c b_m(u)\,du,$$ $$\rho_m(T)=2\left\{(s-\mu_mr)b_m(T)+(r-\mu_m\pi)a_m(T)\right\}.$$

The first semigroup is a finite-state chain killed at rate $\kappa_i$, so the Feynman–Kac representation gives $\lVert e^{A_mT}\rVert_\infty\le e^{-\kappa_*T}$. Irreducibility of $Q_0$ gives constants $M_Q,\gamma_Q>0$ with $\lVert e^{tQ_0}-\mathbf1\pi\rVert\le M_Qe^{-\gamma_Qt}$. Because $(r-\mu_m\pi)\mathbf1=0$, the constant term is therefore bounded by a convolution of $e^{-m\gamma_Q(T-u)}$ and $e^{-\kappa_*u}$. Uniform boundedness of $r,s,\alpha,\beta,p,q$ for $m\ge m_0$ follows from the killed resolvents, the fixed group inverse of $Q_0$, and the stationary moment bounds $\mu_m\le\max_i c_i/\kappa_*$ and $\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}v^2\le(2\max_i c_i+\max_i a_i)\mu_m/(2\kappa_*)$. At a resonant pair of rates, $Te^{-\lambda T}$ is absorbed by $e^{-\lambda T/2}$. Consequently there are constants $C<\infty$ and

$$\gamma={1\over2}\min\{m_0\gamma_Q,\kappa_*\}>0$$ $$\text{such that}\qquad \sup_{m\ge m_0}\sup_{T\ge0}e^{\gamma T} \left|\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_m}(V_T)-\sigma_m^2T-\chi_m\right|\le C.$$

This is a joint maturity–switching-rate bound for the exact coefficients $\sigma_m^2$ and $\chi_m$; it does not make a finite $1/m$ truncation maturity-uniform, because any nonzero error in the slope is still multiplied by $T$. No reversibility is required.

For the strictly positive two-state benchmark above, with $a=(0.04,0.04)$, exact elimination gives

$$\sigma_m^2= {288+4276m+19103m^2+35325m^3+28825m^4+8750m^5 \over 1280000m+7840000m^2+17640000m^3+17150000m^4+6002500m^5},$$ $$\sigma_m^2={1\over686}+{153\over240100m} -{369\over1680700m^2}+O(m^{-3}).$$

The same exact solves give

This theorem identifies the exact stationary long-time slope and intercept, not a maturity-uniform $O(m^{-2})$ approximation to the whole variance curve. Replacing either coefficient by a finite expansion still leaves a joint-limit error.

The exact long-run third-cumulant slope and intercept

The Poisson hierarchy closes one level further. Let $t_i=\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[v^3\mathbf1_{\{Y=i\}}]$ and $C_m=mQ_0-3D_\kappa$. Stationarity gives the additional finite system

$$tC_m=-sD_{3(c+a)}.$$

Put $g=v-\mu_m$ and center the first Poisson solution under the joint invariant law:

$$\phi_i^\circ(v)=\alpha_iv+\beta_i^\circ,\qquad \bar\phi_m=r\alpha+\pi\beta,\qquad \beta^\circ=\beta-\bar\phi_m\mathbf1.$$

This centering is essential: the convenient normalization $\pi\beta=0$ does not imply $\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}\phi=0$. Define $c_2=\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[g\phi^\circ]=\sigma_m^2/2$ and solve the nested Poisson equation

$$-\mathcal L_m\psi=g\phi^\circ-c_2,qquad \psi_i(v)=u_iv^2+p_iv+q_i.$$

Its coefficients again require only finite linear solves:

$$B_mu=-\alpha,$$ $$A_mp=-(\beta^\circ-\mu_m\alpha)-D_{2c+a}u,$$ $$mQ_0q=\mu_m\beta^\circ+c_2\mathbf1-D_cp,qquad \pi q=0.$$

Solvability of the last equation follows by taking the invariant expectation of the nested Poisson equation. Ordering the three time arguments in the stationary third-correlation integral then gives the exact, nonreversible formula

$$\lim_{T\to\infty}{\kappa_3(V_T)\over T}=:\tau_m =6\left\{(t-\mu_ms)u+(s-\mu_mr)p+(r-\mu_m\pi)q\right\}.$$

The same ordered-correlation representation also determines the bounded term exactly. Let $R_m=(-\mathcal L_m)^{-1}$ denote the centered Poisson inverse, use the joint-centered representatives $\phi^\circ=R_mg$ and $\psi^\circ=R_m(g\phi^\circ-c_2)$, and define

$$\eta=R_m\phi^\circ,\qquad \zeta=R_m\psi^\circ,\qquad \omega=R_m\{g\eta-\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[g\eta]\}.$$

If $C_m(a,b)=\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[g_0g_ag_{a+b}]$, stationarity and the six strict time orderings give

$$\kappa_3(V_T)=6\int_{a,b\ge0,\ a+b\le T}(T-a-b)C_m(a,b)\,da\,db.$$

Integrating each time gap in turn yields the exact decomposition

$$\kappa_3(V_T)=\tau_mT+\chi_m+\rho_{3,m}(T),\qquad \chi_m=-6\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[g(\zeta+\omega)],$$ $$\rho_{3,m}(T)=6\int_{a+b>T}(a+b-T)C_m(a,b)\,da\,db.$$

For each fixed $m>0$, stability of the nonconstant degree-two polynomial semigroup gives constants $C_m,\gamma_m>0$ with $|\rho_{3,m}(T)|\le C_m(1+T)e^{-\gamma_mT}$. The two terms in $\chi_m$ are distinct: $\zeta$ weights the first time gap and $\omega$ weights the second. This is the next member of the additive-functional Poisson hierarchy used above and in Pardoux and Veretennikov (2001). The broader analytic-cumulant framework via multiplicative Poisson equations is developed by Kontoyiannis and Meyn (2003); the formula here is a direct continuous-time correlation calculation using switched-CIR polynomial closure.

For the positive two-state benchmark, at $m=8,16,32,64,128$ the exact rates are $0.0000936333$, $0.0000915632$, $0.0000904367$, $0.0000898504$, and $0.0000895514$. They converge to the averaged-CIR value $0.0000892485$ at measured first order $0.991$. An independent degree-three polynomial semigroup, differenced between maturities 20 and 40, agrees within $2.7\times10^{-13}$; a nonreversible three-state check agrees within $9.5\times10^{-14}$. Omitting the joint-invariant centering would change the $m=8$ answer by $0.0002367787$, more than twice the true rate.

At the same speeds the exact intercepts are $-0.0001359353$, $-0.0001318630$, $-0.0001297164$, $-0.0001286160$, and $-0.0001280592$. They converge to the averaged-CIR value $-0.0001274979$ at measured order $0.994$. At maturity 20, the independent degree-three semigroup matches $\tau_mT+\chi_m$ within $3.6\times10^{-13}$; at maturity 10 the nonreversible three-state residual is $7.1\times10^{-14}$. These are exact coefficients for each fixed $m$: no switching-rate-uniform third-cumulant remainder bound or maturity-uniform finite-$1/m$ truncation is claimed.

Leverage can be included without conditional normality by augmenting once more with $M_t=\int_0^t\sqrt{v_s}dW_s$. Allow the instantaneous correlation itself to switch:

$$d\langle W,B\rangle_t=\rho_{Y_t}dt,\qquad |\rho_i|\le1,\qquad \eta_i=\rho_i\xi_i.$$

The two regime-dependent diffusion coefficients are $a_i=\xi_i^2$ and $\eta_i$; neither $\rho_i$ nor $\xi_i$ alone is a generator coordinate. On polynomials in $(M,v,z)$, define

$$A_\eta=v\partial_{Mv},\qquad \bar L=\bar cA_c+\bar\kappa A_\kappa+\bar aA_a+\bar\eta A_\eta +\tfrac12v\partial_{MM}+v\partial_z.$$

The averaged model is again a valid Heston diffusion, with

$$\xi_{\rm eff}=\sqrt{\bar a},\qquad \rho_{\rm eff}=\frac{\bar\eta}{\sqrt{\bar a}},\qquad |\rho_{\rm eff}|\le1.$$

There is a sharper admissibility statement. For fixed positive statewise vol-of-vol values, the exact attainable interval is

$$|\rho_{\rm eff}|\le {\bar\xi\over\sqrt{\overline{\xi^2}}} ={1\over\sqrt{1+\operatorname{CV}_\pi(\xi)^2}}\le1.$$

The first inequality is the triangle inequality $|\mathbb E_\pi(\rho\xi)|\le\mathbb E_\pi\xi$. It is sharp: the two endpoints are attained by $\rho_i\equiv+1$ and $\rho_i\equiv-1$, and every intermediate value is attained by a constant correlation. Hence heterogeneous $\xi_i$ strictly contracts even the most extreme homogenized correlation away from $\pm1$. Equivalently, the determinant of the averaged instantaneous covariance matrix is nonnegative, and at either endpoint equals $v^2\operatorname{Var}_\pi(\xi)$.

The boundary parameter is compatible with the same averaging:

$$2\bar c-\bar a=\sum_i\pi_i(2c_i-\xi_i^2).$$

Thus statewise Feller admissibility implies admissibility of the averaged CIR model. The converse is false, as the explicit two-state hitting example above shows; averaging can conceal a reachable boundary in one regime. This distinction is pathwise and does not depend on the switching rate.

Let $K^{(0)}$ now be the Green–Kubo matrix of the centered feature vector $(c,\kappa,a,\eta)$. For fixed maturity on every invariant finite-degree polynomial space, the corrector is

$$\mathcal D_{\rm lev}=\sum_{r,s\in\{c,\kappa,a,\eta\}}K^{(0)}_{rs}A_rA_s,$$

with an $O_T(m^{-2})$ semigroup remainder. For constant $\rho$, writing $\eta_i=\rho\xi_i$ is exactly equivalent to the earlier $(\xi_i,\rho A_\eta)$ representation. For switched $\rho$, however, separately averaging $\rho$ and $\xi$ is generally wrong; the relevant average and Poisson equation use $\eta=\rho\xi$. The new commutators are

$$[A_c,A_\eta]=\partial_{Mv},\qquad [A_\kappa,A_\eta]=0, \qquad [A_a,A_\eta]=\tfrac12v\partial_{Mvv}.$$

Thus cycle reversal also flips $K^{(0),\rm anti}_{c\eta}\partial_{Mv} +\tfrac12K^{(0),\rm anti}_{a\eta}v\partial_{Mvv}$, while preserving the symmetric correction. Put $x=M-z/2$ and define

$$\alpha_r=[e^{T\bar L_\rho}x^r](0,v_0,0),\qquad \beta_r=\left[\int_0^T e^{(T-s)\bar L_\rho}\mathcal D_\rho e^{s\bar L_\rho}x^rds\right](0,v_0,0).$$

Direct differentiation of the fourth moment–cumulant polynomial gives

$$\kappa_4(X_T)=\bar\kappa_4^X+\frac{\dot\kappa_4^X}{m}+O_T(m^{-2}),$$ $$\dot\kappa_4^X=\beta_4-4(\alpha_1\beta_3+\alpha_3\beta_1)-6\alpha_2\beta_2 +12(\alpha_1^2\beta_2+2\alpha_1\alpha_2\beta_1)-24\alpha_1^3\beta_1.$$

This is the simultaneous-$(\kappa\theta,\kappa,\xi^2,\rho\xi)$ leverage correction. It agrees with the independent-price formula above when $\eta=0$, but does not assume that reduction under leverage.

The independent certificate works on polynomials through degree five, an invariant subspace of the CIR generator. For a nonreversible three-state chain it compares the original coupled semigroup with the displayed Duhamel correction. The averaged and symmetric-only errors remain first order, whereas the full residual has rate 1.994. Reversing the chain transposes $K^{(0)}$; after subtracting the commutator correction, the forward-minus-reverse residual has rate 1.985. The matrix identity between the displayed and abstract correctors holds to $6.9\times10^{-18}$. In the augmented degree-four system, the averaged fourth cumulants are $0.0003932318$ for $M_T$ and $0.0003946899$ for $X_T$; their $m^{-1}$ coefficients are $0.0000922515$ and $0.0000931020$. Both corrected residuals have measured rate 1.992. At $\rho=-0.7$, the averaged $\kappa_4(X_T)$ is $0.0009887961$, its simultaneous-switching coefficient is $0.0001250489$, and the corrected residual has rate 1.990. For switched correlations $(-0.80,-0.25,0.35)$, the effective correlation is $-0.20052942$, the averaged fourth cumulant is $0.0004572129$, the first-order coefficient is $0.0003411583$, and the corrected residual has rate 1.986. Its sharp admissible interval is $[-0.99580913,0.99580913]$, rather than merely $[-1,1]$. The certificate independently verifies the constant-$\rho$ equivalence to $2.1\times10^{-17}$, the three displayed commutators exactly on the polynomial space, and Green–Kubo transposition under cycle reversal to $1.4\times10^{-17}$. A separate 10,000-case randomized certificate checks the sharp correlation envelope and positive-semidefinite averaged covariance matrix. The $\rho=0$ augmented calculation reproduces both independent-price numbers within numerical precision. The parameter choice satisfies the strict Feller inequality in every regime.

This extension is deliberately an operator theorem, not another closed Riccati formula. When $\kappa$ varies, the regime transform coefficients are no longer represented by one common scalar $B_s$, so the explicit one-dimensional call correction below does not carry over unchanged.

What leverage changes exactly

Now allow the price and variance Brownian motions to have correlation $\rho$:

$$dM_t=\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t,\qquad d\langle W,B\rangle_t=\rho\,dt,\qquad X_T=M_T-\tfrac12V_T.$$

Conditional on the variance path, $M_T$ is no longer an independent centered normal with variance $V_T$. The replacement is an exact identity that uses joint cumulants. Write $\kappa_{r,s}$ for the joint cumulant with $r$ copies of $M_T$ and $s$ copies of $V_T$. Multilinearity gives

$$\kappa_4(X_T)=\kappa_{4,0}-2\kappa_{3,1} +\tfrac32\kappa_{2,2}-\tfrac12\kappa_{1,3}+\tfrac1{16}\kappa_{0,4}.$$

This statement needs only fourth moments. When $\rho=0$, conditional normality reduces its five terms to

$$\kappa_{4,0}=3\kappa_{0,2},\qquad \kappa_{3,1}=\kappa_{1,3}=0, \qquad \kappa_{2,2}=\kappa_{0,3},$$

recovering the earlier formula. None of these four reductions should be assumed under leverage.

An independent affine referee

The leveraged certificate solves every polynomial moment $\mathbb E[M_T^aV_T^bv_T^c\mathbf1_{\{Y_T=i\}}]$ with $a+b+c\le4$. This system closes exactly under the switched CIR generator. $$B'=-\kappa B+\tfrac12\xi^2B^2+\rho\xi uB+\tfrac12u^2-\tfrac12u,$$ $$a'=Qa+\kappa B\,\operatorname{diag}(\theta)a, \qquad K_X(u)=B(T)v_0+\log(\pi^\top a(T)).$$

Cauchy coefficient extraction from this transform agrees with the polynomial fourth cumulant below $3\times10^{-9}$.

An explicit leveraged option-price correction

Set $\bar\theta=(\theta_++\theta_-)/2$ and $\delta=(\theta_+-\theta_-)/2$, and let the symmetric chain flip at rate $m$. The price and variance Brownian motions may now have any fixed correlation $\rho$. For an MGF argument $s$, the Heston Riccati coefficient remains independent of the regime:

$$B_s'=-\kappa B_s+\tfrac12\xi^2B_s^2+\rho\xi sB_s+\tfrac12(s^2-s), \qquad B_s(0)=0.$$

Write the two regime amplitudes as $a_\pm$, their mean as $a=(a_++a_-)/2$, their half-difference as $d=(a_+-a_-)/2$, and $r=d/a$. With $g(t)=\kappa\delta B_s(t)$, their exact equations reduce to

$$\frac{d}{dt}\log a=\kappa\bar\theta B_s+g r, \qquad r'=g-2mr-gr^2,\qquad r(0)=0.$$

The stationary start and $B_s(0)=0$ remove the leading initial layer. Therefore, at fixed maturity and uniformly for $s$ in any compact subset of the MGF strip,

$$\log\Phi_m(s)=\log\Phi_{\rm av}(s)+\frac{h_T(s)}{m}+O(m^{-2}),$$ $$h_T(s)=\frac{\kappa^2\delta^2}{2}\int_0^T B_s(t)^2\,dt.$$

This has an elementary finite-rate proof. If $G=\sup|g|$, $L=\sup|g'|$, and $m\ge G$, a bootstrap in the Riccati equation gives $|r|\le G/m$. For $e=r-g/(2m)$,

$$\sup_{t\le T}|e(t)|\le\frac{L}{4m^2}+\frac{G^3}{2m^3},$$

so the remainder in the displayed log transform is bounded by $TG[L/(4m^2)+G^3/(2m^3)]$. This bound is fixed-maturity and compact-transform-domain; it is not the maturity-uniform cumulant bound above.

For log strike $k$, Carr–Madan damping $\alpha>0$, and $D_\alpha(u)=\alpha^2+\alpha-u^2+i(2\alpha+1)u$, the normalized call price consequently has first correction

$$C_m(k)=C_{\rm av}(k)+\frac{c_1(k)}m+O(m^{-2}),$$ $$c_1(k)=\frac{e^{-\alpha k}}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \Re\!\left(\frac{e^{-iuk}\Phi_{\rm av}(\alpha+1+iu) h_T(\alpha+1+iu)}{D_\alpha(u)}\right)du.$$

The stated error follows directly on every truncated frequency interval. Passing to the infinite integral additionally requires the usual Heston moment-strip and Fourier-tail domination conditions. The certificate uses $k=0$, $\alpha=1$, cutoff 80, and doubles the Gauss–Legendre order from 320 to 640; both $C_{\rm av}$ and $c_1$ change by less than $2\times10^{-10}$.

The corresponding implied-volatility correction

The price result transfers directly to Black–Scholes implied volatility, but only where inversion is well conditioned. Let $C_{\rm BS}(k,\sigma)$ be the normalized zero-rate Black–Scholes call, define $\sigma_{\rm av}(k)$ by $C_{\rm BS}(k,\sigma_{\rm av}(k))=C_{\rm av}(k)$, and write $\mathcal V_{\rm av}(k)=\partial_\sigma C_{\rm BS}(k,\sigma_{\rm av}(k))$.

On a compact strike interval $K$, assume the call expansion is uniform, all prices remain strictly inside their no-arbitrage bounds, and $\mathcal V_{\rm av}\ge v_*>0$. The implicit-function theorem then gives, uniformly for $k\in K$,

$$\sigma_m(k)=\sigma_{\rm av}(k)+\frac{b_1(k)}m+O(m^{-2}),\qquad b_1(k)=\frac{c_1(k)}{\mathcal V_{\rm av}(k)}.$$

The error statement has an elementary quantitative form. If $|C_m-C_{\rm av}-c_1/m|\le A/m^2$, $|c_1|\le B$, Vega is at least $v_*$ throughout the intervening volatility interval, and Black–Scholes volga is at most $M$, then

$$\left|\sigma_m-\sigma_{\rm av}-\frac{c_1}{m\mathcal V_{\rm av}}\right| \le\frac1{m^2}\left\{\frac A{v_*}+ \frac{M(A+B)^2}{2v_*^3}\right\}.$$

This follows by the mean-value theorem to obtain $|\sigma_m-\sigma_{\rm av}|\le(A+B)/(v_*m)$, followed by one Taylor expansion of $C_{\rm BS}$. The lower-Vega condition is essential: no strike-uniform wing claim is being made.

Scope

  • The chain starts in stationarity. A known or filtered starting regime adds an initial-memory term.
  • The maturity-uniform Green–Kubo bound above is proved for the independent-price benchmark. The leverage section supplies an exact finite-rate identity and exact computation, not a uniform asymptotic error bound.
  • The call correction assumes a stationary symmetric two-state chain, fixed correlation $\rho$, fixed maturity, and a valid damped Fourier contour. It is uniform only on compact transform contours unless separate tail bounds are supplied; no maturity-uniform leverage bound is claimed.
  • The implied-volatility correction is uniform only on compact strike sets where the price expansion is uniform and Black–Scholes Vega has a positive lower bound. It deliberately excludes vanishing-Vega wings.
  • The exact integrated-variance decomposition, maturity-uniform cumulant bound, and explicit Fourier call correction assume fixed $\kappa$ and switch only the CIR mean level. The simultaneous $(\kappa\theta,\kappa,\xi^2)$ results are separate fixed-maturity polynomial-generator and cumulant theorems; with leverage the feature list additionally contains $\rho\xi$ because that product is the cross-derivative coefficient. The exact two-state mean calculation above proves that their first-order remainder cannot be absolute and maturity-uniform in general. Retaining the exact stationary slope and either the exact principal first-moment mode or its explicit first-order Green–Kubo expansion restores a uniform mean approximation only. Truncating the Poisson recursion at order $p$ instead gives a joint-limit result only through $T=O(m^{p-1})$. The two affine Poisson solves identify the exact long-run second-cumulant slope and intercept under joint stationarity. For every $m\ge m_0>0$, their exact remainder has one switching-rate-uniform exponential envelope. This does not make a finite $1/m$ truncation of the slope maturity-uniform. The nested polynomial Poisson solves identify the exact third-cumulant slope and intercept for each fixed $m$, with a fixed-$m$ exponentially decaying remainder. No switching-rate-uniform third-cumulant envelope, fourth-cumulant long-run formula, or price-level extension is claimed.
  • The statewise Feller theorem is pathwise and exact for every switching speed and initial regime. It is valid when vol-of-vol also switches, and is separate from the stationary-start, fixed-maturity asymptotic expansions.
  • The result identifies the fourth cumulant of the martingale return. The exact formula shows explicitly what else is needed for the risk-neutral log return.

The conditional integrated-variance viewpoint goes back to Hull and White (1987); the positive square-root model is from Cox, Ingersoll and Ross (1985). Boundary classification and the martingale time change use Feller (1951) and Dambis (1965). The leveraged affine referee follows the transform structure of Heston (1993); switched-Heston calibration is treated by Papanicolaou and Sircar (2014). The finite-dimensional moment construction follows the polynomial-process framework of Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012). The contribution here is the exact switched-CIR covariance decomposition, its finite-rate Green–Kubo bound, the explicit joint-cumulant benchmark showing precisely which terms leverage adds, full-transform European option and implied-volatility corrections, and fixed-maturity effective-generator and cumulant corrections when $\kappa\theta$, $\kappa$, and vol-of-vol switch, together with an exact long-maturity obstruction and an exact finite-rate formulas for the stationary second-cumulant slope and intercept and the third-cumulant slope in that simultaneous-switching model. The damped inversion follows Carr and Madan (1999).
