Cumulants of integrated variance under a fast regime

The fourth cumulant of a return whose CIR variance has a fast switching level, and what its Green–Kubo form leaves out at every maturity.

The model

A stationary finite-state chain $Y_t$ switches the long-run level of a square-root (CIR) variance:

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta(Y_t)-v_t)\,dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t, \qquad v_0>0.$$

The Brownian motion $B$ is independent of $Y$ and of the price Brownian motion. We keep $\kappa$ and $\xi$ fixed and assume $2\kappa\min_i\theta_i\ge\xi^2$, so the variance stays strictly positive.

The integrated variance is $V_T=\int_0^T v_s\,ds$. With $Z$ a standard normal independent of it, the Brownian return and the risk-neutral log return are

$$M_T = \sqrt{V_T}\,Z, \qquad X_T = -\tfrac12 V_T + \sqrt{V_T}\,Z.$$

Leverage, a correlation between the price and variance Brownian motions, is taken up below.

A separate initial-regime note extends the integrated-variance bound to every starting prior and every maturity, including random initial variance correlated with the regime. Explicit noise and initial-correlation corrections are generally first order, even with a stationary regime marginal.

The quantity

The cumulants of $V_T$, $M_T$ and $X_T$. Conditional normality gives the cumulant-generating functions

$$K_M(z)=K_V(z^2/2), \qquad K_X(z)=K_V((z^2-z)/2),$$

and comparing coefficients proves the exact identities

$$\kappa_{2r}(M_T)=(2r-1)!!\,\kappa_r(V_T), \qquad \kappa_{2r+1}(M_T)=0,$$ $$\kappa_4(M_T)=3\,\operatorname{Var}(V_T),$$ $$\kappa_4(X_T)=3\kappa_2(V_T)+\tfrac32\kappa_3(V_T)+\tfrac1{16}\kappa_4(V_T).$$

The last two formulas are not interchangeable. The expression $3\operatorname{Var}(V_T)$ is exact for the martingale return $M_T$, but the drift $-V_T/2$ of the log return is random too, and its third and fourth cumulants enter.

The exact variance of integrated variance

Write

$$B_\kappa(t)=\frac{1-e^{-\kappa t}}{\kappa}, \qquad \bar\theta=\mathbb E_\pi\theta(Y_0),$$ $$C_m(u)=\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(\theta(Y_0),\theta(Y_u)), \qquad K_m=\int_0^\infty C_m(u)\,du.$$

Integrating the CIR equation and then integrating over time splits $V_T$ into a regime integral and a martingale, and the two are uncorrelated. Itô isometry gives the exact decomposition

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(V_T)={}&\xi^2\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-s)^2 \big(\bar\theta+(v_0-\bar\theta)e^{-\kappa s}\big)\,ds\\ &+2\kappa^2\int_0^T C_m(u)H_T(u)\,du, \end{aligned}$$ $$H_T(u)=\int_0^{T-u}B_\kappa(x)B_\kappa(x+u)\,dx.$$

The first line is the integrated-variance variance of the averaged CIR model, $\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T)$. The second is exactly the extra variance created by the hidden regimes. This separation does not require an asymptotic expansion.

The Green–Kubo correction and a uniform bound

Put $I_2(T)=\int_0^T B_\kappa(s)^2\,ds$. Replacing the narrow correlation kernel by its mass $K_m$ gives

$$\operatorname{Var}(V_T)=\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T) +2\kappa^2 K_m I_2(T)+R_V(T,m),$$

and the remainder can be bounded without fixing the maturity:

$$\sup_{T\ge0}|R_V(T,m)|\le4\int_0^\infty u\,|C_m(u)|\,du.$$

If the generator is $mQ_0$ and $|C_m(u)|\le A e^{-m\rho u}$, the right side is at most $4A/(m^2\rho^2)$. Consequently

$$\kappa_4(M_T)=3\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T) +6\kappa^2K_m I_2(T)+R_4(T,m),$$ $$\sup_{T\ge0}|R_4(T,m)|\le12\int_0^\infty u\,|C_m(u)|\,du=O(m^{-2}).$$

This is a finite-rate statement, uniform over all maturities.

Leverage

Now allow the price and variance Brownian motions to have correlation $\rho$. Conditional on the variance path, $M_T$ is no longer an independent centered normal with variance $V_T$. Write $\kappa_{r,s}$ for the joint cumulant with $r$ copies of $M_T$ and $s$ copies of $V_T$; multilinearity gives

$$\kappa_4(X_T)=\kappa_{4,0}-2\kappa_{3,1} +\tfrac32\kappa_{2,2}-\tfrac12\kappa_{1,3}+\tfrac1{16}\kappa_{0,4}.$$

Further results in the full note

Option prices. For a symmetric two-state chain with $\delta=(\theta_+-\theta_-)/2$ and any fixed correlation $\rho$, the Heston Riccati coefficient $B_s$ does not depend on the regime. At fixed maturity, uniformly for $s$ in any compact subset of the MGF strip,

$$\log\Phi_m(s)=\log\Phi_{\rm av}(s)+\frac{h_T(s)}{m}+O(m^{-2}),\qquad h_T(s)=\frac{\kappa^2\delta^2}{2}\int_0^T B_s(t)^2\,dt.$$

Carr–Madan inversion turns this into a call correction $C_m(k)=C_{\rm av}(k)+c_1(k)/m+O(m^{-2})$. On compact strike sets where Black–Scholes Vega $\mathcal V_{\rm av}$ has a positive lower bound, the implied volatility is corrected by $b_1(k)/m$ with $b_1(k)=c_1(k)/\mathcal V_{\rm av}(k)$.

Positivity. Let the drift $c_i=\kappa_i\theta_i$, the mean reversion $\kappa_i$ and the vol-of-vol $\xi_i$ all switch. Zero is inaccessible from every positive initial value and every initial regime if

$$2c_i\ge\xi_i^2\qquad\text{for every }i,$$

and for an irreducible finite chain this condition is also necessary. The Feller test cannot be applied only to the averaged model: a two-state example passes on average with $2\bar c/\overline{\xi^2}=1.6$, yet from $v_0=0.04$ in the deficient regime the exact probability of hitting zero before the next switch is $0.2341927683$.

Simultaneous switching. When $\kappa$ switches too, the averaged drift is $\bar c-\bar\kappa v$, whose effective mean level is $\bar c/\bar\kappa$, generally not $\bar\theta$. The first-order correction to the moments of $V_T$ through order four, and so to its cumulants, then holds at fixed maturity with an $O_T(m^{-2})$ remainder, and the dependence on $T$ is necessary. In a two-state example the long-run mean is

$$\mu_m=\frac{25m+13}{50(7m+4)} =\frac1{14}-\frac9{2450m}+\frac{18}{8575m^2}+O(m^{-3}),$$

and $\mathbb E[V_T]=\mu_mT+O_m(1)$ as $T\to\infty$, so the residual left after the averaged and first-order slopes grows linearly in maturity for every fixed $m$. Retaining the exact slope $\mu_m$ and one slow exponential mode restores an $O(m^{-2})$ approximation to $\mathbb E[V_T]$ uniform over $T\ge0$.

Invariant moments. With $a_i=\xi_i^2$, the row vectors $r_i^{(p)}=\mathbb E_{\Pi_m}[v^p\mathbf1_{\{Y=i\}}]$ of the invariant law satisfy an exact triangular recursion:

$$r^{(p)}M_p=r^{(p-1)}D_{d_p},\qquad d_p=pc+{p(p-1)\over2}a,\qquad M_p=pD_\kappa-mQ_0,\qquad r^{(0)}=\pi.$$

All positive integer invariant moments are finite. This needs mean reversion, not the statewise Feller inequality.

Long maturities. Started in the joint invariant law, the variance of $V_T$ has an exact slope and intercept, each from finite linear solves, with an exponentially decaying remainder:

$$\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_m}(V_T)=\sigma_m^2T+\chi_m+\rho_m(T).$$

For every fixed integer $k\ge2$ and every fixed switching rate $m>0$ the limit $\lim_{T\to\infty}\kappa_k(V_T)/T$ exists and is given by a triangular recursion of Poisson equations.

The full note also covers explicit Green–Kubo forms of the slow mode, truncated slopes on growing maturity windows, uniform Gamma and cumulant-disk bounds for the invariant law, inverse-speed expansions of the invariant moments and long-run rates, arbitrary-start boundary constants, the transfer to independent and leveraged return cumulants, and standardized cumulants.

Scope

The chain starts in stationarity; a known or filtered starting regime adds an initial-memory term. The exact decomposition, the maturity-uniform bound and the call correction assume fixed $\kappa$ and switch only the CIR mean level. The maturity-uniform bound is proved for the independent-price benchmark; no maturity-uniform leverage bound is claimed.

The conditional integrated-variance viewpoint goes back to Hull and White (1987), and the finite-dimensional moment construction follows the polynomial-process framework of Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012).
