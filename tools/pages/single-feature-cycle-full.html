One feature can reveal direction at cubic order

A smooth loading schedule is different data from a stationary occupation total.

Let $Q$ be an irreducible finite row generator with stationary law $\pi$, and $Q^\leftarrow=D_\pi^{-1}Q^TD_\pi$ its stationary time reversal. Fix a real centered feature $c$, $\pi c=0$, and a complex $b\in C^3([0,T])$ with $b(0)=0$.

$$f'=(mQ+b(t)D_c)f,\quad f(0)=\mathbf1,\qquad A_{m,Q}(T)=\pi f(T),\qquad S=Q^\#.$$

The ODE uses backward duration, not forward calendar time: $A_{m,Q}(T)=\mathbb E_{\pi,mQ}\exp\{\int_0^T b(T-t)c(Y_t)dt\}$. Reversing that loading convention reverses the directional comparison; the sign below is for the displayed ODE.

All products below are bilinear in the real weighted space: $\langle x,y\rangle_\pi=x^TD_\pi y$. Define

$$U=\langle c,S^2D_cSc\rangle_\pi,\quad V=\langle c,SD_cS^2c\rangle_\pi,\quad \Delta=U-V.$$
$$\log A_{m,Q}(T)-\log A_{m,Q^\leftarrow}(T) ={\Delta\,b(T)^3\over3m^3}+O(m^{-4}).$$

The logarithms are the continuous branches starting at zero. The bound below makes the remainder quantitative, uniformly on families with bounded $C^3$ norms. If $\Delta b(T)^3\ne0$, the cubic order is sharp; neither irreversibility alone nor one selected feature guarantees a signal.

The exact three-cycle visibility criterion

For clockwise rate $p\ge0$, counterclockwise rate $q\ge0$, $p+q>0$, and uniform $\pi$, put

$$Q_{p,q}=\begin{pmatrix}-p-q&p&q\\q&-p-q&p\\p&q&-p-q\end{pmatrix}.$$

Direct inversion of $S=Q_{p,q}^\#$ gives the factorization

$$\Delta=-{(p-q)(c_1-c_2)(c_2-c_3)(c_3-c_1) \over27(p^2+pq+q^2)^2}.$$

Thus a nonreversible three-cycle has a nonzero cubic signal exactly when the centered scalar feature separates all three states. Reversal changes the orientation factor's sign, while relabeling the feature values changes the Vandermonde sign; this criterion concerns the cubic coefficient and still requires $b(T)\ne0$.

The sharp normalized signal

Normalize the feature by $\frac13\sum_i c_i^2=1$ and fix the total jump rate $s=p+q$. Then the factorization has the sharp bound

$$|\Delta|\le {\sqrt{3/2}\over9s^3}.$$

For centered levels, the cubic discriminant identity is

$$\{(c_1-c_2)(c_2-c_3)(c_3-c_1)\}^2 ={1\over2}\Big(\sum_i c_i^2\Big)^3-27(c_1c_2c_3)^2.$$

It makes the feature factor largest exactly when the three values are an evenly spaced permutation of $\sqrt{3/2}(-1,0,1)$. For the rate factor,

$$\begin{aligned} (p^2+pq+q^2)^2-(p-q)(p+q)^3&=q^2(3p^2+4pq+2q^2),\\ (p^2+pq+q^2)^2+(p-q)(p+q)^3&=p^2(2p^2+4pq+3q^2). \end{aligned}$$

Therefore equality holds exactly for a one-way cycle, $pq=0$, and those evenly spaced levels. Every genuinely bidirectional cycle has a strictly smaller normalized cubic signal.

Recovering the directional imbalance

Suppose the distinct feature levels and $s=p+q$ are known, and put $\theta=|p-q|/s$. Relative to the same feature's one-way coefficient, the observed cubic magnitude is

$$\rho={27s^3|\Delta|\over|(c_1-c_2)(c_2-c_3)(c_3-c_1)|} ={16\theta\over(3+\theta^2)^2}.$$

This response increases strictly from zero to one because

$$\rho'(\theta)={48(1-\theta^2)\over(3+\theta^2)^3}.$$

Hence $\theta$ is the unique root in $[0,1]$ of $\rho\theta^4+6\rho\theta^2-16\theta+9\rho=0$. The sign of $p-q$ is $-\operatorname{sign}\{\Delta(c_1-c_2)(c_2-c_3)(c_3-c_1)\}$, so $p$ and $q$ are identified separately.

The inversion is regular near reversibility but not near a pure cycle:

$$\rho={16\over9}\theta-{32\over27}\theta^3+O(\theta^5),\qquad \rho=1-{3\over4}(1-\theta)^2-{1\over2}(1-\theta)^3+O((1-\theta)^5).$$

Thus distinguishing a nearly pure cycle from a pure one is only square-root stable in the cubic coefficient. This identifies rates from the asymptotic coefficient under the stated known-feature and known-$s$ assumptions; it is not a finite-sample estimator claim.

What finite switching speed certifies

Let $G_m=\log A_{m,Q}(T)-\log A_{m,Q^\leftarrow}(T)$, let $C_\leftrightarrow=C_Q+C_{Q^\leftarrow}$ be the explicit directional remainder constant below, and take $m$ above both displayed thresholds. Assume also that $b(T)$ is real and nonzero, and define

$$\widehat\Delta_m={3m^3G_m\over b(T)^3},\qquad E_{\Delta,m}={3C_\leftrightarrow\over m|b(T)|^3}.$$

The fourth-order log-transform bound gives the finite-speed certificate

$$|\widehat\Delta_m-\Delta|\le E_{\Delta,m}.$$

Write $V_c=(c_1-c_2)(c_2-c_3)(c_3-c_1)$ and suppose $V_c\ne0$. The normalized estimate and its error radius are

$$\widehat\rho_m={27s^3|\widehat\Delta_m|\over|V_c|},\qquad \epsilon_m={81s^3C_\leftrightarrow\over m|b(T)|^3|V_c|},$$

so $|\widehat\rho_m-\rho|\le\epsilon_m$. With $R(\theta)=16\theta/(3+\theta^2)^2$, monotonicity gives the exact enclosure

$$\theta\in\left[ R^{-1}\{(\widehat\rho_m-\epsilon_m)_+\}, R^{-1}\{\min(1,\widehat\rho_m+\epsilon_m)\} \right].$$

If $|\widehat\Delta_m|>E_{\Delta,m}$, its sign is also certified and the orientation formula above identifies the sign of $p-q$. The enclosure is deterministic transform error, not a sampling-confidence interval.

Away from the pure-cycle boundary, if both admissible imbalances are at most $\bar\theta<1$, the mean-value theorem yields

$$|\widetilde\theta-\theta|\le {(3+\bar\theta^2)^3\over48(1-\bar\theta^2)} |\widetilde\rho-\rho|.$$

There is also a global square-root bound. Since $R$ is concave on $[0,1]$ and

$$1-R(\theta)={(1-\theta)^2(\theta^2+2\theta+9)\over(3+\theta^2)^2} \ge {3\over4}(1-\theta)^2,$$

any two admissible values satisfy $|\widetilde\theta-\theta|\le2|\widetilde\rho-\rho|^{1/2}/\sqrt3$. Thus the finite-speed coefficient error is $O(m^{-1})$, giving $O(m^{-1})$ rate recovery on compact subsets of $[0,1)$ but only the uniform $O(m^{-1/2})$ guarantee at a pure cycle.

Outer coefficients

Set $D=D_c$, $\ell=\pi D$, $L=(I-\mathbf1\pi)D$ and write $f=A(\mathbf1+z)$, $\pi z=0$. Then $A'/A=b\ell z$ and

$$z'=mQz+b(c+Lz-z\ell z),\quad z(0)=0.$$

Matching three powers of $m^{-1}$ gives $P_m=z_1/m+z_2/m^2+z_3/m^3$, where

$$z_1=-bSc,\qquad z_2=-b'S^2c+b^2SDSc,$$ $$z_3=-b''S^3c+bb'(2S^2DSc+SDS^2c) +b^3\{S^2c\langle c,Sc\rangle_\pi-SDSDSc\}.$$

Integrating $b\ell P_m$ gives the three coefficients of $\log A$:

$$L_1=-\langle c,Sc\rangle_\pi\int_0^T b^2dt,$$ $$L_2=-\tfrac12\langle c,S^2c\rangle_\pi b(T)^2 +\langle c,SDS c\rangle_\pi\int_0^T b^3dt,$$ $$L_3=-\langle c,S^3c\rangle_\pi\int_0^T bb''dt +{2U+V\over3}b(T)^3 +\{\langle c,S^2c\rangle_\pi\langle c,Sc\rangle_\pi -\langle c,SDSDSc\rangle_\pi\}\int_0^T b^4dt.$$

Under reversal $S$ becomes its weighted adjoint. Every scalar in $L_1,L_2$ is invariant, as are the first and last scalars in $L_3$, whereas $U$ and $V$ interchange. Therefore $L_3(Q)-L_3(Q^\leftarrow)=\Delta b(T)^3/3$.

A finite-rate remainder, including the initial layer

Use the sup norm, and choose $M,\lambda>0$ with $\|e^{tQ}-\mathbf1\pi\|_\infty\le Me^{-\lambda t}$. Put $C_c=\|c\|_\infty$, $G=\sup|b|$, $G_1=\sup|b'|$, $Z_j=\sup\|z_j\|_\infty$, and define

$$Z=Z_1+Z_2+Z_3,\quad L_F=\tfrac83GC_c,\quad I=\|z_2(0)\|_\infty+\|z_3(0)\|_\infty,$$ $$R=\sup\|z_3'\|_\infty+G\{\|L\|_\infty Z_3+C_c Z^2\},\qquad m_0=\max\{1,6MC_cG/\lambda,3Z,2ML_F/\lambda\}.$$

Any upper bounds for these suprema may be substituted. For $m\ge m_0$, the centered convolution bootstrap gives $\|z\|_\infty\le1/3$, while $\|P_m\|_\infty\le1/3$. Exponential reconstruction of $A$ proves the mean amplitude is nonzero, rather than assuming the quotient exists globally.

Indeed, $\|L\|_\infty\le2C_c$, so the nonlinear forcing on the ball is at most $16GC_c/9$. Its convolution is at most $16MGC_c/(9\lambda m)\le8/27<1/3$, closing the continuation argument. Reconstruct $A=\exp\{\int_0^t b\ell z\}$ and use uniqueness of the linear ODE to identify $A(\mathbf1+z)=f$.

The residual $r_m=P_m'-mQP_m-b(c+LP_m-P_m\ell P_m)$ satisfies $\sup\|r_m\|_\infty\le R/m^3$. The quadratic map is Lipschitz with constant $L_F$ on this ball, so the decaying-kernel inequality gives

$$\|z(t)-P_m(t)\|_\infty \le {MI\over m^2}e^{-\lambda mt/2}+{2MR\over\lambda m^4}.$$

Here $z_1(0)=0$, but generally $z_2(0)\ne0$; discarding that initial layer would not justify the expansion. Since $|b(t)|\le G_1t$, its contribution to $\int b\ell(z-P_m)$ is nevertheless fourth order:

$$\left|\log A_{m,Q}-\sum_{j=1}^3{L_j\over m^j}\right| \le {C_Q\over m^4},\qquad C_Q=C_c\left\{{4MIG_1\over\lambda^2}+{2MRTG\over\lambda}\right\}.$$

Apply the same construction to $Q^\leftarrow$. The theorem's error is at most $(C_Q+C_{Q^\leftarrow})/m^4$ when $m$ exceeds both thresholds. This supplies a fixed-horizon bound, not only a formal coefficient comparison.

A sharp three-state example

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\0&-1&1\\1&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \pi=(1,1,1)/3,\quad c=(-1,0,1)^T,\quad b(t)=t,\quad T=1.$$

Exact rational arithmetic gives $U=-1/27$, $V=1/27$, and $\Delta=-2/27$. Thus $m^3(\log A_Q-\log A_{Q^\leftarrow})\to-2/81$. The certificate independently solves the original linear amplitudes and checks the cubic term and fourth-order residual.

For this ramp, the displayed bounds give a directional error at most $18.77/m^4$ for every $m\ge7$. This is an analytic upper bound evaluated by the certificate, not an interval-certified floating-point computation.

In both directions one may take $M=\sqrt3$, $\lambda=3/2$, $(Z_1,Z_2,Z_3)=(2/3,5/9,11/27)$, $I=1/3$ and $R=2638/729$. These give the exact threshold $4\sqrt3$ and summed remainder constant $23696\sqrt3/2187$.

CIR interpretation and limits

When only $a_i=\kappa\theta_i$ switches, center $a$ to obtain $c$ and take $b(t)=B_s(t)$, the common Heston Riccati coefficient. The factors $\exp\{v_0B_s(T)+(\pi a)\int B_s\}$ cancel between directions, so the same theorem gives their log-transform difference.

The example can use positive $a=(1,2,3)$ and common $\kappa=2$, $\xi=0.3$, $\rho=-0.6$. Centering still gives $c=(-1,0,1)$, and each regime satisfies the strict Feller inequality $2a_i>\xi^2$.

This applies uniformly on each fixed compact transform set where $B_s$ and its first three time derivatives are bounded on $[0,T]$. It does not yet justify infinite-frequency inversion, a third-order call-price expansion, or a statistical identification rate. Nonstationary starts and switching multiple operator coefficients are different problems and can reveal direction earlier.

Constant loading is exactly blind by path reversal, as proved in the full cycle note; it does not satisfy the present $b(0)=0$ hypothesis unless it vanishes. A time-symmetric loading with $b(0)=0$ also has $b(T)=0$, consistently killing the displayed directional term.

The adjoint framework is classical (Nagasawa 1964), as are matched fast-chain expansions (Khasminskii, Yin and Zhang 1996). The coefficient and finite residual estimate above are derived directly; no priority claim for those frameworks is made.
