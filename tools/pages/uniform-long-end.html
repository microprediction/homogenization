← three numbers in a yield curve certificate

One curve from short to long maturities

Exact Perron growth removes secular error; a moving invariant law has a sharp second-order cost.

A fixed-maturity expansion need not remain accurate at long maturities. In a general chain an omitted term of size $T/m^2$ is order one when $T$ is comparable to $m^2$; in the symmetric two-state case below, symmetry cancels that coefficient and the first secular omission is $T/m^3$. Matched formulas are uniformly accurate for every $T\geq0$, and a moving-projection formula extends the result to any finite irreducible chain. If the fast generator itself varies, the same formula remains uniform but a moving invariant law generically lowers the stationary-observation order from three to two. Periodic forcing, despite having infinite total variation on the half-line, is also tractable after an exact Floquet renormalization. For unequal two-state switching rates, an additional second-order Floquet drift and an explicit fast initial slip appear.

The two-state reduction

Let the regimes switch in either direction at rate $m$, and write their time-dependent pricing forcings as $g_1=\bar g+\delta$ and $g_2=\bar g-\delta$. If $a'=(Q+\operatorname{diag}g)a$ with $a(0)=\mathbf1$, put

$$s=\tfrac12(a_1+a_2),\qquad d=\tfrac12(a_1-a_2),\qquad r=d/s.$$

Positivity of the pricing system gives $|r|<1$, and the two linear equations reduce exactly to

$$\frac{d}{dt}\log s=\bar g+\delta r,\qquad r'=\delta-2mr-\delta r^2,\qquad r(0)=0.$$

Suppose $\bar g(t)\to\bar g_\infty$ and $\delta(t)\to\delta_\infty$. The exact principal eigenvalue of the limiting matrix is

$$\Lambda(m)=\bar g_\infty+\sqrt{m^2+\delta_\infty^2}-m.$$

The matched formula

For a stationary initial regime, the uniformly matched log amplitude is

$$L_c(T)=\Lambda(m)T+\int_0^T(\bar g(t)-\bar g_\infty)dt +\frac1{2m}\int_0^T(\delta(t)^2-\delta_\infty^2)dt.$$

The last integral is precisely the transient Green–Kubo correction. For the unscaled generator $Q_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, its quadratic form satisfies $\mathcal K_0((\delta,-\delta),(\delta,-\delta))=\delta^2/2$. At fixed maturity, expanding $\Lambda$ recovers $\int\bar g+(2m)^{-1}\int\delta^2$. At long maturity, keeping $\Lambda$ exact prevents the omitted $T/m^3,T/m^5,\ldots$ eigenvalue terms from accumulating.

A uniform theorem

Assume $\delta$ is continuously differentiable, $G=\sup_t|\delta(t)|<m$,

$$V_\delta=\int_0^\infty|\delta'(t)|dt<\infty,qquad L_\delta=\int_0^\infty|\delta(t)-\delta_\infty|dt<\infty.$$

Then, simultaneously for every maturity $T\geq0$,

$$|\log s(T)-L_c(T)|\leq \frac{G(G+V_\delta)}{4m(m-G)}+\frac{G^3L_\delta}{2m^3}.$$

The right-hand side is $O(m^{-2})$ and does not grow with $T$. The average forcing $\bar g$ contributes no error because its transient is retained exactly.

Proof

Set

$$R(x)=\frac{x}{m+\sqrt{m^2+x^2}},\qquad h(x)=xR(x)=\sqrt{m^2+x^2}-m.$$

The value $R(\delta)$ is the stable algebraic root of the Riccati equation. For $z=r-R(\delta)$,

$$z'=-\{2m+\delta(r+R)\}z-\frac{d}{dt}R(\delta).$$

Because $|r|,|R|<1$, the damping is at least $2(m-G)$. Also $|R(x)|\leq |x|/(2m)$ and $|R'(x)|\leq1/(2m)$. Variation of constants therefore gives

$$\int_0^T|z(t)|dt\leq\frac{G+V_\delta}{4m(m-G)}.$$

The exact correction is $\int\delta r$. Its difference from the composite is the sum of $\int\delta z$ and

$$\int_0^T\left[q_m(\delta(t))-q_m(\delta_\infty)\right]dt,qquad q_m(x)=h(x)-\frac{x^2}{2m}.$$

Finally $|q_m'(x)|\leq |x|^3/(2m^3)$ for $|x|\leq G$. The two displayed bounds prove the result.

Known starting regimes and the initial layer

If regime 1 has initial probability $p$, put $\eta=2p-1$. The exact log amplitude is $\log s+\log(1+\eta r)$. When $D=\sup|\delta'|<\infty$, define

$$r_b(T)=\frac{\delta(T)-\delta(0)e^{-2mT}}{2m}.$$

The second term is the fast initial layer: it makes $r_b(0)=0$. Adding $\log(1+\eta r_b(T))$ to $L_c$ gives another maturity-uniform approximation. Indeed,

$$|r(T)-r_b(T)|\leq\frac{D}{4m^2}+\frac{G^3}{8m^3}.$$

Thus its additional log error is at most the right-hand side divided by $1-G/m$. In the Vasicek bond problem $g_i(0)=0$, hence $\delta(0)=0$ and the explicit initial-layer term vanishes; the state correction still approaches the correct eigenvector weight at the far end.

The constant spectral projection

For constant $\bar g$ and $\delta$, let $\omega=\sqrt{m^2+\delta^2}$. The stationary-start solution is

$$s(T)=e^{(\bar g-m)T}\left\{\cosh(\omega T)+\frac m\omega\sinh(\omega T)\right\}.$$

Its slow-mode coefficient is not one:

$$s(T)\sim \frac{m+\omega}{2\omega}e^{\Lambda T}.$$

The corresponding constant $\log((m+\omega)/(2\omega))$ is $O(m^{-2})$. This is why the composite has a uniform $O(m^{-2})$ remainder rather than tending exactly to zero at the far end. For a known starting mixture, the coefficient is multiplied by $1+\eta R(\delta)$.

Constant forcing for any irreducible finite chain

The stationary-start result extends cleanly without diagonalizing the generator when the forcing has already reached its limit. Let $Q_0$ be any irreducible finite-state generator, possibly defective, with stationary row vector $\pi$. For a real diagonal matrix $D=\operatorname{diag}g$, set

$$A_m=mQ_0+D,$$

and let $\Lambda_m$ be its principal eigenvalue and $P_m$ its rank-one Riesz spectral projection. Define the exact slow-mode weight $c_m=\pi P_m\mathbf1$.

Finite-chain theorem. There are constants $m_0,C,c>0$, depending only on $Q_0$ and $D$, such that for every $m\ge m_0$ and every $T\ge0$,

$$\left|e^{-\Lambda_mT}\pi e^{A_mT}\mathbf1-c_m\right| \le \frac{C}{m^2}e^{-cmT},\qquad c_m=1+O(m^{-2}).$$

Consequently, after increasing $m_0$ if necessary,

$$\sup_{T\ge0}\left|\log\!\left(\pi e^{A_mT}\mathbf1\right)-\Lambda_mT\right| \le\frac{C'}{m^2}.$$

This theorem retains the exact eigenvalue but does not even need an explicit projection correction to achieve uniform second order for a stationary start. The projection explains the limiting constant: $\log(\pi e^{A_mT}\mathbf1)-\Lambda_mT\to\log c_m=O(m^{-2})$ as $T\to\infty$.

Proof without diagonalizability

Put $\varepsilon=1/m$ and $B_\varepsilon=Q_0+\varepsilon D$. The zero eigenvalue of $Q_0$ is simple and isolated. Analytic perturbation of its Riesz projection gives a simple eigenvalue $\lambda_\varepsilon=\Lambda_m/m$ and an analytic projection $P_\varepsilon$, with $P_0=\mathbf1\pi$. The rest of the spectrum stays uniformly separated, so finite-dimensional contour calculus gives

$$\left\|e^{\tau(B_\varepsilon-\lambda_\varepsilon I)} (I-P_\varepsilon)\right\|\le M e^{-c\tau},\qquad \tau\ge0,$$

uniformly for small $\varepsilon$. This estimate permits Jordan blocks; no eigenbasis is used.

Both endpoint factors vanish at $\varepsilon=0$:

$$\pi(I-P_\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon),\qquad (I-P_\varepsilon)\mathbf1=O(\varepsilon).$$

Since $P_\varepsilon$ commutes with $B_\varepsilon$, the complementary contribution factors as

$$\pi(I-P_\varepsilon)e^{mT(B_\varepsilon-\lambda_\varepsilon I)} (I-P_\varepsilon)\mathbf1,$$

which is $O(\varepsilon^2e^{-cmT})$. Differentiating $P_\varepsilon^2=P_\varepsilon$ and multiplying by $P_0$ on both sides gives $P_0P'_0P_0=0$, hence $\pi P'_0\mathbf1=0$ and $c_m=1+O(\varepsilon^2)$. Positivity of the irreducible Feynman–Kac semigroup and $c_m\to1$ then convert the relative estimate into the logarithmic bound.

Time-dependent forcing for any finite chain

The moving-projection problem also has a clean answer. Let $D(t)$ be a bounded $C^1$ diagonal matrix with $D(0)=0$, $\|D'\|_\infty<\infty$, and $\int_0^\infty\|D'(t)\|dt<\infty$. The last condition implies a limit $D_\infty$. Set $A_m(t)=mQ_0+D(t)$ and let $\lambda_m(t)$ be its instantaneous Perron eigenvalue. For all sufficiently large $m$, choose positive right and left eigenvectors $r_m(t),\ell_m(t)$ by

$$A_mr_m=\lambda_mr_m,\qquad \ell_m^\top A_m=\lambda_m\ell_m^\top,\qquad \pi r_m=1,\qquad \ell_m^\top r_m=1.$$

The normalization fixes the gauge. Its scalar transport connection is

$$\gamma_m(t)=\ell_m(t)^\top r_m'(t),\qquad L_m(T)=\int_0^T\{\lambda_m(t)-\gamma_m(t)\}\,dt.$$

Moving-projection theorem. Let $u_m'=A_m(t)u_m$ and $u_m(0)=\mathbf1$. There are constants $m_0,C<infty$ such that, for every $m\ge m_0$,

$$\sup_{T\ge0}\left|\log(\pi u_m(T))-L_m(T)\right|\le \frac{C}{m^3}.$$

For any fixed regime prior $\nu$, the endpoint-corrected approximation satisfies

$$\sup_{T\ge0}\left|\log(\nu u_m(T))-L_m(T)-\log(\nu r_m(T))\right| \le \frac{C_\nu}{m^2}.$$

The connection term is essential for a nonreversible chain: integrating only the instantaneous eigenvalue omits transport of the moving slow eigenspace. The formula is gauge invariant when the matching endpoint factors are retained; $\pi r_m=1$ is simply the most convenient gauge. The factor $\log(\nu r_m(T))$ is the finite-chain version of the two-state known-start layer above.

Why the orders improve

Write $P_m=r_m\ell_m^\top$, $a=\ell_m^\top u_m$, and $z=(I-P_m)u_m$. Then

$$a'=(\lambda_m-\gamma_m)a+\ell_m'{}^\top z,\qquad z'=(A_m-P_m')z-aP_m'r_m.$$

Uniform spectral separation gives a complementary decay rate $cm$. Analytic resolvent bounds give $P_m'=O(m^{-1}\|D'\|)$, so variation of constants and a standard large-$m$ bootstrap yield

$$\sup_T\left\|{z(T)\over a(T)}\right\|=O(m^{-2}),qquad \int_0^\infty\left|\ell_m'{}^\top{z\over a}\right|dt=O(m^{-3}).$$

Finally $\ell_m-\pi=O(m^{-1})$ and $\ell_m^\top z=0$. Hence $\pi z=(\pi-\ell_m^\top)z=O(m^{-3})$, proving the stationary rate. A general $\nu$ does not have that last cancellation and retains $O(m^{-2})$. The assumption $D(0)=0$ makes $u_m(0)=r_m(0)=\mathbf1$ exactly; if the forcing is already regime-dependent at the near endpoint, an additional decaying initial-slip term is required.

Nonzero near-end forcing: the sharp loss without a slip

The same transport formula remains uniformly useful when $D(0)\ne0$, but its initial slow projection must be retained. Define

$$a_{m,0}=\ell_m(0)^\top\mathbf1,\qquad \widetilde L_m(T)=\log a_{m,0}+L_m(T).$$

Endpoint-mismatch theorem. Under the preceding boundedness and integrability assumptions, but with arbitrary fixed $D(0)$, there are constants $m_0,C,C_\nu$ such that

$$\sup_{T\ge0}\left|\log(\pi u_m(T))-\widetilde L_m(T)\right| \le \frac{C}{m^2},$$ $$\sup_{T\ge0}\left|\log(\nu u_m(T))-\widetilde L_m(T)-\log(\nu r_m(T))\right| \le \frac{C_\nu}{m}.$$

These orders are generically sharp for this formula. At $T=0$ the exact logarithm is zero, whereas the two approximations equal $\log a_{m,0}=O(m^{-2})$ and $\log\{a_{m,0}\nu r_m(0)\}=O(m^{-1})$, respectively. A decaying fast-mode slip is therefore necessary to recover the $O(m^{-3})$ stationary and $O(m^{-2})$ specified-start orders through the near endpoint.

Proof of the endpoint-mismatch orders

At time zero put $z_0=\mathbf1-a_{m,0}r_m(0)$. Analytic perturbation and the two normalizations give

$$r_m(0)-\mathbf1=O(m^{-1}),\qquad \ell_m(0)-\pi=O(m^{-1}),\qquad a_{m,0}-1=O(m^{-2}),\qquad z_0=O(m^{-1}).$$

The cancellation in $a_{m,0}$ follows by differentiating $\pi r_m=\ell_m^\top r_m=1$ at $m^{-1}=0$. Complementary spectral decay and variation of constants then give

$$\left\|\frac{z(T)}{a(T)}\right\| \le C\{m^{-1}e^{-cmT}+m^{-2}\},\qquad \left|\log\frac{a(T)}{a_{m,0}}-L_m(T)\right|\le \frac{C}{m^3}.$$

For stationary observation, $\pi z=(\pi-\ell_m^\top)z$ supplies one more factor $m^{-1}$; a general prior has no such cancellation. Taking logarithms, uniformly away from zero for sufficiently large $m$, proves the two bounds.

The frozen initial slip restores the higher orders

The missing boundary layer is explicit. With $A_{m,0}=A_m(0)$ and $\lambda_{m,0}=\lambda_m(0)$, define

$$b_{m,0}(T)=e^{(A_{m,0}-\lambda_{m,0}I)T}z_0,$$

and, for any fixed prior $\nu$, set

$$L^{\mathrm{slip}}_{m,\nu}(T)=L_m(T)+ \log\{a_{m,0}\nu r_m(T)+\nu b_{m,0}(T)\}.$$

Because $\ell_m(0)^\top z_0=0$, the frozen semigroup contains only fast modes and $b_{m,0}(T)=O(m^{-1}e^{-cmT})$. At $T=0$ the vector inside braces reconstructs the exact initial condition:

$$a_{m,0}r_m(0)+b_{m,0}(0)=\mathbf1.$$

Initial-slip theorem. Under the same assumptions as the endpoint-mismatch theorem,

$$\sup_{T\ge0}\left|\log(\pi u_m(T))-L^{\mathrm{slip}}_{m,\pi}(T)\right| \le \frac{C}{m^3},$$

and, for every fixed prior $\nu$,

$$\sup_{T\ge0}\left|\log(\nu u_m(T))-L^{\mathrm{slip}}_{m,\nu}(T)\right| \le \frac{C_\nu}{m^2}.$$

Thus a frozen fast semigroup is enough: no time-ordered exponential is needed in the boundary layer.

Proof of the slip theorem

Scale out the transported slow exponential by writing $\alpha=e^{-L_m}a$ and $y=e^{-L_m}z$. Then $\alpha(0)=a_{m,0}$, $y(0)=z_0$, and

$$\alpha'=\ell_m'{}^\top y,\qquad y'=\{A_m-\lambda_mI-P_m'+\gamma_mI\}y-\alpha P_m'r_m.$$

The frozen slip solves $b_{m,0}'=(A_{m,0}-\lambda_{m,0}I)b_{m,0}$. Since $D(T)-D(0)=O(T)$ in the $T=O(m^{-1})$ layer, variation of constants, the complementary spectral gap, and $P_m'=O(m^{-1}\|D'\|)$ give, uniformly in $T$,

$$\|y-b_{m,0}\|=O(m^{-2}),\qquad |\alpha-a_{m,0}|=O(m^{-3}).$$

For stationary observation there is one further cancellation. Both $y$ and the frozen slip lie in their respective left-eigenvector complements, and $\ell_m(T)-\ell_m(0)=O(m^{-1}\min\{T,1\})$ inside the layer. Hence

$$|\pi(y-b_{m,0})|=O(m^{-3}).$$

Finally $e^{-L_m}\nu u_m=\alpha\nu r_m+\nu y$. Subtracting the slip approximation gives $O(m^{-2})$ for a general prior and $O(m^{-3})$ for $\nu=\pi$; positivity for sufficiently large $m$ converts these relative estimates into the displayed logarithmic bounds.

When the fast generator moves

The third-order stationary cancellation above uses more than a spectral gap: it uses the fixed invariant row $\pi$. Let $Q(t)$ now be a $C^1$ family of finite-state generators with a common state space, $Q(t)\mathbf1=0$, invariant rows $\pi(t)$, and uniform exponential ergodicity on the complementary subspace. Assume $Q$ and $D$ are bounded and have finite total variation,

$$\int_0^\infty\{\|Q'(t)\|+\|D'(t)\|\}\,dt<\infty,$$

and put $A_m(t)=mQ(t)+D(t)$. Take $D(0)=0$ and fix the gauge row $q=\pi(0)$. For the Perron eigenvectors use

$$A_mr_m=\lambda_mr_m,\qquad \ell_m^\top A_m=\lambda_m\ell_m^\top,\qquad q r_m=1,\qquad \ell_m^\top r_m=1,$$

and retain the same connection and transported exponent

$$\gamma_m=\ell_m^\top r_m',\qquad L_m(T)=\int_0^T(\lambda_m-\gamma_m)\,dt.$$

Moving-generator theorem. For every fixed regime prior $\nu$, there are $m_0,C_\nu<\infty$ such that

$$\sup_{T\geq0}\left|\log(\nu u_m(T))-L_m(T)-\log\{\nu r_m(T)\}\right| \leq {C_\nu\over m^2},\qquad m\geq m_0.$$

The order is generically sharp even for the initial stationary observation $\nu=q$. Thus the fixed-$Q$ $O(m^{-3})$ statement must not be transferred to state-dependent or time-dependent switching rates.

Why one order disappears

Although $\ell_m'=\pi'(t)+O(m^{-1})$ is now order one, $Q'(t)\mathbf1=0$ still gives $r_m'=O(m^{-1})$. Hence the full projector derivative $P_m'$ is order one, but its only forcing of the fast component satisfies

$$P_m'r_m=r_m'-\gamma_mr_m=O(m^{-1}).$$

The $O(m)$ complementary gap therefore still yields $z/a=O(m^{-2})$. The slow-amplitude feedback is now

$$\int_0^\infty \ell_m'{}^\top{z\over a}\,dt=O(m^{-2}),$$

because the total variation is finite. This term was $O(m^{-3})$ for fixed $Q$, where $\ell_m'=O(m^{-1})$. In addition, $q-\ell_m(t)$ is generally order one, so observing with the initial stationary row supplies no extra cancellation. These two changes prove the bound and identify the mechanism behind its sharpness.

Periodic forcing: exact Floquet growth plus a bounded phase

Finite total variation is sufficient for the preceding transport estimates, but it is not necessary. Return to the symmetric two-state reduction and suppose that $\bar g,\delta$ are $P$-periodic, with $\delta\in C^2$. Write $\langle f\rangle=P^{-1}\int_0^P f(t)dt$. For all sufficiently large $m$, the Riccati equation has a unique attracting periodic solution $r_m^*$, and set

$$\lambda_m=\left\langle\bar g+\delta r_m^*\right\rangle,$$ $$\Phi_{m,1}(T)=\int_0^T\left\{\bar g(t)-\langle\bar g\rangle+ {\delta(t)^2-\langle\delta^2\rangle\over2m}\right\}dt.$$

The integrand defining $\Phi_{m,1}$ has zero period mean, so the phase is bounded and periodic. The number $\lambda_m$ is the exact principal Floquet exponent: it is equivalently $P^{-1}$ times the logarithm of the Perron eigenvalue of the one-period monodromy matrix.

Periodic Floquet theorem. For the stationary initial regime, $r(0)=0$, there are $m_0,C<\infty$ such that

$$\sup_{T\geq0}\left|\log s(T)-\lambda_mT-\Phi_{m,1}(T)\right| \leq {C\over m^2},\qquad m\geq m_0.$$

This is genuinely uniform over arbitrarily many periods. Replacing $\lambda_m$ by only its first-order expansion generally destroys that statement.

Proof and the secular term

The interval $(-1,1)$ is invariant for the Riccati flow. If $M=\|\delta\|_\infty$ and $m>M$, its derivative with respect to the initial value contracts over one period by at most $e^{-2(m-M)P}$. The Poincaré map therefore has one fixed point. Substitution and the same contraction give

$$r_m^*(t)={\delta(t)\over2m}-{\delta'(t)\over4m^2}+O(m^{-3})$$

uniformly in $t$. For $q=r-r_m^*$, exact subtraction gives

$$q'=-\{2m+\delta(r+r_m^*)\}q,\qquad |q(t)|\leq {C\over m}e^{-2(m-M)t}.$$

Thus the initial transient contributes only $\int_0^T\delta q\,dt=O(m^{-2})$, uniformly in $T$. On the periodic orbit, subtracting $\lambda_m$ and the derivative of $\Phi_{m,1}$ leaves a periodic, zero-mean function of size $O(m^{-2})$; its integral over any number of complete periods cancels, and the final partial period is also $O(m^{-2})$. This proves the bound.

If $\delta\in C^3$, one further substitution gives

$$\lambda_m=\langle\bar g\rangle+{\langle\delta^2\rangle\over2m} -{\langle(\delta')^2+\delta^4\rangle\over8m^3}+O(m^{-4}).$$

The absent $m^{-2}$ mean follows from $\langle\delta\delta'\rangle=0$. Unless the displayed third-order coefficient vanishes, the first-order exponent accumulates an order-one error at $T\asymp m^3$; retaining the exact Floquet exponent is therefore the periodic analogue of retaining the exact far-end Perron root.

Unequal rates: second-order drift and an explicit slip

The symmetric chain hides a cancellation. Let

$$Q_0=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha&\alpha\\\beta&-\beta\end{pmatrix},\qquad \rho=\alpha+\beta,\qquad \pi_1={\beta\over\rho},\quad \pi_2={\alpha\over\rho},$$

and write $v=\pi_1\pi_2$, $\sigma=\pi_2-\pi_1$ and

$$g_1=\bar g+\pi_2\delta,\qquad g_2=\bar g-\pi_1\delta.$$

For $M=\pi_1a_1+\pi_2a_2$ and $r=(a_1-a_2)/M$, the exact reduction is

$$({\log M})'=\bar g+v\delta r,\qquad r'=\delta+(\sigma\delta-m\rho)r-v\delta r^2,\qquad r(0)=0.$$

If $\bar g,\delta$ are smooth and $P$-periodic, the unique attracting periodic orbit satisfies

$$r_m^*(t)={\delta(t)\over m\rho} +{\sigma\delta(t)^2-\delta'(t)\over m^2\rho^2}+O(m^{-3}),$$

and its exact principal Floquet exponent therefore has the expansion

$$\lambda_m=\langle\bar g\rangle+{v\langle\delta^2\rangle\over m\rho} +{v\sigma\langle\delta^3\rangle\over m^2\rho^2}+O(m^{-3}).$$

The $m^{-2}$ secular coefficient is generically nonzero when $\alpha\ne\beta$. It vanishes for equal rates because then $\sigma=0$; periodicity also gives $\langle\delta\delta'\rangle=0$.

Define the bounded second-order phase

$$\Phi_{m,2}(T)=\int_0^T\left[\bar g-\langle\bar g\rangle +{v\over m\rho}(\delta^2-\langle\delta^2\rangle) +{v\over m^2\rho^2}\{\sigma(\delta^3-\langle\delta^3\rangle)-\delta\delta'\}\right]dt$$

and the frozen initial slip

$$B_{m,2}(T)=-{v\delta(0)^2\over m^2\rho^2}(1-e^{-m\rho T}).$$

Unequal-rate periodic theorem. There are $m_0,C<\infty$ such that

$$\sup_{T\geq0}\left|\log M(T)-\lambda_mT-\Phi_{m,2}(T)-B_{m,2}(T)\right| \leq {C\over m^3},\qquad m\geq m_0.$$

Without the slip and the second-order phase, the preceding first-order Floquet formula has only a uniform $O(m^{-2})$ error. The exponent remains exact: substituting a finite expansion for $\lambda_m$ would again produce secular error over sufficiently many periods.

For the proof, subtract the periodic orbit. The difference $q=r-r_m^*$ obeys exactly

$$q'=\{\sigma\delta-m\rho-v\delta(r+r_m^*)\}q.$$

Uniform contraction gives the leading boundary layer $q_{\rm bl}(t)=-\delta(0)(m\rho)^{-1}e^{-m\rho t}$ and, by variation of constants,

$$|q(t)-q_{\rm bl}(t)|\leq C(m^{-2}+t/m)e^{-cmt}.$$

Because $\delta(t)-\delta(0)=O(t)$, integrating $v\delta q$ differs from $B_{m,2}$ by $O(m^{-3})$ uniformly in its upper limit. On the periodic orbit, subtracting the exact exponent and the displayed phase leaves a zero-mean periodic function of size $O(m^{-3})$; its primitive is uniformly $O(m^{-3})$. These two estimates prove the theorem.

Periodic switching rates and the geometric drift

The Floquet construction also survives when the fast generator itself is periodic. Let $\alpha(t),\beta(t)>0$ be smooth and $P$-periodic, and define

$$Q(t)=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha(t)&\alpha(t)\\\beta(t)&-\beta(t)\end{pmatrix},\qquad \rho=\alpha+\beta,\qquad p={\beta\over\rho},\quad 1-p={\alpha\over\rho},$$ $$v=p(1-p),\qquad \sigma=1-2p,\qquad g_1=\bar g+(1-p)\delta,\quad g_2=\bar g-p\delta.$$

The moving stationary average $M=pa_1+(1-p)a_2$ and ratio $r=(a_1-a_2)/M$ satisfy exactly

$$({\log M})'=\bar g+h r,\qquad r'=\delta+(\sigma\delta-m\rho)r-h r^2,\qquad h=v\delta+p'.$$

The $p'$ term is geometric: it comes from differentiating the moving stationary weights. Omitting it is not an innocuous choice of coordinates; its period mean against the first fast corrector can be nonzero and then changes the Floquet exponent at order $m^{-1}$.

Put

$$R_1={\delta\over\rho},\qquad R_2={\sigma\delta R_1-R_1'\over\rho}.$$

The attracting periodic orbit and exact exponent have the expansions

$$r_m^*={R_1\over m}+{R_2\over m^2}+O(m^{-3}),$$ $$\lambda_m=\langle\bar g\rangle +{\langle hR_1\rangle\over m} +{\langle hR_2\rangle\over m^2}+O(m^{-3}).$$

Define the bounded moving-average phase

$$\Phi^{\rm mov}_{m,2}(T)=\int_0^T\left[ \bar g-\langle\bar g\rangle +{hR_1-\langle hR_1\rangle\over m} +{hR_2-\langle hR_2\rangle\over m^2} \right]dt$$

and its frozen slip

$$B^{\rm mov}_{m,2}(T)=-{h(0)\delta(0)\over m^2\rho(0)^2} (1-e^{-m\rho(0)T}).$$

The pricing observation is fixed at the initial stationary prior, not at the moving row $\pi(T)$. Put

$$P_m(T)=\pi(0)a(T)=M(T)\{1+c(T)r(T)\},\qquad c(T)=p(0)-p(T),$$ $$\Theta^{\rm obs}_{m,2}(T)={cR_1\over m} +{cR_2-\tfrac12c^2R_1^2\over m^2},\qquad C^{\rm obs}_{m,2}(T)=-{c(T)\delta(0)\over m\rho(0)}e^{-m\rho(0)T}.$$

The first two terms are the periodic observation phase. The last term is a separate fast correction caused by holding the observation row fixed while $\pi(t)$ starts to move; it is uniformly $O(m^{-2})$ because $c(0)=0$.

Moving-generator Floquet theorem. There are $m_0,C<\infty$ such that

$$\sup_{T\geq0}\left|\log P_m(T)-\lambda_mT -\Phi^{\rm mov}_{m,2}(T)-\Theta^{\rm obs}_{m,2}(T) -B^{\rm mov}_{m,2}(T)-C^{\rm obs}_{m,2}(T)\right| \leq {C\over m^3},\qquad m\geq m_0.$$

This covers a periodic generator of infinite total variation on the half-line. It does not contradict the earlier sharp $O(m^{-2})$ result for a general moving generator: periodicity permits the remaining local error to be split into its exact mean, absorbed by $\lambda_m$, and a bounded zero-mean phase.

An arbitrary fixed initial prior

The stationary initial row is special. For a fixed prior $\nu=(q,1-q)$, set

$$P_{m,\nu}(T)=\nu a(T)=M(T)\{1+c_\nu(T)r(T)\}, \qquad c_\nu(T)=q-p(T),$$ $$\Phi^{\rm mov}_{m,1}(T)=\int_0^T\left[ \bar g-\langle\bar g\rangle +{hR_1-\langle hR_1\rangle\over m}\right]dt,$$ $$\Theta^{\rm obs}_{m,1}(T)={c_\nu(T)R_1(T)\over m},\qquad C^{\rm obs}_{m,1}(T)=-{c_\nu(T)R_1(0)\over m}e^{-m\rho(0)T}.$$

Arbitrary-prior Floquet theorem. For every fixed $\nu$, there are $m_0,C<\infty$ such that

$$\sup_{T\geq0}\left|\log P_{m,\nu}(T)-\lambda_mT -\Phi^{\rm mov}_{m,1}(T)-\Theta^{\rm obs}_{m,1}(T) -C^{\rm obs}_{m,1}(T)\right|\leq {C\over m^2}.$$

The observation slip is necessary. If $q\ne p(0)$ and $R_1(0)\ne0$, omitting it leaves the near-end error $|q-p(0)|\,|R_1(0)|/m+O(m^{-2})$ already at $T=0$. When $q=p(0)$, that coefficient vanishes; the observation layer is then only $O(m^{-2})$, which is precisely the extra cancellation used by the preceding stationary-prior third-order theorem.

Proof

Uniform positivity of $\alpha,\beta$ makes the Riccati Poincaré map a contraction for large $m$. Substitution gives the displayed expansion of $r_m^*$. For $q=r-r_m^*$, exact subtraction yields

$$q'=\{\sigma\delta-m\rho-h(r+r_m^*)\}q.$$

Its leading initial layer is $q_{\rm bl}=-\delta(0)[m\rho(0)]^{-1}e^{-m\rho(0)t}$. Smoothness of all coefficients gives

$$|q(t)-q_{\rm bl}(t)|\leq C(m^{-2}+t/m)e^{-cmt}.$$

Consequently $\int_0^T hq-B^{\rm mov}_{m,2}(T)=O(m^{-3})$ uniformly in $T$. For general $c_\nu=O(1)$, the same layer estimate first gives, uniformly in $T$,

$$\log\{1+c_\nu(r_m^*+q)\} ={c_\nu R_1\over m}-{c_\nu R_1(0)\over m}e^{-m\rho(0)T}+O(m^{-2}),$$

which proves the arbitrary-prior theorem after combining it with the first-order moving-average phase. For $q=p(0)$, $c_\nu(T)=O(T)$ at the near end; expanding one order further gives the observation phase and $C^{\rm obs}_{m,2}$ with a uniform $O(m^{-3})$ remainder. On the periodic orbit, the exact exponent removes the mean of $\bar g+hr_m^*$, while the two displayed correctors leave a zero-mean periodic remainder of size $O(m^{-3})$. Its primitive is bounded uniformly over arbitrarily many periods.

Any finite periodic chain: the Floquet projection

The two-state Riccati equation supplies explicit coefficients, but it is not needed for a maturity-uniform theorem. Let $Q(t)$ and the real diagonal forcing $D(t)$ be $C^2$ and $P$-periodic, with $Q(t)$ a uniformly irreducible finite-state generator having a uniform fast spectral gap. Write $A_m(t)=mQ(t)+D(t)$ and let $U_m(t,s)$ be its evolution family. The positive monodromy $F_m=U_m(P,0)$ has a Perron multiplier $\mu_m$, right and left vectors $v_m,w_m$, and exact Floquet exponent

$$\lambda_m=P^{-1}\log\mu_m,\qquad w_m^\top v_m=1,\qquad \pi(0)v_m=1.$$

Define the periodic principal profile, its initial coefficient, and the frozen fast layer by

$$r_m(t)=e^{-\lambda_mt}U_m(t,0)v_m,\qquad c_m=w_m^\top\mathbf1,\qquad z_{m,0}=\mathbf1-c_mv_m,$$ $$b_{m,0}(t)=e^{\{A_m(0)-\lambda_mI\}t}z_{m,0}.$$

Thus $r_m(t+P)=r_m(t)$. The profile and exponent are exact Floquet quantities; only the short initial transient is approximated.

Finite-chain periodic theorem. For every fixed probability row $\nu$,

$$\sup_{T\geq0}\left| \log\{\nu U_m(T,0)\mathbf1\}-\lambda_mT -\log\{c_m\nu r_m(T)+\nu b_{m,0}(T)\} \right|\leq {C_\nu\over m^2}.$$

For the stationary initial row $\nu=\pi(0)$, the slip may be omitted while retaining $O(m^{-2})$. For a general fixed prior, omitting it is generically only $O(m^{-1})$. If $Q(t)\equiv Q_0$ is fixed, the stationary-prior formula with the frozen slip improves to $O(m^{-3})$ even when $Q_0$ is defective.

Why the orders differ

Perron decomposition of the monodromy gives $\mathbf1=c_mv_m+z_{m,0}$ with $w_m^\top z_{m,0}=0$. Singular perturbation gives $v_m=\mathbf1+O(m^{-1})$, $w_m=\pi(0)+O(m^{-1})$, and $z_{m,0}=O(m^{-1})$. The normalization produces the extra stationary cancellation $\pi(0)z_{m,0}=O(m^{-2})$; an arbitrary observation row does not have it. The complementary Floquet evolution decays at rate $cm$. On that $t=O(m^{-1})$ layer,

$$m\{Q(t)-Q(0)\}=O(mt),$$

so Duhamel's formula replaces the exact fast evolution by $b_{m,0}$ with a uniform $O(m^{-2})$ error. When $Q$ is fixed, only $D(t)-D(0)=O(t)$ remains and the same calculation gains one further power of $m^{-1}$. This is the periodic finite-chain counterpart of the fixed-horizon matched expansions of Khasminskii, Yin, and Zhang, with the secular growth resummed by Floquet's monodromy exponent.

What remains beyond this theorem

The fixed-generator theorem assumes a diagonal forcing of bounded total variation and $D(0)=0$ only when the boundary-layer term is omitted. The frozen-slip theorem restores the higher orders for arbitrary fixed near-end forcing. The moving-generator extension requires a uniformly ergodic smooth family and integrable total variation. The Floquet theorems above separately cover smooth periodic forcing and smooth periodic two-state generators with a uniform rate gap; the exact-profile theorem extends maturity-uniform control to every finite periodic chain. Explicit multi-state coefficient formulae, rough nonperiodic coefficients with infinite variation, generators that lose their gap, and infinite-state factors still require different estimates.

The fixed-horizon expansion and its initial layers belong to the singular-perturbation framework summarized by Yin and Zhang and originate with Tikhonov's 1952 theorem; the perturbed-semigroup viewpoint goes back to Kurtz. Analytic Riesz projections and the non-diagonalizable perturbation argument follows Kato's perturbation theory; the connection term is the finite-dimensional spectral transport introduced in Kato's original adiabatic theorem. The exact-eigenvalue resummation here addresses the additional growing-maturity scale. State-dependent fast generators and their boundary layers are treated, at fixed horizons, by Nguyen and Yin (2013); the theorem here shows what changes when the maturity horizon is also required to be uniform.
