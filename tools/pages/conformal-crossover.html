When fast mixing justifies pooled residuals

An exact finite-chain horizon-to-switching crossover for conditional coverage, alongside finite-calibration, irregular-grid, burn-in and transient-panel bounds.

Research note, 23 September 2026; extended 28 September 2026. A finite-state benchmark for homogenization and residual pooling; it does not assert distribution-free conditional coverage.

What is being pooled?

Let $Y_t$ be an irreducible finite-state continuous-time chain with generator $mQ_0$ and invariant law $\pi$; the initial law need not equal $\pi$. A score $R_t$ (for example an absolute prediction residual) has continuous conditional CDF $F_i(r)$ when $Y_t=i$. Emissions are independent given the states; the prediction rule and the emission laws are fixed. To forecast a terminal score at horizon $\Delta$ we pool calibration scores and choose the population threshold $q$ satisfying

$$\bar F(q)=1-\alpha,\qquad \bar F=\sum_i\pi_iF_i.$$

The distinction is between coverage averaged over the stationary current state and coverage conditional on a specified current state. It is also essential to separate the forecast horizon $\Delta$ from the calibration spacing $h$: fast mixing along the former does not imply a large effective calibration sample along the latter.

The finite-chain identity and a nonreversible bound

Write $F(r)=(F_1(r),\ldots,F_d(r))^\top$ and $P_t=e^{mQ_0t}$. For any initial regime prior $\nu$, the conditional coverage error at the pooled population threshold is exactly

$$\Pr_\nu(R_\Delta\le q)-(1-\alpha) =(\nu P_\Delta-\pi)F(q).$$

At $\Delta=c/m$ this is $(\nu e^{cQ_0}-\pi)F(q)$, independent of $m$ and generally nonzero; at fixed positive $\Delta$ it vanishes as $m\to\infty$. This is an exact semigroup identity, not an asymptotic or a distribution-free conditional-coverage claim.

The identity also has a general nonreversible rate bound. Let $Q_0^*$ be the $L^2(\pi)$ adjoint, let $Q_s=(Q_0+Q_0^*)/2$, and let $\gamma_s$ be the smallest positive eigenvalue of $-Q_s$ on centered functions. Then

$$\left|\Pr_\nu(R_\Delta\le q)-(1-\alpha)\right| \le e^{-m\gamma_s\Delta} \left\|\frac{\nu}{\pi}-1\right\|_{L^2(\pi)} \left\|F(q)-(1-\alpha){\bf1}\right\|_{L^2(\pi)}.$$

Indeed, for centered $g$, differentiation along the original nonreversible semigroup gives

$$\frac{d}{dt}\|P_tg\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 =2m\langle P_tg,Q_sP_tg\rangle_\pi \le-2m\gamma_s\|P_tg\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2.$$

Thus $\|P_tg\|_{2,\pi}\le e^{-m\gamma_s t}\|g\|_{2,\pi}$; Cauchy–Schwarz yields the displayed result. For a specified state $i$, $\|\delta_i/\pi-1\|_{2,\pi}=\sqrt{1/\pi_i-1}$ and the final score-CDF norm is at most $1/2$. In the reversible case $Q_s=Q_0$, so this reduces to the usual spectral-gap bound. This additive-reversibilization route follows the nonreversible $L^2$ framework of Lawler and Sokal (1988).

The exponent is sharp among bounds with prefactor one. More precisely,

$$\gamma_s=\sup\left\{\gamma: \|e^{tQ_0}g\|_{2,\pi}\le e^{-\gamma t}\|g\|_{2,\pi} \quad\hbox{for every }t\ge0\hbox{ and }\pi g=0\right\}.$$

Sufficiency is the preceding energy estimate. For necessity, choose a unit eigenfunction $g_s$ of $-Q_s$ with eigenvalue $\gamma_s$. The exact right derivative at zero is

$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{tQ_0}g_s\|_{2,\pi}^2\right|_{t=0} =-2\gamma_s.$$

Any larger exponential rate with prefactor one would force a strictly smaller derivative, a contradiction. This is a worst-direction, all-times statement; a particular score vector may contract faster. Equivalently, $-\gamma_s$ is the Hilbert-space logarithmic norm on the centered subspace, in the sense introduced by Dahlquist (1958).

The symmetric two-state formula

For $Q_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $m=1/\varepsilon$, and $\pi=(1/2,1/2)$, the general identity reduces to

$$\Pr(R_{\Delta}\le q\mid Y_0=i) =1-\alpha+e^{-2\Delta/\varepsilon} \bigl(F_i(q)-(1-\alpha)\bigr),\qquad i=1,2.$$

Indeed, $e^{Q\Delta}={\bf1}\pi+ e^{-2\Delta/\varepsilon}(I-{\bf1}\pi)$, so the distribution of the terminal state is its stationary law plus a decaying memory of $Y_0$. The expression is exact for the stated model. If $Y_0$ is stationary, averaging over $i$ gives exactly $1-\alpha$ at the population threshold, for every $\Delta$. This marginal identity says nothing about conditional coverage.

At $\Delta=c\varepsilon$, the conditional error is $e^{-2c}(F_i(q)-(1-\alpha))$. It remains nonzero as $\varepsilon\to0$ whenever the two residual distributions disagree at $q$. Only for $\Delta/\varepsilon\to\infty$ does pooling become conditionally accurate in this benchmark. For $\Delta/\varepsilon\to0$, conditioning retains the full regime-specific difference.

Coverage and width

The shortest upper score threshold with population conditional coverage $1-\alpha$ under prior $\nu$ is $q_{\nu,\Delta}=F_{\nu,\Delta}^{-1}(1-\alpha)$, where $F_{\nu,\Delta}(r)=\nu P_\Delta F(r)$. When the conditional laws have continuously differentiable densities near $q$ and $\bar f(q)>0$, expansion as the chain mixes gives

$$q_{\nu,\Delta}=q- \frac{(\nu P_\Delta-\pi)F(q)}{\bar f(q)} +O(\|\nu P_\Delta-\pi\|^2).$$

The preceding two-state expression follows by substituting its single exponential mode.

A pooled threshold $q$ can be too narrow for one state and unnecessarily wide for the other. If state information is used only to demand coverage for both states with a single threshold, the smallest such threshold is $\max_i q_{i,\Delta}$. Thus marginal validity, conditional validity, and sharpness are distinct objectives.

For example, take exponential scores with conditional means 1 and 3 and $\alpha=0.1$. The pooled threshold is about $4.93784$. At $\Delta=0$ its conditional coverages are approximately $0.99283$ and $0.80717$, despite exactly $0.9$ stationary marginal coverage. At $\Delta=\varepsilon$ they are $0.91256$ and $0.88744$. The certificate simulates the Markov transitions and fresh scores to check these figures.

The certificate also uses a nonreversible three-state chain with a nonuniform invariant law. It obtains $\gamma_s=3.67885921$; over forty scaled horizons the largest score-vector ratio of the exact $L^2(\pi)$ norm to its additive-reversibilization envelope is $0.97508013$. For the gap eigenfunction at $t=10^{-3}$, the ratio is $0.999999999315$, while replacing $\gamma_s$ by $1.01\gamma_s$ gives $1.000036788583>1$ and violates the proposed faster bound. This checks the original nonnormal semigroup rather than diagonalizing it as though it were reversible.

Finite dependent calibration

The population threshold is not what split conformal actually computes. Suppose $n$ calibration scores are sampled from the stationary process at spacing $h>0$, and let $\widehat F_n(r)=n^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^n {\bf1}\{R_{\ell h}\le r\}$. Put $f_r=F(r)$, $p_r=\pi f_r$, $\widetilde f_r=f_r-p_r{\bf1}$, and $P_h=e^{mQ_0h}$. Conditional independence of distinct emissions gives the exact finite-chain Green–Kubo sum

$$\operatorname{Var}\widehat F_n(r) =\frac{p_r(1-p_r)}{n} +\frac{2}{n^2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}(n-\ell) \langle\widetilde f_r,P_h^\ell\widetilde f_r\rangle_\pi.$$

The same nonreversible contraction gives, with $a=e^{-m\gamma_sh}$, the exact finite-panel envelope

$$\operatorname{Var}\widehat F_n(r) \le \frac{p_r(1-p_r)}{n}V_n(a),$$ $$V_n(a)=1+\frac2n\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}(n-\ell)a^\ell =\frac{1+a}{1-a} -\frac{2a(1-a^n)}{n(1-a)^2}.$$

This uses $\|\widetilde f_r\|_{2,\pi}^2\le p_r(1-p_r)$ and retains the finite geometric sum. It remains valid when the covariances oscillate or change sign, and is strictly sharper than the asymptotic factor $(1+a)/(1-a)$ for finite $n$. For the symmetric two-state chain, $\gamma_s=2$ and $\langle\widetilde f_r,P_h^\ell\widetilde f_r\rangle_\pi =d_r^2e^{-2\ell h/\varepsilon}$, recovering the original equality. In fact the variance envelope is sharp: take the symmetric two-state chain and conditional CDF values $(0,1)$ at the threshold. Continuous score laws with disjoint supports realize this case, and every covariance inequality above is then an equality. When $m\gamma_sh\ll1$, adjacent scores carry nearly the same regime information, so $n$ can badly overstate the effective sample size. The first term includes the within-regime emission noise, while the second is exactly the dependence contribution from the hidden chain. In the nonreversible certificate, the exact variance is $0.00091950$ and the finite-panel bound is $0.00123603$. In the reversible three-state specialization, the exact variance is $0.00110385$ versus $0.00110741$ in direct simulation. A separate $n=17$ two-state construction attains its bound exactly at variance $0.04032855$.

Irregular observation times

A regular spacing is not needed. Let $0=t_1<t_2<\cdots<t_n$ be any deterministic observation grid and sample the stationary score process at these times. The exact covariance identity and the additive-reversibilization estimate give

$$\operatorname{Var}\widehat F_{\boldsymbol t}(r) =\frac{p_r(1-p_r)}n+\frac2{n^2}\sum_{i<j} \langle\widetilde f_r,P_{t_j-t_i}\widetilde f_r\rangle_\pi,$$ $$\operatorname{Var}\widehat F_{\boldsymbol t}(r) \le\frac{p_r(1-p_r)}nV_{\boldsymbol t},\qquad V_{\boldsymbol t}=1+\frac2n\sum_{i<j} e^{-m\gamma_s(t_j-t_i)}.$$

The first equality uses only stationarity and conditional independence of distinct emissions. The inequality follows pair by pair from the same nonreversible $L^2(\pi)$ contraction. For a symmetric two-state chain and conditional CDF values $(0,1)$, every pairwise inequality is an equality on every grid, so $V_{\boldsymbol t}$ is sharp.

Consequently, replacing an uneven grid by its average adjacent spacing is not a valid upper bound. The certificate takes the scaled times $(0,0.01,0.02,0.03,6)$ for the sharp two-state example. Its exact empirical-CDF variance is $0.16822116$, whereas the regular-grid formula at the same average spacing $1.5$ gives only $0.07133157$, understating the variance by a factor $2.3583$. The complete pairwise-kernel formula returns $0.16822116$ exactly. One may call $n/V_{\boldsymbol t}$ a variance-equivalent sample size for this statistic, but it is not a substitution for $n$ in conformal rank or coverage laws.

Here is a conservative finite-sample statement that requires no independent-calibration fiction. Assume $\bar F$ is continuous and strictly increasing. Select an integer $M\ge2$ and failure probability $\delta\in(0,1)$, and let $V=V_n(a)$ on a regular grid, or $V=V_{\boldsymbol t}$ on an irregular grid. A grid of $M-1$ population quantiles, Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound imply, with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the stationary calibration sequence,

$$\sup_r|\widehat F_n(r)-\bar F(r)| \le \frac1M+\sqrt{\frac{(M-1)V}{4n\delta}} =:\eta_{n,M,\delta}.$$

Let $\widehat q$ be the ordinary split-conformal order statistic of rank $k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$, assuming $k\le n$ and no score ties. Then on this same event, for each possible observed state $Y_0=i$,

$$\left|\Pr(R_\Delta\le\widehat q \mid\text{calibration history},Y_0=i) -(1-\alpha)\right| \le \eta_{n,M,\delta}+\frac2n+ \frac12\sqrt{\frac1{\pi_i}-1}\,e^{-m\gamma_s\Delta}.$$

The Markov property makes the future emission conditionally independent of past calibration given $Y_0$. The order-statistic convention contributes at most $2/n$ in pooled CDF units. This bound separates calibration uncertainty from the irreducible horizon effect; it is intentionally loose, particularly its Chebyshev factor. For the symmetric two-state chain, the sharper exact horizon term is $e^{-2\Delta/\varepsilon}\sup_r|F_i(r)-\bar F(r)|$. If the current state is hidden, the same reasoning applies with its posterior $\nu$, replacing $\sqrt{1/\pi_i-1}$ by $\|\nu/\pi-1\|_{L^2(\pi)}$.

A sharper one-sided threshold guarantee

The uniform bound pays for a grid of thresholds and both tails. If the question is only whether the realized order-statistic threshold has at least a specified pooled coverage, the variance theorem gives a sharper one-sided result with no discretization parameter. Let $\widehat q=R_{(k)}$ be the $k$th of the $n$ stationary calibration scores, put $C_0=\bar F(\widehat q)$, and choose $0<p<k/n$. If $q_p=\bar F^{-1}(p)$, continuity and absence of ties give the exact event identity

$$\{C_0<p\}=\{\widehat F_n(q_p)\ge k/n\}.$$

Since the empirical CDF has mean $p$ and variance at most $p(1-p)V/n$, Cantelli's one-sided inequality yields

$$\Pr_\pi\{C_0<p\} \le B_{n,k,V}(p) :=\frac{p(1-p)V} {p(1-p)V+n(k/n-p)^2}.$$

Here $V=V_n(e^{-m\gamma_sh})$ on a regular grid and $V=V_{\boldsymbol t}$ on an irregular grid. The proof uses dependence only through the already established spectral variance envelope. Cantelli's denominator makes the bound strictly sharper than the corresponding one-sided Chebyshev bound $p(1-p)V/[n(k/n-p)^2]$. This is the one-sided inequality introduced by Cantelli (1928).

The terminal-horizon error can be included without conflating the two effects. Put

$$H_i(\Delta)=\frac12\sqrt{\frac1{\pi_i}-1} e^{-m\gamma_s\Delta}.$$

For every calibration panel, $|\Pr(R_\Delta\le\widehat q\mid\text{history},Y_0=i)-C_0| \le H_i(\Delta)$. Therefore, whenever $u_i=p+H_i(\Delta)<k/n$,

$$\Pr_\pi\!\left\{ \Pr(R_\Delta\le\widehat q\mid\text{history},Y_0=i)<p \right\} \le B_{n,k,V}(u_i).$$

Replacing $H_i$ by $\max_iH_i$ makes the conclusion simultaneous over all possible current states. This is a PAC statement over random stationary calibration panels. It is training-conditional in the sense that it controls the probability of drawing a badly calibrated panel; it is neither pointwise nor feature-conditional conformal validity.

The exact finite-state count law

For a finite hidden state space, the entire order-statistic failure law is available by a polynomial matrix recursion. This is sharper than a moment inequality and does not require enumerating $2^n$ binary paths. On a deterministic grid $t_1<\cdots<t_n$, let $P_j=e^{mQ_0(t_{j+1}-t_j)}$, let $f_i=F_i(q_p)$, and put

$$D(z)=\operatorname{diag}(1-f+zf).$$

If $S_n=\sum_{j=1}^n{\bf1}\{R_{t_j}\le q_p\}$ and the initial state law is the row vector $\nu$, then conditional independence of the emissions and the Markov property give the exact probability-generating function

$$G_n(z)=\mathbb E_\nu z^{S_n} =\nu D(z)P_1D(z)\cdots P_{n-1}D(z){\bf1},$$ $$\Pr_\nu\{C_0<p\}=\Pr_\nu\{S_n\ge k\} =\sum_{s=k}^n[z^s]G_n(z).$$

The first identity follows by conditioning successively on the hidden states; it is the discrete finite-state Feynman–Kac product associated with the transform method introduced by Kac (1949). Multiplying polynomial state rows computes every coefficient in $O(n^2d^2)$ operations and $O(nd)$ memory for $d$ hidden states. Thus the formula handles arbitrary deterministic spacings and arbitrary initial laws directly. For $k=n$, its highest coefficient is simply the exact all-success probability; this is why each $n=k=9$ failure probability in the preceding table can be recovered without binary-path enumeration.

The same generating function also gives the computable exponential bound

$$\Pr_\nu\{S_n\ge k\} \le \inf_{z>1}z^{-k}G_n(z) =\inf_{\theta>0}e^{-k\theta}G_n(e^\theta).$$

This Chernoff consequence is distribution-specific, unlike the spectral-Cantelli envelope, but it can be substantially sharper. In the same nonreversible model with scaled spacing $0.1$, $p=0.8$, $n=30$, and $k=27$, the coefficient recursion gives exact failure probability $0.187906264$. The optimized generating-function bound is $0.481299437$ at $\theta=0.55627313$, compared with the spectral-Cantelli bound $0.727691942$. Independent enumeration verifies every coefficient for $n=9$ within $2\times10^{-15}$, while the $n=30$ computation replaces more than one billion binary paths by 31 coefficient rows.

These are probabilities over random calibration panels. Even when the count law is computed exactly, the conclusion remains training-conditional/PAC rather than pointwise or feature-conditional conformal validity. Nor does the calculation justify replacing $n$ by an effective sample size in an iid beta law.

A joint long-panel and fast-switching theorem

The preceding exact recursion permits any fixed pair $(n,m)$, while the Perron asymptotics below send $n$ to infinity at fixed $m$. There is also a uniform comparison in which both change. Let ${\cal L}_{n,m}$ be the law of a stationary score panel on a regular grid with $P=e^{mQ_0h}$, and let $\bar{\cal L}^{\otimes n}$ be $n$ independent draws from the pooled score law. For a finite irreducible $d$-state chain and $m,h>0$, put $\Pi={\bf1}\pi$ and define the exact one-step dependence energy

$$\mathfrak E(P):= \left\|D_\pi^{1/2}(P-\Pi)D_\pi^{-1/2}\right\|_F^2 =\sum_i\pi_i\chi^2(P(i,\cdot),\pi).$$

Then

$$d_{\rm TV}\!\left({\cal L}_{n,m},\bar{\cal L}^{\otimes n}\right) \le \delta^{\rm HS}_{n,m}:= \min\!\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}\,\mathfrak E(P)}\right\} \le \delta^{\rm gap}_{n,m}:= \min\!\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)(d-1)}2}\, e^{-m\gamma_s h}\right\}.$$

To prove it, first compare the stationary hidden path with $\pi^{\otimes n}$. The chain rule for relative entropy gives exactly

$$D\!\left({\cal L}(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)\Vert\pi^{\otimes n}\right) =(n-1)\sum_i\pi_iD(P(i,\cdot)\Vert\pi).$$

Pinsker already gives the still sharper computable bound obtained by putting the exact stationary average row KL divergence inside the square root. The elementary inequality $D\le\chi^2$, applied row by row, gives the displayed Hilbert--Schmidt bound because the stationary average row chi-square divergence is exactly $\mathfrak E(P)$. The $L^2(\pi)$ contraction of the adjoint semigroup then gives the gap-only corollary

$$\mathfrak E(P) \le e^{-2m\gamma_sh}\sum_i\pi_i(\pi_i^{-1}-1) =(d-1)e^{-2m\gamma_sh}.$$

Pinsker's inequality yields the displayed total-variation bound. Passing the hidden path through arbitrary conditionally independent state-specific emission kernels can only decrease divergence, the data processing principle developed for general information divergences by Csiszár (1963). Thus the conclusion is uniform over the score laws and over every panel event; it is not merely a covariance or empirical-CDF statement.

For example, continuity of the pooled CDF gives the finite-panel iid comparison

$$\left|\Pr_\pi\{C_0<p\} -\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\ge k\}\right| \le\delta^{\rm HS}_{n,m}.$$

The Hilbert--Schmidt expression is intrinsic: it is the sum of the squared singular values of $P-\Pi$ on centered $L^2(\pi)$. If $Q_0$ is reversible with relaxation rates $0=\lambda_1<\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_d$, the identity becomes

$$\mathfrak E(e^{mQ_0h}) =\sum_{r=2}^d e^{-2m\lambda_rh}.$$

Thus iid calibration-panel rank laws become uniformly accurate under the intrinsic sufficient condition $(n_m-1)\mathfrak E(P_m)\to0$. The simpler condition $n_me^{-2m\gamma_sh}\to0$ remains sufficient, but can waste a factor $d-1$ by treating every mode as if it relaxed at the gap. Equivalently, the reversible effective rank $r_{\rm eff}=e^{2m\lambda_2h}\mathfrak E(P)$ lies between $1$ and $d-1$ and counts the modes still visible at the panel spacing. This remains a marginal law over calibration panels; it does not turn ordinary pooled conformal prediction into pointwise, training-conditional, or feature-conditional coverage. From an initial law $\nu$ followed by a burn-in $b$, either right side simply gains $\tfrac12\|\nu/\pi-1\|_{2,\pi}e^{-m\gamma_sb}$.

A ten-state reversible certificate makes the improvement concrete. Two five-state clusters mix rapidly internally but communicate through one slow contrast. At scaled time $10$,

$$\mathfrak E(P)=0.018315638889, \qquad (d-1)e^{-2\gamma_s t}=0.164840749999,$$

so $r_{\rm eff}=1$ to numerical precision. For $n=20$, the exact Hilbert--Schmidt TV bound is $0.417131357540$, whereas the gap-only bound is the vacuous value $1$. The certificate also checks the weighted row-$\chi^2$, Frobenius, singular-value, and reversible eigenvalue formulas independently.

Irregular panels: the whole spacing vector matters

The comparison is not restricted to a regular grid. Let the successive spacings be $h_1,\ldots,h_{n-1}$ and put $P_j=e^{mQ_0h_j}$. Stationarity is preserved across every unequal transition, so the relative-entropy chain rule gives the exact identity

$$D\!\left({\cal L}(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)\Vert\pi^{\otimes n}\right) =\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_i\pi_i D(P_j(i,\cdot)\Vert\pi).$$

Applying data processing, Pinsker, and the same rowwise chi-square comparison separately at every gap yields, for arbitrary conditionally independent state-specific emission kernels,

$$d_{\rm TV}\!\left({\cal L}_{\boldsymbol h,m}, \bar{\cal L}^{\otimes n}\right) \le \min\!\left\{1, \sqrt{\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak E(P_j)}\right\} \le \min\!\left\{1, \sqrt{\frac{d-1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1} e^{-2m\gamma_sh_j}}\right\}.$$

If $Q_0$ is reversible with relaxation rates $\lambda_r$, the exact energy in the first bound is

$$\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak E(P_j) =\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{r=2}^d e^{-2m\lambda_rh_j}.$$

Thus an irregular triangular array is asymptotically iid whenever this summed modal energy tends to zero. The whole spacing vector is essential: Jensen's inequality gives $\sum_j e^{-2m\gamma_sh_j}\ge (n-1)e^{-2m\gamma_s\bar h}$. Replacing unequal gaps by their mean would therefore make the displayed envelope smaller, not safer; a few short gaps can dominate it.

The nonreversible three-state certificate uses scaled spacings $(0.35,0.50,0.75,1.10,1.70)$. Full enumeration of all $3^6$ hidden paths gives KL divergence $0.088542841901$ and TV distance $0.179564893897$. The exact-KL, summed Hilbert--Schmidt, and gap-only bounds are respectively $0.210407749264$, $0.289820479269$, and $0.325134088385$.

The information scale remains sharp with unequal gaps. In the symmetric two-state chain set $a_j=e^{-2mh_j}$. The path-to-sign bijection now gives

$$L=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+a_jZ_j),$$

where the $Z_j$ are independent fair signs under the iid reference. Hence path TV is exactly heterogeneous Bernoulli-product TV. If $\max_j|a_j|\to0$ and $\sum_j a_j^2\to c$, the triangular-array log-likelihood is locally asymptotically normal in the sense of Le Cam (1960), and the TV distance tends to $2\Phi(\sqrt c/2)-1$. In particular it vanishes when the sum tends to zero and tends to one when it diverges. With equally many correlations $a$ and $2a$ and total squared correlation tending to one, the exact certificate reaches $0.38297545$ at $a=2^{-6}$, versus the limit $0.38292492$. This is still a marginal comparison of random panel laws, not conditional conformal coverage.

The scale cannot be improved in general even on a regular grid. For the stationary symmetric two-state chain, encode the states by $X_j\in\{-1,1\}$ and put $a=e^{-2mh}$. The transition signs $Z_j=X_jX_{j+1}$ are independent with $\Pr(Z_j=1)=(1+a)/2$; under an iid stationary panel they are independent fair signs. The path total variation is therefore exactly the distance between $n-1$ copies of $\operatorname{Bernoulli}((1+a)/2)$ and fair Bernoulli variables. As $a\downarrow0$, it tends to zero if $(n-1)a^2\to0$, to one if $(n-1)a^2\to\infty$, and, if $(n-1)a^2\to c\in(0,\infty)$, to

$$2\Phi(\sqrt c/2)-1.$$

Continuous emissions on disjoint state-specific supports reveal the state and preserve this distance exactly, so the example is a genuine continuous-score counterexample outside the subcritical scale. The certificate checks the exact Bernoulli-product formula by full path enumeration and obtains $0.38292359$ at $a=2^{-7}$, $n-1=a^{-2}$, against the critical limit $0.38292492$. For the nonreversible three-state chain and a 200-score panel, the spectral bounds at scaled spacings $mh=1,1.5,2$ are respectively $0.35621999$, $0.05660622$, and $0.00899518$.

Sharp joint burn-in and panel limit

The additive burn-in term is not merely a proof artifact. In the same symmetric two-state model, let the initial sign imbalance before burn-in be $\eta$, put

$$\delta_m=\eta e^{-2mb_m},\qquad a_m=e^{-2mh_m},$$

and observe $n_m$ states after the burn-in. The path-to-sign map is a bijection, and under the iid stationary reference law $(X_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_{n_m-1})$ are independent fair signs. Under the Markov law they are still independent, but have means $(\delta_m,a_m,\ldots,a_m)$. Consequently the likelihood ratio is exactly

$$L_m=(1+\delta_mX_0) \prod_{j=1}^{n_m-1}(1+a_mZ_j),\qquad d_{\rm TV}=\frac12\mathbb E_0|L_m-1|.$$

Suppose $a_m\to0$, $\delta_m\to\delta\in[-1,1]$, and $(n_m-1)a_m^2\to c\in[0,\infty)$. The log of the product is locally asymptotically normal, in the sense introduced by Le Cam (1960), and uniform integrability follows from its bounded second moment. Hence

$$d_{\rm TV}\longrightarrow \Psi(\delta,c):=\frac14\sum_{s=\pm1} \mathbb E\left|(1+s\delta) e^{\sqrt c\,G-c/2}-1\right|,\qquad G\sim N(0,1).$$

This contains both boundary cases: $\Psi(\delta,0)=|\delta|/2$ is the surviving initial-state memory, while $\Psi(0,c)=2\Phi(\sqrt c/2)-1$ is the stationary panel limit above. Therefore iid panel convergence holds in this sharp example if and only if

$$\delta_m\to0 \quad\hbox{and}\quad (n_m-1)a_m^2\to0.$$

A short within-panel spacing cannot compensate for insufficient burn-in, nor can burn-in remove accumulated dependence among a growing number of calibration scores. With $\delta=0.6$ and $c=1$, the exact finite-panel distance at $a=2^{-7}$ is $0.451714292$, converging to $\Psi(0.6,1)=0.451711767$. Full path enumeration independently verifies the finite factorization. Continuous disjoint-support emissions again preserve the hidden path and make the result a score-panel statement. It remains a comparison of marginal random-panel laws, not a training-conditional or feature-conditional coverage guarantee.

Long regular panels: the exact exponential rate

The generating matrix also resolves the large-panel limit, but only when the spacing is fixed. Let $P=e^{mQ_0h}$ for $h>0$, assume the finite CTMC is irreducible, and suppose $0<f_i<1$ in every state. Then $P$ and the tilted matrix below are strictly positive. Put

$$M_\theta=P D(e^\theta),\qquad \Lambda(\theta)=\log\rho(M_\theta),$$

where $\rho$ is the Perron root. Since

$$G_n(e^\theta)=\nu D(e^\theta)M_\theta^{n-1}{\bf1},$$

Perron–Frobenius theory gives, for every initial state law $\nu$,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n \log\mathbb E_\nu e^{\theta S_n}=\Lambda(\theta),$$ $$I(x)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb R} \{\theta x-\Lambda(\theta)\},\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log \Pr_\nu\{S_n/n\ge x\}=-I(x),\quad p<x<1,$$

with $p=\pi f=\Lambda'(0)$. Positivity makes the Perron root simple and analytic for every real $\theta$, so the differentiability hypothesis in the Gärtner–Ellis theorem is automatic. This is the finite-state specialization of the primary large-deviation results of Gärtner (1977) and Ellis (1984). For $x>p$, the optimizing tilt is positive, and the finite-panel Chernoff exponent converges to $I(x)$. In the iid specialization, $\Lambda(\theta)=\log(1-p+pe^\theta)$ and $I$ reduces to the Bernoulli relative entropy.

For the nonreversible certificate at scaled spacing $0.1$, $p=0.8$ and $x=0.9$, the Perron calculation gives

The sharp lattice tail prefactor

The boundary amplitude also identifies the leading finite-panel tail, rather than only its logarithmic rate. Let $r_\theta,\ell_\theta$ be right and left Perron eigenvectors normalized by $\ell_\theta^\top r_\theta=1$, define $A_\nu(\theta)=\{\nu D(e^\theta)r_\theta\} \{\ell_\theta^\top{\bf1}\}$, and put $B_\nu(\theta)=A_\nu(\theta)e^{-\Lambda(\theta)}$. For integer thresholds $k_n$ such that $x_n=k_n/n$ stays in a compact subset of $(p,1)$, let $\theta_n>0$ solve $\Lambda'(\theta_n)=x_n$, set $v_n=\Lambda''(\theta_n)$, and write $I(x_n)=\theta_nx_n-\Lambda(\theta_n)$. Then

$$\Pr_\nu\{S_n\ge k_n\} =\frac{B_\nu(\theta_n)}{1-e^{-\theta_n}} \frac{e^{-nI(x_n)}}{\sqrt{2\pi n v_n}} \{1+o(1)\}.$$

The proof is a finite-dimensional lattice saddle point. Fourier inversion of the exact matrix MGF gives the central Gaussian factor. Because every $0<f_i<1$ and $P$ is positive, the tilted Fourier matrix has spectral radius strictly below its real Perron root away from frequency zero, so the remaining integral is negligible. Successive lattice masses above $k_n$ have limiting ratio $e^{-\theta_n}$; summing them supplies the factor $(1-e^{-\theta_n})^{-1}$. Finally, the replacement of $(n-1)\Lambda$ by $n\Lambda$ explains why the initial-law factor is $B_\nu=A_\nu e^{-\Lambda}$ rather than $A_\nu$.

This is the finite-state Markov-additive counterpart of the sharp lattice asymptotics of Bahadur and Ranga Rao (1960); the Markov-additive large-deviation framework is due to Ney and Nummelin (1987). For the benchmark $x_n=0.9$, $v_n=0.122606564$, $B_\pi(\theta_n)=0.984845741$, and the $n$-free prefactor is $2.66849646$. The table's fourth column shows the exact tail divided by this sharp approximation, converging to one. A pure exponential approximation omits both the $n^{-1/2}$ factor and the initial-law amplitude. The theorem remains a fixed-$P$ result; it neither covers irregular grids nor asserts a joint limit in panel size and switching rate.

The first two relative saddle-point corrections

The same local Fourier calculation supplies the next two terms, including the derivatives of the initial-law and lattice amplitude. Write

$$H_\nu(\theta)=\frac{B_\nu(\theta)}{1-e^{-\theta}},\qquad v=\Lambda''(\theta),\quad c_3=\Lambda'''(\theta),\quad c_4=\Lambda''''(\theta).$$

With every quantity below evaluated at $\theta_n$, uniformly while $x_n=k_n/n$ remains in a compact subset of $(p,1)$,

$$\Pr_\nu\{S_n\ge k_n\} =\frac{H_\nu e^{-nI(x_n)}}{\sqrt{2\pi n v}} \left\{1+\frac{C_1(\theta_n)}{n} +\frac{C_2(\theta_n)}{n^2}+o(n^{-2})\right\},$$ $$C_1=-\frac{H_\nu''}{2vH_\nu} +\frac{H_\nu'c_3}{2v^2H_\nu} +\frac{c_4}{8v^2}-\frac{5c_3^2}{24v^3}.$$

Writing $H_j=H_\nu^{(j)}(\theta_n)$ and $c_j=\Lambda^{(j)}(\theta_n)$, the second coefficient is

$$\begin{aligned} C_2={}&\frac{H_4}{8v^2H_0} -\frac{H_1c_5}{8v^3H_0} -\frac{5H_2c_4}{16v^3H_0} -\frac{5H_3c_3}{12v^3H_0}-\frac{c_6}{48v^3}\\ &+\frac{35H_2c_3^2}{48v^4H_0} +\frac{35H_1c_3c_4}{48v^4H_0} +\frac{7c_3c_5}{48v^4}+\frac{35c_4^2}{384v^4}\\ &-\frac{35H_1c_3^3}{48v^5H_0} -\frac{35c_3^2c_4}{64v^5} +\frac{385c_3^4}{1152v^6}. \end{aligned}$$

Indeed, after the saddle scaling $t=s/\sqrt n$, expand the analytic Fourier amplitude as $H_\nu+iH_\nu't-H_\nu''t^2/2$ and the Perron exponent as $-vt^2/2-ic_3t^3/6+c_4t^4/24$. Integrating the even terms against the centered Gaussian gives the four displayed contributions; the noncentral arc is exponentially smaller by the same Perron separation used above. Retaining the even terms through order $n^{-2}$ gives $C_2$; analyticity makes the expansion uniform on compact tilt intervals. This is the finite-state Markov-additive version of the asymptotic saddle-point expansion introduced by Daniels (1954).

For the benchmark, $C_1=-14.728744589$ and $C_2=901.839869479$. The last two columns divide the exact probability by the first- and second-order corrected approximations. At $n=480$ and $960$, the second correction reduces the relative discrepancies to $0.0682\%$ and $0.0094\%$, from $0.3353\%$ and $0.0899\%$ after the first correction and $2.7434\%$ and $1.4457\%$ for the leading formula. The overshoot at small $n$ is deliberately retained: this is an asymptotic expansion, not a finite-panel error bound, and truncation is not guaranteed to improve every small panel. The statement is still for a fixed regular-grid $P$; it makes no irregular-grid or joint $n$--switching-rate claim.

The moderate-deviation bridge to the mean

The compact-interior hypothesis can be relaxed in one important direction. Keep the regular-grid transition matrix $P$ fixed, let $p=\Lambda'(0)$ and $\sigma_P^2=\Lambda''(0)>0$, and take integer thresholds $k_n$ with

$$\delta_n=\frac{k_n}{n}-p\downarrow0, \qquad \sqrt n\,\delta_n\longrightarrow\infty.$$

If $\theta_n>0$ solves $\Lambda'(\theta_n)=p+\delta_n$, then $\theta_n\sim\delta_n/\sigma_P^2$ and $n\theta_n^2\to\infty$. For every fixed initial law $\nu$, the sharp lattice formula continues to hold:

$$\Pr_\nu\{S_n\ge k_n\} =\frac{B_\nu(\theta_n)}{1-e^{-\theta_n}} \frac{e^{-nI(p+\delta_n)}} {\sqrt{2\pi n\Lambda''(\theta_n)}} \left\{1+O\!\left(\frac1{n\theta_n^2}\right)\right\},$$ $$\Pr_\nu\{S_n\ge k_n\} =\frac{\sigma_P}{\delta_n\sqrt{2\pi n}} e^{-nI(p+\delta_n)} \left\{1+O(\delta_n) +O\!\left(\frac1{n\delta_n^2}\right)\right\}.$$

The singular lattice factor is essential: as the saddle approaches zero, $(1-e^{-\theta_n})^{-1}\sim1/\theta_n$. After the Fourier scaling $t=s/\sqrt n$, derivatives of that amplitude cost powers of $1/\theta_n$; the first omitted even Gaussian term is therefore of relative order $(n\theta_n^2)^{-1}$. The displayed condition is exactly what makes it vanish. Analyticity gives $B_\nu(\theta)=1+O(\theta)$, $\Lambda''(\theta)=\sigma_P^2+O(\theta)$, and $\theta=\delta/\sigma_P^2+O(\delta^2)$, yielding the second formula. Thus the initial law disappears from the leading moderate-deviation prefactor, though it remains in the exact finite-$n$ amplitude.

This is the near-mean boundary of the same Markov-additive saddle argument of Ney and Nummelin (1987) and the lattice prefactor mechanism of Bahadur and Ranga Rao (1960). It is not a central-limit theorem: when $\sqrt n\,\delta_n=O(1)$, the pole and Gaussian saddle interact and a normal-tail approximation is required instead.

In the certificate, $p=0.5$ and $k_n=\lceil np+\tfrac12n^{3/4}\rceil$. From $n=120$ to $3840$, $\delta_n$ falls from $0.15833333$ to $0.06354167$, while $n\theta_n^2$ rises from $22.2914$ to $107.7808$. The exact-to-Perron ratio rises from $0.923553$ to $0.978915$; after also replacing the exact amplitude by the universal Gaussian prefactor, the ratio falls from $1.133454$ to $1.062593$. The theorem is fixed-$P$ and gives no irregular-grid, finite-$n$, central-zone, or joint switching-rate bound.

All $O(\cdot)$ constants above are fixed-model, fixed-initial-law constants. No uniformity is asserted as the transition matrix loses positivity or its spectral separation degenerates.

The central-limit boundary: a second-order lattice Edgeworth bridge

The complementary zone has a different uniform approximation. Assume $P$ is fixed and positive, every emission probability satisfies $0<f_i<1$, and the initial law $\nu$ is fixed. Put

$$p=\Lambda'(0),\qquad \sigma^2=\Lambda''(0)>0, \qquad \kappa_3=\Lambda'''(0),\qquad \kappa_4=\Lambda^{(4)}(0),\qquad b_\nu=(\log B_\nu)'(0),\qquad d_\nu=(\log B_\nu)''(0).$$

For integer thresholds $k_n$, use the half-integer tail coordinate

$$x_n=\frac{k_n-\tfrac12-np}{\sigma\sqrt n}.$$

Write $H_1(x)=x$, $H_3(x)=x^3-3x$, and $H_5(x)=x^5-10x^3+15x$. Then, uniformly whenever $x_n$ stays in a fixed compact subset of $\mathbb R$,

$$\Pr_\nu\{S_n\ge k_n\} =\overline\Phi(x_n)+\phi(x_n) \left\{\frac{A_{1,\nu}(x_n)}{\sqrt n} +\frac{A_{2,\nu}(x_n)}n\right\}+O_K(n^{-3/2}),$$ $$A_{1,\nu}(x)=\frac{b_\nu}{\sigma} +\frac{\kappa_3}{6\sigma^3}(x^2-1),$$ $$A_{2,\nu}(x)= \left\{\frac{d_\nu+b_\nu^2}{2\sigma^2} -\frac1{24\sigma^2}\right\}H_1(x) +\left\{\frac{\kappa_4}{24\sigma^4} +\frac{b_\nu\kappa_3}{6\sigma^4}\right\}H_3(x) +\frac{\kappa_3^2}{72\sigma^6}H_5(x).$$

Both first-order terms are necessary. The skewness term comes from the cubic Taylor coefficient of the Perron eigenvalue. The boundary term is the order-one mean shift: the spectral decomposition $\mathbb E_\nu e^{\theta S_n} =B_\nu(\theta)e^{n\Lambda(\theta)}+O(\rho^n)$ implies $\mathbb E_\nu S_n=np+b_\nu+O(\rho^n)$. Hence a positive $b_\nu$ raises the upper tail. For a stationary start, $b_\pi=0$ because the finite-panel mean is exactly $np$.

For completeness, the proof is a local Fourier expansion rather than a continuation of the singular large-deviation prefactor. At frequency $t/(\sigma\sqrt n)$, expand $\log B_\nu$ through first order and $n\Lambda$ through third order. Fourier inversion gives the Gaussian, boundary-mean and skewness terms. Summing the lattice masses through $k_n-1$ places the discontinuity halfway between adjacent integers, producing $k_n-\tfrac12$; omitting this continuity correction leaves an order-$n^{-1/2}$ periodic term. At second order, expanding $\log B_\nu$ through quadratic order and $n\Lambda$ through fourth order gives the $d_\nu+b_\nu^2$, $\kappa_4$, $b_\nu\kappa_3$, and $\kappa_3^2$ terms. The midpoint Euler–Maclaurin formula contributes the separate $-H_1/(24\sigma^2)$ lattice term. Positivity of $P$ and $0<f_i<1$ give strict Fourier contraction away from zero, while the next local Taylor order makes the remainder $O_K(n^{-3/2})$. This is the finite-matrix version of the lattice Fourier expansion of Esseen (1945) and the first-order Markov-chain Edgeworth theory of Datta and McCormick (1993); arbitrary-order local lattice expansions for finite Markov chains are established by Pudlo (2010).

The exact-law certificate uses the nonstationary start concentrated in state zero, for which $\sigma^2=0.261098129759$, $\kappa_3=-0.350878968118$, $\kappa_4=0.328775644197$, $b_\nu=0.653945953614$, and $d_\nu=-0.925371653715$. Over target coordinates $x\in\{-1,0,1,2\}$, the maximum uncorrected Gaussian error falls from $0.062972628$ at $n=120$ to $0.022139640$ at $n=960$, with $\sqrt n$ times that error nearly constant. Including skewness but omitting $b_\nu$ still leaves error $0.016504237$ at $n=960$. Including both first-order terms reduces the maximum errors to $0.002466128$, $0.001130799$, $0.000576090$, and $0.000271427$ for $n=120,240,480,960$; the products by $n$ remain between $0.2606$ and $0.2959$. Adding $A_{2,\nu}$ reduces the maximum errors to $4.34444\times10^{-4}$, $1.37683\times10^{-4}$, $5.32133\times10^{-5}$, $1.71588\times10^{-5}$, $6.15325\times10^{-6}$, and $2.22912\times10^{-6}$ for $n=120,240,480,960,1920,3840$. The corresponding $n^{3/2}$-scaled errors stay between $0.510$ and $0.572$.

The $O_K(n^{-3/2})$ statement is uniform only over a fixed compact set of central coordinates for this fixed regular-grid model and fixed initial law. It is not an explicit finite-panel error bound, is not uniform as positivity, variance or the spectral gap degenerates, and makes no irregular-grid or joint panel-size/switching-rate claim. It concerns the calibration-count law and does not upgrade marginal split-conformal coverage to conditional coverage.

Perron curvature is exactly Green–Kubo

The tilted eigenvalue is also consistent with the finite-panel variance theorem, including its boundary term. Let $\widetilde f=f-p{\bf1}$, $\Pi={\bf1}\pi$, and

$$Z=(I-P+\Pi)^{-1}.$$

On centered vectors, $Z=\sum_{\ell\ge0}P^\ell$. Differentiating the Perron eigenvalue at zero, or equivalently summing the stationary indicator covariances, gives

$$\Lambda'(0)=p,\qquad \Lambda''(0)=\sigma_P^2 =p(1-p)+2\langle\widetilde f,PZ\widetilde f\rangle_\pi,$$ $$\chi_P=-2\langle\widetilde f,PZ^2\widetilde f\rangle_\pi,$$ $$\operatorname{Var}_\pi(S_n) =n\sigma_P^2+\chi_P +2\langle\widetilde f,P^{n+1}Z^2\widetilde f\rangle_\pi.$$

The first line is the discrete Green–Kubo formula. The second and third lines are an exact finite-panel refinement: $\chi_P$ is the persistent endpoint intercept, while the final term decays geometrically. No reversibility is required. More generally, analyticity of the simple Perron root gives $\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa_r(S_n)/n=\Lambda^{(r)}(0)$ for every fixed cumulant order $r$. The covariance interpretation is the discrete counterpart of the primary formulas of Green (1954) and Kubo (1957).

For the same nonreversible benchmark,

$$\sigma_P^2=0.261098129759,\qquad \chi_P=-0.196956872111.$$

A five-point numerical derivative gives $\Lambda''(0)=0.261098129855$. The exact coefficient law has $\operatorname{Var}(S_{30})/30=0.254532972569$ and $\operatorname{Var}(S_{60})/60=0.257815515224$; the exact remaining boundary terms after slope and intercept are respectively $2.1564\times10^{-6}$ and $6.86\times10^{-12}$. This is a panel-size asymptotic at fixed $P$, distinct from the fast-switching limit obtained by changing $m$.

All Perron cumulants and near-mean tail asymmetry

The same simple-root perturbation gives every limiting cumulant rate by finite-dimensional linear algebra. Put $B=P\operatorname{diag}(f)$, $r_0={\bf1}$, $a_0=1$, and $\kappa_0=0$. For $k\ge1$, recursively define

$$a_k=\sum_{j=1}^k {k\choose j}\pi B r_{k-j},$$ $$r_k=Z\sum_{j=1}^k {k\choose j}(B-a_jI)r_{k-j}, \qquad \pi r_k=0,$$ $$\kappa_{k,P} =a_k-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{k-1\choose j-1} \kappa_{j,P}a_{k-j}.$$

Here $a_k=\rho^{(k)}(0)$ and $\kappa_{k,P}=\Lambda^{(k)}(0)$, so for every fixed $k$ and every initial state law,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\kappa_k(S_n)}n=\kappa_{k,P}.$$

The recursion follows by differentiating $M_\theta r(\theta)=\rho(\theta)r(\theta)$ and $\rho(\theta)=e^{\Lambda(\theta)}$; every step uses the same fundamental matrix $Z$. This is the finite-dimensional positive-matrix specialization of Kato's (1949) regular eigenvalue perturbation method.

Every finite-panel cumulant has an explicit boundary constant

The limiting slopes above do not describe the complete fixed-order asymptotics. Let $r_\theta$ and $\ell_\theta$ be analytic right and left Perron eigenvectors of $M_\theta$, normalized by $\ell_\theta^\top r_\theta=1$, and put

$$A_\nu(\theta)= \{\nu D(e^\theta)r_\theta\} \{\ell_\theta^\top{\bf1}\}.$$

The scalar $A_\nu$ is independent of the reciprocal rescaling of the two eigenvectors. Positivity and the spectral gap give a complex neighborhood of zero and constants $C<\infty$, $0<q<1$ such that, uniformly there,

$$\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{\theta S_n} =(n-1)\Lambda(\theta)+\log A_\nu(\theta)+R_{n,\nu}(\theta), \qquad |R_{n,\nu}(\theta)|\le Cq^n,$$ $$\kappa_k(S_n)=n\kappa_{k,P}+\beta_{k,\nu}+O_k(q^n), \qquad \beta_{k,\nu}= \left.\frac{d^k}{d\theta^k} \{\log A_\nu(\theta)-\Lambda(\theta)\}\right|_{\theta=0}.$$

This holds for every fixed $k$ and every initial probability law $\nu$. It follows directly by inserting the analytic Perron projector $r_\theta\ell_\theta^\top$ into the exact matrix MGF and differentiating the uniform analytic remainder by Cauchy's formula. Thus the start-up effect is an explicit order-one constant, followed by geometric decay; it is not hidden in an unspecified $o(n)$ term.

For the nonreversible certificate, the stationary boundary constants through order four are

$$\beta_{1:4,\pi}=(0, -0.196956872111, 0.737447302168, -1.805249335882),$$

so the second entry exactly recovers the Green–Kubo endpoint intercept $\chi_P$. Starting instead from state zero gives

$$\beta_{1:4,e_0}=(0.653945953614, -0.925371653715, 1.430373003343, -1.538351359308).$$

At $n=60$, all eight constants agree with cumulants extracted independently from the exact coefficient law within $1.17\times10^{-8}$. These are fixed-$P$ boundary corrections in panel size; they are unrelated to a joint limit that changes the switching rate.

Finally, writing $v=\Lambda''(0)>0$, analytic Legendre duality gives the two-sided local expansion through fourth order

$$I(p+\delta) =\frac{\delta^2}{2v} -\frac{\kappa_{3,P}}{6v^3}\delta^3 +\frac{3\kappa_{3,P}^2-v\kappa_{4,P}}{24v^5}\delta^4 +O(\delta^5).$$

The quadratic term is the Green–Kubo Gaussian approximation; the cubic and quartic terms are the first skewness and kurtosis corrections for the dependent panel. This is a Taylor consequence of the same Gärtner–Ellis rate function above. It is distinct from the sharp lattice prefactor proved above, and is not an Edgeworth approximation to a finite-panel probability or a joint limit in $n$ and $m$.

For the benchmark, $(\kappa_{3,P},\kappa_{4,P}) =(-0.350878968118,0.328775644197)$. Richardson differentiation gives $(-0.350878967774,0.328775569108)$, while the exact coefficient law at $n=480$ gives $(-0.349342619570,0.325014708079)$. At $\delta=0.02$, the exact rate is $0.000793937413$; the quadratic, cubic and quartic errors are respectively $2.7942\times10^{-5}$, $1.6583\times10^{-6}$ and $1.0070\times10^{-7}$. Across $\delta=0.04,0.02,0.01,0.005,0.0025$, their measured residual orders are $3.041083$, $4.042367$ and $5.047594$. In the iid specialization, the recursion agrees with cumulants extracted independently from the exact coefficient law through order eight, with maximum discrepancy $3.32\times10^{-8}$.

Arbitrary starts and burn-in

The preceding calibration confidence was stated for a stationary panel. There is an explicit transfer to any initial regime law. Let the chain start from $\nu$, allow a burn-in $b\ge0$, and then observe the emitted panel $\mathcal P_b=(R_b,R_{b+h},\ldots,R_{b+(n-1)h})$. If $\mathcal P_\pi$ denotes the same panel started from $\pi$, then

$$d_{\rm TV}\{\mathcal L_\nu(\mathcal P_b), \mathcal L_\pi(\mathcal P_\pi)\} \le d_{\rm TV}(\nu e^{mQ_0b},\pi) \le \frac12e^{-m\gamma_sb} \left\|\frac{\nu}{\pi}-1\right\|_{L^2(\pi)} =:\zeta_b.$$

For the first inequality, condition on the state at time $b$ and view all later states and emissions as one Markov kernel; total variation cannot increase under that kernel. This data-processing principle is the finite-state form of the contraction studied by Dobrushin (1956). For the second inequality, the density relative to $\pi$ evolves under the adjoint semigroup. Its symmetric part is again $Q_s$, so the same energy argument gives $L^2(\pi)$ contraction at rate $\gamma_s$; Cauchy–Schwarz then converts $L^2$ to total variation.

Consequently, every stationary-panel event with probability at least $1-\delta$ holds after burn-in with probability at least $1-\delta-\zeta_b$. In particular, the uniform empirical-CDF event and the conditional-coverage inequality above remain unchanged on the good event, but their confidence becomes $1-\delta-\zeta_b$. To make the start-up penalty at most $\epsilon$, it is sufficient that

$$b\ge\frac1{m\gamma_s} \left[\log\frac{\|\nu/\pi-1\|_{L^2(\pi)}}{2\epsilon}\right]_+.$$

For a specified initial state $i$, the numerator is $\sqrt{1/\pi_i-1}$. This is a marginal probability statement over the random calibration panel. It is neither training-conditional nor feature-conditional validity, and the bound does not replace the panel size by an informal effective sample size.

The nonreversible three-state certificate enumerates the exact law of every four-score binary panel. For scaled burn-ins $mb=0,0.25,0.5,1$, the score-panel total variations are respectively $0.13175444$, $0.06439285$, $0.02503886$, and $0.00201894$; each lies below both the exact first-state total variation and the $L^2$ envelope. From state zero, the sufficient burn-in for a one-percent envelope is $mb\ge1.19131949$.

The panel also forgets the start

The total-variation transfer is deliberately uniform over every panel event. For the empirical CDF itself, later observations provide their own burn-in and give a sharper result. Fix $r$, put $f=F(r)$, $p=\pi f$, $P=e^{mQ_0h}$, and $\mu_\ell=\nu e^{mQ_0b}P^\ell$. For $X_\ell={\bf1}\{R_{b+\ell h}\le r\}$ and $\widehat F_n(r)=n^{-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}X_\ell$,

$$\mathbb E_\nu\widehat F_n(r)-p =\frac1n\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(\mu_\ell-\pi)f,$$ $$\left|\mathbb E_\nu\widehat F_n(r)-p\right| \le e^{-m\gamma_sb} \left\|\frac\nu\pi-1\right\|_{2,\pi} \|f-p{\bf1}\|_{2,\pi} B_n(a),$$ $$B_n(a)=\frac{1-a^n}{n(1-a)},\qquad a=e^{-m\gamma_sh}.$$

The identity is just the sum of the transient marginal expectations. For the bound, center $f$, move each transition semigroup onto that centered function, and apply the additive-reversibilization contraction. Since $f_i\in[0,1]$, its stationary standard deviation is at most $1/2$, giving the universal version

$$\left|\mathbb E_\nu\widehat F_n(r)-p\right| \le\frac12e^{-m\gamma_sb} \left\|\frac\nu\pi-1\right\|_{2,\pi}B_n(a).$$

This prefactor and the complete factor $B_n(a)$ are sharp. For the symmetric two-state chain, a point start, and conditional success probabilities $(0,1)$, every summand has the same sign and lies in the gap eigenspace, so equality holds for every $n$, $b$, and $h$. In particular, if $mh$ is bounded away from zero, then $B_n(a)\sim\{n(1-a)\}^{-1}$: within-panel relaxation makes the initial-law bias $O(n^{-1})$ even with no separate burn-in. This does not improve the preceding $\zeta_b$ guarantee for an arbitrary panel event; it uses the averaging structure of this particular statistic.

For an arbitrary deterministic grid $0=t_1<\cdots<t_n$, the same proof replaces $B_n(a)$ by the exact envelope

$$B_{\boldsymbol t}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n e^{-m\gamma_st_i},\qquad \left|\mathbb E_\nu\widehat F_{\boldsymbol t}(r)-p\right| \le e^{-m\gamma_sb}\left\|\frac\nu\pi-1\right\|_{2,\pi} \|f-p{\bf1}\|_{2,\pi}B_{\boldsymbol t}.$$

The same symmetric binary chain attains equality for every grid and burn-in. On the clustered five-point grid above with $b=0.2$, its exact start-up bias is $-0.32283968$, equal in magnitude to the displayed bound. In the nonreversible certificate on times $(0,0.07,0.31,0.9,1.8)$, the exact bias is $0.02702529$ and the score-specific bound is $0.03907180$.

The same conditioning argument gives an exact nonstationary variance, not an effective-sample-size heuristic:

$$\operatorname{Var}_\nu\widehat F_n(r) =\frac1{n^2}\left\{ \sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\mu_\ell f +2\sum_{0\le\ell

Conditional independence of the emissions is used only in the mixed moment $\mathbb E(X_\ell X_j)= \mu_\ell\operatorname{diag}(f)P^{j-\ell}f$. On an irregular grid this term is instead $\mu_i\operatorname{diag}(f)P_{t_j-t_i}f$; no other change is needed. Exact enumeration of all $2^4$ regular-grid and $2^5$ irregular-grid binary panels agrees with the moment identities within $2\times10^{-15}$. In the nonreversible example, the empirical-CDF bias decreases from $0.04088399$ at $mb=0$ to $0.00051374$ at $mb=1$; the corresponding score-specific bounds are $0.07072227$ and $0.00178586$. The sharp two-state certificate has absolute bias and bound both equal to $0.09440221$. These finite sums are the elementary finite-state version of the Markov-additive calculations organized more generally through the Poisson equation; see Glynn and Meyn (1996).

Relation to Markov conformal work and next step

Zheng and Proutiere (2024) analyze the coverage gap for split conformal with Markovian data and the effects of thinning calibration. Barber and Pananjady (2026) analyze temporal dependence, including predictors with memory, through a switch coefficient. Nettasinghe et al. (2023) obtain a different exact-validity construction for hidden Markov models using exchangeable blocks. The calculation here isolates a separate question: how a forecast horizon comparable to the switching time leaves a conditional residual mismatch even with the population pooled quantile.

Scope: the identity and additive-reversibilization bounds cover any irreducible finite-state CTMC and any deterministic regular or irregular observation grid. Scores are independent terminal emissions conditional on the terminal state, with fixed laws and a frozen prediction rule. Path functionals, predictors with memory, and overlapping path-score calibration require different formulas; those are treated separately in PR #60. No result here asserts distribution-free conditional coverage.
