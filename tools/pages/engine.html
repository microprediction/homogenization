The fast-switching engine

One recursion for every model whose state factors out of an exponential-affine formula.

Each example has a slow process $x_t$, the rate, intensity, variance or count of interest, and a fast process $y_t$ that it does not observe: a Markov chain with a finite number of regimes, or a fast mean-reverting diffusion. The quantity wanted is a Feynman–Kac expectation,

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-\int_0^t c\cdot x_r\,dr}\,\phi(x_t, y_t)\big].$$

When the model is exponential-affine in $x$ and the coefficient of $x$ does not depend on the regime, the state factors out and what remains is a linear system in time only:

$$u_i(t,x) = e^{-B(t)\cdot x}\,a_i(t), \qquad a' = \big(Q + \operatorname{diag} g(t)\big)\,a, \qquad a(0) = \text{the payoff in each regime}.$$

The model enters only through the functions $g_i(t)$, one per regime, and the terminal vector.

Deriving the recursion

The derivation uses two ideas from the Background pages: the solvability condition and the initial layer. It is written here for any number of regimes.

Step 1: separate the average from the shape

Let $\pi$ be the stationary distribution of the chain, so that $\pi Q = 0$. Split $a$ into its stationary average and its shape:

$$s = \pi\cdot a, \qquad w = \frac{a}{s} - \mathbf 1, \qquad \pi\cdot w = 0 .$$

Multiply the system $a' = (Q + \operatorname{diag} g)a$ on the left by $\pi$. The switching term drops out because $\pi Q = 0$, which leaves an equation for the average:

$$\frac{s'}{s} = \bar g + \pi\cdot(g\,w), \qquad \bar g = \pi\cdot g .$$

Subtracting this from the equation for $a/s$ gives the equation for the shape. Writing $Q = Q_0/\varepsilon$, with the scale $\varepsilon = n/(-\operatorname{tr} Q)$ used by the code, the reciprocal of the mean exit rate over the $n$ states,

$$\varepsilon\,w' = Q_0\,w + \varepsilon\,F(w), \qquad F(w) = g\,(\mathbf 1 + w) - (\mathbf 1 + w)\,\big(\bar g + \pi\cdot(g\,w)\big).$$

Any positive scale would serve, provided $Q_0$ is held fixed as the chain speeds up. This one equals the mean holding time only when every state leaves at the same rate; for a symmetric two-state chain it is $1/\lambda$.

The right side of the shape equation always averages to zero under $\pi$. That is what makes the next step work.

Step 2: expand the shape

Write $w = \varepsilon w_1 + \varepsilon^2 w_2 + \cdots$ and match powers of $\varepsilon$. At order $\varepsilon^n$ the shape equation reads

$$Q_0\,w_n = w_{n-1}' - F_{n-1},$$

where $F_{n-1}$ collects the terms of $F$ of order $\varepsilon^{n-1}$. By the Fredholm alternative this has a solution because the right side averages to zero, and the solution with $\pi\cdot w_n = 0$ is

$$w_n = Q_0^{\#}\big(w_{n-1}' - F_{n-1}\big),$$

with $Q_0^{\#}$ the group inverse. Each order therefore costs one application of a fixed matrix to terms already known.

Step 3: the first order

At the first order $w_0 = 0$ and $F_0 = g - \bar g\,\mathbf 1$, so, with $Q^{\#} = \varepsilon\,Q_0^{\#}$ the group inverse of $Q$ itself,

$$\varepsilon\,w_1 = -Q^{\#}\big(g - \bar g\big).$$

Putting this into the equation for $s$ gives

$$a_i(t) \approx \exp\Big(\int_0^t \big[\bar g - \pi\cdot\big(g\,Q^{\#}(g - \bar g)\big)\big]\Big)\,\Big(1 - \big[Q^{\#}(g - \bar g)\big]_i(t)\Big).$$

The correction in the exponent has a direct meaning. Because $Q^{\#}f = -\int_0^\infty e^{Q\tau}f\,d\tau$ when $\pi\cdot f = 0$,

$$-\pi\cdot\big(g\,Q^{\#}(g - \bar g)\big) = \int_0^\infty \mathbb{E}_\pi\big[\big(g(y_0) - \bar g\big)\big(g(y_\tau) - \bar g\big)\big]\,d\tau .$$

This is a Green–Kubo integral of the autocorrelation of $g$ along the chain. For real $g$ it is the integrated autocovariance, half the long-run variance rate of $\int g$, as on the idea page, now for any chain.

For complex $g$, as in the Fourier examples, the product carries no conjugate. The integral is then a second cumulant rather than a variance, and it can be complex. The factor in brackets is the memory of the starting regime.

Step 4: closed form

When every $g_i$ is a sum of exponentials $e^{-\alpha t}$, products and derivatives of such sums are again such sums. So every $w_n$ is a sum of exponentials, and every term of $\log s$ integrates in closed form. Otherwise $g_i$ is held as a Chebyshev series, which is also closed under these operations.

Step 5: the initial layer

The outer series does not match the terminal vector at $t = 0$. The mismatch is removed on the fast clock $\tau = t/\varepsilon$:

$$\eta = w - w_{\mathrm{outer}}, \qquad \frac{d\eta}{d\tau} = Q_0\,\eta + \varepsilon\,\big[F(w_{\mathrm{outer}}+\eta) - F(w_{\mathrm{outer}})\big], \qquad \eta(0) = w(0) - w_{\mathrm{outer}}(0).$$

It decays at the rates of the chain and is solved order by order in a Schur basis of $Q_0$ on the mean-zero subspace, by back substitution through the triangular form, in the span of $\tau^k e^{\mu\tau}$. A generator with a Jordan block has no eigenvector basis, but it has a triangular one, and the powers of $\tau$ come out of the back substitution. The result is $a_i = s\,(1 + w_i + \eta_i)$, with error $O(\varepsilon^{N+1})$ after $N$ orders.

Exact constant forcing with unequal rates

For a two-state generator $m\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-a&a\\b&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and constant complex forcing $(g_1,g_2)$, the outer/layer split can be written without assuming $a=b$. Put $\kappa=a+b$, $\vartheta=(a-b)/\kappa$, $\varepsilon=(m\kappa)^{-1}$, $\delta=g_1-g_2$, $z=\varepsilon\delta$, and $\bar g_\pi=(bg_1+ag_2)/\kappa$. Then

$$\phi_\pm=e^{\bar g_\pi T}\left[ \frac12\left(1+\frac{1\pm z}{d}\right)e^{(d-1+\vartheta z)T/(2\varepsilon)}+ \frac12\left(1-\frac{1\pm z}{d}\right)e^{-(d+1-\vartheta z)T/(2\varepsilon)} \right],\qquad d=\sqrt{1-2\vartheta z+z^2}.$$

The two branch points $\vartheta\pm i\sqrt{1-\vartheta^2}$ have modulus one. Consequently the slow mode has the same radius-one expansion as in the symmetric chain, now in forcing contrast divided by total switching rate. More explicitly, put

$$c_1=\frac{T\delta(1-\vartheta^2)}4,\quad c_2=\frac{T\delta\vartheta(1-\vartheta^2)}4,\quad a_{1,\pm}=\frac{\vartheta\pm1}{2},\quad a_{2,\pm}=\frac{3\vartheta^2-1}{4}\pm\frac\vartheta2.$$

The slow multiplier after $e^{\bar g_\pi T}$ has quadratic Taylor polynomial

$$P_{2,\pm}(z)=1+(a_{1,\pm}+c_1)z+ \left(a_{2,\pm}+c_2+a_{1,\pm}c_1+\tfrac12c_1^2\right)z^2.$$

Cauchy's estimate on nested disks $|z|\leq r<\rho<1$ makes its remainder $O(z^3)$. Also $|d(z)|\geq1-\rho$, so $|B_\pm(z)|\leq(1-\rho)^{-1}$; continuity and absence of a branch point give $\eta_{\vartheta,\rho}=\min_{|z|\leq\rho}\operatorname{Re}d(z)>0$. Hence the layer is at most

$$\frac{e^{\operatorname{Re}\bar g_\pi T}}{1-\rho} \exp\left[-\frac{\eta_{\vartheta,\rho}+1-|\vartheta|\rho}{2\varepsilon}T\right].$$

This proves a fixed-frequency $O((m\kappa)^{-3})$ quadratic error plus an exponential layer. The claim is pointwise in a Fourier frequency: using it under an inversion integral still requires $|g_1(u)-g_2(u)|/(m\kappa)\leq\rho$ over the approximated frequency window. This is the matrix-transform form of the two-state occupation problem studied by Pedler (1971).

Finite-horizon stationary ordering

For real $g_1,g_2$ there is also an exact all-speed order for the complete transform, not only for an asymptotic coefficient. Start the chain in its stationary law $\pi=(b,a)/\kappa$ and put

$$\Phi_m(T)=\pi^\top\exp\{T[mQ_0+\operatorname{diag}(g_1,g_2)]\}\mathbf1,$$ $$r=m\kappa-\vartheta\delta,\qquad q^2=(1-\vartheta^2)\delta^2,\qquad \Delta=\sqrt{r^2+q^2},\qquad \tau=T/2.$$

Summing the two starting-state formulas, or directly exponentiating the $2\times2$ matrix, gives

$$\Phi_m(T)=e^{\bar g_\pi T-r\tau} \left\{\cosh(\tau\Delta)+{r\over\Delta}\sinh(\tau\Delta)\right\},$$ $$\frac{\partial\Phi_m(T)}{\partial m} ={\kappa q^2e^{\bar g_\pi T-r\tau}\over\Delta^3} \left\{\sinh(\tau\Delta)-\tau\Delta\cosh(\tau\Delta)\right\}.$$

Since $x\cosh x-\sinh x>0$ for $x>0$, the derivative is strictly negative whenever $T>0$ and $g_1\ne g_2$. Thus for every finite speed $m>0$,

$$e^{\bar g_\pi T}<\Phi_m(T)< {b e^{g_1T}+a e^{g_2T}\over\kappa}.$$

The finite-horizon transform is also strictly convex in speed. Put $x=\tau\Delta$, $u=r/\Delta$ and $f(x)=x\cosh x-\sinh x$; a second differentiation gives

$$\frac{\partial^2\Phi_m(T)}{\partial m^2} ={\kappa^2q^2e^{\bar g_\pi T-r\tau}\over\Delta^4} \left[x f(x)+u\{3f(x)-x^2\sinh x\}\right]>0.$$

For the sign, $C(x)=x^2\sinh x-3f(x)$ satisfies $C(0)=0$ and $C'(x)=xf(x)>0$. For $D(x)=xf(x)-C(x)$, direct simplification gives

$$e^xD(x)=x^2+2x+\tfrac32+(x-\tfrac32)e^{2x},\qquad \{e^xD(x)\}'=2(1+x)-2(1-x)e^{2x}>0.$$

The last sign follows from $\log\{(1+x)/(1-x)\}=2\operatorname{artanh}x>2x$ for $0<x<1$ and is immediate for $x\geq1$. Since $D(0)=0$, this proves $xf-C>0$.

Since $|u|<1$, the bracket is $xf-uC>xf-C>0$. Hence the negative speed derivative increases toward zero: the finite-horizon reduction from faster switching has diminishing magnitude.

The stationary start is essential. With equal transition rates, $g=(0,1)$, $T=1$ and a start in the lower-forcing state, the transform at $m=0.2$ is $1.1255480567$ and its speed derivative is $+0.5459291779$; a fixed nonstationary start can therefore move in the opposite direction.

This theorem is finite-horizon and nonasymptotic, but its scope is exactly a stationary two-state chain with real constant forcing. The certificate checks 64 unequal-rate parameter sets at 13 speeds: its largest normalized transform, derivative and curvature discrepancies are $2.267\times10^{-14}$, $9.687\times10^{-15}$ and $1.958\times10^{-13}$.

The underlying occupation-time law is due to Pedler (1971); the displayed derivative and order are derived directly here, without a priority claim.

Fourier inversion is a separate estimate

For Lewis inversion, if the exact shifted transform and an approximation differ by at most $E(u)$ on $[0,R]$, then stopping the approximate integral at $R$ gives

$$|C-\widehat C_R|\leq\frac{\sqrt{S_0K}e^{-rT}}\pi\left[ \int_0^R\frac{E(u)}{u^2+1/4}\,du+ M_{1/2}\{\pi-2\arctan(2R)\}\right], \qquad M_{1/2}=\mathbb E[e^{X/2}].$$

The second term follows from $|\phi(u-i/2)|\leq M_{1/2}$ and is only $O(R^{-1})$. Consequently a cubic compact-frequency transform error does not by itself imply a cubic price error. An admissible window $R=O(\lambda)$ gives only an $O(\lambda^{-1})$ model-independent tail certificate; the diffusive window $R=O(\sqrt\lambda)$ gives only $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$.

Better price rates need model-specific transform decay. The switched variance-gamma certificate proves the sharper, generator-uniform tail $O(R^{-(2T/\nu_{\max}+1)})$; for its posted parameters this is $O(R^{-5})$.

Other inputs

Time-dependent parameters, such as a Hull–White mean level $\theta(t)$ fitted to a yield curve, change only the functions $g_i$, which the engine then holds as Chebyshev series. A terminal exponential payoff $e^{-\beta x_T}$ changes only the starting value of $B$. A continuous fast factor in place of the chain is treated on its own page.

Scope

The expansion assumes an irreducible fast chain, a fixed horizon $t$ as the switching speeds up, and forcing terms $g_i$ smooth in time. The initial layer is part of the expansion. Under these conditions the certificates observe errors falling by one power of $\varepsilon$ per order.

Maturities that grow with the switching rate are a different limit, governed by the principal eigenvalue on the yield-curve page. For option prices the Fourier integral is truncated where the expansion stops applying, which is a choice of the examples rather than part of the theory.

Code and checks

  • fastswitch.py: the engine, for any irreducible finite chain including defective generators, real or complex coefficients, any terminal vector.
  • models.py and options.py: the $g_i$ for each model, and option prices.
  • verify_engine.py: agreement with the two-state code, and orders 1 to 6 on a three-state chain against a 30-digit numerical solution, and a chain with a Jordan block.
  • verify_constant_forcing.py: exact symmetric- and unequal-rate slow/layer decompositions, radius-one branch geometry, Taylor order, Lewis inversion tail control, and the variance-gamma frequency envelope and polynomial-decay certificate.
  • verify_finite_horizon_order.py: exact stationary two-state finite-horizon speed monotonicity and convexity, the endpoint envelope and a nonstationary counterexample.
  • verify_models.py: Monte Carlo of the switching model for two Gaussian factors, CIR and Vasicek with jumps, and each expansion's convergence.
  • verify_option_mc.py: Monte Carlo over exactly simulated regime paths for Poisson counts, Black–Scholes, Heston and bond options, each path priced in closed form given the path.
  • mc_credit_exact.py: the same for two-name credit.
