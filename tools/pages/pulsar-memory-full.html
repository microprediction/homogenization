Initial-state memory after a timing fit

A sharp continuous-fit boundary law, with explicit bounds and a different fixed-grid limit.

Setup and result

Let a two-state chain have stationary probabilities $\pi_1,\pi_2>0$, total jump rate $\gamma>0$, and spin-down contrast $\Delta\ne0$. Write $g=(\pi_2\Delta,-\pi_1\Delta)$, $q=\Pr(Y_0=1)$, $\eta=q-\pi_1$, $\delta=\pi_2-\pi_1$ and $\Phi(t)=\int_0^t(t-s)g(Y_s)ds$.

Fix $T>0$ and a polynomial degree $m\ge1$. Let $R=I-P_m$ be continuous uniformly weighted orthogonal projection off polynomials through degree $m$ on $[0,T]$. Distinguish total residual mean square from conditional stochastic variance:

$$E_q=\frac1T\mathbb E_q\|R\Phi\|_2^2,\qquad V_q=\frac1T\mathbb E_q\|R(\Phi-\mathbb E_q\Phi)\|_2^2.$$

With $x=\gamma T$, there are nonnegative scalar functions $B_m,N_m$ such that, exactly at every rate,

$$E_q-E_\pi=\frac{\Delta^2\eta\delta}{T}B_m,\qquad E_q-V_q=\frac{\Delta^2\eta^2}{T\gamma^4}N_m.$$

Define $p_4(x)=\sum_{j=0}^4x^j/j!$. The following explicit bounds hold for every $x>0$, without a stationary-start assumption:

$$0\le6-\gamma^5B_m\le a_m(x):=6e^{-x}p_4(x)+\frac{20(m+1)^2}{x},$$ $$0\le\frac12-\gamma N_m\le b_m(x):=\frac{e^{-2x}}2+\frac{(m+1)^2}{x}.$$

Thus, at fixed $m,T,\pi,\Delta$ as $\gamma\to\infty$, uniformly over all starting laws $q\in[0,1]$,

$$\begin{aligned} E_q-E_\pi&=\frac{6\Delta^2\eta\delta}{T\gamma^5}+O(\gamma^{-6}),\\ V_q-E_\pi&=\frac{\Delta^2(6\eta\delta-\eta^2/2)}{T\gamma^5}+O(\gamma^{-6}). \end{aligned}$$

More precisely, the error in the $V_q-E_\pi$ approximation after multiplication by $T\gamma^5/\Delta^2$ is at most $|\eta\delta|a_m(x)+\eta^2b_m(x)$; the total-energy error uses only its first term. The coefficients do not depend on the fitted degree once the fit contains a linear term.

The stationary leading energy is $E_\pi\sim2\pi_1\pi_2\Delta^2T^3c_m/\gamma$, with $c_m=(m+1)/[8(2m-1)(2m+1)(2m+3)(2m+5)]$. Hence the relative starting-law correction is $O(x^{-4})$, sharp for total energy if $\eta\delta\ne0$. At equal occupation, $\delta=0$, total energy is exactly independent of $q$, but variance still differs by the projected squared mean.

Proof and finite-rate remainder

The two-state semigroup gives the raw rate moment and phase mean

$$\mathbb E_q[g(Y_s)g(Y_u)]=\Delta^2\{\pi_1\pi_2e^{-\gamma|s-u|}+\eta\delta e^{-\gamma\max(s,u)}\},$$ $$\mathbb E_q\Phi(t)=\Delta\eta\{t/\gamma-(1-e^{-\gamma t})/\gamma^2\}.$$

Put $k_s(t)=(t-s)_+$ and $h_s(t)=(s-t)_+$. Since $k_s-h_s=t-s$ is removed by $R$, $Rk_s=Rh_s$. Fubini therefore gives the exact nonnegative factors

$$B_m=\int_0^T\!\int_0^T\langle Rh_s,Rh_u\rangle e^{-\gamma\max(s,u)}dsdu, \qquad N_m=\|Re^{-\gamma t}\|_2^2.$$

Nonnegativity of $B_m$ follows from $e^{-\gamma\max(s,u)}=\int_{\max(s,u)}^\infty\gamma e^{-\gamma v}dv$: it is an integral of squared norms of $\int_0^{\min(v,T)}Rh_sds$. Removing the phase mean gives the stated distinction between $E_q$ and $V_q$.

For $0\le u\le s\le T$, $\langle h_s,h_u\rangle=su^2/2-u^3/6$. Its unprojected double integral is exactly

$$2\int_0^T e^{-\gamma s}\int_0^s(su^2/2-u^3/6)duds =\frac6{\gamma^5}\{1-e^{-x}p_4(x)\}.$$

In the orthonormal shifted Legendre basis $\phi_n(t)=\sqrt{(2n+1)/T}\,P_n(2t/T-1)$, $|\langle h_s,\phi_n\rangle|\le\sqrt{(2n+1)/T}\,s^2/2$. Each removed mode has nonnegative integrated energy by the same kernel identity.

Summing the absolute bounds on the removed modes gives at most $(m+1)^2(4T)^{-1}\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty s^2u^2e^{-\gamma\max(s,u)}dsdu =20(m+1)^2/(T\gamma^6)$. This proves the bound for $B_m$.

Similarly $\|e^{-\gamma t}\|_2^2=(1-e^{-2x})/(2\gamma)$ and $|\langle e^{-\gamma t},\phi_n\rangle|\le\sqrt{(2n+1)/T}/\gamma$. Summing squares proves the bound for $N_m$. These are analytic bounds, not fitted rates from numerical data.

A fixed observation grid has a different boundary order

For fixed times $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n\le T$ and positive normalized weights $W$, let $R$ instead be weighted polynomial OLS residualization with full-rank design and degree $m\ge1$. Set $A_0=\|Re_0\|_W^2$, where $e_0$ is the time-zero coordinate vector. For $0\le s<t_1$, $Rk_s=sRe_0$.

Consequently the analogous unnormalized sampled factors satisfy, at fixed design,

$$\gamma^4 B_W\longrightarrow6A_0,\qquad \|R(e^{-\gamma t_i})_i\|_W^2\longrightarrow A_0.$$

The first limit follows by scaling $s,u$ by $\gamma$ and integrating $2\int_0^\infty e^{-s}s\int_0^s u\,duds=6$; the part with $\max(s,u)\ge t_1$ is exponentially small times a polynomial in $\gamma$. Thus sampled total-energy memory is $6\Delta^2\eta\delta A_0/\gamma^4$ to leading order, while projected mean energy is $\Delta^2\eta^2A_0/\gamma^4$. The continuous $\gamma^{-5}$ law cannot be substituted into a fixed discrete trace.

For a quadratic fit on $(0,1/3,2/3,1)$ with equal weights, $A_0=1/80$ and $6A_0=3/40$. If all fixed observation times are strictly positive, then $Rk_s=0$ before the first observation and both starting-law effects are exponentially small instead. These are fixed-design limits, not uniform statements for grids whose first gap shrinks with $1/\gamma$.

Context, checks and scope

The polynomial-projector framework is classical: Lee et al. (2012), Appendix A, explicitly treats the nonstationarity induced by timing fits and uses a Legendre basis. The present result specializes the exact two-state initial-law kernel, not a new general projection principle. It needs neither Gaussian paths nor independent residual samples.

The certificate checks exact rational kernel coefficients through degree six, 42 finite-rate bounds and independent triangular quadrature. It also verifies the fixed-grid constants and checks the existing sampled raw-moment/variance/mean decomposition. Its final line is literal PASS; numerical evaluations are not outward-rounded interval proofs.

The bounds assume a continuous uniformly weighted fit of fixed degree for their continuous conclusion; they do not cover a growing fitted degree, GLS, additional measurement noise or an estimated switching model. Conditional here means a specified initial regime law, not a conformal coverage guarantee. There is no regime-count or maturity-integrated Green–Kubo rank assertion.
