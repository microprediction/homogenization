The initial prior changes the covariance boundary, not its rate

The Green–Kubo covariance rate remains valid for every initial regime prior. Its finite-horizon error has a uniform inverse-speed-squared bound, and an explicit boundary matrix identifies the lasting initial-prior correction.

Setup and the classical boundary coefficient

Let $Q$ be a fixed irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ states, $\pi Q=0$, $P_t=e^{tQ}$ and $H=-Q^\#$. For a centered feature matrix $\pi F=0$, define

$$U=HF,\qquad W=H^2F,\qquad A=F^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)F,\qquad G=\tfrac12\{F^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)U+U^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)F\}.$$

For an initial row distribution $\nu$, put $b_\nu=\nu U$ and

$$B_\nu=F^\top\operatorname{diag}(\nu H)U+U^\top\operatorname{diag}(\nu H)F -F^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)W-W^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)F-b_\nu^\top b_\nu.$$

This is the finite-chain matrix form of a classical Poisson-equation variance correction. For a deterministic start and one observable, it is exactly the coefficient in Awad and Glynn (2007), Theorem 2.8; polarization and total variance give the matrix and arbitrary-prior forms here.

Explicit bounds, uniform over priors and maturities

Choose valid constants $C\ge1$, $\gamma>0$ such that $\|P_t-\mathbf1\pi\|_\infty\le Ce^{-\gamma t}$ for all $t\ge0$, where the norm is the maximum absolute row sum. Let $\pi_* =\min_i\pi_i>0$.

For a chain of generator $mQ$, $m>0$, write $Z_T=\int_0^T F^\top e_{Y_t}dt$, $\Sigma_\nu(T)=\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(Z_T)$ and $s=mT$. For every $\nu$ and $T\ge0$,

$$-\frac{5C^2}{m^2\gamma^2\pi_*}A \preceq\Sigma_\nu(T)-\frac{2T}{m}G \preceq\frac{5C^2}{m^2\gamma^2\pi_*}A,$$ $$-E(s)A\preceq m^2\Sigma_\nu(T)-2sG-B_\nu\preceq E(s)A, \qquad E(s)=\frac{C^3(6+2\gamma s)e^{-\gamma s}}{\gamma^2\pi_*}.$$

The first approximation has an all-maturity $O(m^{-2})$ error uniformly over every prior, including point masses. The second identifies the boundary coefficient with exponential accuracy at fixed positive $T$.

Adding $B_\nu/m^2$ does not improve the uniform inverse-speed order through the near-endpoint scale $T=O(m^{-1})$, and that truncated approximation need not be positive semidefinite there. These are fixed-feature covariance results, not functional limits or rank statements for integrated heterogeneous contract loadings.

The mixing constants can be constructed without diagonalization: choose $t_0$ so that $r=\|P_{t_0}-\mathbf1\pi\|_\infty\in(0,1)$, and take $C=2/r$, $\gamma=-\log r/t_0$. Splitting $t=kt_0+u$ and using $\|P_u-\mathbf1\pi\|_\infty\le2$ proves this envelope.

Proof from exact second moments

Work first at speed one and take centered scalar features $f,h$, with $g=f\odot Hh+h\odot Hf$ and $a_f(s)=\nu(I-P_s)Hf$. Conditional expectation of the later observation and integration of its semigroup give the exact identity

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Cov}_\nu\!\left(\int_0^s f(Y_t)dt,\int_0^s h(Y_t)dt\right) ={}&2sG_{fh}+\nu H(I-P_s)g-a_f(s)a_h(s)\\ &-\int_0^s\nu P_t\{f\odot P_{s-t}Hh+h\odot P_{s-t}Hf\}dt. \end{aligned}$$

Since $\|H\|_\infty\le C/\gamma$ and $H(I-P_s)=\int_0^s(P_t-\mathbf1\pi)dt$, the three correction terms have absolute bounds $2C^2$, $C^2$ and $2C^2$, respectively, times $\|f\|_\infty\|h\|_\infty/\gamma^2$. This proves the first scalar bound.

For the second bound split $\nu P_t=\pi+\nu(P_t-\mathbf1\pi)$ inside the convolution. The stationary part tends to $\pi(f\odot H^2h+h\odot H^2f)$; the other part is bounded by $2C^3s e^{-\gamma s}\|f\|_\infty\|h\|_\infty/\gamma$.

The endpoint error in $\nu H(I-P_s)g$ contributes at most $2C^3$, the stationary-convolution tail at most $2C^3$, and replacing $a_f(s)a_h(s)$ by $(\nu Hf)(\nu Hh)$ at most $2C^3$, times $e^{-\gamma s}\|f\|_\infty\|h\|_\infty/\gamma^2$. Their sum is the stated $6+2\gamma s$ envelope.

Finally set $f=h=Fx$ and use $\|Fx\|_\infty^2\le x^\top A x/\pi_*$. Rescaling time by $m$ proves both matrix inequalities.

Mixtures and a checkable example

The boundary is not linear in the prior. Its exact mixture identity is

$$B_\nu=\sum_i\nu_i B_{e_i} +U^\top\operatorname{diag}(\nu)U-(\nu U)^\top(\nu U).$$

The two terms after the sum form the covariance, across starting states, of the Poisson corrector $U$. Under stationarity $B_\pi=-F^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)W-W^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)F$, recovering the stationary boundary in the identification note.

For the unit-flip symmetric two-state chain and $f=(1,-1)$, $G=1/2$ and

$$B_\pi=-1/2,\qquad B_{e_+}=-3/4.$$

Thus using the stationary boundary for a specified start misses a genuine $-1/(4m^2)$ term at fixed positive maturity, even though the leading variance rate agrees.

Certificate and scope

The certificate checks 192 prior/horizon matrix enclosures, mixture identities, and exact conditional means on four chains, including a defective generator and a sparse directed cycle.

Four independent semigroup-moment quadratures and the stationary closed formula agree with the lifted matrix-exponential moments within $2.56\times10^{-15}$. This observed discrepancy is reported, not used as a sub-floor tolerance.

The largest observed utilization of the conservative first bound is $0.009083756$. The final line is literal PASS; no Gaussian approximation or stationarity assumption is used in the enclosures.

The certificate uses floating-point matrix exponentials, not interval-validated mixing constants. The analytic bounds require a valid envelope; the semigroup construction above supplies one in exact arithmetic.
