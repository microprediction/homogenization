Second-order arbitrary-prior prices

A finite chain contributes exact mean layers and spectral cumulant tensors.

For one or several fields on a finite irreducible chain, keeping the exact mean boundary and the next occupation cumulants gives a second-order price formula whose remainder is $O(m^{-3})$. The bound is uniform over every initial regime prior at fixed positive maturity.

The finite-chain theorem

Let $Q$ be a fixed irreducible finite row generator with invariant row vector $\pi$, and let $f$ be a scalar state field. Write $\bar f=\pi f$, $g=f-\bar f\mathbf1$, and

$$A_m=\int_0^T g(Y_t)dt,\qquad X_m=\bar f+\frac{A_m}{T},$$

where $Y$ has generator $mQ$ and arbitrary initial row law $\nu$. If $\lambda(z)$ and $P(z)$ are the principal eigenvalue and spectral projection of $Q+z\operatorname{diag}(g)$, set

$$\lambda_2=\lambda''(0),\quad \lambda_3=\lambda'''(0),\qquad b_j(\nu)=\left.\frac{d^j}{dz^j}\log\{\nu P(z)\mathbf1\}\right|_{z=0}.$$

The exact integrated-mean correction, with $Q^#$ the group inverse, is

$$\mu_m(\nu)=\mathbb E_\nu A_m =\frac1m\nu(e^{mQT}-I)Q^\# g.$$

If $T>0$ is fixed and $C$ has six continuous derivatives on the interval containing the values of $f$, then

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\nu C(X_m)={}&C(\bar f)+\frac{\mu_m(\nu)}{T}C'(\bar f) +\frac{\lambda_2}{2mT}C''(\bar f)\\ &+\frac1{m^2T^2}\Big[ \tfrac12\{b_2(\nu)+b_1(\nu)^2\}C''(\bar f) +\tfrac16\{\lambda_3+3\lambda_2b_1(\nu)\}C'''(\bar f)\\ &\hspace{43mm}+\tfrac18\lambda_2^2C''''(\bar f)\Big] +O_{Q,f,T,C}(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

The remainder is uniform over the entire prior simplex, including speed-dependent priors $\nu_m$. The statement fixes $Q$, $f$, $T$ and $C$; it is not uniform as $T$ tends to zero or infinity.

Why these four coefficients suffice

Analytic spectral perturbation gives, uniformly over $\nu$,

$$\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{zA_m} =mT\lambda(z/m)+\log\{\nu P(z/m)\mathbf1\}+O(e^{-cmT}).$$

After retaining the first cumulant exactly, the resulting raw moments are

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb EA_m^2&=\frac{T\lambda_2}{m} +\frac{b_2+b_1^2}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb EA_m^3&=\frac{T(\lambda_3+3\lambda_2b_1)}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb EA_m^4&=\frac{3T^2\lambda_2^2}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}),\qquad \mathbb EA_m^5,\ \mathbb EA_m^6=O(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

Taylor expansion through degree five proves the formula, with the sixth moment controlling the remainder. Compactness of the prior simplex makes the spectral-amplitude bounds uniform.

Several observables and a joint payoff

Let $f=(f^1,\ldots,f^d)$ be a fixed vector of state fields, put $g^a=f^a-\pi f^a$, and let $C$ be a scalar $C^6$ function on a neighborhood of the convex hull of their values. For the multivariate tilt $Q+\operatorname{diag}(z_ag^a)$, write

$$\Gamma_{ab}=\partial_{ab}\lambda(0),\quad \Lambda_{abc}=\partial_{abc}\lambda(0),\quad \beta_a=\left.\partial_a\log\{\nu P(z)\mathbf1\}\right|_{z=0},\quad B_{ab}=\left.\partial_{ab}\log\{\nu P(z)\mathbf1\}\right|_{z=0},$$

and let $\mu_m^a=\mathbb E_\nu\int_0^T(f^a(Y_t)-\pi f^a)dt$ be exact. With repeated indices summed,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\nu C(X_m)={}&C(\bar f)+\frac{\mu_m^a}{T}C_a +\frac{\Gamma_{ab}}{2mT}C_{ab}\\ &+\frac1{m^2T^2}\Big[ \tfrac12(B_{ab}+\beta_a\beta_b)C_{ab} +\tfrac16(\Lambda_{abc}+\Gamma_{ab}\beta_c+\Gamma_{ac}\beta_b +\Gamma_{bc}\beta_a)C_{abc}\\ &\hspace{26mm}+\tfrac1{24}(\Gamma_{ab}\Gamma_{cd} +\Gamma_{ac}\Gamma_{bd}+\Gamma_{ad}\Gamma_{bc})C_{abcd}\Big] +O(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

All payoff derivatives are evaluated at $\bar f=\pi f$. The proof is the same cumulant-to-moment partition as in the scalar case; the tensor formula reduces exactly to that theorem along every direction in observable space.

Affine-coordinate covariance

Let $A$ be nonsingular, $c$ be constant, and change observable coordinates by $f'=fA+\mathbf1c^\top$, so $x'=A^\top x+c$. If $C'(x')=C(A^{-\top}(x'-c))$, then the exact price and every displayed asymptotic term are unchanged.

Indeed $\mu'_m=A^\top\mu_m$, $\Gamma'=A^\top\Gamma A$, $\beta'=A^\top\beta$, $B'=A^\top BA$, and $\Lambda'_{ijk}=A_{ai}A_{bj}A_{ck}\Lambda_{abc}$. The inverse Jacobian in each payoff-derivative index cancels these factors term by term, including all three fourth-moment pairings.

This is covariance under a change of coordinates, not a new source of latent directions. It does not change fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank and makes no claim about rank after integrating maturity-dependent loadings.

Projection and redundant coordinates

The same conclusion does not require a square or invertible map. For any $A\in\mathbb R^{d\times k}$, put $f'=fA+\mathbf1c^\top$ and let $D$ be a payoff on $\mathbb R^k$. Computing the expansion for $D$ in the mapped coordinates gives exactly the expansion for $C(x)=D(A^\top x+c)$ in the original coordinates.

Each cumulant-tensor index is multiplied by $A$, while the chain rule multiplies each derivative index by the same $A$, so the contractions agree without an inverse. This covers projection to fewer observables and embedding into redundant coordinates.

In particular $\Gamma'=A^\top\Gamma A$ and $\operatorname{rank}\Gamma'\le\operatorname{rank}\Gamma$. Adding redundant coordinates cannot increase the fixed-feature rank; this remains separate from integrating different maturity loadings.

Exact reduction to the feature rank

Let $g=f-\mathbf1\bar f^\top$ have rank $r$. Choose a centered full-column-rank matrix $U\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}$ and a full-column-rank matrix $R\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}$ such that $g=UR^\top$. For every path, its averaged feature vector has the form $X_m=\bar f+RZ_m$, where $Z_m$ is the corresponding average of $U$.

Therefore an arbitrary payoff $C$ on the original $d$ coordinates reduces exactly to $D(z)=C(\bar f+Rz)$ on $r$ coordinates. The exact prices agree pathwise, and the rectangular-map theorem shows that every term of the second-order expansion agrees as well. If $r=0$, the path is constant and the price is simply $C(\bar f)$.

The fixed-feature theorem gives $\Gamma_U\succ0$ and $\Gamma_f=R\Gamma_UR^\top$, hence $\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_f=r$. This is a minimal-coordinate statement for one fixed feature family, not a claim about rank after maturity-dependent loading integration.

Affine maps are the exact boundary

Let $K$ be the convex hull of the state vectors and let $\psi:K\to\mathbb R^k$. The identity

$$\psi\!\left(\sum_i p_if_i\right)=\sum_i p_i\psi(f_i) \qquad\text{for every probability vector }p$$

holds if and only if $\psi$ is affine on $K$. Indeed the identity applied to convex combinations of two barycentric representations makes $\psi$ Jensen-affine on every segment of $K$; the converse is immediate.

For an irreducible finite chain, attainable occupation vectors are dense in the probability simplex. Hence a continuous transformation commuting with every path average must be affine on $K$.

Nonlinear preprocessing changes the statistic at leading order: as switching accelerates, the average of $\psi(f(Y_t))$ converges to $\pi\psi(f)$, whereas applying $\psi$ after averaging converges to $\psi(\pi f)$. Their generally nonzero Jensen gap is order one, not another term in the fixed-maturity $1/m$ expansion.

Nonlinear maps after averaging

There is nevertheless a precise delta formula for post-averaging transformations. Let $Z_m=\psi(X_m)$ for a $C^6$ map $\psi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^k$, and write $J^i_a=\partial_a\psi^i(\bar f)$ and $H^i_{ab}=\partial_{ab}\psi^i(\bar f)$.

Uniformly over every initial regime prior, at fixed positive maturity,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E Z_m^i&=\psi^i(\bar f)+J^i_a\frac{\mu_m^a}{T} +\frac{\Gamma_{ab}H^i_{ab}}{2mT}+O(m^{-2}),\\ \operatorname{Cov}(Z_m)_{ij}&=\frac{J^i_a\Gamma_{ab}J^j_b}{mT}+O(m^{-2}). \end{aligned}$$

The first identity is the vector price theorem applied componentwise. Applying it to $\psi^i\psi^j$ and subtracting the two component means cancels the mean-layer and curvature terms, leaving $J\Gamma J^\top$ at first order.

Consequently a singular Jacobian can lower the tangent covariance rank, and a critical map with $J=0$ has covariance only at order $m^{-2}$. This local delta-method rank is neither the exact fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor a rank obtained by integrating maturity-dependent loadings.

The critical-map covariance

When $J=0$, the first nonzero covariance coefficient is explicit. With the same fixed-parameter and fixed-positive-maturity scope, uniformly over initial priors,

$$\operatorname{Cov}(Z_m)_{ij} =\frac{H^i_{ab}\Gamma_{ac}\Gamma_{bd}H^j_{cd}}{2m^2T^2}+O(m^{-3}).$$

Covariance is unchanged by subtracting $\psi(\bar f)$, after which each transformed component begins with the quadratic form $H^i_{ab}(X_m-\bar f)_a(X_m-\bar f)_b/2$. The fourth-moment pairings in the vector price theorem give the displayed coefficient, while subtracting the component means removes the disconnected pairing.

The coefficient is the covariance of Gaussian quadratic forms and is positive semidefinite. Its rank is a second-order property of the chosen critical statistic, not the fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank and not a maturity-integrated loading rank.

The two-state correlation reduction

Let the calm-to-crisis and crisis-to-calm rates be $ma$ and $mb$. Put

$$r=a+b,\quad p_1=\frac ar,\quad p_0=\frac br,\quad u=p_0p_1,\quad d=p_0-p_1,\quad \delta=\eta-p_1,\quad \Delta\rho=\rho_1-\rho_0,$$

where $\eta=\mathbb P(Y_0=1)$ and $\bar\rho=p_0\rho_0+p_1\rho_1$. Define the exact mean boundary

$$b_m(\eta)=\Delta\rho\,\delta\frac{1-e^{-mrT}}{mrT}.$$

If $T>0$ is fixed and $C$ has six continuous derivatives on the interval between $\rho_0$ and $\rho_1$, then, uniformly over $\eta\in[0,1]$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\eta C(\hat\rho_T)={}&C(\bar\rho)+b_m(\eta)C'(\bar\rho) +\frac{u(\Delta\rho)^2}{mrT}C''(\bar\rho)\\ &+\frac{1}{m^2r^2T^2}\Big[(\Delta\rho)^2(\delta d-u)C''(\bar\rho) +u(\Delta\rho)^3(d+\delta)C'''(\bar\rho)\\ &\hspace{43mm}+\tfrac12u^2(\Delta\rho)^4C''''(\bar\rho)\Big] +O_{T,C}(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

Here $\lambda_2=2u(\Delta\rho)^2/r$, $\lambda_3=6ud(\Delta\rho)^3/r^2$ and $b_1=\Delta\rho\,\delta/r$. Substitution into the finite-chain theorem gives the displayed formula, including its uniformity for a prior $\eta=\eta_m$ that changes with speed.

Direct two-state moment check

Write $A_m=\int_0^T(\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=1\}}-p_1)dt$, so $\hat\rho_T-\bar\rho=\Delta\rho A_m/T$. The exact arbitrary-start occupation cumulants give, uniformly in $\eta$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E A_m&=\delta\frac{1-e^{-mrT}}{mr},\\ \mathbb E A_m^2&=\frac{2uT}{mr}+\frac{2(\delta d-u)}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^3&=\frac{6u(d+\delta)T}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^4&=\frac{12u^2T^2}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^5&=O(m^{-3}),\qquad \mathbb E A_m^6=O(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

The second line combines the stationary covariance integral with its exact endpoint correction. In the third line, the $d$ term is the stationary third occupation cumulant and the $\delta$ term is the product of the leading variance and mean boundary; the fourth line is the three-pair contribution $3(2uT/(mr))^2$.

The finite-state spectral cumulant expansion controls orders five and six uniformly because the initial-prior coefficients are continuous on the compact simplex; analyticity follows from Kato's spectral projection perturbation theory. Taylor expansion of $C(\bar\rho+\Delta\rho A_m/T)$ through degree five, with the sixth derivative bounding the remainder, gives the theorem.

Exchange-option implementation

For Margrabe's price $M(V)$, let $k=\log(S_1/(qS_2))$ and

$$h(V)=\frac{k^2}{2V^2}-\frac1{2V}-\frac18.$$

Starting from $M'(V)=S_1\phi(d_1)/(2\sqrt V)$, repeated differentiation gives

$$M''=hM',\qquad M'''=(h^2+h')M',\qquad M''''=(h^3+3hh'+h'')M'.$$

Since $V(\rho)$ is affine, $C^{(j)}(\rho)=(-2\sigma_1\sigma_2T)^jM^{(j)}(V(\rho))$. This supplies the variance, skew and kurtosis terms without numerical differentiation.

Numerical certificate

For two observables on the same nonreversible three-state chain, five priors and four joint exponential payoffs, the maximum error falls from $2.88061785\times10^{-4}$ at speed $4$ to $6.46190448\times10^{-8}$ at speed $64$. The last-doubling order is $3.008710$, and directional reduction to the scalar implementation is exact in binary64.

An independent joint-moment ODE tests the genuinely mixed payoff $C(x,y)=x^2y^2$. Its maximum error falls from $4.52863079\times10^{-4}$ at speed $4$ to $1.25201763\times10^{-7}$ at speed $64$, with last-doubling order $2.988710$.

Three non-orthogonal affine coordinate changes, five priors and five speeds preserve the complete vector approximation with maximum discrepancy $2.60\times10^{-18}$. This tests the mean, boundary, covariance, skew and fourth-pairing transformation laws simultaneously.

A one-dimensional projection and a rank-two embedding into three redundant coordinates preserve nonlinear degree-four prices with maximum discrepancy $1.11\times10^{-16}$. The projected and embedded tests use all five priors and speeds.

The same cases reproduce the Green–Kubo congruence with maximum discrepancy $1.11\times10^{-16}$ and mapped ranks one and two. Thus the certificate checks both the nonlinear price reduction and its fixed-feature rank statement.

For $\psi(x,y)=(x^2,xy)$ on the certificate's three state vectors, the stationary Jensen gap is exactly $(5227/16900,-17/130)$. The rational-vector check has binary64 discrepancy $5.55\times10^{-17}$ and shows that nonlinear state preprocessing fails before any finite-rate correction is considered.

For the post-averaging map $\psi(x,y)=((x-\bar x)^2,y)$, whose Jacobian has rank one, the exact joint-moment ODE gives mean errors $5.01528999\times10^{-3}$ and $1.95909768\times10^{-5}$ at speeds $4$ and $64$. The corresponding tangent-covariance errors fall from $2.87245875\times10^{-3}$ to $1.28871522\times10^{-5}$, with last-doubling orders $2.000000$ and $1.987508$.

For its critical first component $(x-\bar x)^2$, the predicted variance is $2\Gamma_{11}^2/(m^2T^2)$. The exact variance error falls from $1.07961924\times10^{-3}$ at speed $4$ to $2.98170334\times10^{-7}$ at speed $64$, with last-doubling order $2.988799$.

On a nonreversible three-state chain, five priors and four exponential payoffs, the matrix-exponential benchmark has maximum errors $4.25236975\times10^{-4}$, $5.50834536\times10^{-5}$, $6.99926229\times10^{-6}$, $8.81798420\times10^{-7}$ and $1.10648302\times10^{-7}$ at speeds $4,8,16,32,64$. The last-doubling order is $2.994468$.

The certificate uses 21 equally spaced initial priors, all nine displayed exchange strikes, and speeds $m=4,8,16,32,64$. The maximum error falls from $5.36315810\times10^{-5}$ to $1.22709523\times10^{-8}$, with last-doubling order $3.006199$.

Independent arbitrary-precision differentiation checks the first four variance derivatives with maximum normalized error $2.59\times10^{-14}$. The observed order supports the implementation; the moment argument above proves the fixed-maturity rate.

Literature boundary

Kato's perturbation theory supplies the analytic eigenvalue and spectral-projection expansion. Yin and Zhang give the broader two-time-scale finite-chain framework; the displayed scalar price coefficients are derived directly here.

The tensor moment partitions are an elementary instance of the classical semi-invariant calculus of Leonov and Shiryaev. The multivariate price contraction is derived directly here, with no priority claim for moment-cumulant identities.

The sharp affine boundary is the equality form of the barycentric identity underlying Jensen's original convexity paper. Its application to finite-state occupation averages is proved directly above.

The transformed covariance $J\Gamma J^\top$ is the standard multivariate delta-method covariance; see van der Vaart, Chapter 3. The curvature bias and uniform finite-chain remainder stated here follow directly from the spectral price theorem above.

The critical coefficient uses the classical covariance identity for Gaussian quadratic forms; see Magnus (1978). The finite-chain $O(m^{-3})$ remainder and its uniformity over starting priors are proved here rather than imported from that Gaussian calculation.

The atom-plus-Bessel occupation law is classical; see Pedler (1971) and Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir (2010). The displayed price expansion follows directly from those occupation moments and Taylor's theorem; no priority claim is made for the occupation law.
