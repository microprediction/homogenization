Second-order arbitrary-prior prices

A finite chain contributes exact mean layers and spectral cumulant tensors.

For one or several fields on a finite irreducible chain, keeping the exact mean boundary and the next occupation cumulants gives a second-order price formula whose remainder is $O(m^{-3})$. The bound is uniform over every initial regime prior at fixed positive maturity.

The finite-chain theorem

Let $Q$ be a fixed irreducible finite row generator with invariant row vector $\pi$, and let $f$ be a scalar state field. Write $\bar f=\pi f$, $g=f-\bar f\mathbf1$, and

$$A_m=\int_0^T g(Y_t)dt,\qquad X_m=\bar f+\frac{A_m}{T},$$

where $Y$ has generator $mQ$ and arbitrary initial row law $\nu$. If $\lambda(z)$ and $P(z)$ are the principal eigenvalue and spectral projection of $Q+z\operatorname{diag}(g)$, set

$$\lambda_2=\lambda''(0),\quad \lambda_3=\lambda'''(0),\qquad b_j(\nu)=\left.\frac{d^j}{dz^j}\log\{\nu P(z)\mathbf1\}\right|_{z=0}.$$

The exact integrated-mean correction, with $Q^#$ the group inverse, is

$$\mu_m(\nu)=\mathbb E_\nu A_m =\frac1m\nu(e^{mQT}-I)Q^\# g.$$

If $T>0$ is fixed and $C$ has six continuous derivatives on the interval containing the values of $f$, then

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\nu C(X_m)={}&C(\bar f)+\frac{\mu_m(\nu)}{T}C'(\bar f) +\frac{\lambda_2}{2mT}C''(\bar f)\\ &+\frac1{m^2T^2}\Big[ \tfrac12\{b_2(\nu)+b_1(\nu)^2\}C''(\bar f) +\tfrac16\{\lambda_3+3\lambda_2b_1(\nu)\}C'''(\bar f)\\ &\hspace{43mm}+\tfrac18\lambda_2^2C''''(\bar f)\Big] +O_{Q,f,T,C}(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

The remainder is uniform over the entire prior simplex, including speed-dependent priors $\nu_m$. The statement fixes $Q$, $f$, $T$ and $C$; it is not uniform as $T$ tends to zero or infinity.

Why these four coefficients suffice

Analytic spectral perturbation gives, uniformly over $\nu$,

$$\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{zA_m} =mT\lambda(z/m)+\log\{\nu P(z/m)\mathbf1\}+O(e^{-cmT}).$$

After retaining the first cumulant exactly, the resulting raw moments are

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb EA_m^2&=\frac{T\lambda_2}{m} +\frac{b_2+b_1^2}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb EA_m^3&=\frac{T(\lambda_3+3\lambda_2b_1)}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb EA_m^4&=\frac{3T^2\lambda_2^2}{m^2}+O(m^{-3}),\qquad \mathbb EA_m^5,\ \mathbb EA_m^6=O(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

Taylor expansion through degree five proves the formula, with the sixth moment controlling the remainder. Compactness of the prior simplex makes the spectral-amplitude bounds uniform.

Several observables and a joint payoff

Let $f=(f^1,\ldots,f^d)$ be a fixed vector of state fields, put $g^a=f^a-\pi f^a$, and let $C$ be a scalar $C^6$ function on a neighborhood of the convex hull of their values. For the multivariate tilt $Q+\operatorname{diag}(z_ag^a)$, write

$$\Gamma_{ab}=\partial_{ab}\lambda(0),\quad \Lambda_{abc}=\partial_{abc}\lambda(0),\quad \beta_a=\left.\partial_a\log\{\nu P(z)\mathbf1\}\right|_{z=0},\quad B_{ab}=\left.\partial_{ab}\log\{\nu P(z)\mathbf1\}\right|_{z=0},$$

and let $\mu_m^a=\mathbb E_\nu\int_0^T(f^a(Y_t)-\pi f^a)dt$ be exact. With repeated indices summed,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\nu C(X_m)={}&C(\bar f)+\frac{\mu_m^a}{T}C_a +\frac{\Gamma_{ab}}{2mT}C_{ab}\\ &+\frac1{m^2T^2}\Big[ \tfrac12(B_{ab}+\beta_a\beta_b)C_{ab} +\tfrac16(\Lambda_{abc}+\Gamma_{ab}\beta_c+\Gamma_{ac}\beta_b +\Gamma_{bc}\beta_a)C_{abc}\\ &\hspace{26mm}+\tfrac1{24}(\Gamma_{ab}\Gamma_{cd} +\Gamma_{ac}\Gamma_{bd}+\Gamma_{ad}\Gamma_{bc})C_{abcd}\Big] +O(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

All payoff derivatives are evaluated at $\bar f=\pi f$. The proof is the same cumulant-to-moment partition as in the scalar case; the tensor formula reduces exactly to that theorem along every direction in observable space.

Affine-coordinate covariance

Let $A$ be nonsingular, $c$ be constant, and change observable coordinates by $f'=fA+\mathbf1c^\top$, so $x'=A^\top x+c$. If $C'(x')=C(A^{-\top}(x'-c))$, then the exact price and every displayed asymptotic term are unchanged.

Indeed $\mu'_m=A^\top\mu_m$, $\Gamma'=A^\top\Gamma A$, $\beta'=A^\top\beta$, $B'=A^\top BA$, and $\Lambda'_{ijk}=A_{ai}A_{bj}A_{ck}\Lambda_{abc}$. The inverse Jacobian in each payoff-derivative index cancels these factors term by term, including all three fourth-moment pairings.

This is covariance under a change of coordinates, not a new source of latent directions. It does not change fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank and makes no claim about rank after integrating maturity-dependent loadings.

Projection and redundant coordinates

The same conclusion does not require a square or invertible map. For any $A\in\mathbb R^{d\times k}$, put $f'=fA+\mathbf1c^\top$ and let $D$ be a payoff on $\mathbb R^k$. Computing the expansion for $D$ in the mapped coordinates gives exactly the expansion for $C(x)=D(A^\top x+c)$ in the original coordinates.

Each cumulant-tensor index is multiplied by $A$, while the chain rule multiplies each derivative index by the same $A$, so the contractions agree without an inverse. This covers projection to fewer observables and embedding into redundant coordinates.

In particular $\Gamma'=A^\top\Gamma A$ and $\operatorname{rank}\Gamma'\le\operatorname{rank}\Gamma$. Adding redundant coordinates cannot increase the fixed-feature rank; this remains separate from integrating different maturity loadings.

Exact reduction to the feature rank

Let $g=f-\mathbf1\bar f^\top$ have rank $r$. Choose a centered full-column-rank matrix $U\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}$ and a full-column-rank matrix $R\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}$ such that $g=UR^\top$. For every path, its averaged feature vector has the form $X_m=\bar f+RZ_m$, where $Z_m$ is the corresponding average of $U$.

Therefore an arbitrary payoff $C$ on the original $d$ coordinates reduces exactly to $D(z)=C(\bar f+Rz)$ on $r$ coordinates. The exact prices agree pathwise, and the rectangular-map theorem shows that every term of the second-order expansion agrees as well. If $r=0$, the path is constant and the price is simply $C(\bar f)$.

The fixed-feature theorem gives $\Gamma_U\succ0$ and $\Gamma_f=R\Gamma_UR^\top$, hence $\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_f=r$. This is a minimal-coordinate statement for one fixed feature family, not a claim about rank after maturity-dependent loading integration.

Affine maps are the exact boundary

Let $K$ be the convex hull of the state vectors and let $\psi:K\to\mathbb R^k$. The identity

$$\psi\!\left(\sum_i p_if_i\right)=\sum_i p_i\psi(f_i) \qquad\text{for every probability vector }p$$

holds if and only if $\psi$ is affine on $K$. Indeed the identity applied to convex combinations of two barycentric representations makes $\psi$ Jensen-affine on every segment of $K$; the converse is immediate.

For an irreducible finite chain, attainable occupation vectors are dense in the probability simplex. Hence a continuous transformation commuting with every path average must be affine on $K$.

Nonlinear preprocessing changes the statistic at leading order: as switching accelerates, the average of $\psi(f(Y_t))$ converges to $\pi\psi(f)$, whereas applying $\psi$ after averaging converges to $\psi(\pi f)$. Their generally nonzero Jensen gap is order one, not another term in the fixed-maturity $1/m$ expansion.

Nonlinear maps after averaging

There is nevertheless a precise delta formula for post-averaging transformations. Let $Z_m=\psi(X_m)$ for a $C^6$ map $\psi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^k$, and write $J^i_a=\partial_a\psi^i(\bar f)$ and $H^i_{ab}=\partial_{ab}\psi^i(\bar f)$.

Uniformly over every initial regime prior, at fixed positive maturity,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E Z_m^i&=\psi^i(\bar f)+J^i_a\frac{\mu_m^a}{T} +\frac{\Gamma_{ab}H^i_{ab}}{2mT}+O(m^{-2}),\\ \operatorname{Cov}(Z_m)_{ij}&=\frac{J^i_a\Gamma_{ab}J^j_b}{mT}+O(m^{-2}). \end{aligned}$$

The first identity is the vector price theorem applied componentwise. Applying it to $\psi^i\psi^j$ and subtracting the two component means cancels the mean-layer and curvature terms, leaving $J\Gamma J^\top$ at first order.

Consequently a singular Jacobian can lower the tangent covariance rank, and a critical map with $J=0$ has covariance only at order $m^{-2}$. This local delta-method rank is neither the exact fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor a rank obtained by integrating maturity-dependent loadings.

The critical-map covariance

When $J=0$, the first nonzero covariance coefficient is explicit. With the same fixed-parameter and fixed-positive-maturity scope, uniformly over initial priors,

$$\operatorname{Cov}(Z_m)_{ij} =\frac{H^i_{ab}\Gamma_{ac}\Gamma_{bd}H^j_{cd}}{2m^2T^2}+O(m^{-3}).$$

Covariance is unchanged by subtracting $\psi(\bar f)$, after which each transformed component begins with the quadratic form $H^i_{ab}(X_m-\bar f)_a(X_m-\bar f)_b/2$. The fourth-moment pairings in the vector price theorem give the displayed coefficient, while subtracting the component means removes the disconnected pairing.

The coefficient is the covariance of Gaussian quadratic forms and is positive semidefinite. Its rank is a second-order property of the chosen critical statistic, not the fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank and not a maturity-integrated loading rank.

The sharp critical rank ceiling

Let $r=\operatorname{rank}\Gamma$ and restrict $\Gamma$ to its range. For $S_i=\Gamma^{1/2}H^i\Gamma^{1/2}$, the critical coefficient $K$ is the Gram matrix

$$K_{ij}=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(S_iS_j),\qquad \operatorname{rank}K=\dim\operatorname{span}\{S_1,\ldots,S_k\} \le \min\!\left\{k,\frac{r(r+1)}2\right\}.$$

This bound is sharp for fixed $\Gamma$. Equip the $r(r+1)/2$-dimensional space of symmetric matrices with inner product $\langle A,B\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(AB)/2$; any positive-semidefinite target Gram matrix of admissible rank gives matrices $S_i$, and $H^i=\Gamma^{+1/2}S_i\Gamma^{+1/2}$ on the range realizes them. Here $\Gamma^{+1/2}$ denotes the Moore–Penrose inverse square root.

In particular, when $r=2$, the three critical coordinates $(x_1-\bar f_1)^2$, $(x_2-\bar f_2)^2$ and $(x_1-\bar f_1)(x_2-\bar f_2)$ can have rank-three leading covariance. This order-$m^{-2}$ local rank can exceed $r$ without changing the fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank, and it says nothing about maturity-integrated loadings.

Higher-order degeneracy

Work in the exact $r$-dimensional reduction $X_m=\bar f+RW_m$, so the Green–Kubo matrix $\Gamma_r$ of $W_m$ is positive definite. Let $p\ge1$, let $\phi(w)=\psi(\bar f+Rw)$ be $C^{p+1}$ on a neighborhood of the attainable feature polytope, and suppose $D^j\phi(0)=0$ for $1\le j<p$.

For $G\sim N(0,\Gamma_r)$ define the homogeneous Gaussian polynomials $P_i(G)=D^p\phi_i(0)[G^{\otimes p}]/p!$ and $K^{(p)}_{ij}=\operatorname{Cov}(P_i(G),P_j(G))$. At fixed positive $T$, uniformly over initial priors,

$$m^pT^p\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(W_m))\longrightarrow K^{(p)},\qquad \operatorname{rank}K^{(p)}\le \min\!\left\{k,{r+p-1\choose p}\right\}.$$

The finite-chain central limit theorem and moment convergence give $\sqrt{mT}W_m\Rightarrow G$ uniformly over the prior simplex. Taylor's formula then gives $(mT)^{p/2}(\phi_i(W_m)-\phi_i(0))=P_i(\sqrt{mT}W_m)+o_{L^2}(1)$; the bounded finite-state rewards and exponential mixing control the remainder uniformly.

Gaussian covariance is an inner product on the space of nonconstant homogeneous degree-$p$ polynomials. That space is isomorphic to the symmetric $p$-tensors and has dimension ${r+p-1\choose p}$, so $K^{(p)}$ is their Gram matrix; choosing a polynomial basis attains the ceiling and proves sharpness.

The cases $p=1$ and $p=2$ recover the tangent and Hessian-Gram results. This theorem concerns one fixed map, feature family and positive maturity; it gives an $o(m^{-p})$ covariance remainder, not a uniform statement as $T\downarrow0$ or an $O(m^{-p-1})$ rate.

The derivative flag and all covariance scales

A $C^{P+1}$ vector map can first vary at different orders in different output directions. For $1\le p\le P+1$ define $V_p=\{a\in\mathbb R^k:a^\top D^j\phi(0)=0\text{ for }1\le j<p\}$ and $d_p=\dim V_p-\dim V_{p+1}$, and suppose $V_{P+1}=\{0\}$.

Choose an orthonormal output basis adapted to this descending flag. For each basis vector $e_{p,a}$ of $V_p\ominus V_{p+1}$ put $P_{p,a}(G)=e_{p,a}^\top D^p\phi(0)[G^{\otimes p}]/p!$, let $K$ be the covariance Gram matrix of all these polynomials, and set

$$D_m=\operatorname{diag}\big((mT)^{-p/2}I_{d_p}:1\le p\le P\big).$$

Uniformly over initial priors, in that adapted basis,

$$\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(W_m))=D_m\{K+o(1)\}D_m,\qquad \lambda_{d_1+\cdots+d_{p-1}+a}=\Theta((mT)^{-p}),\quad 1\le a\le d_p.$$

The leading polynomials are linearly independent: within one degree this follows from the definition of $V_{p+1}$, and across degrees from homogeneity. Thus $K\succ0$; a block Cholesky factorization has uniformly bounded triangular factors after conjugation by $D_m$, which proves the eigenvalue orders.

Each multiplicity satisfies $d_p\le {r+p-1\choose p}$. Nested Schur complements of $K$ give the leading coefficients; raw degrees of the same parity need not be orthogonal, so replacing those complements by diagonal blocks is generally wrong.

These are local post-averaging covariance scales for one fixed map and feature family at fixed positive maturity. They do not alter exact fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank and make no maturity-integrated rank claim.

The complete smooth spectrum trichotomy

For a $C^\infty$ map let $V_\infty=\bigcap_{p\ge1}V_p$ and let $C=\{a\in\mathbb R^k:a^\top\phi\text{ is constant on the attainable polytope}\}$. Write $q=\dim V_\infty$ and $c=\dim C$; always $C\subseteq V_\infty$.

The derivative flag restricted to $V_\infty^\perp$ reaches zero after finitely many orders. If the covariance eigenvalues are decreasing and $d_p=\dim V_p-\dim V_{p+1}$ before stabilization, then the polynomial part remains

$$\lambda_{d_1+\cdots+d_{p-1}+a}=\Theta((mT)^{-p}),\qquad 1\le a\le d_p.$$

The remaining spectrum has a sharp qualitative classification: uniformly over initial priors, for every fixed $N$ and fixed $T>0$,

$$\lambda_{k-q+1},\ldots,\lambda_{k-c}=o((mT)^{-N}),\qquad \lambda_{k-c+1}=\cdots=\lambda_k=0.$$

An empty index range is omitted. Thus a smooth covariance direction is polynomial at a finite derivative order, positive but faster than every power because it is flat, or exactly zero because the map is constant in that output direction.

To prove the super-polynomial bound, let $P_\infty$ project onto $V_\infty$. Taylor's theorem gives, for every $L$, $\|P_\infty\{\phi(w)-\phi(0)\}\|\le C_L\|w\|^L$ near zero. Finite-state additive-functional moments and spectral-gap concentration give, uniformly over priors,

$$\mathbb E\|W_m\|^{2L}=O((mT)^{-L}),\qquad \mathbb P(\|W_m\|>\delta)\le C_\delta e^{-c_\delta mT}.$$

Choosing $L>N$ proves $\|P_\infty\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(W_m))P_\infty\|=o((mT)^{-N})$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the same super-polynomial control on the cross block, so the finite derivative block keeps its polynomial eigenvalues.

For an irreducible finite chain, every initial state gives the full attainable occupation polytope as support at positive time. Hence the covariance null space is exactly $C$: the $c$ constant directions are zero and the other $q-c$ flat eigenvalues are positive at every finite speed, though no common lower asymptotic rate is claimed.

The spectral concentration input is classical; see Lezaud (2001). The trichotomy is local to a fixed post-averaging map and fixed positive maturity, not a change in fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank or a maturity-integrated rank claim.

The Denjoy–Carleman boundary and envelope

Let $M_0=1$ and let $(M_n)$ be a positive log-convex sequence. Suppose a smooth map on a connected neighborhood of the attainable polytope satisfies $\sup_w\|D^n\phi(w)\|\le AB^nM_n$, and define its normalized associated flat envelope by

$$h_M(t)=\inf_{n\ge0}\frac{t^nM_n}{n!}.$$

Every positive-order derivative of $P_\infty\phi$ vanishes at zero. The integral Taylor remainder at arbitrary order therefore gives $\|P_\infty\{\phi(w)-\phi(0)\}\|\le C h_M(B\|w\|)$ near zero.

Splitting at $\|W_m\|\le r$ and applying finite-chain concentration proves, uniformly over initial priors and for each fixed $T>0$,

$$\|P_\infty\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(W_m))P_\infty\| \le C\inf_{0<r<r_0}\left\{h_M(Br)^2+e^{-c mTr^2}\right\}.$$

The constants and the admissible radius depend on the fixed map and chain. This is an upper envelope, not a claim that every sequence or map attains the bound.

The Denjoy–Carleman theorem makes the regularity boundary exact: this class is quasianalytic precisely when

$$\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{M_{n-1}}{M_n}=\infty.$$

In that case a scalar projection flat at one point is constant on the connected neighborhood, so $V_\infty=C$ and every nonzero covariance eigenvalue occurs at a finite derivative order. When the series converges, the class admits nonzero compactly supported, hence flat, functions; nonconstant flat covariance is then possible, with its rate controlled by $h_M$ rather than by convergence alone.

Denjoy (1921) initiated the quasianalytic criterion, and Carleman (1926) completed the characterization. In this convention $M_n=n!$ gives the divergent harmonic series, while $M_n=(n!)^s$ is nonquasianalytic for $s>1$.

A logarithmic class reaches the near-analytic edge

A concrete sequence resolves how abruptly nonconstant flat covariance can appear. For $\sigma>0$, set $M_0=1$ and define the increasing quotients

$$\frac{M_n}{M_{n-1}}=n[\log(e+n)]^\sigma.$$

The sequence is log-convex, and the Denjoy–Carleman series becomes $\sum_{n\ge1}\{n[\log(e+n)]^\sigma\}^{-1}$. It diverges exactly for $0<\sigma\le1$, when every flat direction is constant, and converges for $\sigma>1$, when nonconstant flat functions are permitted.

Write $m_n=M_n/n!$. Since $m_n/m_{n-1}=[\log(e+n)]^\sigma$, the consecutive terms in $h_M(t)=\inf_n t^nm_n$ have ratio $t[\log(e+n)]^\sigma$. The minimizing index and cost therefore satisfy

$$n_t\sim\exp(t^{-1/\sigma}),\qquad -\log h_M(t)\sim\sigma t^{1/\sigma}\exp(t^{-1/\sigma}).$$

Indeed, at the minimizer the logarithmic sum is asymptotic to $-\sigma\operatorname{li}(n_t)$; the last display follows from $\operatorname{li}(x)\sim x/\log x$. Taking $r=B^{-1}[\log(mT)]^{-\sigma}$ in the general envelope gives, for $\sigma>1$ and all sufficiently large $mT$,

$$\|P_\infty\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(W_m))P_\infty\| \le C\exp\left\{-\frac{c\,mT}{[\log(mT)]^{2\sigma}}\right\}.$$

This upper bound is faster than $e^{-c_\beta(mT)^\beta}$ for every fixed $\beta<1$, but it is not claimed sharp. Thus the first nonquasianalytic logarithmic classes sit strictly between the Gevrey powers and analytic collapse; the constants still depend on the fixed map, chain and positive maturity. This is not a fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank or maturity-integrated rank statement.

The logarithmic derivative classes already appear in Denjoy's original note. The associated-function asymptotic and covariance specialization above are derived here from the quotient sequence.

An explicit logarithmic-class flat map

The preceding upper envelope need not be attained by a particular map. For $\sigma>1$, put $a=1/(\sigma-1)$ and define

$$\phi_\sigma(w)= \begin{cases}\exp\{-\exp(|w|^{-a})\},&w\ne0,\\0,&w=0.\end{cases}$$

This is a nonconstant flat member of the logarithmic class above: there are $A,B<\infty$ such that

$$\sup_{|w|\le1}|\phi_\sigma^{(n)}(w)|\le AB^nM_n, \qquad \frac{M_n}{M_{n-1}}=n[\log(e+n)]^\sigma.$$

For small positive $w$, write $u=w^{-a}$. On a complex disk of radius $c\,w/u$, the real part of $\exp(z^{-a})$ is at least $c_0e^u$, so Cauchy's estimate bounds the $n$th derivative by

$$n!C^n u^{\sigma n}e^{-c_0e^u} \le n!C_1^n[\log(e+n)]^{\sigma n}\le C_2^nM_n.$$

The last comparison follows because $n\log\log(e+n)-\sum_{j\le n}\log\log(e+j)=O(n/\log(e+n))$; compact sets away from zero are analytic. Reflection handles negative $w$, while the double exponential makes the extension at zero flat.

Let $W_m$ be the unit-time average of the stationary symmetric telegraph chain switching at rate $m$. Its exact atom-plus-Bessel law has local rate $m\{1-\sqrt{1-w^2}\}=mw^2/2+O(mw^4)$, and for each fixed $q>0$ the moment saddle is

$$q e^u+\frac m2u^{-2(\sigma-1)},\qquad q e^u=m(\sigma-1)u^{-(2\sigma-1)}.$$

Thus $u/\log m\to1$ and the first term is smaller than the second by the factor $2(\sigma-1)/u$. Direct Laplace bounds from the exact density give

$$-\log\mathbb E[\phi_\sigma(W_m)^q] \sim\frac{m}{2(\log m)^{2(\sigma-1)}}.$$

The squared mean has twice this leading log cost and is negligible beside the second moment. Consequently,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\phi_\sigma(W_m)) \sim\frac{m}{2(\log m)^{2(\sigma-1)}}.$$

This explicit rate is faster than the general $m/[\log m]^{2\sigma}$ envelope, so it does not establish sharpness of that envelope. It instead realizes nonconstant flat covariance with a near-exponential decay throughout the nonquasianalytic logarithmic range.

Thilliez (2003) constructs sharp associated-rate flat functions under strong regularity. The normalized sequence here fails the strong nonquasianalytic tail condition by one logarithm, so that theorem does not supply this example; the direct Cauchy estimate above does.

Asymmetry changes the constant, not the logarithmic power

Let a two-state chain jump from $0$ to $1$ at rate $m\alpha$ and back at rate $m\beta$, and put

$$P_{m,T}=\frac1T\int_0^T\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=1\}}\,dt,\qquad p_* =\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta},\qquad Z_{m,T}=P_{m,T}-p_*.$$

For $b>0$, replace the preceding map by $\phi_{\sigma,b}(z)=\exp\{-\exp(b|z|^{-1/(\sigma-1)})\}$ away from zero and set its value at zero to zero. This rescaling remains in the same logarithmic Denjoy–Carleman class.

Writing $L=mT$, the exact two-state occupation density has interior rate

$$I(p)=\left(\sqrt{\alpha(1-p)}-\sqrt{\beta p}\right)^2,\qquad I(p_*+z)=\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^3}{4\alpha\beta}z^2+O(z^3).$$

For every fixed $q>0$, set $u=b|z|^{-1/(\sigma-1)}$. The two moment saddles minimize

$$q e^u+L I\!\left(p_*\mathbin{\pm}b^{\sigma-1}u^{-(\sigma-1)}\right).$$

The minimizing $u$ satisfies $u/\log L\to1$. The quadratic rate therefore supplies the leading cost, while the cubic remainder is smaller by a factor $(\log L)^{-(\sigma-1)}$; the exact Bessel prefactor is subexponential on this scale.

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\phi_{\sigma,b}(Z_{m,T})) \sim \frac{(\alpha+\beta)^3b^{2(\sigma-1)}}{4\alpha\beta} \frac{mT}{[\log(mT)]^{2(\sigma-1)}}.$$

The squared mean has twice the displayed leading cost. The result is uniform over the initial two-state prior: each starting-state density has the same interior exponential rate, and arbitrary priors are their mixtures.

Thus asymmetry and the flat-map scale $b$ can change the leading constant arbitrarily, while the class-sharp logarithmic power is stable. This remains a scalar post-averaging statement and makes no Green–Kubo or maturity-integrated rank claim.

The fixed-map logarithmic power is sharp

The associated-function envelope is uniform once the derivative constants are fixed, but it is not the best eventual estimate for one fixed flat function. The quotient-defined sequence above is exponentially equivalent to the simpler sequence $\widetilde M_n=n![\log(e+n)]^{\sigma n}$:

$$C^{-n}\widetilde M_n\le M_n\le\widetilde M_n.$$

Let $f$ be a fixed scalar member of this class on $[-1,1]$, with every derivative of $f-f(0)$ zero at the origin. Specializing Bang's fixed-function estimate gives constants $c_f,C_f,\delta_f>0$ such that

$$|f(w)-f(0)|\le C_f\exp\{-\exp(c_f|w|^{-1/(\sigma-1)})\}, \qquad0<|w|<\delta_f.$$

Indeed, the distance tail in Bang's argument is

$$S_p=\sum_{n\ge p}\frac{M_n}{M_{n+1}} \sim\frac{1}{(\sigma-1)(\log p)^{\sigma-1}}.$$

His propagation inequality gives $|w|\lesssim S_p\Rightarrow|f(w)-f(0)|\lesssim e^{-p}$; inversion yields the displayed double exponential. The endpoint formulation follows locally by multiplying by a same-class cutoff equal to one near zero, which exists because $\sigma>1$ is nonquasianalytic.

For the unit-time symmetric telegraph average under any initial prior, split at $r_m\asymp(\log m)^{-(\sigma-1)}$. The fixed-function bound controls the inner event and the telegraph concentration bound controls its complement, giving

$$\operatorname{Var}(f(W_m)) \le C\exp\left\{-\frac{c\,m}{(\log m)^{2(\sigma-1)}}\right\}.$$

The explicit map above has the matching log-asymptotic power, so $2(\sigma-1)$ is sharp for fixed scalar maps in this class; the constants are not universal. The constants and valid neighborhood in Bang's estimate depend on the particular function, so this does not replace the uniform class extremal by the stronger scale.

Gaisin and Gaisin (2023) emphasize precisely this distinction: the fixed-function estimate has exponent $1/(\sigma-1)$, whereas promoting it to the extremal $J_M$ is invalid. This reconciles the stronger fixed-map covariance rate with the one-sided associated envelope above.

Gevrey regularity gives a sharp flat rate

The general envelope becomes explicit when $\phi$ is Gevrey of order $s>1$. Suppose on a neighborhood of the attainable polytope that, for constants $A,B$,

$$\sup_w\|D^n\phi(w)\|\le AB^n(n!)^s,\qquad n\ge0.$$

Here $M_n=(n!)^s$, so $h_M(t)=\inf_n t^n(n!)^{s-1}$. Stirling optimization in the general envelope gives

$$\|P_\infty\{\phi(w)-\phi(0)\}\| \le C\exp\{-c\|w\|^{-1/(s-1)}\}.$$

Split according to $\|W_m\|\le r$ and use the finite-chain concentration bound $\mathbb P(\|W_m\|>r)\le C_0e^{-c_0mTr^2}$. Balancing the two exponents at $r\asymp(mT)^{-(s-1)/(2s-1)}$ proves, uniformly over initial priors,

$$\|P_\infty\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(W_m))P_\infty\| \le C_1\exp\{-c_1(mT)^{1/(2s-1)}\}.$$

Only the exponent is universal; the constants depend on the map and chain. The exponent is sharp: with $\alpha=1/(s-1)$, the scalar map $\phi_s(w)=e^{-1/|w|^\alpha}$ away from zero and $\phi_s(0)=0$ is Gevrey-$s$, and the exact telegraph law gives

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\phi_s(W_m))\sim C_{\alpha,2}m^{\alpha/(\alpha+2)} =C_{\alpha,2}m^{1/(2s-1)}.$$

A Cauchy estimate on a complex disk of radius proportional to $|w|$ proves the Gevrey derivative bound for this example; optimizing in $|w|$ gives $(n!)^{1+1/\alpha}$. The class and terminology originate with Gevrey (1918).

Thus $s\downarrow1$ approaches the analytic boundary, where nonconstant flat directions disappear, while $s\to\infty$ loses a positive universal exponent, consistently with the arbitrarily slow $C^\infty$ scales above. This remains a fixed-map, fixed-positive-maturity statement.

Analytic completeness and the smooth flat boundary

Suppose now that $\phi$ is real analytic on a connected open set containing the attainable feature polytope, and put $V_\infty=\bigcap_{p\ge1}V_p$. Then

$$V_\infty=\{a\in\mathbb R^k:a^\top\phi\text{ is constant on the attainable polytope}\}.$$

Indeed $a\in V_\infty$ exactly when every positive-order derivative of $a^\top\phi$ vanishes at zero. Its Taylor series is therefore constant near zero, and the analytic identity theorem extends that constant to the connected domain.

On $V_\infty^\perp$ the descending finite-dimensional flag has zero intersection, so it reaches zero after finitely many orders. Thus the derivative-flag theorem exhausts every nonzero covariance eigenvalue of an analytic map; directions in $V_\infty$ have exactly zero covariance.

This conclusion fails for a merely smooth map. Let a stationary symmetric telegraph chain take values $\pm1$, switch at rate $m$, and set $W_m=\int_0^1Y_t\,dt$ and

$$\phi(w)=\begin{cases}\exp(-1/w^2),&w\ne0,\\0,&w=0.\end{cases}$$

The map is $C^\infty$ and every derivative at zero vanishes, although it is not locally constant. The exact occupation law has atoms $e^{-m}/2$ at $\pm1$ and density

$$f_m(w)=\frac{me^{-m}}2\left[I_0\!\left(m\sqrt{1-w^2}\right) +\frac{I_1\!\left(m\sqrt{1-w^2}\right)}{\sqrt{1-w^2}}\right], \qquad |w|<1.$$

Its Bessel asymptotics give, for every $q>0$,

$$\lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac{1}{\sqrt m}\log\mathbb E\phi(W_m)^q=\sqrt{2q}, \qquad \lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac{1}{\sqrt m}\log\operatorname{Var}(\phi(W_m))=2.$$

For the first limit, insert the Bessel density and rescale $w=m^{-1/4}y$. Its exponential cost becomes $\sqrt m\{q/y^2+y^2/2}+O(1)$, whose minimum is $\sqrt{2qm}$; the endpoint atoms cost order $m$ and are negligible. The variance follows because the squared mean has rate $2\sqrt2>2$.

Hence the covariance is stretched exponential, smaller than every $m^{-p}$ but not zero. This is a local post-averaging phenomenon for a fixed smooth map; the underlying scalar feature still has nonzero fixed-feature Green–Kubo variance, and no maturity-integrated rank claim is involved.

A continuum of flat-map scales

The example is not isolated. For any $\alpha>0$, replace the flat map by $\phi_\alpha(w)=\exp(-1/|w|^\alpha)$ away from zero and set $\phi_\alpha(0)=0$. This extension is $C^\infty$, and the same exact telegraph law gives

$$\lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac{1}{m^{\alpha/(\alpha+2)}} \log\mathbb E\phi_\alpha(W_m)^q =C_{\alpha,q},\qquad C_{\alpha,q}=\frac{\alpha+2}{2}\alpha^{-\alpha/(\alpha+2)}q^{2/(\alpha+2)}.$$

Indeed the rescaling $w=m^{-1/(\alpha+2)}y$ leaves the cost $m^{\alpha/(\alpha+2)}\{q/y^\alpha+y^2/2\}$, whose unique minimum is $C_{\alpha,q}$. The Bessel prefactor is subexponential on this scale, while fixed nonzero occupations and the endpoint atoms cost order $m$.

Since $2C_{\alpha,1}>C_{\alpha,2}$, the second moment dominates the squared mean and

$$\lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac{1}{m^{\alpha/(\alpha+2)}} \log\operatorname{Var}(\phi_\alpha(W_m))=C_{\alpha,2}.$$

As $\alpha$ ranges over $(0,\infty)$, the power $\alpha/(\alpha+2)$ ranges over $(0,1)$. Smooth flat post-averaging maps therefore realize every stretched-exponential covariance power, beyond the finite derivative flag but without changing the fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank.

A slower log-flat scale

Even the stretched-exponential continuum is not the boundary. For $\gamma>1$ define, on $|w|\le1$,

$$\psi_\gamma(w)=\begin{cases} \exp\{-\log(e/|w|)^\gamma\},&w\ne0,\\ 0,&w=0. \end{cases}$$

This is $C^\infty$ and flat at zero because $\log(e/|w|)^\gamma$ dominates every fixed multiple of $\log(1/|w|)$. For the same telegraph average and every $q>0$,

$$\lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac{\log\mathbb E\psi_\gamma(W_m)^q}{(\log m)^\gamma} =\frac{q}{2^\gamma},\qquad \lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac{\log\{(\mathbb E\psi_\gamma(W_m))^2/ \mathbb E\psi_\gamma(W_m)^2\}}{(\log m)^{\gamma-1}} =\frac{\gamma\log2}{2^{\gamma-1}}.$$

The second limit is essential: the first-order moment exponents tie, but the squared mean is still negligible. Consequently

$$\lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac{\log\operatorname{Var}(\psi_\gamma(W_m))}{(\log m)^\gamma} =2^{1-\gamma}.$$

To prove both limits, let $r_q$ solve $2r_q+(\gamma-1)\log r_q=\log m+2-\log(q\gamma)$. The exact Bessel density, after $x=\sqrt{m}\,w$, is Gaussian uniformly on the polylogarithmic saddle window, and one-dimensional Laplace bounds give

$$-\log\mathbb E\psi_\gamma(W_m)^q =q\left(r_q^\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{2}r_q^{\gamma-1}\right)+O(\log\log m).$$

Now $r_q\sim(\log m)/2$ and $r_2=r_1-(\log2)/2+o(1)$. Substitution gives the two displayed constants; this is the slowly varying saddle mechanism studied classically by de Bruijn (1959), specialized here directly to the exact telegraph law.

For $\gamma=2$ at speed $10^{256}$, exact log-domain quadrature gives variance rate $0.493127$ against $0.5$ and mean-separation rate $0.687013$ against $\log2=0.693147$. The maximum absolute saddle-cost error over the four tested speeds is $0.345647$.

This covariance is faster than every polynomial and slower than $\exp(-m^\beta)$ for every $\beta>0$. It remains a fixed-map post-averaging scale, not a fixed-feature or maturity-integrated rank statement.

Arbitrarily slow super-polynomial scales

The log-power family is still not a lower boundary. Let $\ell$ be positive, smooth and slowly varying at infinity, with $\ell(r)\to\infty$ and $r^j\ell^{(j)}(r)/\ell(r)\to0$ for every $j\ge1$, and put $H(r)=r\ell(r)$.

Define $\chi_\ell(w)=\exp\{-H(\log(e/|w|))\}$ away from zero and $\chi_\ell(0)=0$. Then $\chi_\ell$ is smooth and flat at zero, and for $L=\log m$ the exact telegraph law gives

$$-\log\mathbb E\chi_\ell(W_m)^q\sim qH(L/2),\qquad -\log\frac{(\mathbb E\chi_\ell(W_m))^2}{\mathbb E\chi_\ell(W_m)^2} \sim(\log2)H'(L/2),$$ $$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\chi_\ell(W_m)) \sim2H(L/2)=L\ell(L/2).$$

For the proof, let $r_q$ solve $2r_q+\log(qH'(r_q))=L+2$. Uniform Bessel asymptotics on the saddle window and direct Laplace bounds give

$$-\log\mathbb E\chi_\ell(W_m)^q =q\{H(r_q)+H'(r_q)/2\}+o(H'(r_q)).$$

Smooth slow variation implies $r_q\sim L/2$, $H(r_q)\sim H(L/2)$ and $r_2-r_1=-(\log2)/2+o(1)$. Expanding the two saddle costs proves the mean-separation formula, so the squared mean is negligible and the second moment supplies the variance scale.

Because a slowly varying function can diverge arbitrarily slowly, there is no slowest nonzero smooth-flat covariance scale above polynomial decay. For example, $\ell(r)=\log r$ gives $-\log\operatorname{Var}\sim L\log(L/2)$; iterated logarithms move still closer to a power law.

The certificate uses $\ell(r)=(\log r)^2$. At speed $10^{256}$, the variance rate normalized by $L\{\log(L/2)\}^2$ is $0.996613$ against $1$, while mean separation normalized by $H'(L/2)$ is $0.684524$ against $\log2=0.693147$; the maximum saddle-cost error over four speeds is $0.345358$.

This regular-variation step follows the framework introduced by Karamata (1933) and the Laplace-transform refinement of de Bruijn (1959). The exact telegraph constants are derived here.

Two covariance scales

Use the exact minimal reduction above to write $X_m=\bar f+RW_m$, where $W_m$ is $r$-dimensional and has positive-definite Green–Kubo matrix $\Gamma_r$. Put $\phi(w)=\psi(\bar f+Rw)$, $A=D\phi(0)$ and $s=\operatorname{rank}A$.

Choose an orthogonal output basis $U=(U_1,U_0)$ whose columns $U_0$ span $\ker A^\top$, set $A_1=U_1^\top A$, and let $K_0$ be the critical Hessian-Gram coefficient for $U_0^\top\phi$. Uniformly over initial priors, at fixed positive maturity,

$$U^\top\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(W_m))U= \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{A_1\Gamma_rA_1^\top}{mT}+O(m^{-2})&O(m^{-2})\\[2mm] O(m^{-2})&\dfrac{K_0}{m^2T^2}+O(m^{-3}) \end{pmatrix}.$$

If $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ are the positive eigenvalues of $A\Gamma_rA^\top$ and $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_{k-s}$ are those of $K_0$, both in decreasing order, then the covariance eigenvalues obey

$$\lambda_j=\frac{a_j}{mT}+O(m^{-2}),\qquad \lambda_{s+j}=\frac{\kappa_j}{m^2T^2}+O(m^{-3}).$$

The upper block follows from the ordinary delta covariance, the lower block from the critical theorem, and the cross block is $O(m^{-2})$. In the small-eigenvalue Schur complement its contribution is only $O(m^{-3})$ because the inverse upper block is $O(m)$.

Thus $s$ tangent directions appear at order $m^{-1}$ and $q=\operatorname{rank}K_0\le\min\{k-s,r(r+1)/2\}$ further directions can appear at order $m^{-2}$. These are asymptotic covariance scales for one fixed feature family, not exact Green–Kubo or maturity-integrated ranks.

The two-state correlation reduction

Let the calm-to-crisis and crisis-to-calm rates be $ma$ and $mb$. Put

$$r=a+b,\quad p_1=\frac ar,\quad p_0=\frac br,\quad u=p_0p_1,\quad d=p_0-p_1,\quad \delta=\eta-p_1,\quad \Delta\rho=\rho_1-\rho_0,$$

where $\eta=\mathbb P(Y_0=1)$ and $\bar\rho=p_0\rho_0+p_1\rho_1$. Define the exact mean boundary

$$b_m(\eta)=\Delta\rho\,\delta\frac{1-e^{-mrT}}{mrT}.$$

If $T>0$ is fixed and $C$ has six continuous derivatives on the interval between $\rho_0$ and $\rho_1$, then, uniformly over $\eta\in[0,1]$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\eta C(\hat\rho_T)={}&C(\bar\rho)+b_m(\eta)C'(\bar\rho) +\frac{u(\Delta\rho)^2}{mrT}C''(\bar\rho)\\ &+\frac{1}{m^2r^2T^2}\Big[(\Delta\rho)^2(\delta d-u)C''(\bar\rho) +u(\Delta\rho)^3(d+\delta)C'''(\bar\rho)\\ &\hspace{43mm}+\tfrac12u^2(\Delta\rho)^4C''''(\bar\rho)\Big] +O_{T,C}(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

Here $\lambda_2=2u(\Delta\rho)^2/r$, $\lambda_3=6ud(\Delta\rho)^3/r^2$ and $b_1=\Delta\rho\,\delta/r$. Substitution into the finite-chain theorem gives the displayed formula, including its uniformity for a prior $\eta=\eta_m$ that changes with speed.

Direct two-state moment check

Write $A_m=\int_0^T(\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=1\}}-p_1)dt$, so $\hat\rho_T-\bar\rho=\Delta\rho A_m/T$. The exact arbitrary-start occupation cumulants give, uniformly in $\eta$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E A_m&=\delta\frac{1-e^{-mrT}}{mr},\\ \mathbb E A_m^2&=\frac{2uT}{mr}+\frac{2(\delta d-u)}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^3&=\frac{6u(d+\delta)T}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^4&=\frac{12u^2T^2}{m^2r^2}+O(m^{-3}),\\ \mathbb E A_m^5&=O(m^{-3}),\qquad \mathbb E A_m^6=O(m^{-3}). \end{aligned}$$

The second line combines the stationary covariance integral with its exact endpoint correction. In the third line, the $d$ term is the stationary third occupation cumulant and the $\delta$ term is the product of the leading variance and mean boundary; the fourth line is the three-pair contribution $3(2uT/(mr))^2$.

The finite-state spectral cumulant expansion controls orders five and six uniformly because the initial-prior coefficients are continuous on the compact simplex; analyticity follows from Kato's spectral projection perturbation theory. Taylor expansion of $C(\bar\rho+\Delta\rho A_m/T)$ through degree five, with the sixth derivative bounding the remainder, gives the theorem.

Exchange-option implementation

For Margrabe's price $M(V)$, let $k=\log(S_1/(qS_2))$ and

$$h(V)=\frac{k^2}{2V^2}-\frac1{2V}-\frac18.$$

Starting from $M'(V)=S_1\phi(d_1)/(2\sqrt V)$, repeated differentiation gives

$$M''=hM',\qquad M'''=(h^2+h')M',\qquad M''''=(h^3+3hh'+h'')M'.$$

Since $V(\rho)$ is affine, $C^{(j)}(\rho)=(-2\sigma_1\sigma_2T)^jM^{(j)}(V(\rho))$. This supplies the variance, skew and kurtosis terms without numerical differentiation.

The sharp affine boundary is the equality form of the barycentric identity underlying Jensen's original convexity paper. Its application to finite-state occupation averages is proved directly above.

The transformed covariance $J\Gamma J^\top$ is the standard multivariate delta-method covariance; see van der Vaart, Chapter 3. The curvature bias and uniform finite-chain remainder stated here follow directly from the spectral price theorem above.

The critical coefficient uses the classical covariance identity for Gaussian quadratic forms; see Magnus (1978). The finite-chain $O(m^{-3})$ remainder and its uniformity over starting priors are proved here rather than imported from that Gaussian calculation.

The arbitrary-order Gaussian coefficient is evaluated with the product-moment formula of Isserlis (1918). The uniform finite-chain delta limit and the sharp symmetric-tensor rank ceiling are derived directly here.

Itô's multiple-integral construction supplies the classical orthogonal Gaussian-chaos decomposition. The derivative flag here is kept in raw homogeneous coordinates, where same-parity degrees can correlate and the displayed Schur complements are necessary.

The atom-plus-Bessel occupation law is classical; see Pedler (1971) and Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir (2010). The displayed price expansion, smooth-flat counterexample and asymmetric logarithmic constant follow directly from that law; no priority claim is made for the occupation density.

The logarithmic minimization is a direct one-dimensional Laplace argument. Its large-deviation context goes back to Varadhan (1966); the constants here are derived from the exact telegraph density rather than imported from a general principle.

The slower log-flat example uses the slowly oscillating Laplace-transform framework of de Bruijn (1959) as literature context. Its leading and mean-separation constants are derived directly from the exact density.

Lezaud's spectral-gap concentration bound supplies the classical exponential tail control for finite-state Markov additive functionals. Combined with all-order Taylor remainders, it yields the super-polynomial flat-block estimate in the smooth spectrum trichotomy.
