Exact ranks for Poisson-refresh regimes

Exponential-age calibration permits arbitrary continuous score laws on a reset chain, not only a single CDF contrast.

Let $\pi$ be a strictly positive probability row on a finite state space and consider the stationary chain

$$Q_0=c(\mathbf1\pi-I),\qquad c>0.$$

Equivalently, at each event of an independent rate-$c$ Poisson process the regime is drawn afresh from $\pi$. These refreshes include draws equal to the previous state; their clock is not the observed state-change clock. Given the chain and sampling times, score emissions are independent with arbitrary fixed continuous state-specific CDFs $F_i$.

The exact rank rule

Choose $\beta>0$ and independently draw chronological gaps $D_j\sim\operatorname{Exp}(j\beta)$, $1\le j\le N$, where the argument is a rate. Use scores at $0,D_1,\ldots,D_1+\cdots+D_{N-1}$ for calibration and the next score at $D_1+\cdots+D_N$ for testing. If $Q_k$ is the $k$th calibration order statistic, then

$$\boxed{\Pr(S_{\rm test}\le Q_k)=\frac{k}{N+1},\qquad 1\le k\le N.}$$

This averages over the stationary chain, scores and randomized schedule. The sampling rule does not require $c$, $\pi$ or the $F_i$, but exactness requires the reset structure. There is no restriction to one CDF contrast, two states or large switching speed.

The classical Dirichlet occupation law supplies exchangeability

Move the test time to zero by stationarity and define the exponentially weighted occupation vector

$$W_i=\beta\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta a}\mathbf1_{\{Y_{-a}=i\}}\,da,\qquad \alpha=c/\beta.$$

The classical reset-chain occupation law gives

$$W\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\alpha\pi),\qquad W\mid Y_0=i\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\alpha\pi+e_i),\qquad \Pr(Y_0=i\mid W)=W_i.$$

For a constructive proof, backward Poisson inter-refresh intervals $E_j$ are iid $\operatorname{Exp}(c)$, independent of iid state marks $Z_j\sim\pi$. Their normalized exponential weights are the stick-breaking weights with fractions $V_j=1-e^{-\beta E_j}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,\alpha)$, so the resulting occupation vector is Dirichlet. This is the construction of Sethuraman (1994).

The first mark is the current state and $W=V_1e_{Z_1}+(1-V_1)W'$, with an independent Dirichlet remainder $W'$. Given $Z_1=i$, Dirichlet aggregation gives the displayed parameter increment; the density ratio to the unconditioned Dirichlet law is $W_i/\pi_i$, giving the posterior identity. The same joint stationary law is explicit in Herbach (2019), Proposition 4.4.

Now draw labeled calibration ages iid $\operatorname{Exp}(\beta)$, independently of the full process. Given the past chain, the labeled calibration scores are iid with mixture CDF $F_W=\sum_iW_iF_i$, and the test CDF given $W$ is the same by the posterior identity. Their joint law conditional on $W$ is therefore iid and continuous, proving the rank rule.

The reversed exponential order-statistic spacings have chronological rates $\beta,2\beta,\ldots,N\beta$. Sorting the calibration labels does not alter $Q_k$; the chronologically ordered score vector itself need not be exchangeable.

An explicit budget for approximate resets

Let the actual stationary generator $Q$ have the same $\pi$, and compare it with $Q_0=c(\mathbf1\pi-I)$ using

$$\delta=\tfrac12\|Q-Q_0\|_\infty,\qquad \|A\|_\infty=\max_i\sum_j|A_{ij}|.$$

For the same sampling protocol and continuous emission laws,

$$\left|\Pr_Q(S_{\rm test}\le Q_k)-\frac{k}{N+1}\right| \le 1-\prod_{j=1}^N\frac{j\beta}{j\beta+\delta} \le\frac{\delta H_N}{\beta},\qquad H_N=\sum_{j=1}^N\frac1j.$$

Start both chains in the same state drawn from $\pi$, uniformize at a common rate $M$, and couple their transitions maximally while their states agree. The per-step disagreement probability is at most $\delta/M$, so disagreement before a fixed span $T$ has probability at most $1-e^{-\delta T}$. Average over the independent sum of exponential gaps and couple equal-state emissions to prove the bound.

This is a deterministic generator-perturbation budget with a shared stationary law, not a statistical estimate of $\delta$ or a fast-switching truncation error. Evaluating it requires a justified comparison with a reset generator.

Boundary, certificate and scope

Reversibility alone does not suffice. For $Q=\tfrac16\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-5&1&4\\1&-5&4\\4&4&-8\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, uniform $\pi$, state-$i$ emissions uniform on $[2i,2i+1]$, $i=0,1,2$, $\beta=1/2$ and $N=k=2$, exact coverage is $1621/2430=2/3+1/2430$. This is a failure of exactness, not a failure of a conservative one-sided guarantee.

The certificate verifies 1,512 exact chronological count/test identities against the Dirichlet mixture and 342 independent multi-contrast Gaussian rank masses. It also checks 12 approximate-reset budgets on nonreversible perturbations and the rational counterexample.

The theorem is stationary marginal coverage over the randomized schedule, not coverage conditional on realized gaps, calibration scores or current state. It does not cover overlapping path scores, same-panel score refitting, arbitrary non-reset chains or anytime calibration reuse. Dirichlet occupation laws and stick-breaking are classical; the result here is their rank-calibration specialization.
