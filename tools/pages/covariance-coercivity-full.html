A quantitative covariance floor at every horizon

Jump paths prove positivity; conditional bridges make the lower bound grow with time.

The exact finite-horizon feature covariance has the same kernel for every starting prior. The following explicit bound makes that rank statement quantitative, without stationarity, reversibility, diagonalizability or a lower bound on prior masses.

The all-prior, all-horizon bound

Let $Q$ be an irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ states, with stationary row $\pi$, and let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, $m>0$. Let $D$ be the directed diameter of the positive-rate graph and define

$$a=\max_i(-Q_{ii}),\qquad \rho=\frac{\min_{i\ne j:\,Q_{ij}>0}Q_{ij}}a\in(0,1],\qquad c_Q=\frac{e^{-2}}{32}\left\{\frac{(\rho/12)^D}{D!}\right\}^{\!2}.$$

For a centered feature matrix $F\in\mathbb R^{n\times d}$, $\pi F=0$, put $Z_T=\int_0^T F^\top e_{Y_t}dt$ and $s=maT$. For every initial law $\nu$ and every $T>0$,

$$\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(Z_T)\succeq \frac{4c_Q}{(ma)^2}\min\{s^{D+2},s\}\, F^\top\operatorname{diag}(\pi)F.$$

In particular its kernel is exactly $\ker F$. For $s\ge1$ this is an explicit $T/m$ lower bound; below one it is a conservative short-time bound. The constant is deliberately small; the short-time diameter exponent is optimal over all priors and features, as the graph-distance result below proves.

This is a covariance for fixed state features, not a Green–Kubo matrix and not a covariance after heterogeneous maturity loadings have been integrated. It strengthens the finite-horizon rank theorem without changing any regime-count interpretation.

Scalar proof in normalized time

Rescale to generator $Q/a$, whose exit rates are at most one and positive edge rates at least $\rho$. It suffices to show, for every real state function $f$ with range $r_f=\max f-\min f$,

$$\operatorname{Var}_\nu\!\left(\int_0^s f(Y_t)dt\right) \ge c_Qr_f^2\min\{s^{D+2},s\}.$$

Constant $f$ is immediate. Otherwise, for every initial state $i$, choose $j$ with $|f_j-f_i|\ge r_f/2$ and a directed path from $i$ to $j$ of length $1\le k\le D$.

A short interval

Suppose $0<s\le1$. The no-jump path has probability at least $e^{-s}$ and integral $sf_i$.

The event of following the chosen path with all $k$ jumps in $[0,s/6]$, and no other jumps before $s$, has probability at least $e^{-s}\rho^k(s/6)^k/k!$. Its integral differs from $sf_i$ by at least $sr_f/4$: time outside $j$ is at most $s/6$.

For two independent copies of the integral, use $\operatorname{Var}A=\mathbb E(A-A')^2/2$ and both orders of these disjoint events. Hence for every $i$,

$$\operatorname{Var}_i A\ge \frac{e^{-2}}{16}\frac{(\rho/6)^D}{D!}\,r_f^2s^{D+2} \ge c_Qr_f^2s^{D+2}.$$

Total variance extends this bound to every prior $\nu$.

Unit bridges and long intervals

For a unit interval beginning at $i$, also choose a return path $j\to i$ of length $1\le\ell\le D$. Require the outbound jumps in $[0,1/12]$ and return jumps in $[11/12,1]$, with no others.

This event ends at $i$, has probability at least $e^{-1}(\rho/12)^{k+\ell}/(k!\ell!)$, and its integral differs from the no-jump value $f_i$ by at least $r_f/4$.

Let $p_{ii}=\Pr_i(Y_1=i)$. Conditioning both paths on this endpoint and using the same two-copy identity gives

$$p_{ii}\operatorname{Var}_i(A\mid Y_1=i) \ge\frac{e^{-2}}{16p_{ii}} \frac{(\rho/12)^{k+\ell}}{k!\ell!}r_f^2 \ge2c_Qr_f^2.$$

For $s\ge1$, condition on the states at all integer times through $\lfloor s\rfloor$, and at time $s$. Given this skeleton, integrals on separate intervals are independent Markov bridges, so their conditional variances add.

Each complete unit interval contributes at least $2c_Qr_f^2$ after averaging over its endpoint, whatever its starting-state law; the final remainder contributes a nonnegative variance. Thus total variance is at least $2c_Qr_f^2\lfloor s\rfloor\ge c_Qr_f^2s$.

From scalar range to a matrix bound

For $f=Fx$, centering gives $\sum_i\pi_if_i^2=\operatorname{Var}_\pi f\le r_f^2/4$, by centering at the midpoint of its range. Applying the scalar result for every $x$ and undoing the time rescaling proves the Loewner inequality.

Why a uniform short-time quadratic floor is impossible

Take the symmetric two-state chain with unit flip rate, $f=(1,-1)$, and a deterministic positive start. Its exact covariance kernel for $0\le u\le t$ is $e^{-2(t-u)}(1-e^{-4u})$, so

$$V(s)=2\int_0^s\int_0^t e^{-2(t-u)}(1-e^{-4u})du\,dt =\frac43s^3+O(s^4).$$

More precisely, for $0<s\le1/2$ the elementary exponential bounds give

$$\frac43-2s\le V(s)/s^3\le\frac43.$$

The stationary-start variance instead begins at $s^2$. Thus no positive $s^2$ floor can hold uniformly over starting priors, even for two states with $D=1$; the cubic power in this simplest all-prior case is necessary.

Shortest paths give the exact short-time powers

In this section the generator is $Q$ itself and $t\downarrow0$, with $Q$ fixed. Start at $i$, set $K_{jk}=Q_{jk}$ for $j\ne k$ and $K_{jj}=0$, and let $d_j$ be the directed distance from $i$ to $j\ne i$.

For a nonconstant scalar $f$, put $g=f-f_i\mathbf1$, $r=\|g\|_\infty$ and $k=\min\{d_j:g_j\ne0\}$. Then, with $a=\max_j(-Q_{jj})$,

$$\begin{aligned} c_i(f)&=\frac{2(K^k g^{\odot2})_i}{(k+2)!}>0,\\ \left|\operatorname{Var}_i\!\left(\int_0^t f(Y_u)du\right)-c_i(f)t^{k+2}\right| &\le r^2\left\{a^{k+1}\left(\frac2{(k+2)!}+\frac1{(k+1)!}\right)t^{k+3} +\frac{a^{2k}t^{2k+2}}{(k!)^2}\right\}. \end{aligned}$$

This finite bound holds for every $t\ge0$. Before $k$ actual jumps the feature is unchanged; on a $k$-jump path the integral of $g$ is $(t-u_k)g_{Y_{u_k}}$, and its squared simplex integral is $2t^{k+2}/(k+2)!$.

The holding-time density multiplier lies in $[e^{-at},1]$, giving the first remainder term with factor $2/(k+2)!$ after summing path weights, since $\|K\|_\infty\le a$. Uniformization bounds the probability of at least $\ell$ actual jumps by $(at)^\ell/\ell!$.

The paths with more than $k$ jumps contribute at most $r^2t^2(at)^{k+1}/(k+1)!$ to the raw second moment. The squared mean contributes at most $r^2t^2\{(at)^k/k!\}^2$, completing the bound.

There is also a full matrix statement: let $L_j(t)=\int_0^t\mathbf1_{\{Y_u=j\}}du$, $j\ne i$, $S_t=\operatorname{diag}_{j\ne i}(t^{(d_j+2)/2})$ and $c_j=2(K^{d_j})_{ij}/(d_j+2)!$. Then

$$S_t^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}_i(L(t))S_t^{-1} \longrightarrow\operatorname{diag}_{j\ne i}(c_j)\succ0.$$

The scalar bound proves the diagonal limits. For distinct $j,h\ne i$, visiting both requires at least $\ell=\max(d_j,d_h)+\mathbf1_{\{d_j=d_h\}}$ jumps, so

$$|\operatorname{Cov}_i(L_j,L_h)|\le t^2\left\{\frac{(at)^\ell}{\ell!} +\frac{(at)^{d_j+d_h}}{d_j!d_h!}\right\}.$$

After the displayed scaling every off-diagonal entry tends to zero, at least as fast as $t^{1/2}$. Consequently the covariance eigenvalue orders, in these occupation coordinates or any fixed invertible change of coordinates, are the powers $t^{d_j+2}$, with multiplicities given by graph-distance layers.

In particular the smallest eigenvalue at start $i$ has order $t^{2+\max_j d_j}$. The occupation coordinates span all state functions modulo constants; taking the finite minimum over starts and using total variance gives

$$\inf_{\nu}\ \inf_{\operatorname{Var}_\pi f=1} \operatorname{Var}_\nu\!\left(\int_0^t f(Y_u)du\right)=\Theta_Q(t^{D+2}).$$

For the matching upper bound choose a start and destination at directed distance $D$ and take the destination indicator, normalized to stationary variance one. Thus the diameter power is sharp even for a fixed graph, although the all-horizon constant above is not.

For generator $mQ$ use $t=mT$ and divide the covariance by $m^2$. This is a short-time moment hierarchy, not a Gaussian limit: the no-jump event has probability tending to one.

The Poisson domination is the classical uniformization construction, reviewed in van Dijk, van Brummelen and Boucherie (2018), Section 3.2. The path coefficients and explicit remainder here are derived directly, without a literature-priority claim.

Certificate and scope

The certificate checks 224 prior/horizon matrix inequalities on five chains, including sparse directed graphs and a defective generator, plus 15 independent moment-ODE comparisons and 20 physical time/rate rescalings.

The smallest measured ratio of a generalized covariance eigenvalue to its lower bound is $3241.071595$, illustrating the conservatism of the constant. The largest normalized matrix-exponential versus ODE discrepancy is $1.74\times10^{-13}$.

At $s=0.001$ the deterministic two-state value $V(s)/s^3$ is $1.331335199$, within the explicit remainder interval above. All checks end with literal PASS.

A separate graph-distance certificate checks 637 scalar remainders, 112 scaled matrix cases and 32 independent matrix-exponential moments. Its positive-term moment series avoids losing rare-state variances to cancellation at small times.

On the unit-rate five-cycle, the destination four jumps away has leading variance $t^6/360$. At $t=0.001$, its variance divided by $t^6$ is $0.002775397867$, compared with the limit $0.002777777778$.

The proof is a finite jump-path and conditional-bridge argument, not a central limit theorem. Classical long-run additive-functional results such as Kipnis and Varadhan (1986) concern a different asymptotic question; no reversibility or limiting Gaussian law is asserted here.
