Mean waiting time: proof and scope

Let $Q$ be an irreducible finite row generator with stationary probability row $\pi$, and let $d_i>0$. Start $Y$ in $\pi$, independently of a unit-exponential trigger, and set $\tau_m=\inf\{t:\int_0^t d_{Y_s}\,ds\geq E\}$ for generator $mQ$.

Write $D=\operatorname{diag}d$, $\bar d=\pi d$, and $\langle u,v\rangle_\pi=\sum_i\pi_i u_i v_i$. The killed semigroup gives

$$\Pr_\pi(\tau_m>t)=\pi e^{t(mQ-D)}\mathbf1,\qquad W(m)=\mathbb E_\pi\tau_m=\langle\mathbf1,(D-mQ)^{-1}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi.$$

Integration is justified by the upper bound $e^{-t\min_i d_i}$, including at $m=0$. This is the standard phase-type first-event representation in Asanjarani and Nazarathy, Proposition 2.2.

The positive spectral law

Assume detailed balance $\pi_iQ_{ij}=\pi_jQ_{ji}$. Then $L=-Q$ is self-adjoint in $L^2(\pi)$, positive semidefinite, and has kernel $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}$.

$$A=D^{-1/2}LD^{-1/2},\qquad z=D^{-1/2}\mathbf1,\qquad W(m)=\langle z,(I+mA)^{-1}z\rangle_\pi.$$

The spectral theorem gives an orthonormal eigenbasis $v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1}$ of $A$ with $\lambda_0=0$ and $\lambda_k>0$ for $k\geq1$. Its normalized zero mode is $v_0=D^{1/2}\mathbf1/\sqrt{\bar d}$, so $\langle z,v_0\rangle_\pi^2=1/\bar d$.

$$W(m)=\frac1{\bar d}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{w_k}{1+m\lambda_k}, \qquad w_k=\langle z,v_k\rangle_\pi^2\geq0.$$

Repeated eigenvalues may be combined; their total weights do not depend on the chosen eigenbasis. This positive-resolvent mixture is a specialization of the reversible spectral framework in Aldous and Fill, Sections 3.4–3.5, without a priority claim.

$$(-1)^jW^{(j)}(m)=j!\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{w_k\lambda_k^j}{(1+m\lambda_k)^{j+1}},\qquad j\geq1.$$

At least one weight is positive exactly when $d$ is nonconstant: otherwise $z$ is proportional to $v_0$. Therefore all these alternating derivatives are strictly positive for nonconstant hazards, and

$$\frac1{\bar d}<W(m)<\pi(1/d)\quad(m>0),\qquad W(0)=\pi(1/d),\quad \lim_{m\to\infty}W(m)=\frac1{\bar d}.$$

Every truncation has an explicit signed remainder

For $j\geq1$, define $A_j=\sum_kw_k\lambda_k^{-j}$ and $S_p(m)=1/\bar d+\sum_{j=1}^p(-1)^{j-1}A_jm^{-j}$. The finite geometric identity yields, for every $m>0$ and integer $p\geq1$,

$$(-1)^p\{W(m)-S_p(m)\} =\sum_k\frac{w_k(m\lambda_k)^{-p}}{1+m\lambda_k},$$
$$0\leq(-1)^p\{W(m)-S_p(m)\}\leq\frac{A_{p+1}}{m^{p+1}}.$$

This bound holds outside the convergence radius of the infinite inverse-speed series too. For nonconstant hazards its lower inequality is strict; no maturity limit or interchange of limits is used.

The first coefficient is precisely the fixed-feature Green–Kubo form. Let $f=d-\bar d\mathbf1$ and $H=-Q^\#$, the inverse of $L$ on centered vectors.

$$A_1=\frac{\langle f,Hf\rangle_\pi}{\bar d^2} =\frac1{\bar d^2}\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(d_{Y_0},d_{Y_t})\,dt,$$

where the covariance uses the unscaled generator $Q$. To verify the equality, expand $u_m=(D+mL)^{-1}\mathbf1=u_0+m^{-1}u_1+O(m^{-2})$ in the exact equation.

$$u_0=\frac{\mathbf1}{\bar d},\qquad Lu_1=-\frac f{\bar d},\qquad u_1=-\frac{Hf}{\bar d}+c\mathbf1.$$

The next solvability condition $\pi Du_1=0$ gives $c=\langle f,Hf\rangle_\pi/\bar d^2$. Since $\pi H=0$, this is the coefficient of $m^{-1}$ in $W$, agreeing with the spectral expansion above.

A different stationary start gives a different question

For the repeated Cox arrival process, an event epoch samples the regime according to $\alpha_i=\pi_i d_i/\bar d$, not $\pi$. Because $\pi(D-mQ)=\pi D$,

$$\mathbb E_\alpha\tau_m=\alpha(D-mQ)^{-1}\mathbf1=\frac1{\bar d}.$$

This identity does not require reversibility. The time-stationary versus event-stationary distinction is also made explicitly by Asanjarani and Nazarathy.

For any $Q$, let $Q^*$ denote its $L^2(\pi)$ adjoint. Since the two killed resolvents are adjoints,

$$W_Q(m)=\langle\mathbf1,(D-mQ)^{-1}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi =W_{Q^*}(m).$$

Thus this mean cannot detect cycle direction, although the cause of first arrival can. There is no fixed-feature or maturity-integrated matrix rank claim here.

The nonreversible boundary is exact

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\0&-1&1\\1&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \pi=(1,1,1)/3,\qquad d=(1,2,3).$$

Direct inversion gives a stationary positive-hazard counterexample:

$$W(m)=\frac{9m^2+18m+11}{3(6m^2+11m+6)},\qquad W^{(5)}(1/100)=\frac{734042964660710000000000000}{813435571879525131193330129}>0.$$

Complete monotonicity would require this fifth derivative to be nonpositive. This example disproves that extension, not ordinary first-derivative monotonicity for general chains.

Certificate and scope

verify_mean_wait.py checks 56 reversible chains in dimensions 2 through 8 at 11 speeds, derivatives through order 6, and signed truncation bounds through order 4. It compares the first coefficient with a separate group-inverse calculation and the cycle derivative with exact rational arithmetic.

The reversible benchmark uses $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1&0\\1&-3&2\\0&2&-2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $\pi=(1,1,1)/3$ and $d=(1,2,3)$. It prints $W(1)=0.5585585586$, $A_1=0.125$, $A_2=0.1423611111$ and cycle fifth derivative $0.9023984075$.

These are positive constant statewise hazards, not CIR intensity processes. The result controls the integrated mean, not survival at each finite horizon, arbitrary starting priors, or regime-dependent event-trigger resets.
