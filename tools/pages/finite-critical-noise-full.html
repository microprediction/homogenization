Critical noise in a finite regime chain

The leading quadratic-score bias combines a second-moment boundary term with initial-mean memory times stationary skewness.

Coefficient and scope

Fix an irreducible finite row generator $Q$, stationary row $\pi$, and real observable $f$ centered so $\pi f=0$. With pointwise products, define

$$h=Q^\#f,\qquad B=2Q^\#(fh),\qquad \Gamma=-2\pi(fh)>0,\qquad \kappa_3=3\pi(fB).$$

Here $Q^\#$ satisfies $QQ^\#=I-\mathbf1\pi$, $\pi Q^\#=0$ and $Q^\#\mathbf1=0$. The scalars $\Gamma,\kappa_3$ are the stationary second and third cumulant rates of the integrated observable. The vector $B$ is the initial-state second raw-moment boundary term around the stationary mean, not a variance contrast after recentering each start.

$$A_c={1\over c}\int_0^c f(Y_s)ds,\qquad S_c=\mu A_c^2+{\lambda\mu\Gamma\over c}Z,\qquad \mu>0,$$

Take independent standard Gaussian $Z$ and let $q_c$ be the exact stationary pooled $p$-quantile, for fixed $0<p<1$. Let $t_p(\lambda)$ be the $p$-quantile of $N^2+\lambda Z$, with independent standard normals $N,Z$. For $x>0$, set $u=\sqrt x$ and define

$$P_i(x)=\phi(u)\left[-{B_i\over\Gamma}u +{h_i\kappa_3\over3\Gamma^2}(u^3-3u)\right],\qquad K_{i,\lambda}(t)=\mathbb E[P_i(t-\lambda Z)],$$

with $P_i=0$ on $x\le0$. For every fixed $0<\lambda_0\le\lambda_1<\infty$,

$$\Pr_i(S_c\le q_c)-p={1\over c}K_{i,\lambda}(t_p(\lambda))+o(c^{-1}), \qquad\lambda\in[\lambda_0,\lambda_1],$$

uniformly in that interval and over the finitely many starting states. This proves a general-chain critical-noise result without an extra nonlattice assumption, but does not allow $\lambda\to0$ or $\lambda\to\infty$ jointly with $c$. Neither a density expansion nor an all-noise remainder is asserted.

Both coefficient vectors have stationary weighted sum zero because $\pi h=\pi B=0$. Population pooling is exactly marginal; this remains latent-start model-conditional bias, not a finite-calibration or feature-conditional guarantee.

Proof by the even part of the Fourier transform

Put $D=\operatorname{diag}f$. The simple eigenvalue $\Lambda(z)$ of $Q+zD$ near zero and its analytic spectral projector $\Pi(z)$ obey

$$\Lambda(z)={\Gamma z^2\over2}+{\kappa_3z^3\over6}+O(z^4),\qquad (e_i-\pi)\Pi(z)\mathbf1=-h_i z+{B_i z^2\over2}+O(z^3).$$

For completeness, normalize the right eigenvector $r(z)$ by $\pi r(z)=1$. Differentiating its eigen-equation gives $r'(0)=-h$, $r''(0)=B$ and the two displayed eigenvalue derivatives. Normalize the left eigenvector by $\ell(z)r(z)=1$; then $(\ell(z)\mathbf1)'_{z=0}=0$, giving the projector expansion.

For $X_c=\sqrt{c/\Gamma}A_c$, write $\delta\chi_i(u)=(e_i-\pi)e^{c(Q+iuD/\sqrt{c\Gamma})}\mathbf1$. Multiplying the eigenvalue and projector expansions and taking real parts cancels the odd $c^{-1/2}$ term:

$$\operatorname{Re}\delta\chi_i(u)={e^{-u^2/2}\over c} \left[-{B_i\over2\Gamma}u^2-{h_i\kappa_3\over6\Gamma^2}u^4\right]+o(c^{-1}).$$

The CDF test $g_{t,\lambda}(x)=\Phi((t-x^2)/\lambda)$ is even and Schwartz, with uniformly bounded Schwartz seminorms for bounded $t$ and $\lambda\in[\lambda_0,\lambda_1]$. On $|u|\le c^{1/20}$, local analytic perturbation gives an integrable Gaussian-times-polynomial remainder $O(c^{-3/2})$ plus an exponentially small spectral remainder. Outside this region, $|\delta\chi_i|\le2$ and repeated integration by parts in the test transform makes its contribution $o(c^{-1})$.

Thus Fourier inversion is uniform for these tests. Inverting the two Gaussian polynomials gives the signed density $\phi(x)\{B_iH_2(x)/(2\Gamma)-h_i\kappa_3H_4(x)/(6\Gamma^2)\}$, with $H_2=x^2-1$ and $H_4=x^4-6x^2+3$. Integrating over $[-\sqrt x,\sqrt x]$ and convolving with $\lambda Z$ gives $K_{i,\lambda}(t)$.

The same local eigenvalue argument gives $X_c\Rightarrow N$. Hence the pooled scaled quantile $cq_c/(\mu\Gamma)$ converges uniformly to $t_p(\lambda)$ on the stated compact noise interval, by continuity and strict increase of the reference CDF there. Continuity of $K$ transfers the actual-threshold expansion to the pooled quantile.

An actual-threshold Fourier error budget

This identity also supplies a finite-$c$ bound without substituting a limiting quantile. Let $\widehat g(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-iux}g_{t,\lambda}(x)dx$, $a_i=B_i/(2\Gamma)$, $d_i=-h_i\kappa_3/(6\Gamma^2)$, and $R_i(u)=e^{-u^2/2}(-a_i u^2+d_i u^4)/c$. For any $U>0$ the error $|F_i(\mu\Gamma t/c)-F_\pi(\mu\Gamma t/c)-K_{i,\lambda}(t)/c|$ is at most

$$ {1\over\pi}\int_0^U|\widehat g(u)|\, |\operatorname{Re}\delta\chi_i(u)-R_i(u)|du +{\|g^{(4)}\|_1\over\pi}\left[ {2\over3U^3}+{e^{-U^2/2}\over cU} \left({|a_i|\over U^2}+|d_i|\right)\right].$$

Indeed $|\widehat g(u)|\le\|g^{(4)}\|_1/u^4$; use $|\delta\chi_i|\le2$ and the Gaussian tail bound on the remaining integral. All quantities are determined by a finite matrix exponential and one-dimensional integrals. Floating quadratures in the certificate, including their refinement checks, are not outward-rounded enclosures.

Benchmark and relation to fixed noise

The certificate uses the nonreversible generator and feature

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1.8&1.4&.4\\ .3&-1.2&.9\\1.1&.2&-1.3\end{pmatrix}, \qquad f_{\rm raw}=(1,-.7,.2)^\top,$$

centered under its computed stationary law. It gives $\Gamma=0.385937945$ and $\kappa_3=-0.046285861$. At $p=.9,\lambda=1,c=2048$, the $c$-scaled gaps are $(-.032473882,-.036965545,.066119823)$, against limits $(-.032472792,-.036942314,.066093592)$.

The largest scaled quantile discrepancy over six noise/target pairs falls from $0.002173971$ at $c=32$ to $0.000032646$ at $c=2048$. Omitting stationary skewness leaves a nonvanishing coefficient error $0.001381965$ in that benchmark. Independent Bessel-CDF comparisons check the two-state specialization; eigenprojector derivatives independently check the Poisson coefficients.

As a limit of the coefficient function alone, $\int_0^\infty P_i(x)dx=-B_i/\Gamma$ gives $\lambda K_{i,\lambda}(t_p(\lambda))\to-(B_i/\Gamma)\phi(\Phi^{-1}(p))$ as $\lambda\to\infty$. This matches the fixed-noise quadratic coefficient $-\mu B_i\phi(\Phi^{-1}(p))/\sigma$ on the $c^{-2}$ scale, but the compact-noise theorem does not justify that joint limit.

The spectral mechanism is classical; compare Hervé and Pène (2010), Theorem 8.1 and Lemma 8.3, for discrete-time initial bias and stationary skewness. The continuous-time smooth-test calculation is proved directly above, not obtained by importing their nonarithmetic CDF theorem.
