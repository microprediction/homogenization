Critical scores at the vanishing-noise scale

One noise-uniform expansion connects inverse-horizon and inverse-square latent-start bias, with a sign reversal between them.

Setup and result

Let $Y$ jump from $+$ to $-$ at fixed rate $a>0$ and back at fixed rate $b>0$. Put $r=a+b$, $\pi=(b/r,a/r)$, $\bar y=(b-a)/r$, $v=8ab/r^3$, and $A_c=c^{-1}\int_0^cY_sds$. Consider the critical quadratic score

$$S_c=\mu(A_c-\bar y)^2+\sigma_c Z,\qquad \mu>0,\quad Z\sim N(0,1)\text{ independent},\quad \sigma_c={\lambda\mu v\over c},\quad\lambda\ge0.$$

Write $F_+,F_-$ for its fixed-start CDFs, and let $q_c$ be its stationary pooled $p$-quantile for fixed $0<p<1$. If $t_p(\lambda)$ is the $p$-quantile of $N^2+\lambda Z$, for independent standard normals $N,Z$, define

$$h(x)=(\sqrt x-x^{3/2})\phi(\sqrt x)\mathbf1_{\{x>0\}},\qquad H_\lambda(t)=\mathbb E[h(t-\lambda Z)],\qquad C={a-b\over2br}.$$
$$F_+(q_c)-p={C\over c}H_\lambda(t_p(\lambda))+r_c(\lambda),\qquad F_-(q_c)-p=-{b\over a}\{F_+(q_c)-p\}.$$ $$\sup_{\lambda\ge0}c(1+\lambda)|r_c(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0.$$

In fact the remainder is $o(\{c(1+\lambda)\}^{-1})$ uniformly over all $\lambda\ge0$, so arbitrary joint noise scalings are allowed; the second equality is exact whenever the pooled quantile attains $p$. At zero noise this holds for all sufficiently large $c$ because the endpoint atoms move outside the fixed-quantile region. Bounded normalized noise has order $c^{-1}$ when its coefficient is nonzero; fixed positive noise recovers order $c^{-2}$ below.

If $a=b$, reflection makes $(A_c-\bar y)^2$ identically distributed under both starts, so the conditional gaps vanish exactly for every $c$ and noise level. Stationary pooling remains marginal population coverage, not a finite-calibration or distribution-free conditional guarantee.

An exact quadratic CDF profile

For $-1<x<1$ put

$$g_c(x)=e^{-c\{r+(a-b)x\}/2}I_0(c\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{1-x^2}),$$

and set $g_c(x)=0$ at and outside both endpoints. Differentiating the classical telegraph densities gives $f_+-f_-=-g_c'$ on the open interval. For $u\ge0$, define the right-continuous profile

$$J_c(u)={ac\over r}\left[g_c\!\left(\bar y-\sqrt{v/c}\,u\right) -g_c\!\left(\bar y+\sqrt{v/c}\,u\right)\right].$$

The zero endpoint convention incorporates the jumps at $u_-=(1+\bar y)\sqrt{c/v}$ and $u_+=(1-\bar y)\sqrt{c/v}$. Indeed, integrating over the quadratic score's acceptance interval and adding its two endpoint atoms gives, for every real $t$,

$$F_+\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right)-F_\pi\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right) ={1\over c}\mathbb E\!\left[J_c\!\left(\sqrt{t-\lambda Z}\right) \mathbf1_{\{t-\lambda Z\ge0\}}\right].$$

The integrand is defined to be zero for negative arguments before taking a square root. This identity retains atoms even at zero noise; it does not replace their mass by a continuous approximation.

A finite-horizon error budget

Put $\psi(u)=(u-u^3)\phi(u)$ for $u\ge0$, so $h(x)=\psi(\sqrt x)$ on $x\ge0$ and $\psi'(u)=(1-4u^2+u^4)\phi(u)$. A computable exact-arithmetic budget is

$$B_c={1\over2}\left\{\int_0^\infty|J_c'(u)-C\psi'(u)|du +{ac\over r}(e^{-ac}+e^{-bc})\right\}.$$

The derivative excludes the two jump points; their absolute jump sizes are bounded by the displayed exponential terms. Since $J_c-C\psi$ vanishes at zero and infinity, half its total variation bounds its supremum. Also put $L_c=\int_0^\infty 2u|J_c(u)-C\psi(u)|du$.

Probability convolution contracts the supremum, while convolution with a Gaussian density is bounded by its maximum times the $L^1$ norm. Hence, for every actual threshold $t$ and every $\lambda\ge0$,

$$\left|F_+\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right)-F_\pi\!\left({\mu v\over c}t\right) -{C\over c}H_\lambda(t)\right| \le {1\over c}\min\left\{B_c,{L_c\over\lambda\sqrt{2\pi}}\right\}.$$

This bound is centered at the actual threshold $t$, not the limiting quantile $t_p(\lambda)$. At an exact pooled quantile that attains $p$, replace $F_\pi$ by $p$ and take $t=cq_c/(\mu v)$. The second bound is omitted at $\lambda=0$; numerical quadratures of both budgets are regression checks, not outward-rounded enclosures.

Proof of the asymptotic coefficient

Normalize the telegraph kernel by $k_c(u)=r\sqrt{vc}\,g_c(\bar y+\sqrt{v/c}\,u)/2$. Taylor expansion of its saddle exponent and the fixed-order Bessel expansion give, locally uniformly with one derivative,

$$k_c(u)=\phi(u)\left[1+{d\over\sqrt c}(u-u^3)\right]+O(c^{-1}),\qquad d={b-a\over2\sqrt{2abr}}.$$

Its exponent is $S(x)=-\{r+(a-b)x\}/2+\sqrt{ab}\sqrt{1-x^2}$, with $S(\bar y)=S'(\bar y)=0$, $S''(\bar y)=-1/v$, and $S''(x)\le-\sqrt{ab}$. Subtracting $k_c(u)$ from $k_c(-u)$ therefore gives $J_c\to C\psi$ and $J_c'\to C\psi'$ locally uniformly.

These limits control both $B_c$ and the weighted integral $L_c$. On $|u|\le\sqrt{K\log c}$, the differentiated Bessel and Taylor remainders after the subtraction are bounded by $c^{-1/2}$ times a polynomial in $\log c$. On the remaining interior region, concavity bounds the kernels and derivatives by a polynomial in $c,u$ times $e^{-\delta u^2}$; choosing fixed $K$ large makes that integral vanish.

Near the endpoints, $I_0(z)\le2e^z/\sqrt{1+z}$ and $I_1(z)\le(z/2)I_0(z)$ remove the apparent denominator singularities and give exponentially small integrals. The extra factor $2u$ costs only a polynomial on the central region and in the Gaussian tails, so $B_c,L_c\to0$, including the endpoint jumps. No polynomial decay rate for these budgets is asserted.

The stationary density of $\sqrt{c/v}(A_c-\bar y)$ converges locally to $\phi$, and its continuous mass tends to one. Scheffé's lemma, including the vanishing atoms, gives total-variation convergence; squaring and adding $\lambda Z$ cannot increase that distance. Compactify the noise by $\rho=\lambda/(1+\lambda)\in[0,1]$: the normalized reference law is $(1-\rho)N^2+\rho Z$.

Its CDF is jointly continuous and strictly increasing at its $p$-quantile, including the chi-square and Gaussian endpoints. Compactness therefore gives uniform quantile stability, so $\sup_{\lambda\ge0}|cq_c/(\mu v)-t_p(\lambda)|/(1+\lambda)\to0$.

For $\lambda>0$, write $H_\lambda(t)=\lambda^{-1}\int_0^\infty h(x)\phi((t-x)/\lambda)dx$. The functions $(1+\lambda)H_\lambda((1+\lambda)s)$ extend continuously in $(\rho,s)$ to $h(s)$ at $\rho=0$ and $-2\phi(s)$ at $\rho=1$, since $h$ is bounded, uniformly continuous and integrable with integral $-2$. They are thus uniformly equicontinuous on compact $s$ intervals.

The finite-horizon bound, multiplied by $c(1+\lambda)$, is at most $2\max\{B_c,L_c/\sqrt{2\pi}\}\to0$. Combining this with normalized quantile stability and equicontinuity proves the all-noise remainder.

A sign change, not just a slower rate

At zero noise, let $z=\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2)$. The coefficient becomes $C(z-z^3)\phi(z)$, so its sign changes at $p_*=2\Phi(1)-1=0.682689492\ldots$ when $a\ne b$. At $p_*$ only the leading term vanishes; finite-$c$ exact cancellation is not asserted.

Moreover $\int_0^\infty h(x)dx=-2$ and $t_p(\lambda)/\lambda\to z_p$, so $\lambda H_\lambda(t_p(\lambda))\to-2\phi(z_p)$ as $\lambda\to\infty$. For $a>b$ the median coefficient is therefore positive at zero noise and negative at sufficiently large finite $\lambda$, proving at least one sign change by continuity. The uniform remainder now also permits unbounded $\lambda=\lambda_c$.

In particular, for any positive noise sequence with $c\sigma_c\to\infty$,

$$c^2\sigma_c\{F_+(q_c)-p\}\longrightarrow -2C\mu v\phi(\Phi^{-1}(p)).$$

For fixed $\sigma>0$ this is exactly the quadratic specialization of the fixed-noise Poisson boundary-covariance coefficient. No parameter-uniform conclusion as $a$ or $b$ degenerates follows.

Literature and scope

The density and atoms are classical: López and Ratanov (2014), equation (2.14), inspected in the publisher PDF. The differentiated asymptotic inputs are DLMF 10.40.1 and 10.40.3; the critical-score profile and error transfer are derived above. The result fixes positive rates and an interior target and treats a quadratic score with arbitrary nonnegative Gaussian noise scalings, not arbitrary nonlinear scores or general finite chains.
