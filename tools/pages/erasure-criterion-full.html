Which generators can a sampling clock erase?

Complete erasure has an exact spectral criterion, and polynomial positivity constructs smooth clocks that attain it.

Experiment and characterization

Let $Q$ be an irreducible finite row generator on $n\ge2$ states, with stationary row $\pi$ and $P=\mathbf1\pi$. At each observation independently redraw a positive, almost surely finite interval $\tau$, independently of the chain. Retain all labeled states including repetitions, but discard times, clock-component tags and total elapsed time at a fixed sample count.

Call the clock completely erasing if its sampled kernel is $R=\mathbb E e^{\tau Q}=P$. At stationarity the observed labels are then exactly iid with law $\pi$. The following are equivalent:

  1. Some such positive clock completely erases $Q$.
  2. Every nonzero eigenvalue of $Q$ is nonreal.
  3. A finite mixture of positive-shape Erlang clocks, all with one common rate, completely erases $Q$.

The clock in the last statement has a strictly positive analytic density on $(0,\infty)$ and all moments finite. Jordan blocks are allowed. The clock is constructed for the given generator, not chosen universally for all generators in a dimension.

Necessity: a real mode cannot disappear

A nonzero real eigenvalue of an irreducible finite generator is $-r$ for some $r>0$. If $Qv=-rv$, then $\pi v=0$ and

$$Rv=\mathbb E[e^{-r\tau}]v\ne0,\qquad Pv=0.$$

The scalar expectation is strictly positive because $\tau$ is finite almost surely. Therefore $R=P$ is impossible, regardless of the clock's mean or smoothness.

Sufficiency: turn an annihilating polynomial into a clock

Fix any rate $\lambda>0$ and put $C=\lambda(\lambda I-Q)^{-1}$. The stationary decomposition $\mathbb R^n=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf1)\oplus\ker\pi$ is invariant under $C$. Write $C_E$ for its restriction to the centered space and let $m(x)$ be the monic characteristic polynomial of $C_E$.

If every nonzero eigenvalue of $Q$ is nonreal, so is every eigenvalue of $C_E$. In conjugate pairs, with algebraic multiplicities included,

$$m(x)=\prod_j\{(x-\operatorname{Re}\rho_j)^2+(\operatorname{Im}\rho_j)^2\}^{d_j}.$$

Its homogeneous form $M(x,y)=y^d m(x/y)$, where $d=n-1$, is strictly positive on the compact simplex $x,y\ge0$, $x+y=1$. The endpoint values are positive as well, since $C_E$ is invertible and $m$ is monic.

The classical positive-coefficient theorem of Pólya, as stated by Powers and Reznick (2001), supplies an integer $N\ge0$ for which

$$p(x)=(1+x)^N m(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N+d}a_kx^k,\qquad a_k>0.$$

Choose the interval law to be the finite mixture with weights $w_k=a_k/p(1)$ and components $\operatorname{Erlang}(k+1,\lambda)$, where $\lambda$ is a rate. An Erlang interval is a sum of independent exponential stages, hence its sampled kernel is $C^{k+1}$.

$$R=\sum_k w_k C^{k+1}=\frac{Cp(C)}{p(1)}=P.$$

The final equality uses $m(C_E)=0$ by Cayley–Hamilton, while $C\mathbf1=\mathbf1$. Algebraic multiplicities in $m$ annihilate the complete centered operator, including its Jordan blocks: cancelling only distinct eigenvalues would not suffice. Positive Erlang weights give the claimed density and moments, completing the equivalence.

Why the positive-coefficient step terminates

This step is classical, not a new positivity theorem. For $m(x)=\sum_{j=0}^d m_jx^j$, the coefficient $b_{L,k}$ of $x^k$ in $(1+x)^{L-d}m(x)$ obeys

$$\frac{b_{L,k}}{\binom Lk} =\sum_{j=0}^d m_j\frac{(k)_j(L-k)_{d-j}}{(L)_d},\qquad L\ge d,$$

where $(u)_j$ is the falling factorial. Uniformly for integers $0\le k\le L$, the right side differs by $O_m(L^{-1})$ from $M(k/L,1-k/L)$. Since $M$ has a strictly positive minimum on the simplex, all coefficients are positive for sufficiently large $L$.

Thus repeatedly multiplying by $1+x$ and checking coefficients terminates for every generator satisfying the criterion. No numerical eigenvalue test is needed for the exact examples below, and no clock-size optimum is claimed.

Parity, reversibility and identification

The nonstationary dimension $n-1$ must be even, so complete erasure is possible only for odd $n$. Conversely, every odd $n\ge3$ has an example: the unit directed cycle has eigenvalues $e^{2\pi i k/n}-1$, with no nonzero real eigenvalue. Odd dimension alone is not sufficient for a particular generator.

A reversible generator has real spectrum, so no nontrivial reversible chain can be completely erased by these clocks. For an erasable $Q$, its stationary time reversal $Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^\top D_\pi$ is distinct and the same clock also gives $R(Q^*)=P$. Thus the constructed, fully known clock fails generator identification even at the population level.

This characterizes complete erasure, not all aliasing. A clock may leave a nonzero mode visible yet fail to distinguish generators, and failure of complete erasure does not prove identification. No universal inverse-stability, phase-count, waiting-time-efficiency or finite-sample claim is made.

Exact constructive certificates

For the three- and five-state unit cycles at $\lambda=1$, the centered characteristic polynomials, scaled to integers, are

$$m_3(x)=7x^2-5x+1,\qquad m_5(x)=31x^4-49x^3+31x^2-9x+1.$$

The coefficient search finds $N=44$ and $N=121$, respectively, giving positive weights on Erlang shapes $1,\ldots,47$ and $1,\ldots,126$. These are constructions using this multiplier, not minimal possible clocks.

The defective five-state example uses $e_j$ for standard basis columns,

$$S=[\mathbf1\ e_1-e_5\ e_2-e_5\ e_3-e_5\ e_4-e_5],\qquad Q=S\operatorname{diag}(0,A)S^{-1},$$ $$A=-I_4+\frac9{50}\operatorname{diag}(J_2,J_2) +\frac1{100}\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

All off-diagonal rates are at least $7/500$ and the stationary law is uniform. Its conjugate pair $-1\pm9i/50$ has nontrivial Jordan blocks. At $\lambda=1/10$, the centered characteristic polynomial is proportional to $(3106x^2-550x+25)^2$.

The search gives $N=787$ and shapes $1,\ldots,792$; the unsquared quadratic does not annihilate the centered matrix. This separately checks that the construction respects Jordan multiplicity.

The certificate checks 965 strictly positive weights exactly, all three rational matrix annihilators, and exact obstructions for three even cycles and a reversible reset chain. Numerical mixture kernels and two independent semigroup integrals check the construction with portable tolerances; the script ends with literal PASS.

The result changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank after integrating maturity-dependent loadings. It describes information lost under a particular observation clock, with no claim that all erasable generators have different Green–Kubo strengths.
