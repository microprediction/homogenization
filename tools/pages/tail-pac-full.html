A slack-free certificate for both conditional tails

The exact iid Dirichlet allocation law gives a sharper dependent two-tail guarantee than a Markov bound with a free slack. Calibration dependence is paid once; test dependence spends an integrated distance-to-failure budget.

Setup and the one-dimensional margin

Let $X=(S_1,\ldots,S_N)$ be a calibration panel with law $\mu$, let $P_x$ be the conditional test-score law, and assume all scores have the same continuous marginal CDF $F$. Put $\nu=F^{\otimes N}$ and use the half-$L^1$ convention for total variation:

$$\eta_{\rm cal}=\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm TV},\qquad \eta_{\rm test}=\|P-\mu\otimes F\|_{\rm TV}.$$

For ranks $1\le\ell<u\le N$, let $Q_\ell,Q_u$ be calibration order statistics and put $V_{P,L}(X)=P_X(S\le Q_\ell)$, $V_{P,R}(X)=P_X(S>Q_u)$. Fix caps $a,b>0$ with $a+b<1$.

Under iid calibration and an independent test score, the lower, covered and upper masses have law

$$D\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\ell,u-\ell,N+1-u),\qquad \Psi(a,b)=\Pr(D_L\le a,D_R\le b).$$

Define the nonnegative margin $W=\{\min(a-D_L,b-D_R)\}_+$, with $z_+=\max(z,0)$. It is zero on the iid failure set, and for $0\le v<\min(a,b)$,

$$\Pr(W>v)=\Psi(a-v,b-v),\qquad J(q)=E[W\mathbf1_{\{0<W\le q\}}] =\int_0^q\Psi(a-v,b-v)dv-q\Psi(a-q,b-q).$$

The dependent confidence bound

If $0<\eta_{\rm test}<EW$, let $q\in(0,\min(a,b))$ be the unique solution of $J(q)=\eta_{\rm test}$. Then

$$\Pr_\mu\{V_{P,L}\le a,\ V_{P,R}\le b\} \ge[\Psi(a-q,b-q)-\eta_{\rm cal}]_+.$$

At $\eta_{\rm test}=0$ take $q=0$. At $\eta_{\rm test}\ge EW$ the certificate is zero. Thus no free slack remains to optimize.

For nonoverlapping blocks from a stationary absolutely regular process, one may substitute $\eta_{\rm cal}\le\min\{1,\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\beta_X(h_j)\}$ and $\eta_{\rm test}\le\beta_X(h_{N-1})$, using the actual separation gaps from the path note.

Proof and sharpness scope

For an actual panel set $V_{0,L}=F(Q_\ell)$, $V_{0,R}=1-F(Q_u)$ and $w(X)=\{\min(a-V_{0,L},b-V_{0,R})\}_+$. If either actual tail exceeds its cap, at least $w(X)$ probability must have moved from the independent test law, so $w(X)\le\|P_X-F\|_{\rm TV}$ on the failure event $A$.

Disintegrating over the common panel marginal gives $\int_A w\,d\mu\le\eta_{\rm test}$. Couple $\mu$ and $\nu$ maximally; the matched part of $A$ has mass at least $(\mu(A)-\eta_{\rm cal})_+$ and is a subprobability of the iid panel law.

Of all iid subprobabilities with a specified mass, the cheapest integral of $W$ selects its lowest values, with fractional selection at an atom. Hence, writing $Q_W$ for the lower quantile function of $W$,

$$\int_0^{(\mu(A)-\eta_{\rm cal})_+}Q_W(s)ds\le\eta_{\rm test}.$$

The atom at zero costs nothing; the additional selected mass consists of $0<W\le q$. Its cost is $J(q)$ and its total mass is $1-\Psi(a-q,b-q)$, proving the confidence bound.

This rearrangement is sharp for the scalar margin-and-deficit relaxation: moving allocations nearest a violated cap to that boundary spends exactly their margins, and strict violations are approached by an arbitrarily small additional transfer.

It is not a claim that an arbitrary extremal allocation is realizable by a stationary score process with the prescribed common marginal. Optimal-transport worst-event bounds are classical; see Blanchet and Murthy (2019), Theorem 1. The Dirichlet margin and separated dependence budgets here are the specialization being computed.

Computation and benchmark

The iid confidence is a finite multinomial probability: among $N$ iid uniforms, at least $\ell$ must lie below $a$ and at least $N+1-u$ above $1-b$. The certificate computes $J$ by integrating $v$ against the positive margin density, avoiding subtraction near zero.

For the sample range with $N=100$, $a=b=.05$, $\eta_{\rm cal}=0$ and $\eta_{\rm test}=10^{-4}$, iid confidence is $0.988185503$. The integrated-margin certificate is $0.969279247$, with $q=0.00916232072$, versus $0.947808420$ from optimizing the earlier free-slack bound.

The checks include independent Dirichlet integration, finite multinomial enumeration, the cost identity, fractional-knapsack linear programs, and exact state-revealing binary Markov panels. These floating-point checks complement the proof; they are not interval-validated numerical bounds.

This is confidence over random calibration panels that both training-conditional tail caps hold. It is not pointwise validity for every panel, latent-state-conditional validity, or feature-conditional conformal coverage.
