A sharp uniform window-confidence obstruction

Without a mixing restriction, stationary raw-window moments have a nonvanishing minimax confidence radius at every finite record length. This holds even with a known exit-rate cap and a complete labeled jump record. The obstruction concerns an unknown stationary law and a known deterministic start; it does not rule out useful data-dependent certificates on informative records.

Precisely which radius is impossible to shrink

Fix $T>0$, an integer $N\ge1$, record length $H=3NT$, a known exit-rate cap $\bar a>0$, and $0<\delta<1/2$. Let $\mathcal Q_{\bar a}$ be all irreducible two-state row generators with $\max_i(-Q_{ii})\le\bar a$. The observed path starts in known state one, not in stationarity.

The targets are nevertheless the stationary raw ordered moments

$$O_j=\int_{jT}^{(j+1)T}e_{Y_t}dt,\qquad A_i(Q)=\mathbb E_{\pi_Q}[O_0O_{i+1}^T],\quad i=0,1.$$

For an estimator pair $\widetilde A=(\widetilde A_0,\widetilde A_1)$ based on the full observed path on $[0,H]$, define $\mathcal L_Q(\widetilde A)=\max_{i=0,1}\|\widetilde A_i-A_i(Q)\|_F$. Estimators may be randomized and may output arbitrary real matrices. Let $R_\delta(H,T;\bar a)$ be the infimum of constant radii $R$ for which some estimator satisfies

$$\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q_{\bar a}}\Pr_{Q,1}\{\mathcal L_Q(\widetilde A)>R\}\le\delta.$$
$$\boxed{R_\delta(H,T;\bar a)=T^2/\sqrt2.}$$

The result already allows more information than occupation-window matrices, grid observations or snapshots. It concerns a uniform radius over the generator class, not pointwise consistency for one fixed generator.

Exact separated targets and a shared path

Parameterize every irreducible two-state generator as

$$Q_{r,p}=r\begin{pmatrix}-(1-p)&1-p\\p&-p\end{pmatrix},\qquad r>0,\quad0<p<1,\quad\pi_p=(p,1-p).$$

Put $v=p(1-p)$, $C=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $a_i=\{(1-e^{-rT})/(rT)\}^2e^{-irT}\in(0,1)$. The ordered stationary kernel gives

$$A_i(Q_{r,p})=T^2\{\pi_p^T\pi_p+a_ivC\}.$$

Indeed $e^{tQ}=\mathbf1\pi_p+e^{-rt}(I-\mathbf1\pi_p)$. Integrating the kernel over the two ordered windows yields the displayed expression. Since $p$ and $1-p$ share $v$ and $a_i$, their target gap is exactly

$$A_i(Q_{r,p})-A_i(Q_{r,1-p})=T^2(2p-1)\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),\qquad D:=\|A_i(Q_{r,p})-A_i(Q_{r,1-p})\|_F=\sqrt2T^2(1-2p)$$

for $0<p<1/2$, for both channels and every $r>0$. But the constant observed path $d_0=(Y_t=1\text{ for all }t\le H)$ has probabilities $e^{-r(1-p)H}$ and $e^{-rpH}$ in the two models. Thus both path laws contain the same point mass of size at least $a=e^{-r(1-p)H}$.

If $R<D/2$, the success sets for the two targets are disjoint on this common record, even after adding independent algorithmic randomization. If their conditional success probabilities are $s_1,s_2$, then $s_1+s_2\le1$. The sum of the two unconditional failure probabilities is therefore at least $a\{(1-s_1)+(1-s_2)\}\ge a$, proving

$$\max_{q\in\{p,1-p\}}\Pr_{Q_{r,q},1}\{\mathcal L_{Q_{r,q}}(\widetilde A)>R\} \ge\tfrac12e^{-r(1-p)H},\qquad R<T^2(1-2p)/\sqrt2.$$

For any $R<T^2/\sqrt2$, first choose $p$ small enough for this strict radius inequality. Then choose $r$ small enough that $r\max(p,1-p)\le\bar a$ and $e^{-r(1-p)H}>2\delta$. No estimator can satisfy the uniform confidence requirement at that $R$.

A matching upper bound using no observations

Choose $\widetilde A_0=\widetilde A_1=(T^2/2)I$ on every record. For $z=p-1/2$ and $v=p(1-p)$, the exact target formula gives

$$T^{-4}\|A_i-(T^2/2)I\|_F^2 =2z^2+4v^2(1-a_i)^2 \le2z^2+4v^2=\tfrac14+4z^4\le\tfrac12.$$

Both targets lie in these balls with probability one, for every generator in the class. This proves the matching upper bound and hence the exact minimax value. The supremum is approached near a reducible boundary; no reducible generator is included in the parameter class.

What a data-dependent raw-moment budget must do

The empirical triple-window averages $\widehat A_{i,N}=N^{-1}\sum_{k<N}O_{k0}O_{k,i+1}^T$ use occupations on $[(3k+j)T,(3k+j+1)T]$. On $d_0$, both empirical matrices equal $T^2e_1e_1^T$. If a deterministic data-dependent radius $\eta(d)$ is uniformly valid around these empirical matrices at any $0<\delta<1$, then

$$\boxed{\eta(d_0)\ge\sqrt2\,T^2.}$$

To prove this, let $p\downarrow0$ and $r\downarrow0$. Both stationary targets tend to $T^2e_2e_2^T$, while $\Pr_{Q_{r,p},1}(d_0)\to1$. Any smaller fixed value of $\eta(d_0)$ therefore fails with probability exceeding $\delta$ for some strictly positive-rate model under the cap.

This bound is sharp on that record: any two nonnegative matrices whose entries sum to $T^2$ have Frobenius distance at most $\sqrt2T^2$. Consequently an honest procedure must allow a vacuous raw-moment radius or abstain there. It may still give smaller data-dependent radii on other records.

An exact finite-record certificate

Take $T=1$, $N=10^6$, $H=3N$, $p=1/100$, $r=1/(100H)$, known cap $\bar a=1$, and nominal $\delta=1/20$. The half-gap is approximately $0.692964646$, so the radius $R=0.69$ has disjoint success sets. The common-record bound is certified without floating exponentials:

$$\tfrac12e^{-r(1-p)H}=\tfrac12e^{-99/10000} \ge\frac{9901}{20000}=0.49505>\delta.$$

Thus a million blocks do not make this uniform radius valid: at least one of the two models has failure probability at least $0.49505$. The exact minimax radius is approximately $0.707106781T^2$; the constant-path raw-estimator radius is approximately $1.414213562T^2$.

The certificate verifies $561$ exact rational target-gap, positivity and matching-center identities, including closure points, and $231$ exact randomized-decision inequalities on the shared atom. It also checks $120$ target formulas independently with Feynman–Kac matrix derivatives. These finite checks support the explicit proof; they are not a Monte Carlo proof of a minimax theorem.

Literature, scope and what remains possible

The lower bound uses the classical two-point indistinguishability argument. Related discrete-time sample-path obstructions and useful fully empirical intervals appear in Hsu et al. (2019), Sections 3.1 and 4. This note derives the continuous-path stationary-window target gap and its matching constant directly; it does not claim a new general lower-bound technique.

There is no contradiction with fixed-design mixing-controlled window confidence or mixing-free direct generator confidence from jump records. Direct rate intervals may remain uninformative, and stationary-law propagation can fail its sufficient tests near reducibility. Nor does this contradict exact identification from stationary population moments.

The worst-case generator changes with record length and can be arbitrarily slow; a known lower mixing rate excludes the witness. The theorem does not establish the same sharp constant for a stationary start or an externally known stationary law, and it does not rule out pointwise consistency or informative data-dependent certificates. Procedures allowed to keep sampling without any deterministic time cap are also outside this finite-record obstruction.
