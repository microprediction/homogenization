Tangent-gauge covariance

Only the local homogeneous tangent determines the logarithmic constant.

Exact homogeneity is unnecessary for the gauge-flat variance law. Smooth or non-smooth perturbations away from the origin may change finite-speed saddles without changing the leading Green–Kubo constant.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain setup of the gauge theorem, with centered $g:\mathsf S\to\mathbb R^d$, fixed $T>0$, occupation average $W_{m,T}$, and nonzero symmetric Green–Kubo matrix $\Gamma$. Let $\rho$ be a continuous positive homogeneous gauge and let $r$ be continuous, positive away from zero, with

$$\frac{r(w)}{\rho(w)}\longrightarrow1\qquad(w\to0).$$

For $\sigma>1$, $b>0$ and $w\ne0$, define

$$\Psi_{\sigma,b,r}(w)=\exp\{-\exp(br(w)^{-1/(\sigma-1)})\}, \qquad \Psi_{\sigma,b,r}(0)=0.$$

With $\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)=\max_{\|z\|_2=1}\rho(\Gamma^{1/2}z)^2$, every fixed $T>0$ obeys, uniformly over arbitrary initial regime priors,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Psi_{\sigma,b,r}(W_{m,T})) \sim\frac{b^{2(\sigma-1)}}{2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)} \frac{mT}{[\log(mT)]^{2(\sigma-1)}}.$$

Shrinking-boundary sandwich

For every $\varepsilon>0$, a neighborhood of zero satisfies $(1-\varepsilon)\rho(w)\le r(w)\le(1+\varepsilon)\rho(w)$. The relevant level sets shrink at scale $[\log(mT)]^{-(\sigma-1)}$.

Their quadratic contraction costs therefore lie between the two homogeneous costs obtained by replacing $\rho$ with $(1-\varepsilon)\rho$ and $(1+\varepsilon)\rho$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives the displayed constant.

Excursions outside the local neighborhood have a fixed positive large-deviation cost of order $mT$. They are negligible beside $mT/[\log(mT)]^{2(\sigma-1)}$, so no global comparison between $r$ and $\rho$ is needed.

Coordinate and rank scope

Under $w'=Rw$, transport both scales by $r'(w')=r(R^{-1}w')$ and $\rho'(w')=\rho(R^{-1}w')$, with $\Gamma'=R\Gamma R^\top$. The tangent relation and $\Lambda_\rho$ are invariant.

This remains a fixed-feature, fixed-maturity scalar variance statement. It neither identifies nonlinear covariance rank nor integrates maturity-dependent loadings.

Numerical certificate

The certificate perturbs the asymmetric gauge from the preceding note by

$$r(w)=\rho(w)(1+.8\|w\|_2).$$

This changes the finite-level boundary while leaving the tangent gauge unchanged. At level $.005$, the exact Legendre contraction has normalized cost $.996879139$ and its minimizing direction differs from the tangent direction by $.000138556$ radians.

Literature boundary

Gärtner (1977) supplies the spectral large-deviation principle, and Ney and Nummelin (1987) treat Markov additive processes. The tangent-gauge stability argument is the shrinking-boundary sandwich above. Regular profiles replace the fixed logarithmic power.
