Sharp clock-moment bounds for reversible regimes

An unknown exponential-mixture clock need not identify a generator, but two known interval moments give sharp modal bounds and a deterministic Green–Kubo uncertainty budget.

Let $Q$ be a finite irreducible reversible row generator with stationary law $\pi$. Sample fully observed regime labels at iid intervals $\tau=TE$, where $E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)$ and $T>0$ is redrawn independently of $E$ and the chain each interval. Discard all timestamps and total elapsed time, but suppose the population kernel $R$ and the two physical-time moments are known exactly:

$$\mu=\mathbb E\tau=\mathbb ET>0,\qquad s=\tfrac12\mathbb E\tau^2=\mathbb ET^2<\infty,\qquad v=s-\mu^2\ge0,\quad p=\frac vs.$$

The exponential-mixture model requires $\mathbb E\tau^2\ge2\mu^2$; equality forces $T=\mu$ almost surely, so the clock law is then known. No support bound or other information about the mixing law is assumed. A nonstationary generator mode with decay rate $r>0$ appears in $R$ with eigenvalue $\rho=F(r)=\mathbb E(1+rT)^{-1}\in(0,1)$.

The exact modal identified set

If $v=0$, the rate is exactly $r=(1-\rho)/(\mu\rho)$. If $v>0$, the possible rates under the two moment constraints are precisely

$$\boxed{\begin{cases} \displaystyle \frac{1-\rho}{\mu\rho}<r<\frac{\mu(1-\rho)}{\mu^2-s(1-\rho)},&\rho>p,\\[6pt] \displaystyle \frac{1-\rho}{\mu\rho}<r<\infty,&0<\rho\le p. \end{cases}}$$

These are open sets because $T$ is strictly positive and nonconstant. Each scalar set is sharp, including the unbounded case at the threshold $\rho=p$. It is not asserted that independently choosing every modal rate in these intervals yields one common clock law or a valid generator.

For the bounds, Jensen's inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz give

$$\frac1{1+\mu r}\le F(r)\le1-\frac{\mu^2r}{\mu+sr},\qquad \mu^2\le\mathbb E\frac{T}{1+rT}\;\mathbb E[T(1+rT)].$$

The second inequality uses $F(r)=1-r\mathbb E[T/(1+rT)]$. Both transform inequalities are strict for $v>0$ and $r>0$, and rearranging gives the displayed rate bounds. The upper transform bound tends to $p$, explaining why no finite upper rate bound remains when $\rho\le p$.

Why every interior rate is possible

For each $0<\ell<\mu$, use the strictly positive two-point component-mean law

$$u=\mu+\frac v{\mu-\ell},\qquad \Pr(T=\ell)=\frac v{v+(\mu-\ell)^2},\qquad \Pr(T=u)=\frac{(\mu-\ell)^2}{v+(\mu-\ell)^2}.$$

Its first two moments are exactly $\mu,s$. Let $r_\ell$ be the unique positive root $F_\ell(r_\ell)=\rho$; it varies continuously with $\ell$. As $\ell\uparrow\mu$, its transform tends pointwise to $(1+\mu r)^{-1}$, so $r_\ell$ tends to the lower rate endpoint.

As $\ell\downarrow0$, the transform tends to $1-\mu^2r/(\mu+sr)$. Thus $r_\ell$ tends to the upper endpoint when $\rho>p$, and diverges when $\rho\le p$. Continuity supplies every intervening rate; taking $Q=r(\mathbf1\pi-I)$ on two states realizes the same full observed kernel $\mathbf1\pi+\rho(I-\mathbf1\pi)$ for every such rate.

The endpoint constructions use increasingly small or large component means; imposing additional support bounds can tighten the result. The mixing laws at the open endpoints need not be admissible, and no endpoint attainment is claimed.

A fixed-feature Green–Kubo matrix budget

Work on $\mathcal E=\{h:\pi h=0\}$ with inner product $\langle f,h\rangle_\pi$. Reversibility makes $H=(-Q|_{\mathcal E})^{-1}$ and $K_R=\mu R_{\mathcal E}(I_{\mathcal E}-R_{\mathcal E})^{-1}$ commuting self-adjoint positive operators. Applying the scalar bounds to their common eigenbasis gives

$$\boxed{[K_R-(v/\mu)I_{\mathcal E}]_+\preceq H\preceq K_R,\qquad 0\preceq K_R-H\preceq(v/\mu)I_{\mathcal E}.}$$

Here $[\cdot]_+$ is the positive spectral part, not entrywise clipping. The potential uncertainty stays bounded even when a generator rate has no finite upper bound. The constant $v/\mu$ is sharp uniformly over this class by the two-state upper-end construction with $\rho>p$.

For any fixed centered feature matrix $B$, define $\Gamma_Q=2B^*HB$, $\Gamma_R=2B^*K_RB$ and $\Sigma=B^*B$, where the adjoint is in $L^2(\pi)$. These are physical-time integrated-covariance and one-time covariance matrices, respectively, and

$$0\preceq\Gamma_R-\Gamma_Q\preceq\frac{2v}{\mu}\Sigma.$$

This is an exact population uncertainty budget, not a fast-switching approximation. It concerns fixed features, not rank after integrating maturity-dependent loadings. The operator bounds need not be jointly attainable under a single clock and generator for an arbitrary multimode $R$.

Numerical scale and scope

For $\mu=1$, $\mathbb E\tau^2=13/6$, one has $v=1/12$ and $p=1/13$. At $\rho=1/2$, the exact rate set is $(1,12/11)$ and a centered unit-variance two-state feature has Green–Kubo strength in $(11/6,2)$. At $\rho=1/20$, the rate set is $(19,\infty)$ despite the same known finite clock moments.

The certificate checks the rational example, strictly positive two-point endpoint sequences, modal bounds and the operator/feature-Gram inequalities. Moment bounds for transforms are classical; Eckberg (1977) studies sharp Laplace–Stieltjes bounds from partial distribution information. The rational exponential-mixture inequalities and their generator/Green–Kubo propagation are derived above, without attributing these particular formulas to that paper or making a priority claim.

Reversibility is essential to the stated self-adjoint matrix order; no nonreversible extension is asserted. The moment and kernel inputs are exact population quantities, not estimates with confidence guarantees. No finite-sample, calendar-time, conditional-coverage or restricted-support claim is made.
