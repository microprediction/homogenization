Bartlett sector contraction without normality

A positive spectral average of paired resolvents preserves the full-chain sector, but not necessarily a restricted block's own sector.

Let $Q$ be a finite irreducible row generator, $\pi$ its stationary law, and $F$ fixed real centered features. Set $C_F(t)=F^TD_\pi e^{tQ}F$, $K_F=\int_0^\infty C_F(t)dt$ and $B_{T,F}=\int_0^T(1-t/T)C_F(t)dt$ for $T>0$. If the columns of $F$ form a basis of the centered state space, then

$$s(B_{T,F})\le s(K_F),\qquad s(M)=\|\operatorname{sym}(M)^{-1/2}\operatorname{skew}(M)\operatorname{sym}(M)^{-1/2}\|_2.$$

There is no normality or diagonalizability assumption. The proof below applies to every strictly accretive real matrix and every nonzero finite positive even spectral measure. It resolves the full-space candidate in issue #229.

A finite-dimensional sector lemma

Write $L=S+A$, where $S=S^T\succ0$ and $A=-A^T$, and put $\kappa=s(L)$. Equivalently $-\kappa S\preceq iA\preceq\kappa S$, with Hermitian inequalities. Inversion preserves this bound, because

$$\operatorname{sym}(L^{-1})=L^{-T}SL^{-1},\qquad \operatorname{skew}(L^{-1})=-L^{-T}AL^{-1}.$$

In fact $s(L^{-1})=s(L)$ by applying inversion twice. Positive sums and real congruences also preserve any common sector bound. These are numerical-range sectors, not merely bounds on eigenvalue arguments; see Arlinskiĭ and Zagrebnov (preprint Definitions 1.1 and 1.6) for the classical framework.

Let $\mu$ be a nonzero finite positive even measure on $\mathbb R$, and define $w(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}\cos(\omega t)\mu(d\omega)$ and $B_w(L)=\int_0^\infty w(t)e^{-tL}dt$. The window need not be pointwise nonnegative.

$$B_w(L)=\int_{\mathbb R}R_\omega(L)\mu(d\omega),\qquad R_\omega(L)=\tfrac12[(L-i\omega I)^{-1}+(L+i\omega I)^{-1}] =(L+\omega^2L^{-1})^{-1}.$$

For $\gamma=\lambda_{\min}(S)>0$, the energy inequality gives $\|e^{-tL}\|_2\le e^{-\gamma t}$. Hence Fubini is justified by $\mu(\mathbb R)/\gamma$, and pairing opposite frequencies gives the displayed identity. The sum $L+\omega^2L^{-1}$ and its inverse each have positive definite symmetric part and sector at most $\kappa$.

Positive integration therefore proves $\operatorname{sym}B_w(L)\succ0$ and $s(B_w(L))\le s(L)$. Strict positivity follows by testing each nonzero real vector against the positive definite integrands. This elementary proof does not use a spectral decomposition.

Back to chains and the Bartlett window

Choose a $\pi$-orthonormal basis $U$ of $H_0=\{f:\pi^Tf=0\}$ and set $L=-U^TD_\pi QU$. Irreducibility gives $\operatorname{sym}L\succ0$, since its quadratic form is $\tfrac12\sum_{ij}\pi_iq_{ij}(f_j-f_i)^2$ for $f=Ux$. Writing $F=UV$ yields $K_F=V^TL^{-1}V$ and the windowed form $B_{w,F}=V^TB_w(L)V$.

$$\kappa_Q=s(L)=s(L^{-1}),\qquad -\kappa_QG_{w,F}\preceq iA_{w,F}\preceq\kappa_QG_{w,F},\qquad \ker G_{w,F}=\ker F.$$

Here $G_{w,F}=\operatorname{sym}B_{w,F}$ and $A_{w,F}=\operatorname{skew}B_{w,F}$. A square invertible $V$ preserves the optimal sector constant, proving the opening full-space comparison. A restricted or redundant embedding inherits $\kappa_Q$, not necessarily $s(K_F)$.

$$w_T(t)=(1-|t|/T)_+,\qquad \mu_T(d\omega)=\frac{1-\cos(T\omega)}{\pi T\omega^2}\,d\omega,\qquad B_T(L)=L^{-1}-T^{-1}L^{-2}(I-e^{-TL}).$$

The density has removable value $T/(2\pi)$ at zero and total mass one; direct Fourier integration gives $w_T$. Thus Bartlett is covered for every finite irreducible chain, including defective ones. The classical finite-time covariance identity $2TG_{T,F}=\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(\int_0^T F(Y_t)dt)$ is already on the identification page; the triangular conversion also appears in Viscardy and Gaspard, Appendix B.

The same sector proof covers finite positive cosine mixtures, Gaussian windows and $e^{-\delta|t|}$ with $\delta>0$. Bartlett tends to $L^{-1}$ as $T\to\infty$, so its full-space sector bound is asymptotically sharp. None of this asserts a Loewner upper bound by the long-run symmetric form or a sector theorem for rectangular hard cutoffs.

Why the restricted-block comparison is false

On a uniform one-way five-cycle take $Q=P-I$, with $P$ advancing one state, and $f=(1,-1,0,0,0)^T$, $g=(-1,1,-1,1,0)^T$, $F=(f,g)$. Exact rational Poisson identities give

$$K_F=\tfrac15\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad s(K_F)=0,\qquad [\operatorname{skew}(F^TD_\pi QF)]_{12}=1/10.$$

The Taylor expansion $B_{T,F}=\tfrac T2F^TD_\pi F+\tfrac{T^2}{6}F^TD_\pi QF+E_T$ has $\|E_T\|_2\le\|F\|_\pi^2\|Q\|_2^2T^3e^{T\|Q\|_2}/24$. Use $\|F\|_\pi^2\le6/5$, $\|Q\|_2\le2$, and $e^{1/50}\le50/49$ to obtain, at $T=1/100$,

$$[\operatorname{skew}B_{T,F}]_{12}\ge\frac1{600000}-\frac1{4900000} =\frac{43}{29400000}>0.$$

Therefore $s(B_{T,F})>s(K_F)$ even for a normal chain and independent observed features. The full-chain bound remains valid.

Checks and scope

The certificate checks the rational counterexample, a defective three-cycle, random nonnormal chains with nonuniform stationary laws, and strictly accretive matrices that need not be generators. It compares time integration, block exponentials and paired-resolvent frequency integration.

For the Bartlett frequency integral truncated at $|\omega|\le\Omega$, where $\Omega\ge\sqrt2\|L\|_2$, the omitted operator norm is at most $8\|L\|_2/(3\pi T\Omega^3)$. This follows from $\|R_\omega(L)\|_2\le2\|L\|_2/\omega^2$ and $1-\cos(T\omega)\le2$. Floating consistency checks are not interval enclosures; the sector proof and the restricted-block sign are analytic.

These are stationary population statements for fixed features, not arbitrary-prior or empirical guarantees. A rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings does not count hidden regimes. The general matrix lemma is finite-dimensional; no unbounded-operator extension is asserted.
