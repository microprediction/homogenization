Translation-hull covariance

Without adiabaticity, the whole local profile replaces the instantaneous logarithmic slope.

A bounded positive slope always has compact local translates, but those translates need not become linear. Each limiting translate produces its own Gaussian functional, which characterizes every subsequential covariance constant and shows exactly why one derivative value can be insufficient.

The subsequence theorem

Use the fixed-maturity finite-chain and tangent-gauge setup of the adiabatic theorem. Assume only that $h$ is eventually $C^1$ and, for fixed constants,

$$0<c_-\le h'(x)\le c_+<\infty.$$

For a sequence $n_k=m_kT\to\infty$, put $L_k=\tfrac12\log n_k$ and

$$q_k(s)=h(L_k+s)-h(L_k).$$

Suppose $q_k\to q$ locally uniformly on $\mathbb R$. Define

$$A_q(z)=\begin{cases} \exp\{-q(-\log\rho(z))\},&z\ne0,\\ 0,&z=0. \end{cases}$$

Then for $p=1,2$, uniformly over arbitrary initial regime priors,

$$e^{p h(L_k)}\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m_k,T})^p \longrightarrow\mathbb E A_q(G)^p,\qquad G\sim N(0,\Gamma).$$

Consequently,

$$e^{2h(L_k)}\operatorname{Var}(\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m_k,T})) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Var}\{A_q(G)\}.$$

Why this is complete

The functions $q_k$ vanish at zero and are equi-Lipschitz. Arzelà–Ascoli and a diagonal argument therefore give a locally uniform limit along a further subsequence of every sequence $L_k\to\infty$.

Every limit satisfies $c_-(s-t)\le q(s)-q(t)\le c_+(s-t)$ for $s\ge t$. This bounds $A_q$ between fixed powers near zero and infinity, so spectral concentration gives uniform integrability and the finite-chain central limit theorem passes both moments.

Thus every covariance subsequence has a further limit of the displayed form, and every attainable translation-hull element supplies one. When $h''\to0$, each hull element is linear, $q(s)=cs$, recovering the moving power constant in the preceding theorem.

Error scope

Let $\delta_{k,R}=\sup_{|s|\le R}|q_k(s)-q(s)|$. On the annulus $e^{-R}\le\rho(z)\le e^R$, the normalized $p$th-power integrands differ multiplicatively by at most

$$\exp(p\delta_{k,R})-1.$$

The global statement has additive $o(1)$ error after normalization. A numerical rate requires rates for $\delta_{k,R}$, tangent-gauge convergence, the finite-chain central limit approximation and both truncated tails; none is hidden in the qualitative theorem.

A same-slope counterexample

Take $h(t)=t+0.8\sin t$, so $0.2\le h'(t)\le1.8$. Along $L_k=2\pi k+\theta$,

$$q_\theta(s)=s+0.8\{\sin(\theta+s)-\sin\theta\},$$ $$A_\theta(z)=\rho(z)\exp\{0.8[\sin\theta-\sin(\theta-\log\rho(z))]\}.$$

The phases $\theta=\pi/2$ and $3\pi/2$ both have instantaneous slope $h'(L_k)=1$, but for the scalar Gaussian gauge their variance constants are respectively

$$0.487847157924\qquad\text{and}\qquad0.291381213425.$$

The power-law constant from the instantaneous slope alone would be $1-2/\pi=0.363380227632$. The equal-slope phase gap is $0.196465944499$, proving that the adiabatic hypothesis in the preceding theorem cannot simply be dropped.

Literature and scope

Bochner (1927) gives the classical compact-translate context for almost-periodic functions; compactness here follows directly from Arzelà–Ascoli. Ney and Nummelin (1987) supply the spectral Markov limit theory.

Kovchegov, Meredith and Nir (2010) derive the Bessel occupation density used by the certificate. The translation-hull covariance theorem is derived here.

This is a fixed-feature, fixed-positive-maturity scalar variance theorem. Singular $\Gamma$ is handled on its support, and no covariance-rank statement after integrating distinct maturity loadings is made.

For several outputs, the translation-hull Gram theorem identifies every normalized cross-covariance and gives the exact nonlinear-output rank criterion.
