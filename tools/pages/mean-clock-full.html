Finite clock moments do not identify the generator

Matching any finite number of sampling-interval moments can conceal nonproportional reversible generators and their Green–Kubo strengths; mean calibration can even conceal the transition graph.

Let $Q$ be a finite conservative row generator, with fully observed regime labels, including repeats. Observation intervals are iid, independent of the chain, but all timing information is discarded and the sample count is fixed. Normalize the externally known mean interval to one physical time unit.

A mean-preserving pair of clocks and generators

For $0<a<1$, let $E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)$ and let $\sigma=\pm1$ with equal probabilities, independently. In the first model use generator $Q$ and intervals $\tau_a=(1+a\sigma)E$, redrawing both variables each interval. In the second model use unit exponential intervals and the generator

$$\boxed{\widetilde Q_a=(1-a^2)Q+a^2\{(I-Q)^{-1}-I\}.}$$

Both interval means are exactly one. Since $S=(I-Q)^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}e^{tQ}dt$ is stochastic, $S-I$ is a conservative generator, so $\widetilde Q_a$ is one too. Every stationary row of $Q$ remains stationary for $\widetilde Q_a$; reversibility is preserved when present.

The two full sampled-label transition matrices coincide exactly:

$$R_a(Q)=\tfrac12\{I-(1-a)Q\}^{-1}+\tfrac12\{I-(1+a)Q\}^{-1} =(I-\widetilde Q_a)^{-1}.$$

Indeed, writing $A=-Q$, the scalar rational transform and its reciprocal are

$$F_a(z)=\frac{1+z}{1+2z+(1-a^2)z^2},\qquad F_a(z)^{-1}-1=(1-a^2)z+a^2\frac z{1+z}.$$

The same identities hold for matrices because the factors commute and all denominators are invertible for generator spectra. For any common initial distribution, every finite label path has probability $\mu_{i_0}\prod_{j=1}^N R_{i_{j-1}i_j}$ under both models. Thus no label-only statistic or estimator can distinguish them, even with the mean interval supplied.

A minimal reversible graph-changing example

Take $a=1/2$, so the first clock mixes exponential rates $2$ and $2/3$ with equal weights. Both generators below are irreducible and reversible with stationary law $(1/3,1/3,1/3)$:

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\1&-2&1\\0&1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \widetilde Q=\frac1{32}\begin{pmatrix}-27&26&1\\26&-52&26\\1&26&-27\end{pmatrix}.$$

The shared sampled kernel is

$$R=\frac1{165}\begin{pmatrix}107&39&19\\39&87&39\\19&39&107\end{pmatrix}.$$

The nonzero decay rates are $(1,3)$ for $Q$ and $(7/8,39/16)$ for $\widetilde Q$, so this is not an unidentified scalar time unit. The original path graph has no direct endpoint transition, whereas $\widetilde Q_{13}=1/32>0$. Three states are minimal for this non-scalar ambiguity with a common positive stationary law: two-state generators with that law are proportional.

For the same centered feature $h=(1,0,-1)^\top$, define the physical-time Green–Kubo strength by $\Gamma_Q(h)=2\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(h(Y_0),h(Y_t))dt$. Since $Qh=-h$ and $\widetilde Qh=-(7/8)h$,

$$\Gamma_Q(h)=\frac43,\qquad \Gamma_{\widetilde Q}(h)=\frac{32}{21}.$$

This is failure to identify a fixed-feature covariance strength, not a change in the fixed-feature Gram rank theorem or a claim about maturity-integrated rank.

Arbitrarily small clock uncertainty

The coupling with the same $E$ gives $W_1(\mathcal L(\tau_a),\operatorname{Exp}(1))\le\mathbb E|\tau_a-E|=a$. At the same time, $\widetilde Q_a-Q=a^2\{(I-Q)^{-1}-I-Q\}$ exactly. Consequently the ambiguity exists with arbitrarily close mean-matched clocks and arbitrarily small generator perturbations, not only widely separated sampling laws.

For irreducible $Q$, $S$ is strictly positive, so every off-diagonal entry of $\widetilde Q_a$ is positive for every $a>0$. An arbitrarily small clock perturbation can therefore hide the distinction between a sparse graph and a complete graph. This is exact nonidentification when the clock law is unknown, not instability of inversion for one fixed known law.

No finite list of clock moments suffices

For every integer $K\ge1$ there exist two independent iid exponential-mixture clocks with identical first $K$ interval moments and mean one, and nonproportional three-state reversible generators with the same uniform stationary law, giving identical complete sampled-label laws. The generators can both have strictly positive off-diagonal rates. To construct the clocks, put $M=K+1$, $t_j=1/2+j/M$ and

$$b_j=2^{-M}\binom Mj,\qquad w_j^\pm=b_j\{1\pm\epsilon(-1)^j\},\quad 0<\epsilon<1,\qquad \tau_\pm\mid j\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1/t_j).$$

The finite-difference identity $\sum_{j=0}^M(-1)^j\binom Mj t_j^\ell=0$ for $0\le\ell<M$ proves equality of masses and the first $K$ interval moments, since $\mathbb E\tau_\pm^\ell=\ell!\sum_jw_j^\pm t_j^\ell$. Both means are one, and all component rates lie in $[2/3,2]$. With $F_\pm=F_0\pm\epsilon D$, where $F_0(z)=\sum_jb_j/(1+t_jz)$, direct partial fractions give

$$D(z)=\sum_j\frac{(-1)^jb_j}{1+t_jz} =\frac{M!(z/M)^M}{2^M\prod_{j=0}^M(1+t_jz)}>0\quad(z>0).$$

For $x>0$ let $r_\epsilon(x)$ be the unique positive solution of $F_-(r_\epsilon(x))=F_+(x)$. Each transform decreases continuously from one to zero, so the root exists, and $r_\epsilon(x)<x$. At $\epsilon=0$, implicit differentiation yields

$$\frac{\partial_\epsilon r_\epsilon(x)|_{\epsilon=0}}x =\frac{2D(x)}{xF_0'(x)} =-\frac{2M!}{2^MM^M}x^{M-1}+O(x^M).$$

For sufficiently small $x>0$ this relative derivative differs at $x$ and $2x$, because $M\ge2$. Let $P_1=hh^\top/2$ for $h=(1,0,-1)^\top$ and $P_2=gg^\top/6$ for $g=(1,-2,1)^\top$, and take $Q=-xP_1-2xP_2$. The off-diagonal rates are $2x/3,2x/3,x/6$, hence strictly positive.

For sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$, $Q'=-r_\epsilon(x)P_1-r_\epsilon(2x)P_2$ still has strictly positive rates, but is not proportional to $Q$ by the unequal relative derivatives. Its sampled kernel under the minus clock equals that of $Q$ under the plus clock on both nonstationary modes and the stationary mode, proving the claim. The same feature has strengths $4/(3x)$ and $4/\{3r_\epsilon(x)\}$, which differ.

The two clock laws have total variation distance at most $\epsilon$, by the corresponding mixture-weight bound. Thus the finite-moment ambiguity also exists with arbitrarily close clocks. The pairs depend on $K$: this does not assert nonidentification after the entire moment sequence is supplied.

This is failure of global identification over the clock class, not ambiguity for every particular moment vector. For example, within exponential mixtures, $\mathbb E\tau=1$ and $\mathbb E\tau^2=2$ force the unit-exponential law: the component mean has mean one and variance zero.

For an explicit mean-and-variance example, take $K=2$, $\epsilon=1/2$, $x=1$ and modes $(1,2)$. Use scales $(1/2,5/6,7/6,3/2)$ with weights $(3,3,9,1)/16$ and $(1,9,3,3)/16$, respectively; both clocks have $\mathbb E\tau=1$ and $\mathbb E\tau^2=13/6$. Exact rational sign tests enclose the alternative decay rates as

$$r_{1/2}(1)\in[0.992415305905,0.992415305907],\qquad r_{1/2}(2)\in[1.972100882987,1.972100882989].$$

The enclosures prove $r_{1/2}(2)<2r_{1/2}(1)<3r_{1/2}(1)$, certifying nonproportional generators and positive endpoint rates without trusting floating-point root finding. Kernel equality is exact for the uniquely defined roots, not an approximate alias caused by rounded rates.

Classical construction and scope

The transformed generator is a classical subordinated generator: $\widetilde Q_a=(1-a^2)Q+a^2\int_0^\infty e^{-s}(e^{sQ}-I)ds$. Equivalently, run the original chain at $T_t=(1-a^2)t+\sum_{j=1}^{N_t}E_j$, where $N_t$ has rate $a^2$ and the $E_j$ are iid unit exponentials, independently of the chain.

This latent random time has mean $t$; it is a construction of $\widetilde Q_a$, distinct from either observation clock. The drift-plus-jump generator formula is a finite-dimensional instance of Phillips (1952), Theorem 4.3. No priority claim for subordination or unknown-clock confounding is made.

Duffie and Glynn (2004), Section 4, distinguish known arrival intensities from observed sampling times. Here even a known mean does not replace the full iid interval law: the example changes spectral shape and the graph, rather than merely rescaling all speeds. Retained timestamps, elapsed-time stopping, a specified parametric clock family, noisy labels and state-dependent clocks require separate analyses.

The certificate proves the displayed benchmark identities with rational arithmetic, checks 126 kernel pairs including a defective chain and absorption, and compares three independent semigroup-integral pairs. It also checks the subordinated semigroup with an explicit omitted-Poisson-mass bound, finite-moment identities and the rational root enclosures above. These are exact population identities and focused numerical checks, not finite-sample confidence guarantees.
