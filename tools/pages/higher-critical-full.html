Higher-order critical path scores

A flatter score delays initial-regime memory; it does not generally remove it.

Let $Q$ be a fixed irreducible finite-state row generator, $\pi Q=0$, and $f$ a real nonconstant feature centered by $\pi f=0$. Write $I_c=\int_0^c f(Y_s)\,ds$, $A_c=I_c/c$, and use the group inverse $QQ^\#=Q^\#Q=I-\mathbf1\pi$, $\pi Q^\#=0$, $Q^\#\mathbf1=0$.

$$h=Q^\#f,\qquad B=2Q^\#(f\mathbin{\odot}h),\qquad \Gamma=-2\pi(f\mathbin{\odot}h)>0,\qquad \kappa_3=3\pi(f\mathbin{\odot}B).$$

The product $\odot$ is componentwise. $B$ is a raw second-moment boundary correction around the common stationary mean, not a variance correction after separately recentering each initial state.

Even-moment boundary law

For each fixed integer $k\ge2$, uniformly over the finitely many initial states,

$$\mathbb E_i A_c^{2k}-\mathbb E_\pi A_c^{2k} ={D_{k,i}\over c^{k+1}}+O(c^{-(k+2)}),$$ $$D_{k,i}=k(2k-1)!!\left[B_i\Gamma^{k-1} -{2(k-1)\over3}h_i\kappa_3\Gamma^{k-2}\right],\qquad \pi D_k=0.$$

In particular $D_{2,i}=6\Gamma B_i-4\kappa_3h_i$. The initial-mean boundary layer couples to the stationary third-cumulant rate at the same order as the second-moment boundary term.

Here is a finite-dimensional proof, requiring no occupation-time density expansion. For $Q_z=Q+z\operatorname{diag}f$, let $\Lambda(z)$ be its eigenvalue near zero and $\Pi(z)$ its spectral projector, both analytic for small $z$.

$$\Lambda(z)={\Gamma z^2\over2}+{\kappa_3z^3\over6}+O(z^4),\qquad (e_i^\top-\pi)\Pi(z)\mathbf1=-h_i z+{B_i z^2\over2}+O(z^3).$$

Differentiating the eigenvalue equations and imposing $\pi v(z)=1$ gives $v'(0)=-h$, $v''(0)=B$ and $\Lambda'''(0)=3\pi(f\odot B)$. The scalar projector normalization has zero first derivative, so its common second-order term cancels under $e_i^\top-\pi$.

The moment-generating function is $e^{cQ_z}\mathbf1$. Every fixed derivative of its nonprincipal spectral part is exponentially small times a polynomial in $c$; extracting $z^{2k}$ from the principal part, the only terms of degree $c^{k-1}$ are

$$ {B_i z^2\over2}{(c\Gamma z^2/2)^{k-1}\over(k-1)!},\qquad -h_i z\,{c\kappa_3z^3\over6}{(c\Gamma z^2/2)^{k-2}\over(k-2)!}.$$

All other terms have degree at most $c^{k-2}$. Multiplication by $(2k)!/c^{2k}$ proves the law; stationary cancellation follows from $\pi B=\pi h=0$.

The same extraction before subtracting starts gives $\mathbb E_i A_c^{2r}=O(c^{-r})$ for every fixed integer $r\ge1$.

The local spectral method is classical; see Hervé and Pène (2010). Only fixed-order moments are used here, so no high-frequency spectral or nonlattice assumption is needed.

Population coverage with fixed positive noise

Fix $\mu,\sigma>0$ and $p\in(0,1)$, take an independent standard Gaussian $Z$, and let $q_c$ be the exact stationary pooled $p$-quantile of $S_c=\mu A_c^{2k}+\sigma Z$. With $z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)$,

$$\Pr_i(S_c\le q_c)-p =-{\mu\phi(z_p)D_{k,i}\over\sigma c^{k+1}}+O(c^{-(k+2)}),\qquad k\ge2.$$

Indeed, a first-order Taylor expansion of the Gaussian CDF leaves a remainder bounded by a constant times $\mathbb E_i A_c^{4k}=O(c^{-2k})$. Also $q_c-\sigma z_p=O(c^{-k})$, so replacing its Gaussian derivative by $\phi(z_p)$ changes the state-minus-stationary gap only at order $c^{-(2k+1)}$.

All constants are fixed-model and fixed-$k$; there is no uniformity as $\sigma$ or the spectral gap vanishes. This is latent-start population coverage, not finite-calibration or distribution-free conditional conformal validity; stationary-weighted gaps cancel exactly at every $c$.

At zero noise, every even power has exactly the same pooled-quantile event, since $\{A_c^{2k}\le r^{2k}\}=\{|A_c|\le r\}$. Thus the quartic fixed-noise order cannot be extended to vanishing noise without a separate crossover analysis.

A finite-horizon Taylor and quantile budget

Set $T=\mu A_c^{2k}\ge0$, $m_{i,j}=\mathbb E_i(T/\sigma)^j$, $m_{\pi,j}=\pi m_{\cdot,j}$, and $\Delta m_{i,j}=m_{i,j}-m_{\pi,j}$. Write $H_j$ for the probabilists' Hermite polynomial and set

$$M_j={2^{(j-1)/2}\operatorname{Gamma}((j+1)/2)\over\pi} \ge\sup_x|\phi^{(j)}(x)|,\qquad R_i={M_Jm_{i,J+1}\over(J+1)!}.$$

The bound on the Gaussian derivative follows by integrating $|u|^je^{-u^2/2}/(2\pi)$. Taylor's theorem gives, uniformly in $q$,

$$F_i^{[J]}(q)=\Phi(z)-\phi(z)\sum_{j=1}^J{H_{j-1}(z)m_{i,j}\over j!},\quad z=q/\sigma, \qquad |F_i(q)-F_i^{[J]}(q)|\le R_i.$$

Choose a verified bracket $[l,u]$ for the true pooled quantile, with $F_\pi^{[J]}(l)+R_\pi<p<F_\pi^{[J]}(u)-R_\pi$. For $\delta>\mathbb E_\pi T$, Markov's inequality gives a true pooled-density lower bound throughout this bracket:

$$d={1-\mathbb E_\pi T/\delta\over\sigma} \min_{x\in[(l-\delta)/\sigma,u/\sigma]}\phi(x)>0.$$

For any $\widehat q\in[l,u]$ put $\tau=|F_\pi^{[J]}(\widehat q)-p|$. Then $|\widehat q-q_c|\le(\tau+R_\pi)/d$; expanding the density difference as well gives

$$L_i={1\over\sigma}\sum_{j=1}^J{M_j|\Delta m_{i,j}|\over j!} +{M_{J+1}(m_{i,J+1}+m_{\pi,J+1})\over\sigma(J+1)!},$$ $$\left|F_i(q_c)-p-\{F_i^{[J]}(\widehat q)-F_\pi^{[J]}(\widehat q)\}\right| \le R_i+R_\pi+L_i{\tau+R_\pi\over d}.$$

Here $L_i$ bounds $|F_i'-F_\pi'|$ globally, retaining stationary cancellation when transporting the quantile error. These are analytic, exact-arithmetic bounds, not outward-rounded bounds on matrix-exponential or quadrature evaluation.

Numerical checks

The certificate obtains $v_j(t)=\mathbb E[I_t^j]$ statewise from $v_j'=Qv_j+j\operatorname{diag}(f)v_{j-1}$, $v_0=\mathbf1$, $v_j(0)=0$ for $j>0$. This finite matrix-exponential method is a polynomial-semigroup calculation, as in Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012).

For $Q=[[-1.7,1.7],[0.4,-0.4]]$, the feature $(1,-1)$ centered by $\pi$, $\mu=1$, $\sigma=.5$ and $p=.9$, the quartic $c^3$-scaled coefficient is $(-2.24921169,0.52922628)$. Omitting skewness incorrectly halves it to $(-1.12460584,0.26461314)$.

With $J=3$, the computed positive-start scaled gap is $-2.33035202$ at $c=32$ and $-2.24604572$ at $c=512$; the maximum scaled analytic Taylor/quantile budgets are $0.00944901843$ and $1.09134867\times10^{-9}$ respectively. The budget controls the finite-moment CDF approximation, not its discrepancy from the leading asymptotic coefficient.

A nonreversible three-state check reduces the quartic coefficient error from $0.003849657$ to $0.000350222$ over the same horizons. Checks for $k=2,3,4$, independent two-state Bessel moments and independently recomputed pooled-quantile coverage all pass.
