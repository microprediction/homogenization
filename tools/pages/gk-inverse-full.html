A full Green–Kubo Gram still leaves a skew family

An exact generator identified set, even with known stationary law and known state features.

Data and result

Fix $n\ge2$, a known strictly positive stationary row law $\pi$, and the time unit. Write $D_\pi=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)$ and choose a known full centered basis $U\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}$, $r=n-1$, with

$$\pi U=0,\qquad U^\top D_\pi U=I_r,\qquad UU^\top D_\pi=I-\mathbf1\pi.$$

For an irreducible row generator $Q$, the ordered Poisson matrix and the observed symmetric Gram are

$$H=-U^\top D_\pi Q^\#U,\qquad G=\operatorname{sym}H\succ0.$$

These are fixed-state features spanning all centered functions, not maturity-dependent loadings. The exact set of generators compatible with $\pi,U,G$ is

$$\mathcal Q(G)=\left\{Q_K=-U(G+K)^{-1}U^\top D_\pi: K^\top=-K,\ (Q_K)_{ij}\ge0\text{ for }i\ne j\right\}.$$

No extra irreducibility test is required in this formula. Every displayed candidate satisfying the off-diagonal inequalities is an irreducible generator with invariant law $\pi$ and Gram $G$.

At every member with all off-diagonal rates strictly positive, the compatible set has local dimension $r(r-1)/2=(n-1)(n-2)/2$. Thus a full centered Gram generically fails to identify the generator once $n\ge3$, even when neither $\pi$ nor the feature embedding is unknown.

There is at most one reversible member: the candidate $K=0$, if its off-diagonal entries are nonnegative. For $n=2$ there is no skew parameter and the full Gram does identify the generator. Stationary time reversal sends $K$ to $-K$; it is only one symmetry inside the identified set.

For $n\le3$, every realizable full Gram has a reversible representative. With four states this can fail even when all original transition rates are strictly positive: a symmetric Gram can exclude reversibility without identifying the generator.

Proof

On the centered space put $L=-U^\top D_\pi Q U$. Since $U$ is a complete weighted orthonormal basis, $H=L^{-1}$ and $Q=-ULU^\top D_\pi$. Fixing $\operatorname{sym}H=G$ is therefore exactly the condition $H=G+K$ with $K$ skew.

Conversely $G+K$ is invertible because $x^\top(G+K)x=x^\top Gx>0$ for every nonzero real $x$. Its inverse $L$ has positive-definite symmetric part:

$$\operatorname{sym}L=(G+K)^{-\top}G(G+K)^{-1}\succ0.$$

The reconstructed $Q_K$ has zero row sums, $\pi Q_K=0$, and rank $n-1$. Off-diagonal nonnegativity makes it a Markov generator. A reducible finite generator with a strictly positive stationary law has at least two closed classes and hence a kernel of dimension at least two, so this candidate is irreducible.

Reversibility is equivalent to $L=L^\top$, hence to $H=H^\top$ and $K=0$. The reconstruction and its inverse are analytic; strict off-diagonal inequalities persist under all sufficiently small skew perturbations, proving the dimension count. Taking weighted adjoints proves the time-reversal statement.

A complete rational three-state example

For $\pi=(1,1,1)/3$, put $P=I-\mathbf1\pi$ and

$$C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad C^2=-3P.$$

For $-1\le s\le1$, define

$$a_s=\frac{1+s}{3+s^2},\qquad b_s=\frac{1-s}{3+s^2},\qquad Q_s=\begin{pmatrix}-a_s-b_s&a_s&b_s\\ b_s&-a_s-b_s&a_s\\a_s&b_s&-a_s-b_s\end{pmatrix}.$$

Every $Q_s$ is irreducible, including the one-way cycles at the endpoints, and

$$-Q_s^\#=P+\frac{s}{3}C,\qquad \operatorname{sym}(-Q_s^\#)=P,\qquad G=I_2.$$

Indeed $-Q_s=(3P-sC)/(3+s^2)$, and multiplication by $P+sC/3$ gives $P$. The skew state operator restricts to $(s/\sqrt3)J$ in an oriented centered orthonormal basis, where $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Since every skew matrix in two dimensions is a multiple of $J$, this is the entire identified set for $G=I_2$.

For any fixed centered feature matrix $F$, all these chains have $G_F=F^\top D_\pi F$. In particular $s=1/4$ has rates $(20/49,12/49)$, whereas $s=1/2$ has rates $(6/13,2/13)$: they have the same full Gram but are not time reversals or relabelings of one another. Their total exit rates differ, while reversal sends $s$ to $-s$.

When does the reversible representative exist?

In three states write $K=kJ$, with $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and put $d=\det G>0$. A direct two-by-two inverse gives

$$\operatorname{sym}(G+kJ)^{-1} =\frac{d}{d+k^2}G^{-1},\qquad \frac{Q+Q^*}{2}=\frac{d}{d+k^2}Q_{\rm rev},\qquad Q_{\rm rev}=-UG^{-1}U^\top D_\pi.$$

The additive reversibilization $(Q+Q^*)/2$ is an irreducible Markov generator (van Ginkel, van Gisbergen and Redig (2021), Lemma A.2). Thus $Q_{\rm rev}$ is its positive scalar multiple and is a valid reversible representative. Together with the two-state result, this proves existence for every realizable full Gram on at most three states, not for every arbitrary positive-definite matrix.

Four states are the first possible obstruction. For uniform $\pi$ take the irreducible generator

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-4&0&3&1\\0&-1&1&0\\ 1&0&-4&3\\3&1&0&-4\end{pmatrix}.$$

Writing $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$ and $B=\operatorname{sym}(-Q^\#)$, direct rational inversion gives the unique reversible candidate

$$Q_{\rm rev}=\Pi-(B+\Pi)^{-1} =\frac1{44}\begin{pmatrix}-192&-8&100&100\\ -8&-48&28&28\\100&28&-207&79\\100&28&79&-207\end{pmatrix}.$$

Its entry $(1,2)$ is $-2/11$, so it is not a Markov generator. This is not just a missing-edge phenomenon: for $Q_+=Q+\tfrac1{10}(\Pi-I)$ every off-diagonal rate is at least $1/40$, yet its reversible candidate has entry $(1,2)$ equal to $-43/280$. The negative entry persists under sufficiently small perturbations, since inversion is continuous.

Positive definiteness of the centered inverse is therefore weaker than the Markov sign constraints. The criterion concerns the same known stationary law, state labels and full fixed-feature embedding; it does not exclude reversible models with different hidden-state representations.

What extra data would recover the generator?

The full ordered matrix does determine it:

$$H_{ab}=\int_0^\infty\mathbb E_\pi[U_a(Y_0)U_b(Y_t)]\,dt,\qquad Q=-UH^{-1}U^\top D_\pi.$$

Thus the missing data are the antisymmetric integrated cross-correlations $K$. Such ordered information can also enter pricing through noncommuting operators. Equal symmetric Grams do not imply equal full first-order pricing operators, finite-time covariances, path laws or exact yield curves.

Scope, attribution and certificate

The result assumes the complete fixed-feature Gram is known, with fixed state labels and known $\pi$. Partial feature observations add further ambiguity; if $Q=mQ_0$ with unknown $m$, even ordered $H$ identifies only the product $Q$, not its factorization. No regime-count conclusion is drawn from a matrix obtained by integrating different maturity loadings.

The symmetric-Poisson-inverse viewpoint is classical; see Duncan, Lelièvre and Pavliotis (2016), equation (28) and Lemma 3. Their variance-reduction comparison fixes the symmetric generator part; the family here fixes its symmetric inverse instead, allowing both generator parts to change.

The certificate checks reconstruction, time reversal, positive-rate perturbations, arbitrary projected features and independent semigroup quadrature. The displayed three-state Poisson identity is also checked in exact rational arithmetic.

A reversible-realization certificate checks the three-state identity for nonuniform stationary laws and verifies both four-state obstructions with exact rational arithmetic and independent semigroup quadrature.
