No negative hard cutoff with three regimes

A stationary population theorem that allows nonnormal and defective generators.

Let $Q$ be an irreducible row generator on two or three states, $\pi$ its stationary law, and $F$ fixed real features with $\pi^TF=0$. For every $T>0$,

$$R_{T,F}=\int_0^T F^TD_\pi e^{tQ}F\,dt,\qquad \operatorname{sym}R_{T,F}\succeq0,\qquad \ker\operatorname{sym}R_{T,F}=\ker F.$$

No reversibility, normality or diagonalizability is required. This is a hard correlation cutoff, not the triangularly weighted covariance of an integrated feature. The result does not assert positivity for arbitrary empirical lag estimates or nonstationary starts.

The stationary three-state sector

Choose a $\pi$-orthonormal basis $U$ of the centered state space and put $L=-U^TD_\pi QU$. Its symmetric part is positive definite by irreducibility. For three states its strong sector satisfies

$$\kappa(L)=\|\operatorname{sym}(L)^{-1/2}\operatorname{skew}(L)\operatorname{sym}(L)^{-1/2}\|_2 \le1/\sqrt3.$$

Here is a direct proof, distinct from an eigenvalue-sector argument. Write $p_{ij}=\pi_iq_{ij}$, $u_{ij}=(p_{ij}+p_{ji})/2$, and $j=p_{12}-p_{21}=p_{23}-p_{32}=p_{31}-p_{13}$. Balance gives the equalities, while nonnegative flows give $u_{ij}\ge|j|/2$.

Pinning the third coordinate in $-D_\pi Q$ gives symmetric and skew parts

$$S_0=\begin{pmatrix}u_{12}+u_{13}&-u_{12}\\-u_{12}&u_{12}+u_{23}\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&-j/2\\j/2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \kappa^2=\frac{j^2}{4(u_{12}u_{13}+u_{12}u_{23}+u_{13}u_{23})}\le\frac13.$$

Subtracting constants turns the pinned basis into a centered basis without changing this bilinear form. The optimal sector constant is invariant under invertible real congruence, so it is also the constant for $L$. Balanced cycle decomposition is classical; see Landim and Xu, Section 4 and Lemma 4.1, not for a claimed sharp constant but for that framework.

A two-dimensional matrix lemma

For any real $2\times2$ matrix $L$ with positive definite symmetric part, $\operatorname{sym}\int_0^T e^{-tL}dt\succ0$ for every $T>0$ if either its spectrum is real or $\kappa(L)\le1/\sqrt3$. Orthogonally diagonalize its symmetric part to write

$$L=aI+N,\qquad N=\begin{pmatrix}-d&c\\-c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad a>d\ge0,\quad N^2=(d^2-c^2)I,\quad \kappa^2=c^2/(a^2-d^2).$$

The plus integrand is positive. The minus integrand is initially positive and can change sign at most once, from positive to negative, since $\tanh(ht)$ increases. Its running integral is therefore positive: it either keeps increasing or decreases after its maximum to the positive limit $(a-d)/(a^2-h^2)$.

The first local minima of $H_-$ and $H_+$ occur at $bT_-=3\pi/2-\phi$ and $bT_+=3\pi/2+\phi$, respectively. Direct integration gives

$$H_-(T_-)=\frac{a-d-r e^{-aT_-}}{a^2+b^2}>0,\qquad H_+(T_+)=\frac{a+d-r e^{-aT_+}}{a^2+b^2}>0.$$

The three-state sector puts every complex-spectrum chain in this lemma; the real-spectrum case needs no sector bound. For two states $L$ is a positive scalar. Finally write $F=UV$ and apply congruence by $V$ to obtain the opening positivity and kernel statements.

A defective chain with negative correlations

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\0&-1&1\\4&0&-4\end{pmatrix},\qquad \pi=(4/9,4/9,1/9)^T,\qquad f=(1,1,-8)^T.$$

The centered eigenvalue is $-3$ with a nontrivial Jordan block. Exact rational identities give $\pi^Tf=0$ and $(Q+3I)^2=9\mathbf1\pi^T$, hence

$$C_f(t)=e^{-3t}(8-12t),\quad R_{T,f}=\tfrac43[1+(3T-1)e^{-3T}],\quad K_f=\tfrac43,\quad B_{T,f}=\tfrac43(1-e^{-3T}).$$

Here $B_{T,f}=\int_0^T(1-t/T)C_f(t)dt$. Correlation is negative for $t>2/3$, but its hard-cutoff integral stays positive and exceeds $K_f$ for $T>1/3$. At $T=1$, $C_f\approx-0.19914827$ and $R_{T,f}\approx1.46609885$.

What remains open

A negative stationary hard cutoff needs at least four states. The exact twelve-cycle counterexample in the normal-chain note gives an upper bound of twelve; within normal chains twelve is sharp. This note does not determine the unrestricted minimum between four and twelve.

The certificate checks the stationary-flow sector formula, both spectral cases of the matrix lemma, the defective identities, and independent time integration. Floating finite-grid checks supplement the all-horizon proof; they are not interval enclosures or evidence of a sharp unrestricted threshold.

These statements concern fixed features and stationary population correlations, not conditional conformal coverage or covariance rank after integrating different maturity loadings. Positivity alone does not turn $R_{T,F}$ into a covariance or identify a hidden generator.
