Rare-tail Gram modes interlace below the collision scale

A Gaussian mode appears first, then the coalescing-tail contrast, before the next Gaussian mode.

At the critical scale, the next Gaussian mode and a rare-tail contrast form one rank-two block. If tail separation lies strictly between exponent spacing and its square, those mechanisms instead occupy two consecutive spectral scales with explicit coefficients.

Subcritical-interlacing theorem

Fix $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, $1\le r\le k-4$, a slope $\tau>0$, and weights $q_1,q_2>0$. Put $Q=q_1+q_2$, $\gamma_1=-q_2/Q$, and $\gamma_2=q_1/Q$.

Let $\varepsilon_n,\delta_n\downarrow0$ satisfy

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n}\longrightarrow0, \qquad \frac{\varepsilon_n^2}{\delta_n}\longrightarrow0.$$

Thus $\varepsilon_n^2\ll\delta_n\ll\varepsilon_n$. Define the same Gaussian-plus-two-atom triangular law as in the critical theorem, with

$$\tau_{\ell n}=\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n,\qquad A_{\ell n}=\exp(\tau_{\ell n}/\varepsilon_n),\qquad p_{\ell n}=q_\ell\varepsilon_n^{2r}A_{\ell n}^{-2c},$$

and let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$.

Write $\phi_a=(x_j^a/a!)_j$, $v=(e^{\tau x_j})_j$, and $\dot v=(x_je^{\tau x_j})_j$. Let $P_0$ and $P_1$ project orthogonally away from

$$L_0=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_r,v\},$$ $$L_1=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+1},v\},$$

and put $g=P_0\phi_{r+1}$ and $b_1=P_1\dot v$.

For $G\sim N(0,1)$, center $Z_a=|G|^c(\log|G|)^a$ and define

$$h_{r+1}=\left\|Z_{r+1} -\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}\{Z_0,\ldots,Z_r\}}Z_{r+1}\right\|_{L^2}^2.$$

Then the two consecutive small eigenvalues satisfy

$$\lambda_{r+3}(K_n)=h_{r+1}\|g\|^2\varepsilon_n^{2r+2} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r+2}),$$ $$\lambda_{r+4}(K_n)=\frac{q_1q_2}{Q}\|b_1\|^2 \varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2 +o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2).$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2)$. The first $r$ Gaussian coefficients and the two primary order-$\varepsilon_n^{2r}$ coefficients are unchanged from the preceding results.

Why the modes separate

After $L_0$ is removed, Gaussian Gram–Schmidt leaves $\varepsilon_n^{r+1}\sqrt{h_{r+1}}\,g$. The tail contrast has feature size $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n$ and is smaller because $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n\to0$; the min–max principle gives the first displayed limit.

For the next Schur complement, removing the Gaussian direction enlarges the leading space from $L_0$ to $L_1$. Weighted centering of the two tail labels then leaves $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n\sqrt{q_1q_2/Q}\,b_1$, which gives the second coefficient.

The next Gaussian covariance scale is $\varepsilon_n^{2r+4}$. It is negligible relative to $\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2$ exactly because $\varepsilon_n^2/\delta_n\to0$; higher tail-confluence terms are smaller by another factor $\delta_n^2$. Dividing that Schur complement by $\varepsilon_n^{2r}\delta_n^2$ therefore leaves only $(q_1q_2/Q)b_1b_1^\top$, proving the second limit and the remainder claim.

The coefficients are positive. The extended Chebyshev property makes the evaluations of $1,x,\ldots,x^{r+1},e^{\tau x},xe^{\tau x}$ independent at $k\ge r+4$ distinct nodes, so neither projected direction vanishes.

Six-output certificate

Take $c=\tau=q_1=1$, $q_2=2$, $x=(-5,-3,-1,1,3,5)/2$, $r=2$, $\varepsilon_n=1/n$, and $\delta_n=\varepsilon_n^{3/2}$. The predicted coefficients are

$$(h_3\|g\|^2,\ (q_1q_2/Q)\|b_1\|^2) =(0.005820458842075,\ 0.002828594686281).$$

At $n=2048$, the exact coefficient ratios are $(1.010095756755,0.990174721327)$. The 220-digit certificate also checks both primary fourth-order modes, monotone convergence, positivity, and full confluent rank.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for the displayed triangular laws and nonlinear outputs at one fixed positive maturity. It asserts the stated little-$o$ remainders, not a finite-$n$ error bound or uniformity over all separations between the two boundary scales.

At the lower boundary $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n^2\to\rho\in(0,\infty)$, the tail contrast meets the following Gaussian mode. The second-collision theorem gives the resulting rank-two block.

The result concerns the output covariance Gram at fixed maturity. It changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi gives the classical column-confluence operation, Kato supplies finite-dimensional self-adjoint perturbation theory, and Karlin and Studden supply the zero-count argument.
