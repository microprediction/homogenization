A finite-chain gamma-Poisson error bound

Let $Q$ be a row generator of a finite irreducible chain, with stationary row $\pi$, $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$ and semigroup $P_t=e^{tQ}$. Let $\ell\geq0$ be nonconstant, $\bar\ell=\pi\ell>0$, $f=\ell-\bar\ell\mathbf1$ and $F=\|f\|_\infty$.

The chain $Y$ has generator $mQ$, initial prior $\nu$ and horizon $T>0$. Write $J=\int_0^T f(Y_t)\,dt$, $\Lambda=\bar\ell T+J$, $\mu_\nu=\mathbb E_\nu\Lambda$, $v_\nu=\operatorname{Var}_\nu\Lambda$ and $M_{j,\nu}=\mathbb E_\nu(\Lambda-\mu_\nu)^j$.

Choose constants $C,\gamma>0$ satisfying the induced sup-norm bound $\|P_t-\Pi\|_{\infty\to\infty}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}$ for all $t\geq0$. Put $\beta=m\gamma$ and $\mu_{\min}=\min_i\mathbb E_i\Lambda>0$.

The all-prior bound

For every $\nu$, let $N_\nu\mid\Lambda\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda)$, and replace $\Lambda$ by a gamma variable with shape $\mu_\nu^2/v_\nu$ and scale $v_\nu/\mu_\nu$. Its Poisson mixture $N_{*,\nu}$ matches the first two count moments exactly.

$$V=\frac{2CF^2T}{\beta},\qquad B_3=\frac{18C^2F^3T}{\beta^2}+\frac{2C^3F^3}{\beta^3},\qquad B_4=\frac{24C^2F^4T^2}{\beta^2}+\frac{60C^3F^4T}{\beta^3}.$$
$$\sup_\nu d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(N_\nu),\mathcal L(N_{*,\nu})) \leq\min\{1,B(m,T)\},$$ $$B(m,T)=\frac23\left(B_3+\frac{2V^2}{\mu_{\min}}\right) +\frac13\left(B_4+3V^2+\frac{6V^3}{\mu_{\min}^2}\right).$$

This holds at every $m,T>0$, though the bound may be uninformative at small speed. Since $|\mu_\nu-\bar\ell T|\leq CF/\beta$, $\mu_{\min}\to\bar\ell T>0$ as $m\to\infty$. Hence the bound is $O(m^{-2})$ for fixed $Q,\ell,T$, uniformly over every initial prior.

Use the exact prior-specific moments, not stationary moments for every prior. The constant-intensity case is exactly Poisson and needs no gamma parameterization.

Computable constants without an eigenvector assumption

For any $h>0$, set $\rho=\tfrac12\max_{i,j}\sum_k|(P_h)_{ik}-(P_h)_{jk}|$. For at least two states, positivity and invertibility of $P_h$ give $0<\rho<1$.

$$C=\frac2\rho,\qquad \gamma=-\frac{\log\rho}{h}.$$

The row-difference contraction of a stochastic matrix is submultiplicative. Indeed, separating a zero-sum row into positive and negative parts gives $\|aP_h\|_1\leq\rho\|a\|_1$. Since $\pi$ is a convex combination of the rows of $P_{kh}$, $\|P_{kh}-\Pi\|_{\infty\to\infty}\leq2\rho^k$.

For $t=kh+s$, $0\leq s<h$, stochastic sup-norm contraction gives the same bound. The inequality $\rho^{\lfloor t/h\rfloor}\leq\rho^{-1}e^{-\gamma t}$ supplies the displayed envelope. Thus defective generators are included; the constants are conservative, not optimal.

The exact positive number $\mu_{\min}$ is computable as $\min_i[\int_0^T P_{ms}\ell\,ds]_i$. Every component of $P_{ms}\ell$ is positive for $s>0$ when $\ell$ is nonzero, even if some individual intensities vanish.

Why the signed third moment is second order

Write $a=\mathbb E_\nu J$ and $R_j=\mathbb E_\nu J^j$. Markov conditioning and $\pi f=0$ give $|a|\leq CF/\beta$ and $0\leq R_2\leq2CF^2T/\beta$.

For three ordered times $(t,t+s,t+s+u)$, put $g=fP_{mu}f$, with products componentwise. Split $P_{ms}g=\Pi g+(P_{ms}-\Pi)g$. The constant part uses the decay of $\nu P_{mt}f$, and the remainder uses the decay across $s$:

$$|\mathbb E_\nu[f(Y_t)f(Y_{t+s})f(Y_{t+s+u})]| \leq C^2F^3\{e^{-\beta(t+u)}+e^{-\beta(s+u)}\}.$$

Each integral over $t,s,u\geq0$, $t+s+u\leq T$, is at most $T/\beta^2$. Multiplying by $3!$ yields $|R_3|\leq12C^2F^3T/\beta^2$. Centering via $M_{3,\nu}=R_3-3aR_2+2a^3$ proves $|M_{3,\nu}|\leq B_3$.

For four ordered times $(t,t+s,t+s+u,t+s+u+w)$, split the propagation across the middle gap $u$. With $g=fP_{mw}f$, the constant term is $(\pi g)\mathbb E_\nu[f(Y_t)f(Y_{t+s})]$. Sup-norm contraction bounds the remainder, giving

$$|\mathbb E_\nu[f(Y_t)f(Y_{t+s})f(Y_{t+s+u})f(Y_{t+s+u+w})]| \leq C^2F^4\{e^{-\beta(s+w)}+e^{-\beta(u+w)}\}.$$

Each four-variable ordered integral is at most $T^2/(2\beta^2)$. Thus $0\leq R_4\leq24C^2F^4T^2/\beta^2$. The identity $M_{4,\nu}=R_4-4aR_3+6a^2R_2-3a^4$ gives $0\leq M_{4,\nu}\leq B_4$.

Finally $v_\nu=R_2-a^2\leq V$. Bounding the absolute third moment instead would not preserve the signed cancellation used above. No reversible spectral expansion or stationary-start assumption enters this argument.

From intensity moments to count total variation

Let $p(x)$ be the Poisson probability vector and $S$ its right-shift operator. Then $p^{(j)}(x)=(S-I)^jp(x)$ and $\|p^{(j)}(x)\|_1\leq2^j$ for $x\geq0$. Taylor expansion through degree three at $\mu_\nu$ has remainder norm at most $16|x-\mu_\nu|^4/24$.

The matched mean and variance cancel the first three terms after expectation. Gamma centered moments are $2v_\nu^2/\mu_\nu$ and $3v_\nu^2+6v_\nu^3/\mu_\nu^2$. Taking half the $\ell^1$ distance gives

$$d_{\rm TV}\leq\frac23\left|M_{3,\nu}-\frac{2v_\nu^2}{\mu_\nu}\right| +\frac13\left(M_{4,\nu}+3v_\nu^2+\frac{6v_\nu^3}{\mu_\nu^2}\right).$$

Substitution of the uniform moment bounds proves the theorem. Mixed-Poisson approximation by Poisson laws and finite signed corrections is classical; see Roos (2003). The comparison and constants here are proved directly, with no sharpness or priority claim.

Certificate and scope

verify_general_positive_counts.py computes intensity moments through a triangular matrix exponential and count probabilities through the independent matrix generating function. It checks every pure prior, a stationary prior and a uniform prior on unequal-rate, directed-cycle, defective-cycle and four-state examples, each including a zero-intensity regime.

The certificate prints the mixing constants, finite-rate bounds, moment-matching discrepancies and a Poisson-domination plus negative-binomial omitted-tail budget. Its final line is literal PASS.

There are 420 moment checks over horizons $0.2,1,3$ and 100 count-law comparisons at $T=1$. For the directed cycle $Q_{12}=Q_{23}=Q_{31}=1$ with intensities $(3,1,0)$, pure first-state start and $T=1$, the following errors are below the bound uniform over all priors.

The last measured error order is $1.983104$; the proof, not the fit, establishes the rate. Here $m^2M_{3,\nu}=1.473843$ at speed 320, so the telegraph's extra signed-third-moment cancellation is not required for second-order count accuracy.

The maximum omitted-tail TV budget across all count comparisons is $1.658\times10^{-80}$, separate from matrix-exponential roundoff. The largest scaled negative-binomial moment-matching discrepancy is $1.615\times10^{-13}$, below the roundoff tolerance $7.276\times10^{-12}$.

This is a marginal approximation at one fixed horizon, not a gamma law for the actual integrated intensity, independent increments, a full-process approximation or hidden-chain identification. Constants depend on the chain's mixing envelope and horizon, so no joint long-maturity or vanishing-gap uniformity is asserted.
