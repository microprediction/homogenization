Integer-power multi-tail collisions form a lattice

At $\delta\asymp\varepsilon^m$, tail derivative $j$ meets Gaussian order $mj$.

The two-tail staircase and the diagonal multi-tail cascade are sections of one lattice. Between its collision points, the remaining Gaussian modes stay separated with explicit coefficients.

Collision-lattice theorem

Use the $s$-tail triangular law, positive weights, distinct centered offsets, and notation of the multi-tail confluence theorem. Fix integers $s\ge2$, $m\ge1$, and $r\ge1$, assume $k\ge r+(m+1)(s-1)+2$, and let

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n^m}\longrightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty).$$

The first $r$ Gaussian modes and the two primary modes remain those of the common-slope theorem.

For $1\le a\le m(s-1)$ with $m\nmid a$, put $\ell_a=\lfloor(a-1)/m\rfloor$. Let $P_a$ project away from

$$L_a=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+a-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{\ell_a}\},$$

and set $g_a=P_a\phi_{r+a}$. Then the intervening Gaussian mode satisfies

$$\lambda_{r+a+2+\ell_a}(K_n)=h_{r+a}\|g_a\|^2\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)}).$$

At collision $j$, $1\le j\le s-1$, let $Q_j$ project away from

$$C_j=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+mj-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{j-1}\},$$

put $u_j=Q_j\phi_{r+mj}$ and $b_j=Q_jv_j$, and define

$$M_j=h_{r+mj}u_ju_j^\top+\eta_j\rho^{2j}b_jb_j^\top.$$

This matrix has rank two. If $\nu_{j,1}\ge\nu_{j,2}>0$ are its positive eigenvalues, then

$$\lambda_{r+(m+1)j+p}(K_n)=\nu_{j,p}\varepsilon_n^{2(r+mj)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+mj)}),\qquad p=1,2.$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+m(s-1))})$.

Why the lattice is complete

The Gaussian feature of order $a$ has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^{r+a}$. Tail derivative $j$ has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n^j\sim\rho^j\varepsilon_n^{r+mj}$, so equality occurs exactly at $a=mj$.

Successive Schur complements sort this finite list of amplitudes. Away from equality there is one Gaussian residual; at equality the independent Gaussian and weighted-label coordinates give the two summands of $M_j$.

The final evaluation system contains polynomials through degree $r+m(s-1)$ and exponential derivatives through $x^{s-1}e^{\tau x}$. Its $r+(m+1)(s-1)+2$ functions form an extended-Chebyshev system, proving every displayed coefficient is positive and every collision block has rank two.

Three-tail, second-power certificate

Take $s=3$, $m=2$, $k=9$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3)$, centered offsets $(-4/3,-1/3,2/3)$, nodes $(-4,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,4)$, $\rho=5/4$, and $\delta_n=\rho\varepsilon_n^2$.

The separated Gaussian coefficients are $0.862684463767136$ and $0.007452000537664$. The two collision pairs are

$$(150.157054644275519,0.038304929859394),$$ $$(0.016006901818482,0.000015159755348).$$

At $n=2048$, the six normalized ratios, in spectral order, are

$$(1.000280196238,0.998517325361,1.001450643528,$$ $$1.002748003426,1.000982192742,1.005855340472).$$

The 260-digit certificate also checks centered offsets, exact rare weights, primary modes, full confluent rank, positivity, convergence, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for fixed $s,m,k,r,c,\tau,q,\gamma,x,$ and $\rho$ at one fixed positive maturity. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not a finite-$n$ bound or uniformity over moving dimensions, powers, tail counts, nodes, offsets, or boundary ratios.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
