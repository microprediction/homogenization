Bond curves distinguish hidden Erlang stages

An equal current mean level does not imply equal prices of future cash flows.

The exact price and the observation problem

Use the two-regime Vasicek model of the summary, with $k$ stages per regime, rates $\mu_i=k/m_i>0$, $\kappa>0$, $\theta_1\ne\theta_2$ and common volatility $\sigma\ge0$. The stage chain is independent of the Brownian motion, and all these parameters and today's short rate $r$ are known. We ask about the hidden starting stage, not simultaneous identification of the parameters or generator.

The linear amplitude, not its logarithm, solves

$$P_j(T,r)=a_j(T)e^{-B(T)r},\quad a'=(Q+\operatorname{diag}g(T))a,\quad a(0)=\mathbf1, \qquad g_j(T)=-\kappa\theta_jB(T)+\tfrac12\sigma^2B(T)^2.$$

Thus $P_j(0,r)=1$. For an initial stage mixture $\nu$, average prices: $P_\nu=e^{-Br}\nu a$, rather than exponentiating a mixture of log amplitudes.

How many stages remain sets the first visible order

Fix the known current regime $i$, and write $\ell=1,\ldots,k$ for the number of exponential stages still needed to leave it, including the current one. Let $P_{i,\ell}$ denote its exact bond price and $P_i^{\rm fr}$ the Vasicek price with the level frozen at $\theta_i$. As $T\downarrow0$, at fixed model parameters,

$$D_{i,\ell}(T):=\frac{P_{i,\ell}(T,r)}{P_i^{\rm fr}(T,r)}-1 =c_{i,\ell}T^{\ell+2}+O(T^{\ell+3}),\qquad c_{i,\ell}=\frac{\kappa(\theta_i-\theta_{3-i})\mu_i^\ell}{(\ell+2)!}\ne0.$$

The same leading coefficient holds for the log-price ratio. Distinct remaining-stage counts give distinct small-maturity curves, despite identical current levels. These are short-maturity statements for a fixed chain, not estimates uniform as its switching rates also diverge.

Proof of the response order

Write $Y_s$ for the coarse regime. The common Gaussian short-rate noise cancels in the price ratio, leaving $1+D_{i,\ell}=\mathbb E e^{V_T}$, where

$$V_T=\kappa\int_0^T(\theta_i-\theta_{Y_s})B(T-s)\,ds.$$

The first exit time $U$ has Erlang density $\mu_i^\ell u^{\ell-1}e^{-\mu_i u}/(\ell-1)!$. Before exiting, $V_T=0$; after the first exit its leading contribution is $\kappa(\theta_i-\theta_{3-i})(T-U)^2/2$, since $B(t)=t+O(t^2)$. A return to regime $i$ requires a further $k$ transitions and contributes only $O(T^{\ell+k+2})$ in expectation.

$$\mathbb EV_T= \frac{\kappa(\theta_i-\theta_{3-i})\mu_i^\ell}{2(\ell-1)!} \int_0^T u^{\ell-1}(T-u)^2\,du+O(T^{\ell+3}) =c_{i,\ell}T^{\ell+2}+O(T^{\ell+3}).$$

Also $|V_T|\le CT^2$ and $\Pr(U\le T)=O(T^\ell)$. Hence $\mathbb E|e^{V_T}-1-V_T|=O(T^{\ell+4})$, proving the claim without substituting a stationary covariance for a specified stage.

A stage mixture from finitely many short maturities

Suppose $k\ge2$ and $\nu_\ell$ is any probability vector over the stages of the known current regime. Eliminate $\nu_k$ using normalization and observe $k-1$ prices at $T_j=\varepsilon t_j$, with any fixed $0<t_1<\cdots<t_{k-1}$. The linear response matrix for $(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_{k-1})$ is

$$H_{j\ell}(\varepsilon)=D_{i,\ell}(\varepsilon t_j)-D_{i,k}(\varepsilon t_j),\qquad \frac{P_\nu(T_j,r)}{P_i^{\rm fr}(T_j,r)}-1-D_{i,k}(T_j) =\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}H_{j\ell}\nu_\ell.$$

Factoring the leading power from each column gives the explicit determinant

$$\det H(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{(k-1)(k+4)/2} \left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}c_{i,\ell}\right) \left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}t_j^3\right) \left(\prod_{a<b}(t_b-t_a)\right)\{1+O(\varepsilon)\}.$$

Its coefficient is nonzero, so the mixture is identified at every sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$ for this fixed node tuple. This is a model-specific finite-output observability calculation, in the classical framework of Kalman (1960), Section 5; no new general observability principle is claimed.

It is not a stability guarantee. Indeed $H=(V+O(\varepsilon))\operatorname{diag}(c_{i,\ell}\varepsilon^{\ell+2})$, with $V_{j\ell}=t_j^{\ell+2}$ invertible, so $\sigma_{\min}(H)=\Theta(\varepsilon^{k+1})$ and $\|H^{-1}\|_2=\Theta(\varepsilon^{-(k+1)})$. These constants depend on the fixed model and nodes; no uniform noise or sample-complexity guarantee is asserted.

Certificate and scope

For $k=4$, the summary's parameters and nodes $(1,2,3)$ give determinant power $12$ and exact coefficient $512/135$. Independent high-precision ODE evaluation gives normalized determinant $0.974062724$ at $\varepsilon=1/2048$ and measured least-singular-value power $4.980995230$. The discrepancy from the limiting values is a finite-maturity remainder, not rounding certification.

At maturity one year, the pure high-regime stages with $4,3,2,1$ stages remaining give yields $325.436882$, $314.999189$, $303.885424$, $292.126447$ basis points, respectively. Thus equality of current mean levels does not imply equality of today's bond prices.

The certificate checks exact rational Taylor coefficients, unit terminal payoffs in the production routine, the determinant and singular-value orders, and stage-prior recovery. Its last line is literal PASS and its floating tolerances are portable; high-precision evaluations are not outward-rounded interval bounds.

The theorem assumes the current regime, stage count and all model parameters are known. It does not establish joint identification with unknown parameters, recovery from a noisy single quote or any regime-count inference from maturity-integrated Green–Kubo rank.
