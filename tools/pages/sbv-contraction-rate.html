Sharp contraction rates for special-BV quantiles

The regular variation contributes a fixed quarter-variation coefficient, while every quantile jump contributes an exact grid-phase oscillation.

Equal-mass conditional-mean contraction

Let $Q$ be a bounded nondecreasing quantile on $(0,1)$ and let $P_NQ$ be its mean on each interval $[(k-1)/N,k/N)$. The law $\mu_N$ of $P_NQ(U)$ is an $N$-point conditional-mean contraction of the law $\mu$ of $Q(U)$.

Both quantiles are nondecreasing, so their common-uniform coupling is optimal and

$$W_1(\mu_N,\mu)=\|Q-P_NQ\|_{L^1(0,1)}.$$

This is an equal-probability barycentric scheme. It is not unrestricted optimal scalar quantization.

The special-BV decomposition

Assume that $Q$ has no singular-continuous derivative. Then it has the special-BV representation

$$Q(u)=g(u)+\sum_{j\ge1}\Delta_j\mathbf1_{[s_j,1]}(u), \qquad g\in W^{1,1}(0,1),$$

where $0<s_j<1$, $\Delta_j>0$, $g'\ge0$ almost everywhere, and $\sum_j\Delta_j<\infty$. Put $A=\int_0^1g'(u)\,du$ and $\varphi(x)=x(1-x)$.

$$\boxed{ NW_1(\mu_N,\mu) =\frac A4+2\sum_{j\ge1}\Delta_j \varphi\!\left(\{Ns_j\}\right)+o(1). }$$

The series is absolutely convergent for every $N$, since $0\le\varphi\le1/4$. The remainder tends to zero along the full integer sequence; it is not restricted to a favorable phase subsequence.

Proof for finitely many jumps

First retain $M<\infty$ jumps and take $N$ large enough that distinct jump sites occupy distinct cells. On a jump-free cell the step part is constant, so the cell error equals the error of $g$.

On the cell containing $s_j$, the pure step has left proportion $r=\{Ns_j\}$ and right proportion $1-r$. Its exact scaled error is

$$2\Delta_jr(1-r).$$

For any two functions $v,w$ on one cell $I$,

$$\left|\|(I-P_N)(v+w)\|_{L^1(I)} -\|(I-P_N)v\|_{L^1(I)}\right| \le\|(I-P_N)w\|_{L^1(I)}.$$

The cellwise $L^1$ Poincaré bound gives $N\|(I-P_N)g\|_{L^1(I)}\le\int_I|g'|$. The right side tends to zero on the finitely many shrinking jump cells.

Off those cells, the sharp cell-average theorem gives $N\|(I-P_N)g\|_1\to\|g'\|_1/4=A/4$. Adding the exact step contributions proves the displayed formula for finite $M$.

Countably many jumps

Let $R_M$ be the tail after the first $M$ jumps. The same Poincaré estimate gives the uniform bound

$$N\|(I-P_N)R_M\|_1\le\operatorname{Var}(R_M) =\sum_{j>M}\Delta_j.$$

The omitted phase series is at most $\frac12\sum_{j>M}\Delta_j$. Both bounds vanish as $M\to\infty$, so the finite-jump result passes to countably many jumps without interchanging an uncontrolled limit and series.

The exact cluster set

Write $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, $s=(s_j)_{j\ge1}\in\mathbb T^{\mathbb N}$, and let $H=\overline{\{ns:n\in\mathbb Z\}}$ be its compact orbit closure. Positive multiples have the same closure by recurrence in the compact group.

The uniformly convergent series

$$\Phi(x)=2\sum_{j\ge1}\Delta_j\varphi(x_j)$$

defines a continuous function on $H$. The full set of subsequential limits is exactly

$$\boxed{\quad \frac A4+\Phi(H).\quad}$$

Indeed, density of the orbit gives every value in this image, compactness gives a convergent phase subsequence from every integer subsequence, and the asymptotic remainder vanishes along the full sequence.

Exactly when the scaled limit exists

Because $0\in H$ and $\Phi\ge0$, the minimum of the cluster set is $A/4$. If a jump is present, the corresponding coordinate projection of $H$ is nontrivial because $0<s_j<1$, so $\max_H\Phi>0$.

$$\liminf_{N\to\infty}NW_1(\mu_N,\mu)=\frac A4, \qquad \limsup_{N\to\infty}NW_1(\mu_N,\mu)=\frac A4+\max_H\Phi.$$

Therefore, within bounded nondecreasing special-BV quantiles, the scaled error has a limit if and only if $Q$ has no jumps. In that case the limit is $(Q(1-)-Q(0+))/4$.

Why the special-BV scope matters

A general bounded monotone quantile may also have a singular-continuous derivative. The Cantor staircase has no jumps and zero absolutely continuous variation, yet its scaled errors equal $1/6$ at $N=3^k$ and $4/15$ at $N=2\cdot3^k$.

Thus neither deleting the jump series nor replacing $A$ by only the classical derivative extends the theorem to all BV quantiles. The singular-continuous component requires its own grid-scale analysis.

Exact certificate and literature context

The exact rational certificate uses slope $2$ and jumps $(s_j,\Delta_j)=(1/3,1/2),(2/5,1/3),(5/7,1/4)$. It checks 124 exact identities, including the pure-step identity, the finite-$N$ residual bound, and the complete period-$105$ phase orbit.

The largest scaled residual is $0.011462585034$, or $0.110219198791$ of its proved bound. At $N=105$ the scaled error is exactly $0.5$; at $N=106$ it is $0.982683865999$, against phase prediction $0.984263038549$ with residual $0.001579172550$.

The rational fixture has 24 distinct cluster values, minimum $1/2$, and exact maximum $22153/22050=1.004671201814\ldots$. These finite-orbit checks illustrate the compact-group theorem; they do not replace its proof.
