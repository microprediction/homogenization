Which random clocks remove generator aliases?

Known gamma clocks have dimension-dependent alias-free ranges, with sharp thresholds for three and four labeled states.

Let $Y$ be a fully labeled finite-state chain with irreducible row generator $Q$. Independently sample at $S_k=\tau_1+\cdots+\tau_k$, where the intervals are iid gamma with known shape $\alpha>0$ and known rate $\lambda>0$. Retain every label $Z_k=Y_{S_k}$, including repeats, but discard all timing information, including total elapsed time.

For a fixed sample count, the observed labels form a Markov chain with kernel

$$R_{\alpha,\lambda}(Q)=\mathbb E e^{\tau Q} =\frac{\lambda^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\lambda t}e^{tQ}dt =(I-Q/\lambda)^{-\alpha}.$$

The power on the right uses the principal matrix logarithm of $I-Q/\lambda$. Independent sampling preserves the stationary law: $\pi R=\pi$; this is not a state-dependent Cox clock or a hidden-emission model.

A global sufficient condition

If $0<\alpha\le2$, the population kernel determines the entire generator, without a speed cap:

$$\boxed{Q=\lambda\{I-\exp[-\alpha^{-1}\operatorname{Log}R]\}.}$$

In particular, two exponential stages remain alias-free: for an Erlang-two interval, $Q=\lambda(I-R^{-1/2})$ with the principal inverse square root. This is an exact finite-rate statement, not a homogenization approximation or a finite-sample confidence bound.

To prove it, set $A=I-Q/\lambda$. Every eigenvalue $z$ of $A$ has $\operatorname{Re}z\ge1$, hence $|\arg z|<\pi/2$ and $|\alpha\arg z|<\pi$. Thus $R=A^{-\alpha}$ has no spectrum on the closed negative real axis, and $\operatorname{Log}R=-\alpha\operatorname{Log}A$.

The scalar logarithm identity holds on neighborhoods of the spectrum and therefore passes to matrices by holomorphic functional calculus, including every Jordan block. Exponentiating gives the displayed inverse, so two generators with the same kernel must agree. The argument does not assume reversibility or diagonalizability.

A stronger dimension-dependent theorem

For a known number $n\ge3$ of labeled states, the same principal inverse identifies every generator whenever

$$\boxed{0<\alpha\le\alpha_n:=\frac{2n}{n-2}.}$$

The classical generator-sector bound is $|\operatorname{Im}\mu|\le-\operatorname{Re}\mu\cot(\pi/n)$ for every $\mu\in\sigma(Q)$. See Davies (2010), proof of Theorem 9, equations (5)–(6), which attributes the sector to earlier work of Dmitriev and Dynkin. Writing $\mu=-u+iv$, every eigenvalue $z=1-\mu/\lambda$ of $A$ therefore satisfies

$$|\arg z|\le\arctan\frac{u\cot(\pi/n)}{\lambda+u} <\frac\pi2-\frac\pi n,\qquad \alpha_n\left(\frac\pi2-\frac\pi n\right)=\pi.$$

The strict inequality comes from $\lambda>0$, even if $\mu$ lies on the closed generator-sector boundary. Thus $|\alpha\arg z|<\pi$ still holds at $\alpha=\alpha_n$, and the same holomorphic logarithm identity proves the inverse, including defective generators. For two states all generator eigenvalues are real, so every $\alpha>0$ is alias-free.

The sufficient shape limits for $n=3,4,5,6$ are respectively $6,4,10/3,3$. They tend to two as the number of labels grows; this does not classify every larger shape in dimensions at least five.

A four-state alias for every shape greater than four

Let $C$ be the cyclic permutation on four labeled states, and take

$$\alpha>4,\qquad t=\tan(\pi/\alpha),\qquad r=\frac{\lambda t}{1-t}, \qquad Q_+=r(C-I),\quad Q_-=r(C^\top-I).$$

Both generators are irreducible and distinct, but their sampled kernels coincide exactly:

$$R_{\alpha,\lambda}(Q_+)=R_{\alpha,\lambda}(Q_-).$$

The four Fourier eigenvalues of $A_+=I-Q_+/\lambda$ are $1$, $1+2r/\lambda$ and $1+r/\lambda\pm ir/\lambda$. The complex pair has arguments $\pm\pi/\alpha$, so after taking power $-\alpha$ both eigenvalues equal $-\{(1+r/\lambda)^2+(r/\lambda)^2\}^{-\alpha/2}$.

The two other eigenvalues remain real, making the resulting real circulant kernel symmetric. Transposition therefore leaves it unchanged, proving the alias. Consequently the complete label-path law agrees for every fixed number of samples and every common initial law, not only its stationary covariance.

For the Erlang-five clock with $\lambda=1$, the cycle jump rate is $r=2.6568757573$, while $\|Q_+-Q_-\|_\infty=5.3137515147$. This clock has a continuous density strictly positive at every positive time, yet discarding the times loses the cycle direction. Retaining waiting times or using a calendar-time stopping rule is a different observation experiment.

A sharp three-state threshold as well

For a three-cycle $C$, put $t=\tan(\pi/\alpha)$ and

$$\alpha>6,\qquad r=\frac{\lambda t}{\sqrt3/2-3t/2},\qquad Q_+=r(C-I),\quad Q_-=r(C^\top-I).$$

For $\alpha>2$, the denominator is positive exactly when $\alpha>6$, and the complex eigenvalues of $I-Q_+/\lambda$ are $1+3r/(2\lambda)\pm i\sqrt3r/(2\lambda)$. Their arguments are $\pm\pi/\alpha$, so their $-\alpha$ powers coincide and the sampled kernel is symmetric. Hence the opposite cycles have exactly the same label-path law for every fixed sample count and common initial law.

At $\alpha=12$, $\lambda=1$, $r=1/\sqrt3$, the certificate proves $A_+^{12}=(A_+^{12})^\top$ in exact radical arithmetic. Consequently the maximal shape interval identifying every three-state generator is exactly $0<\alpha\le6$; for four states it is exactly $0<\alpha\le4$. A shape outside these ranges need not alias a particular generator, and the all-dimensions question for $2<\alpha\le4$ remains separate.

Exact recovery is not uniform stability

For $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$ and $Q_m=m(\Pi-I)$,

$$R_{\alpha,\lambda}(Q_m)=\Pi+(1+m/\lambda)^{-\alpha}(I-\Pi).$$

As $m\to\infty$, the kernels at $m$ and $2m$ approach one another while $\|Q_{2m}-Q_m\|_\infty=m\|I-\Pi\|_\infty$ diverges. Hence no uniformly continuous inverse exists over all speeds, even in the globally identified shape-two model.

With four uniform states, $\alpha=2$, $\lambda=1$ and $m=1000$, the kernel gap is $1.1223792129\times10^{-6}$ while the generator gap is $1500$. Finite-sample uncertainty must therefore be propagated through the inverse, with possible abstention; exact identification alone supplies no uniform sample complexity.

Certificate, literature and scope

The certificate checks 420 principal inversions on random, cyclic and defective generators, 20 independent gamma-density quadratures, and 15 numerical aliases. At shape six it also proves $A_+^6=(A_+^6)^\top$ in exact radical arithmetic, independent of floating-point matrix functions.

The dimension-aware extension adds 186 principal-inverse checks at the sector-bound endpoints and real defective spectra, 14 sector checks, and 12 three-cycle aliases. Two additional gamma-density integrals check the new aliases independently of fractional matrix powers; the sector theorem itself is classical, not inferred from this mesh.

Random-time identification is classical; Duffie and Glynn (2004) develop random-arrival estimation. Principal and nonprimary stochastic roots are treated by Higham and Lin (2011). The calculation here specializes those matrix-function ideas to discarded gamma waiting times, without a priority claim or a claim about arbitrary renewal clocks.

The clock law and physical time scale are known; scaling $Q$ and $\lambda$ together leaves $R$ unchanged if that scale is not supplied. No regime inference from unlabeled financial returns, estimated-clock uncertainty, or anytime confidence theorem is asserted.
