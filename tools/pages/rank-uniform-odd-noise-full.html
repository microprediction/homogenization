Odd-score calibration uniformly in panel size

A probability-coordinate profile bound removes the calibration-size factor, including at extreme ranks.

Model and result

Fix a finite irreducible row generator $Q$, stationary row $\pi$, nonconstant real $f$ with $\pi f=0$, and a fixed odd integer $d\ge1$. Write $h=Q^\#f$, $\Gamma=-2\pi(fh)>0$, $b_i=h_i/\sqrt\Gamma$ and

$$A_c={1\over c}\int_0^c f(Y_t)dt,\quad X_c=\sqrt{c/\Gamma}A_c,\quad S_c=\mu A_c^d+\sigma Z,\quad \lambda={c^{d/2}\sigma\over\mu\Gamma^{d/2}},\qquad \mu>0,\quad\sigma\ge0.$$

Use $N$ independent stationary calibration paths and an independent test path starting in state $i$, with independent standard Gaussian noises. Let $C_i(c,N,k,\lambda)$ be the probability that the test score is below the $k$th calibration score, averaged over the panel, for $1\le k\le N$. At zero noise order pairs $(S,V)$ lexicographically with independent uniform tie variables for every calibration and test observation.

The exact marginal baseline is $p_N=k/(N+1)$, so $\sum_i\pi_i C_i=p_N$. Define the reference profile, with independent standard Gaussians $G,Z$, by

$$P_d(t)=\phi(\operatorname{sgn}(t)|t|^{1/d}),\quad K_{i,\lambda}(t)=b_i\mathbb E P_d(t-\lambda Z),\quad \mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)=\mathbb E_U K_{i,\lambda}(t_U(\lambda)),$$

where $U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,N+1-k)$ and $t_u(\lambda)$ is the $u$ quantile of $G^d+\lambda Z$. For this fixed model and degree,

$$\boxed{C_i-p_N={\mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)\over\sqrt c}+R_{i,c,N,k}(\lambda),\qquad \sup_{N\ge1,\,1\le k\le N,\,i,\,\lambda\ge0}\sqrt c(1+\lambda)|R_{i,c,N,k}(\lambda)|\longrightarrow0.}$$

In particular $N=N_c$ may grow arbitrarily fast and the rank may be extreme. This is an absolute remainder statement, not a relative error bound when the leading coefficient vanishes. It strengthens the sufficient growth condition in the sample-coupling bound; that earlier bound is not incorrect.

A finite bound with no panel-size term

Let $F_{i,c}$ and $F_{\pi,c}$ be the state and stationary CDFs of $X_c$, and set

$$D_c=\|F_{\pi,c}-\Phi\|_\infty,\quad E_{i,c}(x)=\sqrt c(F_{i,c}(x)-F_{\pi,c}(x))-b_i\phi(x),$$ $$U_{i,c}=\|E_{i,c}\|_\infty,\quad L_{i,d,c}=d\int |x|^{d-1}|E_{i,c}(x)|dx,\quad m_r=\mathbb E|G|^r,\quad M_{d,p}=2d(m_{p(d-1)}m_p)^{1/p}.$$

For any even integer $p\ge2$ and every finite $c,N$,

$$\boxed{\sqrt c(1+\lambda)|R_{i,c,N,k}(\lambda)| \le(1+\lambda)\min\left(U_{i,c},{L_{i,d,c}\over\lambda\sqrt{2\pi}}\right) +|b_i|M_{d,p}D_c^{1-1/p}.}$$

At $\lambda=0$ omit the second entry of the minimum. Here $m_0=1$ and $m_{2r}=(2r-1)!!$, so $M_{d,2}=2d\sqrt{(2d-3)!!}$ with $(-1)!!=1$. This inequality is also an abstract transfer theorem for any family of laws with the displayed finite errors.

Gaussian profile in probability coordinates

Put $\rho=\lambda/(1+\lambda)$, $s=1-\rho$, and let $F_\rho$ be the CDF of $Y_\rho=sG^d+\rho Z$. This CDF is continuous and strictly increasing, including at $\rho=0,1$. For $\rho<1$ define

$$H_\rho(t)={1\over s}\mathbb E P_d((t-\rho Z)/s),\qquad H_1(t)=d!!\phi(t).$$

Thus $(1+\lambda)K_{i,\lambda}((1+\lambda)t)=b_iH_\rho(t)$. For $x<y$, $\delta=F_\rho(y)-F_\rho(x)$ and $a=1-1/p$, the key bound is

$$\boxed{|H_\rho(y)-H_\rho(x)|\le M_{d,p}\delta^a\qquad(0\le\rho\le1).}$$

For $\rho\le1/2$, the truncated-normal identity gives $H_\rho(t)=-s^{-1}\mathbb E[G\mathbf1_{\{Y_\rho\le t\}}]$. For $B=\{x<Y_\rho\le y\}$, Hölder therefore bounds the increment by $2m_p^{1/p}\mathbb P(B)^a\le M_{d,p}\delta^a$.

For $\rho\ge1/2$, Gaussian integration by parts in $G$ gives $H_\rho(t)=(d/\rho)\mathbb E[G^{d-1}\phi((t-sG^d)/\rho)]$, also valid at $\rho=1$. Integrating its derivative from $x$ to $y$ gives

$$H_\rho(y)-H_\rho(x)=-{d\over\rho}\mathbb E[G^{d-1}Z\mathbf1_B].$$

Hölder and independence bound this by $2d(m_{p(d-1)}m_p)^{1/p}\delta^a$. All differentiations and integrations are justified by finite Gaussian moments. The bound needs no interior-quantile restriction or positive lower density.

Transfer the randomized probability, not the sample

Let $\widehat F_c$ be the stationary CDF of $sX_c^d+\rho Z$. Conditioning on $Z$ and using the increasing odd root proves $\|\widehat F_c-F_\rho\|_\infty\le D_c$. For the actual $k$th augmented calibration pair $(q,V)$, its distributional transform is

$$U=\widehat F_c(q-)+V[\widehat F_c(q)-\widehat F_c(q-)]\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,N+1-k).$$

Both CDF endpoints are within $D_c$ of the continuous reference $F_\rho(q)$, hence $|F_\rho(q)-U|\le D_c$ even at atoms. The profile lemma gives the pointwise bound $|H_\rho(q)-H_\rho(F_\rho^{-1}(U))|\le M_{d,p}D_c^a$. Averaging over any beta law introduces no factor depending on $N$ or $k$.

The state-minus-pooled CDF error at the actual threshold is at most $\min(U_{i,c},L_{i,d,c}/(\lambda\sqrt{2\pi}))$ in the original $X_c^d+\lambda Z$ coordinate. This follows from supremum contraction and the Gaussian density bound after the odd-root substitution. At a zero-noise atom its augmented contrast is a convex combination of the left and right contrasts; continuity of the profile preserves the same bound.

Subtract the exact pooled rank baseline, average this contrast, and apply the probability-coordinate transfer to obtain the finite inequality. Independence of the test path from calibration is used here. This step does not assert coverage conditional on the realized panel.

Finite-chain input and the population limit

For fixed finite irreducible $Q,f,d$, the inputs are $D_c=O(c^{-1/2})$, $U_{i,c}=O(c^{-1/2})$ and $L_{i,d,c}\to0$. The spectral proof and computable Fourier/moment budgets are given in the empirical all-noise note; the argument is summarized here to specify the assumptions.

The simple dominant eigenvalue of $Q+z\operatorname{diag}f$ is $\Gamma z^2/2+O(z^3)$, and its spectral projector applied to $\mathbf1$ is $\mathbf1-zh+O(z^2)$. Signed Fourier smoothing through cubic order gives an $O(c^{-1})$ CDF remainder, with the same stationary skewness term in every state, which cancels in the contrast.

The Dirichlet form excludes imaginary-axis eigenvalues of $Q+i\theta\operatorname{diag}f$ for $\theta\ne0$. At large $|\theta|$, feature-level blocks are proper transient killed generators, giving a uniform spectral gap; unitary Schur bounds handle defective matrices. No discrete-time nonlattice assumption is needed.

Small real tilts give uniform Chernoff tails, so truncating at a sufficiently large multiple of $\sqrt{\log c}$ turns the signed CDF estimate into $L_{i,d,c}\to0$. The first term of the finite bound is at most $2\max(U_{i,c},L_{i,d,c}/\sqrt{2\pi})$, proving the asserted uniform limit.

The same probability-coordinate lemma also compares empirical and population coefficients at $p_N$, uniformly over noise:

$$(1+\lambda)|\mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)-K_{i,\lambda}(t_{p_N}(\lambda))| \le |b_i|M_{d,p}\left({p_N(1-p_N)\over N+2}\right)^{a/2}.$$

Indeed $\mathbb E U=p_N$ and $\operatorname{Var}(U)=p_N(1-p_N)/(N+2)$; Hölder followed by Jensen proves the inequality. This bound is conservative, especially at extreme ranks, but requires no truncation.

Checks, literature and scope

The certificate checks degrees $1,3,5$, five normalized noise levels, 90 probability-modulus inequalities, 150 pointwise transfers and 27 beta averages, including $N=999,k=999$. It prints $M_{d,2}=2,10.3923048454,102.4695076596$ and $J_{9,9}=.1538752731$. The linear benchmark is exact: $\mathcal K_i^{N,k}(\lambda)=b_i\mathbb E\phi(\Phi^{-1}(U))/\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}$.

Independent integration-by-parts identities, beta-order refinement with a $3\times10^{-5}$ allowance and 63 atomic-PIT endpoint comparisons check the implementation. These are numerical consistency checks, not rigorous interval enclosures or a proof of uniformity over the continuum.

The randomized probability transform is classical Rüschendorf (2009). Hervé and Pène (2010), Theorem 8.1 and Lemma 8.3 provide spectral initial-bias background, not this continuous-time calibration theorem. Lei et al. (2018), Theorem 2.2 and Remark 2.3 distinguish marginal split-conformal validity from conditional coverage.

The result keeps $Q,f,d$ fixed and requires independent calibration paths and the stated tie rule. It does not cover overlapping panels, same-panel fitting, feature-conditional validity, or degenerating mixing gaps. It develops issue 248 without closing it.
