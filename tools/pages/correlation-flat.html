Radial flat covariance

The largest Green–Kubo eigenvalue sets the logarithmic constant.

For a vector of finite-chain observables, a radial logarithmic flat map selects the easiest moderate-deviation direction. Its leading constant depends on the largest eigenvalue of the fixed-feature Green–Kubo matrix, not merely on that matrix's rank.

The theorem

Let $Q$ be an irreducible finite-state generator with stationary law $\pi$, and let $g:\mathsf S\to\mathbb R^d$ be centered and nonzero. Write $C(t)=\mathbb E_\pi[g(X_0)g(X_t)^\top]$ and define the symmetric Green–Kubo matrix

$$\Gamma=\int_0^\infty\{C(t)+C(t)^\top\}\,dt.$$

This matrix is positive semidefinite. Let $\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)>0$ denote its largest eigenvalue, and for the speed-$m$ chain set

$$W_{m,T}=\frac1T\int_0^Tg(X_{mt})\,dt.$$

For $\sigma>1$, $b>0$ and $w\ne0$, put

$$\Phi_{\sigma,b}(w)=\exp\{-\exp(b\|w\|^{-1/(\sigma-1)})\}, \qquad \Phi_{\sigma,b}(0)=0.$$

At every fixed $T>0$, uniformly over arbitrary initial regime priors,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Phi_{\sigma,b}(W_{m,T})) \sim\frac{b^{2(\sigma-1)}}{2\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)} \frac{mT}{[\log(mT)]^{2(\sigma-1)}}.$$

Why the top eigenvalue appears

Let $\Lambda(\theta)$ be the principal eigenvalue of $Q+\operatorname{diag}(\theta^\top g)$. Analytic spectral perturbation gives

$$\Lambda(\theta)=\tfrac12\theta^\top\Gamma\theta+O(\|\theta\|^3).$$

Put $L=mT$, $\beta=\sigma-1$ and $Y_L=(\log L)^\beta W_{m,T}/b^\beta$. On the active feature space $H=\operatorname{range}\Gamma$, the spectral moderate-deviation principle has speed $L/(\log L)^{2\beta}$ and rate

$$J(y)=\frac{b^{2\beta}}2y^\top\Gamma^+y,\qquad y\in H,$$

with infinite rate off $H$. The flat penalty becomes $\exp\{-L^{\|Y_L\|^{-1/\beta}}\}$, so shrinking inner and outer bounds meet on the sphere $\|y\|=1$.

The Rayleigh quotient gives

$$\min_{y\in H:\,\|y\|=1}J(y) =\frac{b^{2\beta}}{2\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)}.$$

The same leading cost holds for every fixed positive moment power. Therefore the squared first moment has twice the second-moment cost and is negligible in the variance subtraction; finite-state Perron prefactors make the argument uniform over the initial-prior simplex.

Coordinate and rank scope

The Euclidean norm is part of the fixed map, so a non-orthogonal change of observable coordinates changes both the map and $\lambda_{\max}$. Orthogonal changes preserve the theorem exactly; a transforming quadratic metric gives a coordinate-covariant extension.

Only the largest eigenvalue controls this radial tail. The rank of $\Gamma$ records the number of active linear feature directions, while post-averaging nonlinear covariance can have a different rank.

No maturity-dependent loadings are integrated here. The result therefore makes no claim about rank after pooling different maturities.

Numerical certificate

For a nonreversible three-state chain with two centered features, the Poisson equation gives Green–Kubo eigenvalues $0.171265094$ and $0.772547415$. A finite-difference Hessian of the tilted principal eigenvalue differs from the Poisson matrix by $3.022\times10^{-9}$.

With $\sigma=2$ and $b=1.5$, the predicted constant is $1.456221$. The exact two-dimensional Legendre rate is contracted over each Euclidean circle and then used in the full radial saddle.

Literature boundary

Gärtner (1977) supplies the spectral large-deviation principle for Markov additive functionals. The radial boundary contraction, top-eigenvalue constant and variance-separation step are derived above.
