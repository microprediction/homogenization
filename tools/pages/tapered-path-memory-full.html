Tapering the start of a path score

A path average remembers its starting regime at first order because it weights early observations. Making the weight vanish to order $d$ at the origin suppresses that memory to order $(r\Delta)^{-d-1}$ in this finite-chain benchmark, at the cost of a larger stationary path-variance constant.

Setup and finite-rate bound

Use states $0,1$, rather than the summary's $-1,+1$ convention. Let $p\in(0,1)$ and let the row generator be $$Q=r\begin{pmatrix}-p&p\\1-p&-(1-p)\end{pmatrix},\qquad \pi=(1-p,p),\qquad r>0.$$ Thus $r$ is the relaxation rate, not either individual jump rate.

Fix a deterministic nonnegative weight $w\in L^2[0,\Delta]$ with $\int_0^\Delta w=1$, and define $$A=\int_0^\Delta w(t)(Y_t-p)\,dt,\qquad S=\mu A+\sigma Z,$$ with independent $Z\sim N(0,1)$, fixed $\mu\in\mathbb R$, and $\sigma>0$. Write $F_i$ for the CDF conditional on $Y_0=i$, and $F_\pi=(1-p)F_0+pF_1$.

The unique pooled population quantile $q_\pi$ satisfies $F_\pi(q_\pi)=1-\alpha$, with $\alpha\in(0,1)$. It has exact stationary marginal coverage, not exact coverage conditional on either latent start.

$$\sup_x|F_i(x)-F_\pi(x)|\le \frac{|\mu|\,|i-p|}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}L_r(w),\qquad L_r(w)=\int_0^\Delta w(t)e^{-rt}\,dt.$$

If $w(t)\le Ct^d$ almost everywhere for an integer $d\ge0$, then $L_r(w)\le Cd!/r^{d+1}$. The displayed CDF bound is nonasymptotic and applies at the exact pooled threshold.

Proof by a common refresh clock

At rate $r$, refresh the state with a fresh Bernoulli($p$) draw, allowing self-transitions. Its generator is exactly $Q$, a special case of classical Poisson uniformization (van Dijk, van Brummelen and Boucherie, 2018, Section 3.2).

Couple a start at $i$ to a stationary start $J\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$ using the same future refreshes. The paths coalesce at the first refresh $\tau\sim\operatorname{Exp}(r)$, independent of $J$. Consequently, with $W(t)=\int_0^t w(s)\,ds$,

$$\delta A:=A_i-A_\pi=(i-J)W(\tau\wedge\Delta),\qquad \mathbb E\delta A=(i-p)L_r(w),\qquad \mathbb E|\delta A|=|i-p|L_r(w).$$

The derivative of $h_x(a)=\Phi((x-\mu a)/\sigma)$ is bounded by $|\mu|/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$. Apply this Lipschitz bound under the coupling to prove the CDF inequality.

A sharp leading term with an explicit remainder

Suppose also $w(t)\le Ct^d$ as above, and set $b_i=|i-p|$. Uniformly over real $x$,

$$F_i(x)-F_\pi(x) =-\frac\mu\sigma\phi(x/\sigma)(i-p)L_r(w)+R_i(x),$$ $$|R_i(x)|\le M_2\left(\sqrt{D_i V}+\frac{D_i}{2}\right),\qquad M_2=\frac{\mu^2}{\sigma^2\sqrt{2\pi e}},$$ $$D_i=\frac{b_i C^2(2d+2)!}{(d+1)^2r^{2d+2}},\qquad V=\frac{2p(1-p)}r\|w\|_2^2.$$

Indeed $\mathbb E(\delta A)^2\le D_i$ follows from $W(\tau\wedge\Delta)\le C\tau^{d+1}/(d+1)$ and exponential moments. Stationarity gives $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_s,Y_t)=p(1-p)e^{-r|t-s|}$; extending $w$ by zero and using the convolution kernel's $L^1$ norm $2/r$ gives $\mathbb EA_\pi^2\le V$.

Since $\sup_{x,a}|h_x''(a)|=M_2$, the integral Taylor formula gives

$$|h_x(A_i)-h_x(A_\pi)-h_x'(0)\delta A| \le M_2|\delta A|\left(|A_\pi|+\frac{|\delta A|}{2}\right).$$

Cauchy–Schwarz proves the remainder bound; no independence between $A_\pi$ and $\delta A$ is assumed. This is a fixed-$\mu$ result, not a small-signal expansion.

Normalized polynomial ramps and their variance cost

For $w_d(t)=(d+1)t^d/\Delta^{d+1}$ and $\lambda=r\Delta$, direct integration gives

$$L_r(w_d)=\frac{(d+1)!}{\lambda^{d+1}} \left[1-e^{-\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^d\frac{\lambda^j}{j!}\right],\qquad D_i=\frac{b_i(2d+2)!}{\lambda^{2d+2}},\qquad V=\frac{2p(1-p)(d+1)^2}{(2d+1)\lambda}.$$

For fixed $d,p,\mu,\sigma,\alpha$, as $\lambda\to\infty$,

$$F_i(q_\pi)-(1-\alpha) =-\frac\mu\sigma\phi(z_{1-\alpha})(i-p) \frac{(d+1)!}{\lambda^{d+1}}+O(\lambda^{-d-3/2}).$$

To justify evaluation at the quantile, $\mathbb EA_\pi=0$ and Taylor's formula give $\sup_x|F_\pi(x)-\Phi(x/\sigma)|\le M_2V/2$, hence $q_\pi=\sigma z_{1-\alpha}+O(\lambda^{-1})$. Combine this with the explicit remainder above and the exponentially small tail in $L_r$; no optimal remainder exponent is claimed.

For $\mu\ne0$ the leading coefficient is nonzero in both states, so the power is sharp for each fixed $d$. However the approximate-identity limit of the same covariance kernel gives

$$\operatorname{Var}_\pi A\sim \frac{2p(1-p)}\lambda\frac{(d+1)^2}{2d+1}.$$

Thus the stationary variance factor relative to the boxcar is $1,4/3,9/5$ for $d=0,1,2$. This is a bias/variance tradeoff in the path component, not a statement that total forecast error is optimized.

Any finite irreducible chain

The power law does not require a two-state, reversible, or diagonalizable generator. Let $Q$ be any fixed irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ states, with stationary law $\pi$, and let $f$ be real with $\pi f=0$. On the rescaled interval $[0,1]$, let $X$ have generator $\lambda Q$ and define

$$A_d=(d+1)\int_0^1 t^d f(X_t)\,dt,\qquad H=(-Q+\mathbf1\pi)^{-1}-\mathbf1\pi=-Q^\#.$$

Keep the same Gaussian score, pooled quantile, and $M_2$ as above, now with states indexed by $i=1,\ldots,n$. Here $\lambda=m\Delta$ corresponds to physical generator $mQ$ over horizon $\Delta$.

$$F_i(q_\pi)-(1-\alpha)= -\frac\mu\sigma\phi(z_{1-\alpha})\frac{(d+1)!(H^{d+1}f)_i}{\lambda^{d+1}} +O_Q(\lambda^{-d-3/2}).$$

All parameters other than $\lambda$ are fixed. For nonzero $f$ and $\mu\ne0$, at least one state has a nonzero leading coefficient, because $H$ is invertible on the centered subspace. An individual state coefficient may vanish; no uniformity over degenerating chains is asserted.

Computable finite-rate constants

Choose any $h>0$ and put $P_h=e^{hQ}$, $\epsilon=\sum_j\min_i(P_h)_{ij}>0$. Define $M_k(\epsilon)=\mathbb EN^k$ for $N\sim\operatorname{Geometric}(\epsilon)$ on $\{1,2,\ldots\}$; these constants obey

$$M_0=1,\qquad M_k=\frac{1+(1-\epsilon)\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{k\choose j}M_j}{\epsilon} \quad(k\ge1).$$

Write $R=\max_i f_i-\min_i f_i$, $\|u\|_\pi^2=\sum_i\pi_i u_i^2$, and let $\gamma=\inf_{\pi u=0,u\ne0}-\langle u,Qu\rangle_\pi/\|u\|_\pi^2>0$ be the additive-reversibilization gap. Set

$$B_d=R\min\left\{1,\frac{h^{d+1}M_{d+1}}{\lambda^{d+1}}\right\},\qquad D_d=R^2\min\left\{1,\frac{h^{2d+2}M_{2d+2}}{\lambda^{2d+2}}\right\},\qquad V_d=\frac{2\|f\|_\pi^2(d+1)^2}{(2d+1)\gamma\lambda}.$$

With the exact mean vector $a_d=(d+1)\int_0^1t^d e^{\lambda Qt}f\,dt$, for every $\lambda>0$,

$$\sup_x|F_i(x)-F_\pi(x)|\le\frac{|\mu|B_d}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad \left|F_i(x)-F_\pi(x)+\frac\mu\sigma\phi(x/\sigma)(a_d)_i\right| \le M_2\left(\sqrt{D_dV_d}+D_d/2\right).$$

Indeed $P_h\ge\epsilon\mathbf1\nu$, where $\nu_j=\min_i(P_h)_{ij}/\epsilon$. Split each skeleton transition into a common draw from $\nu$ with probability $\epsilon$ and a residual draw, coupling a fixed start to a stationary start; fill each intervening segment with its conditional Markov bridge.

After the first common endpoint, use the same future path, so the paths coalesce by time $hN/\lambda$. Normalization and $\int_0^u(d+1)t^d\,dt=u^{d+1}$ give $\mathbb E|\delta A|\le B_d$ and $\mathbb E(\delta A)^2\le D_d$. This is the classical minorization coupling of Rosenthal (1995, Section 2 and Appendix), applied to the skeleton; the conditional bridges preserve both continuous-time marginal laws.

The energy inequality gives $\|e^{tQ}u\|_\pi\le e^{-\gamma t}\|u\|_\pi$ for centered $u$. The stationary covariance bound then gives $\mathbb E_\pi A_d^2\le V_d$. The preceding Lipschitz/Taylor coupling proof applies unchanged, without requiring reversibility.

The semigroup integral for inverse powers also supplies an explicit mean-tail bound:

$$\left\|a_d-\frac{(d+1)!}{\lambda^{d+1}}H^{d+1}f\right\|_\pi \le\frac{(d+1)!\|f\|_\pi}{(\gamma\lambda)^{d+1}} e^{-\gamma\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^d\frac{(\gamma\lambda)^j}{j!}.$$

To obtain a component bound, divide the right side by $\sqrt{\pi_i}$. This follows by bounding the omitted tail of $\int_0^\infty s^d e^{Qs}f\,ds=d!H^{d+1}f$; the same pooled-quantile argument proves the displayed expansion.

The variance tradeoff likewise extends: integrability of the stationary covariance and rescaling give

$$\lambda\operatorname{Var}_\pi A_d\longrightarrow 2\langle f,Hf\rangle_\pi\frac{(d+1)^2}{2d+1}.$$

Thus the same relative variance factor applies for every fixed finite irreducible chain. These conservative coupling constants are computable bounds, not sharp constants.

Numerical checks and scope

For $\Delta=1$, $p=.3$, $\mu=.8$, $\sigma=.7$, and $\alpha=.1$, the certificate solves the time-dependent backward Feynman–Kac equation and inverts the Gaussian-smoothed transform. At $r=128$, the start-$1$ coverage gaps for $d=0,1,2$ are respectively $-1.0995153\times10^{-3}$, $-1.7093332\times10^{-5}$, and $-4.0015134\times10^{-7}$.

The final doubling orders are $1.003428$, $1.996824$, and $2.994699$. All fifteen cases check the finite-rate and leading-remainder inequalities at the pooled threshold and four further CDF arguments; an independent forward moment ODE checks the variance bound.

The certificate refines both Fourier quadrature and ODE tolerances and supplies an analytic Fourier-tail bound, but does not certify numerical ODE errors by interval arithmetic. The proof is self-contained within the classical uniformization framework; no priority claim is made.

The finite-chain certificate adds a nonreversible three-state chain, an exactly defective three-state chain, and a sparse directed four-cycle. Its 45 model cases and 135 CDF arguments check the coupling bounds, mean-tail bound, exact inverse-power mean formula, variance limit, and orders for $d=0,1,2$, using independent mean quadrature and moment ODEs.

The score map and weights are frozen and deterministic. These population, latent-start-conditional results do not supply a finite-calibration, adaptive-predictor, or distribution-free feature-conditional guarantee. They neither give exponential forgetting nor assert monotone improvement at every finite switching rate.
