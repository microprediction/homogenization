Irrational powers make the multi-tail spectrum simple

For fixed irrational $\alpha$, Gaussian and tail exponents interlace but never collide.

The rational theorem locates every collision block. Its irrational complement has no blocks: every post-primary mode is simple, and two floor functions give the complete ordering.

Irrational spectral-merge theorem

Use the fixed $s$-tail triangular law, positive weights, distinct centered offsets, and notation of the multi-tail confluence theorem. Fix an irrational $\alpha>0$, put $A=\lfloor\alpha(s-1)\rfloor$, assume $k\ge r+A+s+1$, and let

$$\frac{\delta_n}{\varepsilon_n^\alpha}\longrightarrow\rho\in(0,\infty).$$

The first $r$ Gaussian modes and the two primary modes are unchanged.

For each Gaussian order $1\le a\le A$, put $J_a=\lfloor a/\alpha\rfloor$, let $P_a$ project away from

$$\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+a-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{J_a}\},$$

and set $g_a=P_a\phi_{r+a}$. Then

$$\lambda_{r+a+J_a+2}(K_n)=h_{r+a}\|g_a\|^2\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+a)}).$$

For each tail derivative $1\le j\le s-1$, put $A_j=\lfloor\alpha j\rfloor$, let $R_j$ project away from

$$\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r+A_j},v_0,\ldots,v_{j-1}\},$$

and set $b_j=R_jv_j$. Then

$$\lambda_{r+A_j+j+2}(K_n)=\eta_j\rho^{2j}\|b_j\|^2 \varepsilon_n^{2(r+\alpha j)}+o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+\alpha j)}).$$

Every remaining eigenvalue is $o(\varepsilon_n^{2(r+\alpha(s-1))})$.

Why no Diophantine condition is needed

Gaussian order $a$ has amplitude $\varepsilon_n^{r+a}$ and tail derivative $j$ has amplitude $\rho^j\varepsilon_n^{r+\alpha j}$. Equality would require $\alpha=a/j$, impossible for irrational $\alpha$.

Only the finite sets $1\le a\le A$ and $1\le j\le s-1$ occur, so their exponent gaps have a positive minimum for each fixed parameter set. Sorting the amplitudes and taking successive Schur complements gives the displayed projectors and indices; no quantitative irrationality assumption enters.

The final evaluation system contains polynomials through degree $r+A$ and exponential derivatives through $x^{s-1}e^{\tau x}$. Its $r+A+s+1$ functions form an extended-Chebyshev system, so every residual coefficient is positive. Together with the rational theorem, this classifies every fixed real power $\alpha>0$.

Three-tail square-root certificate

Take $\alpha=\sqrt2$, $s=3$, $k=7$, $r=1$, $c=\tau=1$, weights $(1,2,3)$, centered offsets $(-4/3,-1/3,2/3)$, nodes $(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3)$, $\rho=5/4$, and $\delta_n=\rho\varepsilon_n^{\sqrt2}$. The event order is Gaussian $a=1$, tail $j=1$, Gaussian $a=2$, then tail $j=2$.

The four predicted coefficients are $(0.158456045596511,6.300139495145086,0.001568621700484,0.002623888839414)$. At $n=131072$, their normalized ratios are $(1.018642968388,0.981679467001,1.000022890783,1.000547196352)$.

The 260-digit certificate checks centered offsets, exact rare weights, both primary modes, full confluent rank, positivity, refinement toward all four coefficients, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is sequencewise for fixed irrational $\alpha$, $s,k,r,c,\tau$, weights, offsets, nodes, and $\rho$ at one fixed positive maturity. It gives little-$o$ remainders, not finite-$n$ bounds or uniformity as $\alpha$ moves toward a rational resonance; it makes no Diophantine-rate claim.

The result concerns a nonlinear output covariance Gram. It asserts neither fixed-parameter Green–Kubo rank nor rank after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies column-confluence geometry, Karlin and Studden supply the extended-Chebyshev zero count, and Kato supplies finite-dimensional spectral perturbation.
