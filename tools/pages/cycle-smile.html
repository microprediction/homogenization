The direction of a regime cycle in the Heston smile

A chain or continuous periodic factor that cycles in one direction contributes a commutator correction invisible to averages and symmetric covariances.

The smile from fast volatility

When volatility fluctuates fast and independently of the stock, the option price is Black–Scholes averaged over integrated variance (Hull and White 1987). Expanding that average around the mean variance gives a smile at first order in the fluctuation time scale.

Sircar and Papanicolaou (1999), section 3.3, derive it for a stationary ergodic volatility driver whose integrated fluctuations obey a central limit theorem, which a finite-state Markov chain does. The implied volatility is quadratic in log-moneyness, with a curvature set by the variance of integrated variance. Fouque, Lorig and Sircar (2016) carry the fast-factor expansion to second order with correlation and a terminal layer.

In that work the fast driver is a one-dimensional diffusion or a generic ergodic process whose correlation function enters through a variance. A stationary scalar diffusion on an interval or the line is reversible under the usual zero-current boundary conditions. Dimension alone is not enough: a diffusion on a periodic domain can carry stationary current, break detailed balance, and have an antisymmetric Green–Kubo matrix.

A continuous cycle in closed form

Let the fast factor be drifted Brownian motion on the circle,

$$dY_t=c\,dt+\sqrt{2D}\,dW_t\pmod {2\pi},\qquad D>0.$$

Its stationary law is uniform, but its stationary current is $c/(2\pi)$ and time reversal changes $c$ to $-c$. For every Fourier mode $n\ge1$, use the centered feature pair $\phi_{n,c}(y)=\cos(ny)$ and $\phi_{n,s}(y)=\sin(ny)$. Fourier orthogonality makes every cross-mode entry vanish, while the block for mode $n$ is exactly

$$K_n=\frac1{2\{D^2n^4+c^2n^2\}} \begin{pmatrix}Dn^2&cn\\-cn&Dn^2\end{pmatrix}.$$

Indeed, the off-diagonal correlation is $\mathbb E[\cos(nY_0)\sin(nY_t)]=\tfrac12e^{-Dn^2t}\sin(cnt)$; the reverse ordering has the opposite sign. Thus $c\mapsto-c$ preserves $K_n^{\rm sym}$ and flips $K_n^{\rm anti}$ for every mode.

If $\cos Y$ and $\sin Y$ multiply two fixed operators $A_c,A_s$, their first correction is

$$\frac{D}{2(D^2+c^2)}(A_c^2+A_s^2) +\frac{c}{2(D^2+c^2)}[A_c,A_s].$$

For $D=1,c=2$, the block is $K_1=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0.1&0.2\\-0.2&0.1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Direct correlation quadrature agrees below $9\times10^{-16}$. A periodic CTMC discretization converges to this block at rate 2.001, and an independent coupled-operator evolution gives a 2.002 rate for the residual after the full first-order rule. Keeping only the symmetric block leaves a first-order error; the forward-minus-reverse residual after the commutator correction converges at rate 2.000.

The theorem for every scalar periodic diffusion

The constant-coefficient example is not special. Let

$$dY_t=b(Y_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2a(Y_t)}\,dW_t\pmod {2\pi},\qquad a>0,$$

have a smooth positive stationary density $p$. Its stationary probability current

$$J=b(y)p(y)-(a(y)p(y))'$$

is constant around the circle, and its generator has the exact decomposition

$$L=p^{-1}\partial_y(ap\,\partial_y)+Jp^{-1}\partial_y=:S+A.$$

In $L^2(p)$, $S$ is self-adjoint and $A$ is skew-adjoint. Consequently the stationary time-reversed diffusion has generator $L^\dagger=S-A$, current $-J$, and drift $b^\dagger=b-2J/p$. In particular, a regular scalar diffusion on the circle is reversible if and only if $J=0$. This is the periodic specialization of the diffusion time-reversal formula of Haussmann and Pardoux (1986).

For centered observables $f,g$, define

$$K_L(f,g)=\int_0^\infty\langle f,e^{tL}g\rangle_p\,dt =\langle f,(-L)^{-1}g\rangle_p.$$

Adjointness gives the exact identity

$$K_{L^\dagger}(f,g)=K_L(g,f).$$

Thus time reversal preserves the entire symmetric Green–Kubo form and flips the entire antisymmetric form, not merely the Fourier block above. If $J\ne0$, some smooth centered pair has a nonzero antisymmetric entry; otherwise $(-L)^{-1}$, hence $L$, would be self-adjoint. A particular feature pair can still miss the current, so this is an existence statement rather than universal detectability.

If features $f_r$ multiply slow operators $A_r$, the first corrector is $\sum_{r,s}K_L(f_r,f_s)A_rA_s$. Reversal leaves its anticommutator part unchanged and flips $\sum_{r<s}K_L^{\rm anti}(f_r,f_s)[A_r,A_s]$. A feature pair can therefore contribute directional sensitivity only when its antisymmetric Green–Kubo entry and operator commutator are both nonzero.

The certificate also uses a genuinely nonconstant example, $p(y)=(1+0.35\cos y)/(2\pi)$, $a(y)=0.8+0.2\sin y$, and $J=0.08$. A flux-form periodic CTMC preserves the self/skew-adjoint decomposition exactly. For $(\cos y,\sin y+0.25\cos2y)$, its $K_{12}-K_{21}$ converges to a nonzero limit at rate 2.001, while reversing $J$ transposes the full matrix with error below $2.2\times10^{-13}$.

The central limit condition sets the order of the correction. A stationary ergodic driver with long memory, whose correlation decays like $t^{-\alpha}$ with $0 < \alpha < 1$, has no finite Green–Kubo integral.

With $\varepsilon$ the fluctuation time scale, the variance of its integrated variance scales like $\varepsilon^\alpha$ in place of $\varepsilon$, and so does the smile correction. The chain below mixes exponentially fast and has the order-$\varepsilon$ correction.

The model

The log price $X_t$ and the variance $v_t$ follow Heston's model with zero correlation. The variance level and the vol-of-vol depend on a hidden regime $y_t$:

$$dX_t = -\tfrac12 v_t\,dt + \sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t, \qquad dv_t = \kappa\,\big(\theta_{y_t} - v_t\big)\,dt + \xi_{y_t}\sqrt{v_t}\,dZ_t .$$

The regime is a three-state chain that cycles mostly one way, $1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 1$. The reverse chain has the same stationary law and the same symmetric Green–Kubo matrix. The variance reverts over $1/\kappa$, half a year, while the regimes last a few weeks.

Parameters: $\kappa = 2$; levels $\theta = (0.02,\ 0.10,\ 0.05)$; vol-of-vol $\xi = (0.2,\ 0.4,\ 1.0)$; $v_0 = 0.04$; $S_0 = 100$; zero interest; one year to expiry. Forward rate 2 and reverse rate 0.1 per year along the cycle, multiplied by a speed factor $m$.

The quantity

European call prices and their Black–Scholes implied volatilities, for a regime drawn from its stationary law. The prices come from the characteristic function of the return,

$$\phi(u) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{iu(X_T - X_0)} \mid v_0\big],$$

through Lewis's formula:

$$C = S_0 - \frac{\sqrt{S_0K}}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \operatorname{Re}\big[e^{iu\log(S_0/K)}\,\phi(u - \tfrac i2)\big]\,\frac{du}{u^2 + 1/4}.$$

Averaging

When the chain switches infinitely fast, the limit is Heston's model with the stationary averages $\bar\theta$ and $\bar\xi^2$.

The first-order rule

After the Fourier transform in $x$, the switched parameters $\theta$ and $\xi^2$ multiply two operators in the variance:

$$A_\theta = \kappa\,\partial_v, \qquad A_{\xi^2} = \tfrac12\,v\,\partial_{vv} .$$

They do not commute:

$$\big[A_\theta,\,A_{\xi^2}\big] f = \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\big(\partial_v(v f'') - v f'''\big) = \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\,f'' .$$

By the first-order rule, the averaged operator gains

$$\sum_{j,k} K^{\mathrm{sym}}_{jk}\,A_j A_k \;+\; K^{\mathrm{anti}}_{\theta\xi^2}\,\tfrac{\kappa}{2}\,\partial_{vv}, \qquad K^{\mathrm{anti}}_{\theta\xi^2} = \tfrac12\big(K_{\theta\xi^2} - K_{\xi^2\theta}\big).$$

The last term adds a constant diffusion to the variance. Its sign flips when the cycle is reversed, and the symmetric terms do not change.

The intuition is a lag. The variance follows its level with a delay of about $1/\kappa$.

When high vol-of-vol tends to follow a high level, it arrives while the variance is high and shakes it harder. When high vol-of-vol comes first, it acts on a low variance.

Method

For each Fourier frequency the coupled equations for the three regimes are solved on one finite-difference grid in $v$, which gives the numerical solution. The rule is applied on the same grid, as a first-order Duhamel correction to the averaged operator, so both share the discretisation error.
