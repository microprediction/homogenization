Two-name credit

Two companies whose default intensities share a hidden credit cycle, and the default correlation the cycle creates.

The model

Two companies have default intensities $x_1$ and $x_2$. Each follows its own square-root (CIR) process, driven by independent Brownian motions, but both take their long-run level from one hidden regime $y_t \in \{1, 2\}$, a credit cycle:

$$dx_{j} = \kappa\,\big(\theta_{j,\,y_t} - x_j\big)\,dt + \sigma\sqrt{x_j}\,dW_j, \qquad j = 1, 2 .$$

The intensities stay positive, so they are genuine default intensities. Regime 1 is a crisis and regime 2 is normal times. The cycle switches at rate $\lambda$ in each direction and starts from its stationary law, since an observer cannot see it.

There are two time scales: each intensity reverts over $1/\kappa$, and the cycle turns every $1/\lambda$.

Parameters: $\kappa = 2$, $\sigma = 0.2$; the first company has $\theta_1 = (0.8,\ 0.005)$, the second $\theta_2 = (0.6,\ 0.005)$; $x_0 = (0.05,\ 0.05)$; horizon $T = 3$.

The quantity

The survival of each company and of both:

$$S_c(T) = \mathbb{E}\Big[e^{-\int_0^T (c_1 x_1 + c_2 x_2)\,dr}\Big], \qquad c = (1,0),\ (0,1),\ (1,1).$$

The default probabilities follow from them:

$$p_1 = 1 - S_{(1,0)}, \qquad p_2 = 1 - S_{(0,1)}, \qquad p_{12} = 1 - S_{(1,0)} - S_{(0,1)} + S_{(1,1)} .$$

The correlation of the two default indicators is

$$\operatorname{corr} = \frac{p_{12} - p_1 p_2}{\sqrt{p_1(1 - p_1)\,p_2(1 - p_2)}} = \frac{S_{(1,1)} - S_{(1,0)}S_{(0,1)}}{\sqrt{p_1(1 - p_1)\,p_2(1 - p_2)}} .$$

Averaging

When the cycle switches infinitely fast, each intensity is a CIR process with the averaged level, and the two are independent. The averaged model has no default dependence at all.

Reduction to a linear system

Conditional on the regime path the two intensities are independent CIR processes. Try $u_i = e^{-B(t)(c\cdot x)}a_i(t)$. The Riccati equation for $B$,

$$B' = 1 - \kappa B - \tfrac12\sigma^2B^2, \qquad B(0) = 0,$$

involves $\kappa$ and $\sigma$, which are shared, so one $B$ serves both companies and both regimes. What remains is $a' = (Q + \operatorname{diag} g)a$ with

$$g_i(t) = -\kappa\,\big(c_1\theta_{1,i} + c_2\theta_{2,i}\big)\,B(t) .$$

This is the CIR example with the levels of the chosen companies added, and its expansion applies directly.

The survival probabilities written out

Given the regime path the two intensities are independent CIR processes, and each has the same coefficient $B$. For a stationary start the memory terms cancel, and the survival of any set of names is the CIR formula with the levels added:

$$S_c(T) = \exp\Big(-B\,(c\cdot x_0) - \kappa\,\bar\theta_c\,I_1 + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\kappa^2\tilde\theta_c^2\,I_2 - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\,\kappa^2\tilde\theta_c^2\,B^2\Big) + O(\varepsilon^3),$$ $$\begin{aligned} \bar\theta_c &= c_1\bar\theta_1 + c_2\bar\theta_2, \qquad \tilde\theta_c = c_1\tilde\theta_1 + c_2\tilde\theta_2, \qquad \bar\theta_j, \tilde\theta_j = \frac{\theta_{j,1} \pm \theta_{j,2}}{2}, \\ B &= \frac{2(e^{hT} - 1)}{(h + \kappa)(e^{hT} - 1) + 2h}, \qquad h = \sqrt{\kappa^2 + 2\sigma^2}, \\ I_1 &= \frac{2}{\sigma^2}\Big(\log\big((h + \kappa)(e^{hT} - 1) + 2h\big) - \log 2h - \frac{(\kappa + h)\,T}{2}\Big), \\ I_2 &= \frac{4}{h}\Big(\frac{hT}{(h - \kappa)^2} + \frac{a_2}{h + \kappa}\log\frac{(h + \kappa)e^{hT} + h - \kappa}{2h} \\ &\qquad\quad - \frac{a_3}{h + \kappa}\Big(\frac{1}{(h + \kappa)e^{hT} + h - \kappa} - \frac{1}{2h}\Big)\Big), \\ a_2 &= \frac{1}{h + \kappa} - \frac{h + \kappa}{(h - \kappa)^2}, \qquad a_3 = -2 - \frac{2(h + \kappa)}{h - \kappa} - a_2\,(h - \kappa). \end{aligned}$$

Since $\tilde\theta_{(1,1)}^2 - \tilde\theta_1^2 - \tilde\theta_2^2 = 2\tilde\theta_1\tilde\theta_2$, the joint survival exceeds the product of the marginals by

$$\log\frac{S_{(1,1)}(T)}{S_{(1,0)}(T)\,S_{(0,1)}(T)} = \varepsilon\,\kappa^2\,\tilde\theta_1\tilde\theta_2\,I_2 - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\,\kappa^2\,\tilde\theta_1\tilde\theta_2\,B^2 + O(\varepsilon^3).$$

This is the whole of the dependence. The averaged model has none, and the first term is the Green–Kubo covariance of the two hazards.
