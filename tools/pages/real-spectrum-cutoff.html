Real spectrum does not ensure a positive hard cutoff

An irreducible stationary chain with distinct real eigenvalues and a negative running correlation integral.

Research note, 20 October 2026. The example has 23 states and is diagonalizable over the reals, but is not normal in the stationary inner product.

Real nonpositive generator eigenvalues do not imply positivity of every stationary hard correlation cutoff. The explicit row generator below has simple entirely real spectrum and a real centered feature $f$ with

$$R_{T,f}=\int_0^T f^TD_\pi e^{tQ}f\,dt<0.$$

This is not a negative occupation covariance: that covariance has a triangular Bartlett weight and stays nonnegative. The example rules out a real-spectrum shortcut, not the separate normal-chain theorem or the all-horizon three-state result.

The chain and the feature

On states $0,1,\ldots,22$, take a single one-way cycle $0\to1\to\cdots\to22\to0$. Its exit rates are $q_0=\epsilon=10^{-100}$ and $q_i=1+i/1000$, $1\le i\le22$, with $Q_{ii}=-q_i$.

$$c=\left(\epsilon^{-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{22}q_i^{-1}\right)^{-1},\qquad \pi_0=c/\epsilon,\quad\pi_i=c/q_i,\quad T=33/2.$$

Let $g$ be the following vector, indexed from one, and set $S=\sum_i g_i/q_i$ and $f=(-\epsilon S,g)^T$.

$$g=(29,29,25,19,11,2,-7,-15,-21,-26,-28, -28,-26,-21,-15,-7,2,11,19,25,29,29)^T.$$

All eigenvalues are real and simple

The cycle's characteristic polynomial is

$$p(z)=(z+\epsilon)\prod_{i=1}^{22}(z+q_i)-\epsilon\prod_{i=1}^{22}q_i.$$

Put $h=1/3000$ and consider the 22 disjoint negative intervals $I_i=(-q_i-h,-q_i+h)$. At either endpoint, $|z+\epsilon|>1/2$ and every $|z+q_j|\ge h$, while $\prod_jq_j<2^{22}$.

$$\epsilon 2^{22}<\tfrac12 h^{22}.$$

The first term of $p$ changes sign between the endpoints of each $I_i$, and the displayed bound makes it dominate the constant perturbation. Hence each interval contains a real root; $p(0)=0$ supplies the remaining root. These are 23 distinct roots of a degree-23 polynomial, so every root is real and simple, and all nonzero roots are negative.

No floating eigensolver is used to establish this claim. The nearly repeated decay rates make numerical eigenvalues particularly unhelpful here.

A rational sign certificate

First suppress the exit from state zero and set $f_0=0$. The moving states then have a killed-path generator $J$, with $J_{ii}=-q_i$ and $J_{i,i+1}=q_i$. Their unnormalized integrated correlation is

$$H_T=\int_0^T g^T\operatorname{diag}(q_i^{-1})e^{tJ}g\,dt, \qquad A=\sum_i|g_i|/q_i,\quad M=\|g\|_\infty.$$

For $P_N=\sum_{k=0}^N T^{k+1}J^k/(k+1)!$ and $x=2(\max_iq_i)T$, the ordinary matrix power series gives

$$\left|H_T-g^T\operatorname{diag}(q_i^{-1})P_Ng\right| \le AMT\frac{x^{N+1}}{(N+2)!}\frac1{1-x/(N+3)},\qquad xThis uses $\|J\|_\infty\le2\max_iq_i$ and a geometric bound on the omitted factorial ratios. With $N=160$, exact rational arithmetic gives a polynomial value about $-103.866682489$ and a tail below $2.07\times10^{-38}$.

Restore the exit rate $\epsilon$ and the centering correction $f_0=-\epsilon S$. Both full generators, with and without that exit, have infinity-norm contractive Markov semigroups; Duhamel's formula bounds their difference by $2\epsilon t$. Summing the weighted starting-state errors and integrating gives

$$|R_{T,f}/c-H_T|\le \epsilon(|S|+A)\{T|S|+T^2M\}<3.86\times10^{-94}.$$

For the state-zero term, its weight times $f_0$ is $-S$, while the semigroup difference and the feature correction bound its evolved feature by $\epsilon(|S|+2tM)$. For the moving states the same error is multiplied by a total absolute weight $A$. Combining the rational Taylor and perturbation bounds proves the enclosure for the actual irreducible chain, not only its absorbing limit.

Interpretation and checks

The certificate checks the stationary identities, every polynomial sign change, and both error budgets using fractions. Block exponentials and time quadrature are independent value controls, not the sign or spectral proof. Matrix-exponential approximation and roundoff analysis are classical; see Higham (2005), Section 2, not as a source for this counterexample.

This construction does not improve the unrestricted minimum-state bracket $4\le n_*\le12$ tracked in issue 233. It shows why even real diagonalizability cannot replace stationary normality in a spectral argument. No minimum-state claim within the real-spectrum class is made.

The quantities here are stationary population correlations of one fixed feature, not empirical estimates, conditional conformal coverage, or ranks after integrating different maturity loadings. The extreme rate separation is explicit, and no noise-robust detection guarantee is asserted.
