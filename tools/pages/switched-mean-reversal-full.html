When simultaneous CIR switching preserves two call-crossing constraints

An exact finite-speed mean criterion, not a sharp count of all price crossings.

Let $Y$ start in the stationary law $\pi$ of an irreducible finite row generator $mQ$, $m>0$. For independent Brownian motions $B,W$, fixed $v_0\ge0$, $X_0=0$, common $\kappa>0$ and $|\rho|\le1$, consider

$$dv=(a_Y-\kappa v)dt+\xi_Y\sqrt v\,dB,\qquad dX=-v\,dt/2+\sqrt v(\rho\,dB+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,dW),\qquad a_i,\xi_i>0.$$

The regime chain is independent of the Brownian motions; only immigration and vol-of-vol switch. Compare $Q$ and $Q^\leftarrow=D_\pi^{-1}Q^TD_\pi$ with the same fixed initial variance. Assume $S=e^X$ is a true martingale and both MGFs are finite on an open interval containing $[0,1]$ at the maturities considered.

The ordinary log-return means always agree under reversal. If $k_i=\kappa-\rho\xi_i$ is constant, the share-weighted means also agree; for any number of states, $a=\alpha\mathbf1+\beta k$ is another sufficient condition.

For a nonreversible three-state chain and nonconstant $k$, this affine condition is necessary and sufficient for share-weighted mean equality at every maturity, at each fixed speed.

Whenever both means agree, a nonzero full call-price difference must have at least two sign changes and thus two distinct exact blind strikes. Zero leverage is included without any affine restriction on $a$, since then $k$ is constant. No assertion of exactly two crossings, or of four crossings under simultaneous switching, follows.

A killed-semigroup formula for the missing mean

Under the share measure $d\mathbb P^S/d\mathbb P|_{\mathcal F_T}=e^{X_T}$, the continuous change of measure leaves regime jump intensities unchanged and gives variance drift $a_Y-k_Yv$. The log-price drift becomes $+v/2$, so $\mathbb E[e^{X_T}X_T]=\tfrac12\int_0^T\mathbb E^S v_tdt$. This is the usual change of numéraire, applied here before taking moments.

Put $A=mQ-D_k$, $\langle f,g\rangle_\pi=\sum_i\pi_i f_i g_i$ for real vectors, and let $\delta$ denote forward minus reversed expectation. The exact formula is

$$\delta\mathbb E[e^{X_T}X_T]=\frac12\int_0^T(T-u)F(u)du,\qquad F(u)=\langle a,e^{uA}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,e^{uA}a\rangle_\pi.$$

To prove it, the conditional share-measure mean from variance $v$ and regime $i$ has the affine form $b_i(t)v+d_i(t)$. The backward equations give $b'=Ab$, $b(0)=\mathbf1$, $d'=mQd+D_ab$, $d(0)=0$. Stationarity therefore gives

$$\mathbb E^S_Qv_t=v_0\langle\mathbf1,e^{tA}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi +\int_0^t\langle a,e^{uA}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi du.$$

Under reversal, $A$ becomes its $\pi$-adjoint; the first term is unchanged and the integrand becomes $\langle\mathbf1,e^{uA}a\rangle_\pi$. Integrating in $t$ proves the formula and shows the mean difference is independent of $v_0$.

If $a=\alpha\mathbf1+\beta k$, the constant part cancels trivially. For the $k$ part use $A\mathbf1=-k$ and $\pi A=-\pi D_k$:

$$\langle k,e^{uA}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi =-\langle\mathbf1,Ae^{uA}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi =\langle\mathbf1,e^{uA}k\rangle_\pi.$$

Hence $F\equiv0$ for every speed and maturity, in any finite dimension. If instead $k=k_0\mathbf1$, then $e^{uA}\mathbf1=e^{-k_0u}\mathbf1$ and $\pi e^{uA}=e^{-k_0u}\pi$, so again every $a$ cancels.

A finite cancellation test, and necessity for three states

For $n$ states at any fixed speed, equality of the share means for every $T$ is equivalent to the finite set of scalar tests

$$\langle a,A^j\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,A^ja\rangle_\pi=0, \qquad j=2,\ldots,n-1.$$

Indeed, the second maturity derivative of the mean difference is $F(T)/2$. Cayley–Hamilton expresses all powers of $A$ in terms of powers through $n-1$, and $F(0)=F'(0)=0$ automatically, so these tests are equivalent to $F\equiv0$. They concern this one scalar mean, not reversibility or equality of full price laws.

Stationarity gives $F(0)=F'(0)=0$ and

$$F''(0)=m\{\langle k,Qa\rangle_\pi-\langle a,Qk\rangle_\pi\},\qquad \delta\mathbb E[e^{X_T}X_T] =\frac{mT^4}{48}\{\langle k,Qa\rangle_\pi-\langle a,Qk\rangle_\pi\}+O(T^5).$$

This is a fixed-speed small-maturity expansion, not a fast-switching or long-maturity approximation. With $\ell=\|A\|_\infty$, its remainder is at most $\|a\|_\infty e^{\ell T}\ell^3T^5/120$, by bounding the matrix exponential after degree two in $F$.

For three states, set $J=\pi_1q_{12}-\pi_2q_{21}$. The skew matrix $D_\pi Q-Q^TD_\pi$ has zero row sums, and hence

$$\langle k,Qa\rangle_\pi-\langle a,Qk\rangle_\pi =J\det\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\k_1&k_2&k_3\\a_1&a_2&a_3\end{pmatrix}.$$

Nonreversibility is exactly $J\ne0$. When $k$ is nonconstant, the determinant vanishes exactly when $a\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,k\}$. A nonzero determinant gives a nonzero $T^4$ coefficient, proving necessity for equality at all maturities.

Outside the affine family, equality at isolated maturities is not ruled out. Four states already allow nonaffine cancellation: on the unit directed four-cycle, $k=(2.12,2.24,2.12,2.24)$ and $a=(1,2,3,4)$ pass the finite tests at $m=1$ although $a$ is not affine in $k$. Thus the three-state necessity must not be extended to arbitrary finite chains.

Surviving weighted strike areas

Common original reversion gives $\mathbb E v_t=v_0e^{-\kappa t}+(\pi a)(1-e^{-\kappa t})/\kappa$, independently of cycle direction and vol-of-vol. Consequently $\delta\mathbb E X_T=-\tfrac12\delta\int_0^T\mathbb E v_tdt=0$.

For $G(k)=\mathbb E_Q(e^{X_T}-e^k)_+-\mathbb E_{Q^\leftarrow}(e^{X_T}-e^k)_+$, let $\Phi$ be the return MGF. The bilateral log-strike transform is

$$H(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{(s-1)k}G(k)dk =\frac{\Phi_Q(s)-\Phi_{Q^\leftarrow}(s)}{s(s-1)},\qquad H(0)=-\delta\mathbb E X_T=0,\quad H(1)=\delta\mathbb E[e^{X_T}X_T].$$

For $s_-<0<1<s_+$ inside the moment strip, call and put bounds give $|G(k)|\le M_+e^{-(s_+-1)k}$ on $k\ge0$ and $|G(k)|\le M_-e^{(1-s_-)k}$ on $k\le0$, where $M_\pm=\Phi_Q(s_\pm)+\Phi_{Q^\leftarrow}(s_\pm)$. These bounds justify absolute convergence and analytic continuation of the payoff-integral identity from $s>1$ to zero and one.

Thus $\int e^{-k}G(k)dk=0$ always; if the share means agree, also $\int G(k)dk=0$. A nonzero continuous $G$ cannot then have one sign, and it cannot change sign only once at $x$: $(e^{-k}-e^{-x})G(k)$ would have one nonzero sign but zero integral. This proves at least two sign changes, whereas the always-valid first constraint alone proves only at least one.

At $\rho=0$, conditional on integrated variance $V$, $X_T$ is $N(-V/2,V)$. Its put–call symmetry gives $G(k)=e^kG(-k)$, so nonzero crossings occur in reflected pairs. No such reflection symmetry is asserted for leveraged members of the affine family.

Positive-volatility witnesses and checks

Use the unit directed three-cycle, $\pi=(1,1,1)/3$, $m=T=1$, $\kappa=2$, $\rho=-0.6$, $\xi=(0.2,0.4,1)$ and $v_0=0.04$. Then $k=(2.12,2.24,2.6)$; with $a=(1,2,3)$, the determinant is $-6/25$, the $T^4$ coefficient at $m=1$ is $-1/600$, and the exact share-mean gap is approximately $-0.000286821579737$.

Instead set $a=k$, retaining simultaneous immigration and vol-of-vol switching with leverage. The share means now agree at every maturity, but a rational enclosure proves $\delta\mathbb E X_1^2>10^{-5}$, with numerical value $1.10663863634\times10^{-5}$. This proves the two call curves are different: equality at all strikes would determine the same stock law and hence the same log moments.

Both examples satisfy strict Feller inequalities $2a_i>\xi_i^2$. A common positive loading $b=1/2$ at return moment $r=2$ satisfies $\xi_i^2b^2/2-(\kappa-r\rho\xi_i)b+r(r-1)/2<0$ in every state, giving the positive moment and true-stock-martingale conditions by localization. The negative moment $r=-1/10$ has the same strict inequality with $b=1/10$, supplying the other side of the moment strip.

The certificate truncates exact rational matrix exponentials after degree $160$, with a separate induced-norm remainder for each direction. For matrix $M$ and initial moment vector $y$, put $L=\lceil\|M\|_\infty\rceil$; a target row $r$ has tail at most $\|r\|_1\|y\|_\infty 3^L L^{161}/161!$. The summed tails are below $10^{-137}$ for the share-mean obstruction and $10^{-100}$ for the second-moment witness, so neither sign relies on floating cancellation.

Independent polynomial moments and numerical integration of the killed-semigroup formula check $36$ random finite-horizon cases in three, four and six states, with errors below $10^{-12}$. Polynomial moment closure follows the framework of Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012); the affine cancellation and three-state necessity are proved above, not attributed to that reference.

The second-moment witness shows the higher cancellations used for four crossings with a single switched immigration cannot be assumed here. It does not exclude four or more actual crossings; sharpness and controlled global crossing counts remain open in issue #204. No maturity-uniform approximation, fixed-strike identification guarantee or statistical rate is claimed.
