Regular-profile covariance

The inverse flatness profile replaces the fixed power of the logarithm.

Power profiles are not essential to logarithmic flat covariance. Any increasing regularly varying profile of positive index gives a sharp scale through its asymptotic inverse.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain setup of the tangent-gauge theorem. Let $r(w)/\rho(w)\to1$ as $w\to0$, where $\rho$ is a continuous positive homogeneous gauge, and let $H:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be continuous, strictly increasing and regularly varying with index $\alpha>0$:

$$\frac{H(cx)}{H(x)}\longrightarrow c^\alpha\qquad(x\to\infty).$$

Write $A=H^{-1}$ and define, for $w\ne0$,

$$\Theta_{H,r}(w)=\exp\{-\exp(H(1/r(w)))\}, \qquad \Theta_{H,r}(0)=0.$$

For $n=mT\to\infty$ and $\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)=\max_{\|z\|_2=1}\rho(\Gamma^{1/2}z)^2$, uniformly over arbitrary initial priors,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Theta_{H,r}(W_{m,T})) \sim\frac{mT}{2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)[A(\log(mT))]^2}.$$

Profile contraction

Put $a_n=A(\log n)$. Regular variation gives $a_n\to\infty$, $a_n=o(\sqrt n)$ and

$$\frac{H(a_n/c)}{\log n}\longrightarrow c^{-\alpha}.$$

Thus the flat penalty is overwhelming for $\rho(a_nW_{m,T})<1$ and negligible at the $n/a_n^2$ moderate-deviation scale for $\rho(a_nW_{m,T})>1$. The transition boundary converges to $\rho(y)=1$.

Contracting the quadratic Green–Kubo rate over that boundary gives $1/(2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma))$. The tangent-gauge sandwich handles $r/\rho\to1$, while the first- and second-moment boundaries coincide to leading order and give the variance asymptotic.

Power profiles are a special case

Taking $H(x)=bx^{1/(\sigma-1)}$ gives $A(u)=(u/b)^{\sigma-1}$. The theorem becomes

$$-\log\operatorname{Var} \sim\frac{b^{2(\sigma-1)}}{2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)} \frac{mT}{[\log(mT)]^{2(\sigma-1)}},$$

which recovers the preceding gauge results exactly.

Numerical certificate

The certificate uses the asymmetric gauge from the preceding notes and the non-power profile $H(x)=x\log(1+x)$, whose regular-variation index is one. At $\log(mT)=8192$,

$$A(8192)=1160.724521816,$$

and the exact contracted boundary rate is $1.000024635$ times its quadratic prediction.

Literature boundary

Karamata (1933) gives the primary regular-variation framework. Gärtner (1977) and Ney and Nummelin (1987) supply the spectral Markov large-deviation framework; the inverse-profile contraction is derived above.

Scope

This remains a fixed-feature, fixed-maturity scalar variance result. It makes no nonlinear-rank or maturity-integrated identification claim. At regular-variation index zero, the Gevrey-gauge phase has a fractional-power rate.
