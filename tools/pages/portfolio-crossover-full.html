When the averaged portfolio fraction is accurate

A unique optimum at every horizon need not approach the averaged Merton fraction uniformly over all horizons. For any finite irreducible chain, an explicit optimizer bound separates the inverse-speed error from the initial-regime layer; a short-horizon profile shows why both scales matter.

Setup and bound

Let $Q$ be an irreducible finite row generator with stationary row law $\pi$, and let $Y$ have generator $mQ$. Assume common constant $r$, excess returns $b_i$, variances $s_i\ge s_{\min}>0$, $\gamma>0$, $p=1-\gamma$, and Brownian stock noise independent of $Y$.

For one deterministic unrestricted fraction $w$ held throughout the horizon, put

$$h_i(w)=r+b_iw-\gamma s_iw^2/2,\quad q_i(w)=b_i-\gamma ws_i,\quad J_{m,T,\nu}(w)=\frac{\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{p\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)dt}}{pT}.$$

At $\gamma=1$, interpret $J$ as $\mathbb E_\nu\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)dt/T$, the expected log-wealth rate. All statements below include that case.

Every global maximizer lies in $I=[L,U]$, where $L=\min_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)$ and $U=\max_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)$: $J'$ has the appropriate strict sign outside $I$, and $J(w)\le r+\max_i|b_i||w|-\gamma s_{\min}w^2/2$ gives existence. If $L=U$, this common fraction is optimal at every speed, so suppose $L<U$.

$$B=\max_I\operatorname{osc}q,\quad D=\max_I\operatorname{osc}h,\quad\delta=|p|D,\quad \bar s=\pi s,\qquad w_0=\frac{\pi b}{\gamma\bar s}.$$

For a stochastic matrix $P$, write $\beta(P)=\tfrac12\max_{i,j}\sum_k|P_{ik}-P_{jk}|$. Supply a valid mixing-area bound

$$\mathcal M\ge\int_0^\infty\beta(e^{tQ})dt.$$

Such a finite bound always exists here: if $e^{\theta Q}(i,\cdot)\ge a\mu(\cdot)$ for every row and a probability law $\mu$, $a>0$, then $\mathcal M=\theta/a$ suffices by block contraction. Positive path terms in the uniformization series give a constructive minorization even when direct rates vanish; a sampled integral alone is not a certified bound.

Solve the centered finite Poisson equation $Q\psi(w)=q(w)-(\pi q(w))\mathbf1$, $\pi\psi(w)=0$, and define

$$\Psi=\max_{w\in I}\operatorname{osc}\psi(w),\qquad \Gamma=\max_{w\in I,i}\sum_{j\ne i}Q_{ij}|\psi_j(w)-\psi_i(w)|,\qquad \Omega=\delta\mathcal M.$$

Both maxima involving $\psi$ use only the endpoints of $I$, since $\psi$ is affine in $w$ and the norms are convex. The maxima for $B,D$ check endpoints and, for $D$, the interior critical points of quadratic differences.

$$|J'_{m,T,\nu}(w)-\pi q(w)|\le \frac{\Psi}{mT}+\frac{\Omega\Gamma}{m-\Omega} \qquad(w\in I,\ m>\Omega).$$ $$|w_*-w_0|\le\frac1{\gamma\bar s} \left(\frac{\Psi}{mT}+\frac{\Omega\Gamma}{m-\Omega}\right) \quad\text{for every global maximizer }w_*.$$

The bounds hold for every $T>0$ and every initial prior $\nu$, without a uniqueness assumption. Thus averaging holds uniformly over priors and maximizers along any sequence with $m\to\infty$ and $mT\to\infty$, including arbitrarily long horizons; this is not an if-and-only-if criterion for each individual model.

Keep the tilted future close to constant

Fix $w\in I$ and subtract the common potential $\min_i(ph_i(w))$, which changes neither the tilted law nor value ratios. With $v_i=ph_i-\min_j(ph_j)$, $0\le v_i\le\delta$, set $z(t)=e^{t(mQ+D_v)}\mathbf1$.

Positivity gives $z'(t)=e^{t(mQ+D_v)}v\ge0$ componentwise. Duhamel's formula and oscillation contraction give

$$\operatorname{osc}z(t)\le\int_0^t\beta(e^{m(t-s)Q})\operatorname{osc}(D_vz(s))ds \le\frac{\Omega}{m}\max_i z_i(t).$$

Hence $\max z/\min z\le m/(m-\Omega)$ at every future duration when $m>\Omega$. The exact finite-horizon tilted chain therefore has off-diagonal rates $\widehat Q_{ij}(t)=mQ_{ij}z_j(T-t)/z_i(T-t)$ satisfying

$$|[(\widehat Q(t)-mQ)\psi]_i|\le m\frac{\Omega}{m-\Omega}\Gamma.$$

Under the full path tilt, the initial law is reweighted to $\widehat\nu_i=\nu_i z_i(T)/(\nu z(T))$. Dynkin's identity for this time-inhomogeneous chain yields the exact endpoint/perturbation decomposition

$$J'(w)-\pi q(w)=\frac{\mathbb E_w\psi(Y_T)-\mathbb E_w\psi(Y_0)}{mT} -\frac1{mT}\int_0^T\mathbb E_w[(\widehat Q(t)-mQ)\psi](Y_t)dt.$$

The endpoint difference is bounded by $\Psi$, not by an assumption of a stationary start. This proves the score bound; at a global optimum $J'=0$ and $\pi q(w)=\gamma\bar s(w_0-w)$, giving the optimizer bound.

The profile inside the initial layer

Write $c=mT$ and define the unweighted occupation prior and its optimal quadratic fraction

$$\rho_c=\frac1c\nu\int_0^c e^{tQ}dt,\qquad w_c=\frac{\rho_cb}{\gamma\rho_cs}.$$

For every $m,c>0$, every prior and every global maximizer at $T=c/m$,

$$|w_*-w_c|\le\frac{B\delta c}{4m\gamma\rho_cs}.$$

To see this, interpolate the path tilt by $e^{\lambda p\int h}$, $0\le\lambda\le1$. The derivative of its mean score $\int q/T$ is a covariance whose absolute value is at most $B\delta T/4$, by the two range bounds.

At zero tilt the mean score is $\rho_cq(w)$; at full tilt it is $J'(w)$. Integrating the covariance bound and using $J'(w_*)=0$ proves the claim, without an asymptotic expansion or a uniqueness assumption.

For log utility $\delta=0$, so $w_*=w_c$ exactly at every speed and horizon.

A nonvanishing obstruction to uniform averaging

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad b=(1,-1),\quad s=(1,1),\quad \gamma=1/2,\quad\nu=(1,0).$$ $$I=[-2,2],\quad B=2,\quad D=4,\quad\mathcal M=1/2,\quad \Psi=\Gamma=\Omega=1,\quad w_0=0.$$

The mixing coefficient is exactly $e^{-2t}$, and $\psi=(-1/2,1/2)$.

The two tilted jump rates are $mz_2/z_1$ and $mz_1/z_2$, whose sum is at least $2m$, so oscillations contract at least as $e^{-2m(t-s)}$. The range-covariance bound gives $\operatorname{Var}_w(\int q)/T\le1/m$, hence $J''=-1/2+\operatorname{Var}_w(\int q)/(2T)\le-1/2+1/(2m)$; every $m>1$ therefore has one optimum at every horizon.

$$|w_*-w_0|\le\frac2{mT}+\frac2{m-1}\quad(m>1),\qquad w_c=\frac{1-e^{-2c}}c,\qquad |w_*-w_c|\le\frac{2c}{m}.$$

At $T=1/m$, every optimizer tends to $1-e^{-2}\approx0.864665$, not zero. Indeed every $m\ge8$ already has $w_*>1/2$, since $e^2>4$ implies $w_1>3/4$ and the profile error is at most $1/4$.

This is a controlled short-horizon counterexample, not a market calibration or a failure of fixed-horizon averaging. The initial prior cannot be discarded merely because $m$ is large; the number of switching times inside the horizon matters.

Validation and literature

The certificate checks independent score formulas, Poisson identities, future-value ratios and a quadrature evaluation of the exact tilted Dynkin decomposition. It also checks the two-scale optimizer budgets and the critical profile; floating roots are consistency checks, not interval enclosures or a proof of the uniform theorem.

Poisson-equation martingale and perturbation methods are classical; see Glynn and Meyn (1996), §§1,3. The finite-horizon, time-dependent Doob transform is given in Chetrite and Touchette (2015), Appendix D; the present constants are derived directly without a priority claim for those mechanisms.

The approximation concerns a constant fraction, not adaptive or filtered trading. No claim of sharp constants, concavity outside $I$, price-error control or convergence uniform over all positive horizons is made.
