Equity options under stochastic rates

A stock and a short rate driven by one hidden regime, priced through a discounted characteristic function.

The model

A stock with Black–Scholes dynamics is discounted at a Vasicek short rate, and one hidden regime $y_t \in \{1, 2\}$ sets the parameters of both:

$$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = (r_t - q)\,dt + \sigma_{y_t}\,dW^S_t, \qquad dr_t = a\,(b_{y_t} - r_t)\,dt + \eta_{y_t}\,dW^r_t, \qquad d\langle W^S, W^r\rangle = \rho\,dt .$$

The regime switches at rate $\lambda$ in each direction. Even with $\rho = 0$ the stock and the rate are dependent, because the same regime moves the equity volatility, the level the rate reverts to, and the rate volatility.

Parameters: $\sigma = (0.30, 0.15)$, $a = 0.5$, $b = (0.05, 0.02)$, $\eta = (0.015, 0.008)$, $r_0 = 0.03$, $\rho = -0.3$, $q = 0$, $S_0 = 100$, $T = 1$, and a start in regime 1, where the stock is volatile and rates are heading up.

The quantity

With a stochastic rate the discount factor cannot be taken outside the expectation, so the object to compute is the discounted characteristic function

$$\Psi_i(w) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{-\int_0^T r_s\,ds}\,S_T^{\,iw} \mid r_0,\ y_0 = i\big].$$

It contains the bond, $\Psi_i(0) = P_i(0,T)$, and the forward, $\Psi_i(-i) = S_0e^{-qT}$. A European call is Lewis's formula in discounted form,

$$C = S_0e^{-qT} - \frac{\sqrt K}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \operatorname{Re}\big[K^{-iu}\,\Psi(u - \tfrac i2)\big]\,\frac{du}{u^2 + 1/4}.$$

To see why, write the payoff as $S_T - \min(S_T, K)$. The first part is worth $S_0e^{-qT}$. For the second, $\min(S, K) = \frac{\sqrt K}{\pi}\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Re}\big[K^{-iu}S^{\,iu + 1/2}\big]\frac{du}{u^2 + 1/4}$, and taking the discounted expectation under the integral replaces $S^{\,i(u - i/2)}$ by $\Psi(u - \tfrac i2)$.

Averaging

When the regime switches infinitely fast the limit is the Black–Scholes–Vasicek model with the averaged equity variance, rate level, rate variance and equity–rate covariance. Its discounted characteristic function is the exponential of a quadratic in $w$.

Reduction to a linear system

Solve the stock's equation: $S_T = S_0\exp\big(\int_0^T (r_s - q)\,ds + X_T\big)$ with $X_T = \int_0^T\sigma_{y_s}dW^S_s - \tfrac12\int_0^T\sigma_{y_s}^2ds$. Then

$$e^{-\int_0^T r}\,S_T^{\,iw} = S_0^{\,iw}\,e^{-iwqT}\,\exp\Big(-c\int_0^T r_s\,ds + iw\,X_T\Big), \qquad c = 1 - iw .$$

The rate is integrated with the complex weight $c$ in place of one. The function $f_i(t, r) = \mathbb{E}[\exp(-c\int_0^t r + iwX_t) \mid r_0 = r,\ y_0 = i]$ solves

$$\begin{aligned}\partial_t f_i = \;&a(b_i - r)\,\partial_r f_i + \tfrac12\eta_i^2\,\partial_{rr}f_i - c\,r\,f_i - \tfrac12\sigma_i^2(w^2 + iw)\,f_i \\ &+ iw\,\rho\,\sigma_i\eta_i\,\partial_r f_i + \sum_j Q_{ij}\,f_j, \qquad f_i(0, r) = 1 .\end{aligned}$$

The first three terms are Vasicek's with weight $c$. The fourth is the Black–Scholes exponent of $e^{iwX}$. The fifth is the cross term: a move in $W^S$ changes $e^{iwX}$ by the factor $iw\sigma_i\,dW^S$ and a correlated move in $W^r$ changes $r$, which gives $iw\rho\sigma_i\eta_i\,\partial_r$.

Try $f_i = e^{-cB(t)\,r}\,a_i(t)$. Then $\partial_r f_i = -cB f_i$ and $\partial_{rr}f_i = c^2B^2f_i$, and the terms proportional to $r$ are

$$-cB'\,r = a\,cB\,r - c\,r,$$

which cancel when $B' = 1 - aB$, so $B(t) = (1 - e^{-at})/a$ as for a bond: the weight $c$ factors out. What remains involves $t$ only. So

$$\Psi_i(w) = S_0^{\,iw}\,e^{-iwqT}\,e^{-cB(T)\,r_0}\,a_i(T), \qquad a' = (Q + \operatorname{diag} g)a, \quad a(0) = \mathbf 1,$$ $$g_i(t) = \underbrace{-a\,b_i\,c\,B + \tfrac12\eta_i^2\,c^2B^2}_{\text{rates, weight } c}\; \underbrace{-\,\tfrac12\sigma_i^2\,(w^2 + iw)}_{\text{equity}}\;\underbrace{-\,iw\,c\,\rho\,\sigma_i\eta_i\,B}_{\text{correlation}} .$$

The forcing is the sum of the rates forcing at weight $c = 1 - iw$ and the equity forcing at frequency $w$, plus the cross term. That is the whole construction: one linear system, with the discount and the return coupled through the regime.

The expansion

Let the regime switch at rate $\lambda$ in each direction, and write

$$\varepsilon = \frac1\lambda, \qquad \bar g = \tfrac12(g_1 + g_2), \qquad \tilde g = \tfrac12(g_1 - g_2),$$

where $\tilde g = \tilde\alpha\,B + \tilde\beta\,B^2 + \tilde\gamma$, with the coefficients given below. The average $\bar g$ is what the averaged model sees. The half-difference $\tilde g$ measures how much the two regimes disagree.

First order

To first order in $\varepsilon$, at fixed $t > 0$,

$$a_i(t) = \exp\Big(\int_0^t \bar g + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^t \tilde g^{\,2}\Big)\Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde g(t)\Big) + O(\varepsilon^2),$$

with the upper sign for a start in regime 1. The three factors have separate meanings, explained on the idea page.

  • The factor below is the averaged model: $$e^{\int_0^t \bar g} .$$
  • The term below is half the second cumulant of the fluctuating integral of $g$: $$\frac\varepsilon2\int_0^t\tilde g^{\,2} .$$ The square carries no conjugate, and here $g$ is complex for every $w$, so the term is complex and is not a variance. For real $g$ it is half the variance, as on the idea page. It is the same for both starting regimes.
  • The bracket is the memory of the starting regime. The switch stays near its starting state for about $1/(2\lambda)$, which shifts the integral by $\pm\tilde g/(2\lambda)$.

Every order

Add and subtract the two equations for $a_1$ and $a_2$. The mean $m = \tfrac12(a_1 + a_2)$ and the normalized difference $\omega = (a_1 - a_2)/(a_1 + a_2)$ then satisfy

$$\omega' = \tilde g\,(1 - \omega^2) - 2\lambda\,\omega, \qquad \omega(0) = 0,$$ $$\log m(t) = \int_0^t \big(\bar g + \tilde g\,\omega\big), \qquad a_{1,2} = m\,(1 \pm \omega).$$

The first equation has a large restoring term $-2\lambda\omega$: the switch pulls the two regimes together at rate $2\lambda$, while $\tilde g$ pushes them apart. So $\omega$ stays close to the value that balances the two, and expanding in $\varepsilon$ finds that value order by order. Write

$$\omega = \sum_n \varepsilon^n\omega_n$$

and match powers of $\varepsilon$:

$$\omega_1 = \frac{\tilde g}{2}, \qquad \omega_{n+1} = -\frac12\Big(\omega_n' + \tilde g\sum_{i+j=n}\omega_i\,\omega_j\Big).$$

This is the two-state case of the solvability argument: at each order the restoring term can be inverted, which here is a division by $2\lambda$.

The series does not satisfy $\omega(0) = 0$.

An initial layer of width about $1/(2\lambda)$ repairs the start and is solved order by order in the same way. Its leading term adds $\mp\tfrac12\varepsilon\,\tilde g(0)\,e^{-2\lambda t}$ to the first-order bracket, which returns the bracket to $1$ at $t = 0$; when $\tilde g(0) \ne 0$ the first-order formula above is therefore accurate to $O(\varepsilon^2)$ only at fixed $t > 0$. The engine page gives the version for any number of regimes.

The expansion needs $|\tilde g|/\lambda$ to be small, and $\tilde g$ grows like $w^2$. The Fourier integral is therefore stopped at the frequency where the averaged function falls below $e^{-40}$.

Explicit formulas

Second order

Two more steps give the next order. The recursion gives $\omega_2 = -\omega_1'/2 = -\tilde g'/4$, which adds $-\tfrac14\varepsilon^2\int_0^T\tilde g\,\tilde g' = -\tfrac18\varepsilon^2\big(\tilde g(T)^2 - \tilde g(0)^2\big)$ to the exponent. The initial layer subtracts $\tfrac12\varepsilon\,\tilde g(0)\,e^{-2\lambda t}$ from $\omega$, and through $\int\tilde g\,\omega$ that adds $-\tfrac14\varepsilon^2\,\tilde g(0)^2$. Together, up to terms of size $e^{-2\lambda T}$,

$$a_{1,2}(T) = \exp\Big(\int_0^T \bar g + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^T \tilde g^{\,2} - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\big(\tilde g(T)^2 + \tilde g(0)^2\big)\Big) \Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde g(T) \mp \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\,\tilde g'(T)\Big) + O(\varepsilon^3).$$

What remains is to evaluate $\int\bar g$, $\int\tilde g^{\,2}$, $\tilde g$ and $\tilde g'$ for this model.

Step 1: the forcing

The forcing is a quadratic in $B$ with complex coefficients. Collect them by power of $B$:

$$g_i = \alpha_i\,B + \beta_i\,B^2 + \gamma_i, \qquad \alpha_i = -c\,\big(a\,b_i + iw\,\rho\,\sigma_i\eta_i\big), \quad \beta_i = \tfrac12c^2\eta_i^2, \quad \gamma_i = -\tfrac12\sigma_i^2\,(w^2 + iw),$$

and write $\bar\alpha, \tilde\alpha = \tfrac12(\alpha_1 \pm \alpha_2)$ and likewise for $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Then $\tilde g = \tilde\alpha B + \tilde\beta B^2 + \tilde\gamma$, with $\tilde g(0) = \tilde\gamma$, which is not zero, and $\tilde g' = (\tilde\alpha + 2\tilde\beta B)\,e^{-at}$.

Step 2: the integrals

With $I_n = \int_0^TB^n$,

$$\int_0^T\bar g = \bar\alpha\,I_1 + \bar\beta\,I_2 + \bar\gamma\,T,$$ $$\int_0^T\tilde g^{\,2} = \tilde\alpha^2I_2 + 2\tilde\alpha\tilde\beta\,I_3 + \tilde\beta^2I_4 + 2\tilde\alpha\tilde\gamma\,I_1 + 2\tilde\beta\tilde\gamma\,I_2 + \tilde\gamma^2\,T .$$

The first three terms are the Green–Kubo term of the rates alone, the last is that of the equity alone, and the two in the middle are the covariance between them: the regime that raises the equity volatility also moves the rate.

Step 3: the result

$$\begin{aligned} \Psi_{1,2}(w) = \;&S_0^{\,iw}\,e^{-iwqT}\,\exp\Big(-c\,B\,r_0 + \bar\alpha\,I_1 + \bar\beta\,I_2 + \bar\gamma\,T + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,A - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\big(G^2 + \tilde\gamma^2\big)\Big) \\ &\times\Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,G \mp \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\,G'\Big) + O(\varepsilon^3), \end{aligned}$$ $$\begin{aligned} A &= \tilde\alpha^2I_2 + 2\tilde\alpha\tilde\beta\,I_3 + \tilde\beta^2I_4 + 2\tilde\alpha\tilde\gamma\,I_1 + 2\tilde\beta\tilde\gamma\,I_2 + \tilde\gamma^2\,T, \\ G &= \tilde\alpha\,B + \tilde\beta\,B^2 + \tilde\gamma, \qquad G' = \big(\tilde\alpha + 2\tilde\beta\,B\big)\,E, \\ \alpha_i &= -c\,\big(a\,b_i + iw\,\rho\,\sigma_i\eta_i\big), \qquad \beta_i = \tfrac12c^2\eta_i^2, \qquad \gamma_i = -\tfrac12\sigma_i^2\,(w^2 + iw), \qquad c = 1 - iw, \\ B &= \frac{1 - E}{a}, \qquad E = e^{-aT}, \\ I_1 &= \frac{1}{a}\Big(T - \frac{1 - E}{a}\Big), \\ I_2 &= \frac{1}{a^2}\Big(T - \frac{2(1 - E)}{a} + \frac{1 - E^2}{2a}\Big), \\ I_3 &= \frac{1}{a^3}\Big(T - \frac{3(1 - E)}{a} + \frac{3(1 - E^2)}{2a} - \frac{1 - E^3}{3a}\Big), \\ I_4 &= \frac{1}{a^4}\Big(T - \frac{4(1 - E)}{a} + \frac{3(1 - E^2)}{a} - \frac{4(1 - E^3)}{3a} + \frac{1 - E^4}{4a}\Big) . \end{aligned}$$

The upper sign is for a start in regime 1. Two checks are built in. At $w = 0$ the equity terms vanish, $c = 1$, and the card is the second-order Vasicek bond. At $w = -i$ the weight is $c = 0$, every $\alpha_i$, $\beta_i$ and $\gamma_i$ vanishes, and $\Psi = S_0e^{-qT}$ exactly.

Other pairs

  • Hull–White rates. Write $r = x + \varphi(t)$ as on the Hull–White page. The level term drops, $\alpha_i = -c\,iw\,\rho\,\sigma_i\eta_i$, and the prefactor $e^{-cBr_0}$ is replaced by $\exp\big(-c\int_0^T\varphi\big)$.
  • Heston equity. With $\rho = 0$ the equity part of the forcing is $\kappa\theta_iD(t; w)$ and the prefactor gains $e^{D(T;w)v_0}$, as on the Heston page. An equity–rate correlation is not admitted: it adds a term $\rho\,\eta_i\sqrt v\,\partial_x\partial_r$ that no exponential-affine form absorbs, as noted in the catalogue.
