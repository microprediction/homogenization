Poisson smoothing removes maturity growth from the error bound

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, where $Q$ is finite and irreducible, stationary row $\pi$ and $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$. Assume $0<a\leq\ell_i\leq b$, with nonconstant $\ell$, and $\|e^{tQ}-\Pi\|_{\infty\to\infty}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}$ for $t\geq0$.

Put $f=\ell-(\pi\ell)\mathbf1$, $F=\|f\|_\infty$, $\beta=m\gamma$, $\Lambda_T=\int_0^T\ell(Y_t)\,dt$, $\mu=\mathbb E_\nu\Lambda_T$, $v=\operatorname{Var}_\nu\Lambda_T$ and $M_j=\mathbb E_\nu(\Lambda_T-\mu)^j$. Conditional on $\Lambda_T$, the Cox count $N_T$ is Poisson with that mean. Fit $G$ with gamma shape $\mu^2/v$ and scale $v/\mu$, and mix a Poisson variable over $G$ to obtain $N_*$.

Explicit bound, uniform in both maturity and prior

Choose any split horizon $\tau>0$. Define $\alpha=2CF^2$, $t_3=18C^2F^3$, $u_3=2C^3F^3$, $t_4=24C^2F^4$, $u_4=60C^3F^4$ and $c=(\log2-\tfrac12)a^2/\alpha$.

$$S_2=\frac{2\tau}{3}\left(t_3+\frac{2\alpha^2}{a}\right) +\tau^2\left(\frac{t_4}{3}+\alpha^2\right),\qquad S_3=\frac{2u_3}{3}+\tau\left(\frac{u_4}{3}+\frac{2\alpha^3}{a^2}\right),$$ $$L_2=\frac{\sqrt6}{12a^{3/2}\sqrt\tau}\left(t_3+\frac{2\alpha^2}{a}\right) +\frac{\sqrt{24}}{48a^2}(t_4+12\alpha^2),\qquad L_3=\frac{\sqrt6u_3}{12(a\tau)^{3/2}} +\frac{\sqrt{24}}{48a^2\tau}\left(u_4+\frac{24\alpha^3}{a^2}\right).$$
$$A_2=\max\{S_2,L_2\},\qquad A_3=\max\{S_3,L_3\},\qquad A_4=\frac{8b^4}{c^4},$$ $$\sup_{T>0,\nu}d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L_\nu(N_T),\mathcal L(N_{*,T,\nu})) \leq\min\{1,A_2/\beta^2+A_3/\beta^3+A_4/\beta^4\}=O(m^{-2}).$$

This holds at every positive speed; the constants are deliberately conservative. All priors and horizons may depend on $m$. Constant intensity is exactly Poisson and is handled separately, without dividing by $F$ or $v$.

Finite-chain moment estimates

Write $J=\Lambda_T-(\pi\ell)T$ and $R_j=\mathbb E_\nu J^j$. Markov conditioning gives $|\mathbb E_\nu J|\leq CF/\beta$ and $0\leq R_2\leq\alpha T/\beta$.

Splitting the propagation across a gap into $\Pi+(P-\Pi)$ gives the following bounds for ordered three- and four-point correlations, with $f_t=f(Y_t)$:

$$|\mathbb E_\nu(f_t f_{t+s}f_{t+s+u})| \leq C^2F^3\{e^{-\beta(t+u)}+e^{-\beta(s+u)}\},$$ $$|\mathbb E_\nu(f_t f_{t+s}f_{t+s+u}f_{t+s+u+w})| \leq C^2F^4\{e^{-\beta(s+w)}+e^{-\beta(u+w)}\}.$$

For three points, condition on the last gap, then split the preceding propagator; the constant part uses $\pi f=0$ and decay from the initial time. For four points, split the middle propagator; its constant part leaves a two-point correlation, and sup-norm contraction bounds the remainder.

Integrating over ordered times gives $|R_3|\leq12C^2F^3T/\beta^2$ and $0\leq R_4\leq24C^2F^4T^2/\beta^2$. Centering these raw moments yields

$$v\leq V=\frac{\alpha T}{\beta},\qquad |M_3|\leq B_3=\frac{t_3T}{\beta^2}+\frac{u_3}{\beta^3},\qquad M_4\leq B_4=\frac{t_4T^2}{\beta^2}+\frac{u_4T}{\beta^3}.$$

Poisson smoothing and the gamma lower tail

For the Poisson probability vector $p(x)$, the classical Charlier orthogonality gives $\|p^{(j)}(x)\|_1\leq\sqrt{j!}\,x^{-j/2}$ for $x>0$. See Zacharovas and Hwang (2010), Lemma 2.1; the identity needed here has a direct proof.

Indeed, $p_k(x+h)/p_k(x)=e^{-h}(1+h/x)^k$. For $K\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(x)$, the expected product at parameters $h,s$ is $e^{hs/x}$. Comparing coefficients gives $\sum_k p_k^{(j)}(x)^2/p_k(x)=j!x^{-j}$, and Cauchy–Schwarz proves the norm bound.

Taylor-expand at $\mu$ through degree three, with matched means and variances. Since $\Lambda_T,\mu\geq aT$, the true intensity remainder uses the smoothed fourth derivative. For $G\geq\mu/2$ its gamma counterpart uses the same bound with an extra factor four.

On $G<\mu/2$, instead use $\|p^{(4)}(x)\|_1\leq16$ and $|G-\mu|\leq\mu$. Gamma Laplace transformation gives $\Pr(G<\mu/2)\leq e^{-(\log2-1/2)\mu^2/v}$. Here $\mu^2/v\geq a^2\beta T/\alpha$ and $\mu\leq bT$.

$$d_{\rm TV}\leq \frac{\sqrt6}{12(aT)^{3/2}}\left(B_3+\frac{2V^2}{aT}\right) +\frac{\sqrt{24}}{48(aT)^2} \left\{B_4+4\left(3V^2+\frac{6V^3}{(aT)^2}\right)\right\} +\frac{b^4T^4}{3}e^{-c\beta T}.$$

For $T\geq\tau$, the first two terms are at most $L_2/\beta^2+L_3/\beta^3$. Since $e^x\geq x^4/24$, the last is at most $A_4/\beta^4$ for every $T>0$. This treats the gamma lower tail rather than assuming it shares the true intensity's positive floor.

For $0<T\leq\tau$, use the unsmoothed derivative bound $\|p^{(j)}(x)\|_1\leq2^j$, which gives $\tfrac23|M_3-2v^2/\mu|+\tfrac13(M_4+3v^2+6v^3/\mu^2)$. Substitution of $\mu\geq aT$ and the moment estimates bounds this by $S_2/\beta^2+S_3/\beta^3$, completing the proof.

Certificate and scope

verify_uniform_positive_counts.py independently checks Poisson derivative norms, the Charlier squared-norm identity and gamma tail bounds. It compares 168 count laws at horizons $0.02,0.2,1,5,20,100$, using exact triangular intensity moments and matrix generating functions; both pure and stationary priors are included.

For symmetric switching with intensities $(3,1)$, $C=1$, $\gamma=2$, $\tau=1$ and speed 80, the all-horizon bound is $0.0125223590$. Pure first-state TV errors at $T=1,20,100$ are $0.0000077200$, $0.0000015441$ and $0.0000006892$. The defective-chain example at speed 80 has bound $0.0115323596$ and error $0.0000000703$ at $T=100$.

The largest omitted-tail TV budget is $3.773\times10^{-22}$, separate from roundoff. Matrix-exponential checks scale the base tolerance $1.455\times10^{-11}$ by the generator-horizon norm. The final certificate line is literal PASS.

The symmetric cases also cross-check the matrix generating function against an independently evaluated, overflow-safe closed-form two-state transform. The maximum probability-vector $\ell^1$ discrepancy is $7.340\times10^{-12}$.

Uniformity concerns individual count marginals at all horizons, not a single negative-binomial arrival process with consistent independent increments. The positive intensity floor and fixed mixing envelope are assumptions of this theorem; zero-intensity regimes, vanishing floors and closing spectral gaps are outside its uniform claim.
