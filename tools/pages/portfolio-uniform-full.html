One sufficient speed for every investment horizon

A direct change-of-measure bound can grow exponentially with the horizon. Bounding the mixing of the tilted path law instead gives a horizon-independent sufficient speed for a unique constant-fraction optimum below log utility, under a strictly positive transition-rate assumption.

The result

Let $Q$ be an $n$-state row generator, $n\ge2$, with $q_{ij}\ge q_*>0$ for every $i\ne j$. The stock's finite regime coefficients have excess returns $b_i$, variances $s_i\ge s_{\min}>0$, and common constant interest rate $r$.

Fix $0<\gamma<1$ and $p=1-\gamma$. For a chain $Y$ with generator $mQ$, any initial prior $\nu$, and Brownian stock noise independent of $Y$, the investor holds one deterministic fraction $w$ throughout the horizon:

$$h_i(w)=r+b_iw-\tfrac12\gamma s_iw^2,\qquad J_{m,T,\nu}(w)=\frac1{pT}\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{p\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)dt}.$$

Write $q_i(w)=b_i-\gamma ws_i$ and $I=[L,U]$, where $L=\min_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)$ and $U=\max_i b_i/(\gamma s_i)$. Every global maximizer lies in $I$: the derivative has the sign of the tilted mean of $\int q(Y_t)dt$, so is positive below $L$ and negative above $U$.

The quadratic upper bound $J(w)\le r+\max_i|b_i||w|-\gamma s_{\min}w^2/2$ gives existence. If $L=U$, that common regimewise fraction is uniquely optimal at every speed; otherwise define

$$B_* =\max_{w\in I}\operatorname{osc}q(w),\qquad D_* =\max_{w\in I}\operatorname{osc}h(w),\qquad \delta=pD_*.$$

Here $\operatorname{osc}v=\max_i v_i-\min_i v_i$. The first maximum uses only the endpoints of $I$; the second uses endpoints and the interior critical points of the quadratic differences $h_i-h_j$.

$$a=mq_*,\qquad R_m=\frac{\delta+\sqrt{\delta^2+4a^2}}{2a},\qquad \eta_m=\frac{na}{R_m},\qquad \kappa_m=\gamma s_{\min}-\frac{pB_*^2}{2\eta_m}.$$ $$J_{m,T,\nu}''(w)\le-\kappa_m\quad(w\in I),\qquad\text{for every }T>0\text{ and every }\nu.$$

In particular, let $c=pB_*^2/(2n\gamma s_{\min})$.

$$m>m_H:=\frac{\sqrt{c(c+\delta)}}{q_*} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \kappa_m>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{one global maximizer for every }T>0,\ \nu.$$

This is a sufficient speed, not a sharp transition. The optimizer itself may still depend on the horizon and prior, and no concavity outside $I$ is claimed.

A uniform value-ratio bound

Fix $w\in I$, put $A=mQ+pD_{h(w)}$, and $z(\tau)=e^{\tau A}\mathbf1$. Positivity gives $z_i(\tau)>0$ for every $\tau\ge0$.

Let $M=\max_i z_i$ and $\ell=\min_i z_i$. At a maximizing state, the transition to a minimizing state contributes at most $a(\ell-M)$, while all other generator terms are nonpositive; the reverse argument holds at a minimizing state.

Consequently the locally Lipschitz ratio $\rho=M/\ell$ satisfies, almost everywhere,

$$\rho'\le\delta\rho-a(\rho^2-1),\qquad \rho(0)=1.$$

The positive equilibrium of the scalar right-hand side is $R_m\ge1$. Scalar comparison therefore proves $\max_i z_i(\tau)/\min_i z_i(\tau)\le R_m$ at every $\tau\ge0$, without an eigenvector approximation.

The finite-horizon tilt is a mixing Markov chain

Under the path tilt proportional to $e^{p\int_0^T h(Y_t)dt}$, the exact transition matrix between $s$ and $t$ is

$$K^T_{s,t}=D_{z(T-s)}^{-1}e^{(t-s)A}D_{z(T-t)},\qquad 0\le s\le t\le T.$$

Its rows sum to one, and its off-diagonal time-dependent rates are $\widehat q_{ij}(t)=mq_{ij}z_j(T-t)/z_i(T-t)\ge a/R_m$. Its initial law is $\widehat\nu_i=\nu_i z_i(T)/(\nu z(T))$, not generally $\nu$.

Subtract from this generator a common refresh to the uniform law at rate $\eta_m=na/R_m$. The remaining off-diagonal rates are nonnegative, so a shared refresh couples any two starts and gives

$$\operatorname{osc}(K^T_{s,t}f)\le e^{-\eta_m(t-s)}\operatorname{osc}f.$$

This is the exact finite-horizon Doob transform, not the stationary driven process obtained by substituting a Perron eigenvector. The kernel is the specialization of Chetrite and Touchette (2015), Appendix D, equation (D4).

Mixing bounds the positive curvature term

Write $\mathbb E_w$ for that tilted path law, $R_w=\int_0^Tq_{Y_t}(w)dt$, and $V=\int_0^Ts_{Y_t}dt$. Direct differentiation gives

$$J'(w)=\mathbb E_wR_w/T,\qquad J''(w)=-\gamma\mathbb E_wV/T+p\operatorname{Var}_w(R_w)/T.$$

For any initial law, conditioning at $s\le t$, Cauchy–Schwarz, and the variance bound by one quarter of the squared range imply

$$|\operatorname{Cov}_w(q(Y_s),q(Y_t))| \le\tfrac14\operatorname{osc}q\operatorname{osc}(K^T_{s,t}q) \le\tfrac14B_*^2e^{-\eta_m(t-s)}.$$

Integrating this bound over the ordered time triangle yields

$$\frac{\operatorname{Var}_w(R_w)}T\le\frac{B_*^2}{2} \left\{\frac1{\eta_m}-\frac{1-e^{-\eta_mT}}{T\eta_m^2}\right\} \le\frac{B_*^2}{2\eta_m}.$$

Combining this with $V\ge Ts_{\min}$ proves the curvature theorem and uniqueness on the exact optimizer interval. The threshold formula follows by solving $a/R_m>c$, equivalently $a^2>c(c+\delta)$.

When $\kappa_m>0$, every proposed $w\in I$ also satisfies the usual derivative-residual bounds

$$|w-w_*|\le |J'(w)|/\kappa_m,\qquad 0\le J(w_*)-J(w)\le J'(w)^2/(2\kappa_m).$$

These follow by integrating the curvature inequality and maximizing its quadratic upper model. With a certified derivative error $\epsilon$, replace $|J'|$ by $|\widehat J'|+\epsilon$; unvalidated numerical derivatives do not themselves give a rigorous numerical certificate.

An explicit long-end certainty-equivalent bound

Let $\Lambda_m(w)$ be the exact Perron eigenvalue of $mQ+pD_{h(w)}$. For all $w\in I$, every prior and every $T>0$,

$$\left|J_{m,T,\nu}(w)-\frac{\Lambda_m(w)}p\right|\le\frac{\log R_m}{pT}.$$

Indeed, the eigenvalue equations at the largest and smallest entries of a positive Perron eigenvector $v$ give $a(\rho-1/\rho)\le\delta$, hence $\max v/\min v\le R_m$. Normalize $\min v=1$, apply the positive semigroup to $v/R_m\le\mathbf1\le v$, and then average under $\nu$ to bound $e^{-\Lambda_mT}\nu z(T)$ between $1/R_m$ and $R_m$.

This is an explicit $1/T$ CE-rate error using the exact eigenvalue, not an inverse-speed truncation or a price-error bound. Positive left and right Perron eigenvectors express $(\Lambda_m/p)'$ as a positive weighted average of $q_i(w)$, localizing its maximizer to $I$ too. When $\kappa_m>0$, the long-run objective $\Lambda_m/p$ inherits strong concavity on $I$ and has one optimizer $w_\infty$; the finite-horizon optimizers obey

$$\sup_\nu|w_{T,\nu}-w_\infty|\le 2\sqrt{\frac{\log R_m}{p\kappa_mT}}.$$

Uniform convergence of the values on $I$ transfers strong concavity to the limit. The two value errors bound the long-run objective loss by $2\log R_m/(pT)$, and its quadratic curvature bound gives the displayed optimizer-distance estimate.

A rational all-horizon example

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-3&2&1\\1&-2&1\\1/2&3/2&-2\end{pmatrix},\quad b=(3/25,-2/25,1/20),\quad s=(1/25,9/100,1/16),\quad\gamma=1/2.$$ $$q_*=1/2,\quad I=[-16/9,6],\quad B_*=7/20,\quad D_*=33/20,\quad \delta=33/40,\quad c=49/96,\quad m_H^2=31409/11520<4.$$

Thus $m\ge2$ suffices at every positive horizon and every prior. At $m=2$, the scalar comparison polynomial at $R=3/2$ is $R^2-1-(33/40)R=1/80>0$, so $R_m<3/2$, $\eta_m>2$ and $\kappa_m\ge3/640$; larger speeds retain these bounds.

Certificate, literature and limits

The certificate checks exact rational constants and independent derivative, tilted-moment, canonical-transition and covariance-quadrature formulas. It includes nonreversible and defective generators, pure and stationary priors, and horizons spanning $10^{-4}$ to $10^4$, ending with literal PASS.

Those are floating-point consistency tests, not interval enclosures or a proof obtained by sampling horizons. The analytic comparison and coupling argument prove the uniform statement for all horizons.

Doob transforms and time-uniform Feynman–Kac stability are classical; see also Del Moral and Miclo (2002), §§2–3, for the broader Dobrushin-semigroup framework. The explicit finite-state value-ratio, reset-rate and portfolio-curvature bounds above are derived directly, without a priority claim for these ingredients.

The strictly positive off-diagonal assumption is essential to this particular bound: an irreducible sparse generator can have $q_*=0$. This note does not extend the formula to that case or optimize adaptive trading; its long-end CE-rate bound is not a price approximation.
