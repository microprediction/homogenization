Regime-switching survival

A hazard rate whose mean level and volatility switch between two regimes, expanded to all orders in the inverse switching rate.

The problem comes from the second half of my 2001 Stanford thesis. The hazard rate, or short rate, is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process whose mean level and volatility switch between two regimes at rate $\lambda$.

The quantity wanted is the survival probability, which is also a zero-coupon bond price. When the regimes switch infinitely fast the answer is Vasicek's formula with the regime averages plugged in. The corrector adds terms in $\varepsilon = 1/\lambda$.

The averaged model forgets the regime. The corrector remembers it at order ε.

The expansion

The hazard follows

$$dx_t = \kappa(\theta_{y_t} - x_t)\,dt + \sigma_{y_t}\,dW_t,$$

where $y_t \in \{1,2\}$ switches at rate $\lambda$ in each direction. The survival probability is

$$u_y(t,x) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{-\int_0^t x_r\,dr} \mid x_0 = x,\ y_0 = y\big].$$

Write

$$B(r) = \frac{1-e^{-\kappa r}}{\kappa}, \qquad \bar\theta, \tilde\theta = \frac{\theta_1 \pm \theta_2}{2}, \qquad \bar s, \tilde s = \frac{\sigma_1^2 \pm \sigma_2^2}{2},$$ $$\bar g(r) = -\kappa\bar\theta B(r) + \tfrac12 \bar s B(r)^2, \qquad \tilde g(r) = -\kappa\tilde\theta B(r) + \tfrac12 \tilde s B(r)^2 .$$

With $\varepsilon = 1/\lambda$, the upper sign when the process starts in regime 1, and $t > 0$ fixed as $\lambda \to \infty$,

$$\begin{aligned} u_y(t,x) &= e^{-B(t)x}\; M(t)\; \big(1 \pm R(t)\big) + O(\varepsilon^3), \\[4pt] \log M(t) &= \int_0^t \bar g(r)\,dr + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^t \tilde g(r)^2\,dr - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\,\tilde g(t)^2, \\[4pt] R(t) &= \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\tilde g(t) - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\,\tilde g'(t). \end{aligned}$$

The factor $e^{-B(t)x} e^{\int_0^t \bar g}$ is Vasicek's bond price with the averaged parameters. The $\varepsilon$ term in $\log M$ is the same for both regimes and grows with maturity. The factor $1 \pm R$ depends on the starting regime, and it vanishes as $\lambda \to \infty$.

The expansion can be checked against a numerical solution. Because $\kappa$ does not switch, $u_y(t,x) = a_y(t)\,e^{-B(t)x}$, where $a$ solves a two-dimensional linear ODE driven by $\bar g \pm \tilde g$ and the switching generator. This reduction is known: Elliott and Mamon (2002) solve the case of a switching mean level, and Elliott and Siu (2009) give Markov-modulated exponential-affine bond prices in general.

Against the numerical solution, the error after zero, one and two orders falls like $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon^2$ and $\varepsilon^3$. The convergence demo shows the three slopes, and the survival demo shows the curves. The published fast-switching expansions treat option prices and transition densities, and a search of the literature did not find the corrector for this bond price or survival probability.

The remainder is $O(\varepsilon^3)$ at each fixed $t > 0$, not uniformly down to $t = 0$. The outer series does not satisfy $u_y(0,x) = 1$: at $t = 0$ the bracket is $1 \mp \tfrac14\varepsilon^2\tilde g'(0)$, with $\tilde g'(0) = -\kappa\tilde\theta$, because the regime has not yet had time to switch.

An initial layer repairs the start. To leading order it adds to $R(t)$ the transient

$$\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\,\tilde g'(0)\,e^{-2\lambda t},$$

which is of order $\varepsilon^2$ for $t$ up to a few multiples of $1/(2\lambda)$ and falls below $\varepsilon^3$ only once $t$ exceeds about $\log(1/\varepsilon)/(2\lambda)$. With the transient included, the remainder is $O(\varepsilon^3)$ uniformly on bounded intervals of $t$.

Derivation of the first-order formula

The idea page explains the same two corrections without calculus: one is half the variance of the fluctuating hazard, the other is the memory of the starting regime.

The coupled equations

By the Feynman–Kac formula the two survival probabilities, one per starting regime, solve

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_t u_1 &= \kappa(\theta_1 - x)\,\partial_x u_1 + \tfrac12\sigma_1^2\,\partial_{xx}u_1 - x\,u_1 + \lambda\,(u_2 - u_1), \\ \partial_t u_2 &= \kappa(\theta_2 - x)\,\partial_x u_2 + \tfrac12\sigma_2^2\,\partial_{xx}u_2 - x\,u_2 + \lambda\,(u_1 - u_2), \end{aligned}$$

both starting from $u_i(0,x) = 1$. The last terms are the switching: over a short interval $dt$ the regime jumps with probability $\lambda\,dt$, and the expectation then continues from the other regime.

Removing the rate

Try $u_i = e^{-B(t)x}\,a_i(t)$. Each derivative in $x$ brings down a factor of $-B$, and the terms proportional to $x$ cancel when $B' = 1 - \kappa B$. Because $\kappa$ is the same in both regimes, one $B$ works for both, and what is left is a pair of ordinary differential equations:

$$a_1' = g_1\,a_1 + \lambda\,(a_2 - a_1), \qquad a_2' = g_2\,a_2 + \lambda\,(a_1 - a_2), \qquad a_i(0) = 1,$$

with $g_i = -\kappa\theta_i B + \tfrac12\sigma_i^2B^2$. Solving this pair numerically gives the reference used throughout the examples.

Mean and ratio

Write the two solutions through their mean and their normalized difference:

$$a_1 = m\,(1 + \omega), \qquad a_2 = m\,(1 - \omega).$$

Adding the two equations gives the mean, and the switching terms cancel:

$$m' = m\,\big(\bar g + \tilde g\,\omega\big).$$

Subtracting them, and dividing by $m$, gives the ratio:

$$\omega' = \tilde g\,(1 - \omega^2) - 2\lambda\,\omega, \qquad \omega(0) = 0 .$$

The quasi-steady balance

In the equation for $\omega$ the term $-2\lambda\,\omega$ is large. Switching pulls the two regimes back together at rate $2\lambda$, while $\tilde g$ pushes them apart. When $\lambda$ is large, $\omega$ settles quickly near the value that balances the two:

$$\omega \approx \frac{\tilde g}{2\lambda}.$$

Putting this into the equation for $m$ and integrating gives

$$\log m(t) \approx \int_0^t \bar g + \frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_0^t \tilde g^{\,2}.$$

With $a_{1,2} = m\,(1 \pm \omega)$ and the factor $e^{-B(t)x}$ restored, this is the first-order formula above. The same balance, carried to higher orders, gives every term.

All orders

The balance can be carried to every order, because the two equations for $m$ and $\omega$ are exact.

Expanding $\omega = \sum_n \varepsilon^n \omega_n$ gives $\omega_1 = \tilde g/2$ and

$$\omega_{n+1} = -\tfrac12\big(\omega_n' + \tilde g \sum_{i+j=n} \omega_i\omega_j\big).$$

Every $\omega_n$ is a polynomial in $E = 1 - e^{-\kappa t}$, because $dE/dt = \kappa(1-E)$, so every term of $\log m$ integrates in closed form.

The outer series does not start at $\omega(0) = 0$. The fast transient that corrects it decays within about $1/(2\lambda)$, but it leaves a permanent shift in $\log m$:

$$-\varepsilon^4\,\omega_2(0)\,\tilde g'(0)/4 + O(\varepsilon^5).$$

The layer is solved to all orders in the span of $\tau^k e^{-2j\tau}$, with $\tau = \lambda t$. With both series the error after $n$ orders falls like $\varepsilon^{n+1}$.

The certificate verify_all_orders.py checks orders 1 to 6 against a 30-digit numerical solution. The survival demo shows the terms adding up.

Unequal switching rates

Let regime 1 switch to regime 2 at rate $\lambda_{12}$ and back at rate $\lambda_{21}$, with total rate $\gamma = \lambda_{12} + \lambda_{21}$ and stationary probabilities $p = \lambda_{21}/\gamma$ and $q = \lambda_{12}/\gamma$. Write $G_i = -\kappa\theta_i B + \tfrac12\sigma_i^2 B^2$ and $D = G_1 - G_2$. In the coordinates $s = p\,a_1 + q\,a_2$ and $r = (a_1 - a_2)/s$ the generator separates exactly, and

$$\begin{aligned} r' &= D\,\big(1 + (q-p)\,r - pq\,r^2\big) - \gamma\,r, \qquad r(0) = 0, \\[4pt] \log s(t) &= \int_0^t \big(p\,G_1 + q\,G_2 + pq\,D\,r\big)(v)\,dv, \\[4pt] u_1 &= e^{-B(t)x}\, s\,(1 + q\,r), \qquad u_2 = e^{-B(t)x}\, s\,(1 - p\,r). \end{aligned}$$

With $\varepsilon = 1/\gamma$ the outer series has $r_1 = D$ and

$$r_{n+1} = (q-p)\,D\,r_n - pq\,D\sum_{i+j=n} r_i r_j - r_n',$$

again polynomials in $E$. The initial layer decays like $e^{-\gamma t}$ and is solved to all orders in the same way. With $p = q = \tfrac12$ and $\gamma = 2\lambda$ this is the symmetric case above.

The certificate verify_general_orders.py checks orders 1 to 6 at $p = 0.3$ against a 30-digit numerical solution. The homogenize package implements both cases.
