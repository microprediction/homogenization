A finite vanilla strip can certify a nonzero entropy gap

Explicit strike truncation, discretization and quote-error budgets; no density or negative moment required.

Compare two terminal laws $P,R$ of a nonnegative normalized stock $S$, with $\mathbb E_PS=\mathbb E_RS=1$ and $M_r=\max\{\mathbb E_PS^r,\mathbb E_RS^r\}<\infty$ for a supplied $r>1$. Interest and forward normalization are already removed, and $0\log0$ means zero.

$$C_i(K)=\mathbb E_i(S-K)_+,\qquad D=\mathbb E_P[S\log S]-\mathbb E_R[S\log S].$$

Fix $0<L<1<U$, an integer $N\ge1$, and log-spaced strikes $K_j=Le^{jh}$, where $h=\log(U/L)/N$. Define

$$d_h=\frac{h}{1-e^{-h}}-1,\quad \alpha(h)=d_h-\log(1+d_h),\quad w_0=d_h,\quad w_j=h\ (0<j<N),\quad w_N=h-d_h,$$ $$A_N=\sum_{j=0}^Nw_j\{C_P(K_j)-C_R(K_j)\},\qquad E_N=\alpha(h)+L+\frac{M_rU^{1-r}}{r(r-1)}.$$
$$|A_N-D|\le E_N,\qquad \max_{0\le j\le N}|C_P(K_j)-C_R(K_j)| \ge\frac{(|D|-E_N)_+}{\log(U/L)}.$$

If each price in each curve is supplied with absolute error at most $\eta$, the strip using the displayed exact weights has total error at most $E_N+2\eta\log(U/L)$ relative to $D$. Its sign is therefore reliable whenever $|D|$ exceeds this budget.

All weights are positive and sum to $\log(U/L)$. An atom at zero, perfect leverage or an irregular density does not invalidate this terminal-law theorem. This is deterministic separation of two specified laws, not statistical identification of an unknown generator.

Proof by a piecewise-linear entropy payoff

Set $f(s)=s\log s-s+1$ and $f(0)=1$. Let $f_N$ linearly interpolate $f$ between the strikes and continue by the tangent line at $L$ to the left and at $U$ to the right.

The secant slope over $[K_j,K_{j+1}]$ is $\log K_j+d_h$. Hence its slope jumps are exactly the displayed weights, giving the finite replication identity

$$f_N(s)=f(L)+\log L\,(s-L)+\sum_{j=0}^Nw_j(s-K_j)_+.$$

Equal stock means cancel the affine terms, so $A_N=\mathbb E_Pf_N-\mathbb E_Rf_N$ and $D=\mathbb E_Pf-\mathbb E_Rf$. Equal and correctly normalized forwards are essential to this cancellation.

On one grid interval write $s=K_jx$, $1\le x\le e^h$, and $b=1+d_h$. Direct algebra gives the relative interpolation error

$$\frac{f_N(s)-f(s)}s=b(1-1/x)-\log x\in[0,\alpha(h)].$$

The maximum is attained at $x=b$, proving that $\alpha(h)$ is the sharp pointwise coefficient for this interpolant. A simpler bound is $\alpha(h)\le(e^h-1)^2/8$, from $f''(s)=1/s$ and the chord-error estimate on each interval.

Outside the grid, convexity gives a negative interpolation error with magnitude $f(s)-f_N(s)=s\log(s/H)-s+H$, where $H=L$ or $U$ is the adjacent endpoint. For $s<L$ this is at most $L$.

For $s>U$, put $x=s/U\ge1$. Comparing second derivatives at the common value and slope at one gives

$$x\log x-x+1\le\frac{x^r-1-r(x-1)}{r(r-1)}\le\frac{x^r}{r(r-1)}.$$

Thus for either law $i$, its signed expected interpolation error lies in $[-\{L+\mathbb E_iS^r U^{1-r}/(r(r-1))\},\alpha(h)]$. Subtract these intervals: the two-law budget is one $\alpha(h)$ plus the larger tail budget, not the sum of two absolute budgets.

This proves the first inequality. Positive weights and their total mass prove the maximum-quote bound; summing the per-curve price errors gives the quoted-noise budget. No Fourier inversion or differentiation of noisy prices is used.

A general finite-quote design

For an approximation target $0<\varepsilon<1$, the following explicit choices ensure $E_N\le\varepsilon$:

$$L=\varepsilon/3,\quad U=\max\left\{2,\left(\frac{3M_r}{r(r-1)\varepsilon}\right)^{1/(r-1)}\right\},\quad h_0=\log(1+\sqrt{8\varepsilon/3}),\quad N=\left\lceil\frac{\log(U/L)}{h_0}\right\rceil.$$

For fixed $r,M_r$, this uses $N+1=O(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\log(1/\varepsilon))$ quotes per curve as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This is a constructive quote-count bound, not an optimal design or a sampling-error rate.

The continuous identity behind the construction is classical static replication:

$$\mathbb E_i[S\log S]=\int_0^1\frac{\mathbb E_i(K-S)_+}{K}dK+ \int_1^\infty\frac{C_i(K)}K dK,\qquad D=\int_{\mathbb R}\{C_P(e^x)-C_R(e^x)\}dx.$$

Put–call parity and the moment bound justify the difference integral even when a negative moment does not exist. Static replication and its discretization are established methods; see Bossu, Carr and Papanicolaou (2020), section 2.1 and equation (3). The finite-grid error and separation certificate above are derived directly, without a priority claim for the method.

At strikes below one, the call difference can be replaced by the put difference by parity, avoiding subtraction of two prices near one. The same error theorem applies to the resulting out-of-the-money quote differences.

A rational CIR direction-separation certificate

Use the stationary unit directed three-cycle and its reversal, with common fixed $v_0=0.04$, $T=m=1$ and

$$dv=(a_Y-2v)dt+\xi_Y\sqrt v\,dB,\quad dX=-v\,dt/2+\sqrt v(-0.6\,dB+0.8\,dW),\quad a=(1,2,3),\quad\xi=(0.2,0.4,1),\quad S=e^X.$$

The variance is strict-Feller in every regime. The shared positive supersolution loading $b=1/2$ at return moment $r=2$ gives $M_2\le\exp(bv_0+b\max a_i T)=e^{1.52}<4.6$, and also proves the stock is a true martingale by localization.

Under the share measure, reversion is $k_i=2+0.6\xi_i$ and $\mathbb E[S_TX_T]=\tfrac12\int_0^T\mathbb E^S v_tdt$. The finite system $p'=Q^Tp$, $z'=D_ap+(Q^T-D_k)z$, $J'=\mathbf1^Tz$, started at $(\pi^T,v_0\pi^T,0)$, computes this mean as $J(T)/2$.

A rational degree-$160$ matrix Taylor enclosure proves the forward-minus-reverse entropy gap satisfies $D<-1/4000$, with numerical value $-0.000286821579737$ and total tail below $10^{-100}$. This is a scalar-moment enclosure, not a numerical calculation of the whole CIR call curve.

Choose $L=10^{-5}$, $U=10^5$ and $N=2303$, giving $2304$ quotes per curve and $h<0.01$. Rational exponential bounds give $\alpha(h)<13\times10^{-6}$ and $E_N<46\times10^{-6}$.

At least one of these exact call-price differences therefore exceeds $8.5\times10^{-6}$ in magnitude. If every price in each curve has absolute error at most $10^{-6}$, the computed strip is still less than $-156\times10^{-6}$. The broad strike range and quote count are conservative sufficient choices, not a claim of market feasibility.

Checks and a necessary blind-spot warning

The certificate checks $40$ pairs of normalized atomic laws at moment powers $1.25,2,3.5$, including default atoms, the polygon identity, adversarial quote errors, four sharp chord-error controls and nine target-error designs. The maximum observed error/budget ratio is $0.551347$.

A zero entropy gap does not imply equal call curves. A half-and-half mixture of lognormal stock laws with log variances $0.01,0.49$ has the same entropy as log variance $0.25$, but its at-the-money call differs by approximately $-0.0406431943519$.

The CIR quote-separation conclusion follows a priori from the proved inequality and the rational mean gap; no CIR call inversion is claimed in this certificate. The result does not identify an arbitrary generator, certify an individual preselected strike, or settle the global crossing counts in issue #204. Model misspecification, uncertain forward normalization and numerical weight or summation errors require separate budgets.
