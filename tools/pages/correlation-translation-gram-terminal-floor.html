Finite output dimension ends the rare-tail Gram staircase

At the last boundary, two mechanisms share one direction and their coefficients add.

Interior tail/Gaussian collisions make rank-two blocks. At the spectral floor there is only one output direction left, so the final collision is scalar and smaller tail separation becomes invisible.

Terminal-floor theorem

Fix $c>0$, distinct real nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_k$, $1\le r\le k-3$, a slope $\tau>0$, and weights $q_1,q_2>0$. Put $d=k-r-2$, $Q=q_1+q_2$, $\gamma_1=-q_2/Q$, and $\gamma_2=q_1/Q$.

Use the Gaussian-plus-two-atom triangular law

$$\tau_{\ell n}=\tau+\gamma_\ell\delta_n,\qquad A_{\ell n}=\exp(\tau_{\ell n}/\varepsilon_n),\qquad p_{\ell n}=q_\ell\varepsilon_n^{2r}A_{\ell n}^{-2c},$$

and let $K_n$ be the covariance Gram of $|X_n|^{c+\varepsilon_nx_j}$. Assume $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n^d\to\rho\in[0,\infty)$.

Write $\phi_a=(x_j^a/a!)_j$, $v=(e^{\tau x_j})_j$, and $\dot v=(x_je^{\tau x_j})_j$. Let $P$ project orthogonally away from the $(k-1)$-dimensional space

$$L=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{k-3},v\},$$

and put $g=P\phi_{k-2}$ and $b=P\dot v$. For centered $Z_j=|G|^c(\log|G|)^j$, let $h_j$ be its residual variance after projection onto $Z_0,\ldots,Z_{j-1}$.

The $d-1$ earlier post-tail Gaussian modes retain the staircase coefficients from the interior theorem. The last eigenvalue satisfies

$$\lambda_k(K_n)=\left( h_{k-2}\|g\|^2+\frac{q_1q_2}{Q}\rho^2\|b\|^2 \right)\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)} +o(\varepsilon_n^{2(k-2)}).$$

For $\rho=0$, the tail is below the finite-dimensional spectral floor and only $h_{k-2}\|g\|^2$ remains. There is no additional eigenvalue in which the tail contribution can appear.

Why the final block has rank one

The space $L$ has dimension $k-1$, so its orthogonal complement is one-dimensional. Both $g$ and $b$ are nonzero by the extended Chebyshev independence of the polynomial/exponential evaluation systems, hence they are collinear.

Gaussian Gram–Schmidt contributes $\varepsilon_n^{k-2}\sqrt{h_{k-2}}\,g$. Weighted tail-label centering contributes $\varepsilon_n^r\delta_n\sqrt{q_1q_2/Q}\,b$, which has the same order when $\delta_n/\varepsilon_n^d\to\rho$.

The two latent coordinates are orthogonal but their output directions coincide. Their rank-one covariance coefficients therefore add, while higher Gaussian and tail-confluence features are smaller.

Six-output certificate

Take $k=6$, $r=1$, $d=3$, $c=\tau=q_1=1$, $q_2=2$, $x=(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2)$, $\rho=3/2$, and $\varepsilon_n=1/n$. The Gaussian floor and critical tail increment are

$$(0.000363463895283,\ 0.002341309123101),$$

giving the terminal coefficient $0.002704773018385$. At $n=4096$, the exact critical ratio is $0.999878778575$.

For the sub-floor sequence $\delta_n=\varepsilon_n^{7/2}$, the exact ratio to the pure Gaussian floor is $1.001697464827$. The 240-digit certificate also checks both evaluation ranks, the two earlier Gaussian scales, monotone convergence, and literal PASS.

Scope and literature

This is a sequencewise theorem for the displayed triangular laws at one fixed positive maturity. It covers fixed finite $\rho$, including zero; it gives no finite-$n$ error bound or uniformity in $k$, $r$, or moving $\rho$.

The result concerns the nonlinear output covariance Gram. It changes neither fixed-feature Green–Kubo rank nor rank obtained after integrating different maturity loadings.

Gautschi supplies the column-confluence geometry, Kato supplies finite-dimensional asymptotic perturbation theory, and Karlin and Studden supply the zero-count argument.
