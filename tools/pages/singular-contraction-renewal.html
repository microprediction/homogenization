Renewal limits for unequal-scale staircase contractions

Commensurable horizontal scales turn exact phase invariance into a finite renewal equation.

Unequal base-power branches

Fix a base $b\ge2$ and ordered active intervals $I_r=[\alpha_r,\alpha_r+b^{-\ell_r}]$, where the positive integers $\ell_r$ have maximum $L$. Assume the intervals have endpoints on the $b^{-L}$ grid, the first starts at zero, the last ends at one, and their total length is less than one.

Choose weights $w_r>0$ with sum one and put $W_r=\sum_{j<r}w_j$. The corresponding continuous singular staircase is constant on the gaps and satisfies

$$C(x)=W_r+w_r C\!\left(b^{\ell_r}(x-\alpha_r)\right),\qquad x\in I_r.$$

For $\mu=\mathcal L(C(U))$ and the equal-mass cell-average projection $P_N$, write $E_N=\|C-P_NC\|_1=W_1(\mu_N,\mu)$ and $c_K^{(m)}=mb^K E_{mb^K}$.

Exact renewal equation

Aggregate weights by horizontal exponent, $p_j=\sum_{r:\ell_r=j}w_r$. For every fixed $m\ge1$ and every $K\ge L$,

$$\boxed{c_K^{(m)}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}p_j c_{K-j}^{(m)}.}$$

Indeed, branch $r$ is grid-aligned, contains $mb^{K-\ell_r}$ cells, and contributes $b^{-\ell_r}w_rE_{mb^{K-\ell_r}}$. Multiplication by $mb^K$ gives the displayed recurrence.

The equal-horizontal-scale identity is the special case $p_1=1$, when $c_K^{(m)}$ is exactly constant.

Limit and lattice phases

Let $g=\gcd\{j:p_j>0\}$ and $\mu_\ell=\sum_jjp_j$. For a residue $s\in\{0,\ldots,g-1\}$, put $a_q=c_{s+gq}^{(m)}$, $\bar p_j=p_{gj}$, $\bar L=L/g$, and $\bar\mu=\mu_\ell/g$.

Define the finite boundary forcing $r_{s,q}=a_q-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\bar p_j a_{q-j}$ for $0\le q<\bar L$. Then

$$a_q=C_s+O(\theta^q),\qquad C_s=\frac1{\bar\mu}\sum_{q=0}^{\bar L-1}r_{s,q},$$

Let $R>1$ be the smallest modulus of any other zero of $1-\sum_j\bar p_jz^j$, with $R=\infty$ if there is none. The remainder holds for every fixed $\theta\in(R^{-1},1)$; thus $g=1$ gives one scaled coefficient along $N=mb^K$, while $g>1$ gives one limit per residue class.

The proof uses the boundary polynomial divided by $1-\sum_j\bar p_jz^j$. Coprimality leaves $z=1$ as the only unit-circle root, giving the coefficient and the stated root-gap error on every residue class.

Exact cluster and phase laws

The complete set of subsequential limits along the fixed-$m$ geometric grid is

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Cluster}_{K\to\infty}\!\left(c_K^{(m)}\right) =\{C_0,\ldots,C_{g-1}\}.}$$

Every residue sequence converges to its $C_s$, so each displayed value is attained. Conversely, among finitely many residues every subsequence has a constant-residue subsubsequence, which rules out any other cluster value.

Consequently,

$$\liminf_{K\to\infty}c_K^{(m)}=\min_s C_s,\qquad \limsup_{K\to\infty}c_K^{(m)}=\max_s C_s,$$

and the full sequence converges exactly when all $C_s$ agree. The asymptotic frequency of the lattice phases is also exact:

$$\frac1M\sum_{K=0}^{M-1}\delta_{c_K^{(m)}} \Longrightarrow \frac1g\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\delta_{C_s}.$$

Indeed, split the empirical average into the $g$ residue classes and test against any bounded continuous function. Each class has asymptotic density $1/g$ and its function values converge to the corresponding phase value; coincident $C_s$ combine their masses automatically.

A computable finite-power bound

For $\bar L\ge2$, form the row-stochastic companion matrix $P$ whose first row is $(\bar p_1,\ldots,\bar p_{\bar L})$ and whose remaining rows shift one coordinate to the right. Coprimality makes $P$ primitive, so one can compute an $M$ for which every entry of $P^M$ is positive.

Let $\pi^\top P=\pi^\top$ be the invariant row and, for $n\ge0$, put $\beta_n=\frac12\sum_j|(P^n)_{1j}-\pi_j|$. Then, for every $q\ge\bar L-1$, with $n=q-\bar L+1$,

$$\boxed{|a_q-C_s|\le \beta_{q-\bar L+1} \left(\max_{0\le t<\bar L}a_t-\min_{0\le t<\bar L}a_t\right).}$$

Indeed, if $x_0=(a_{\bar L-1},\ldots,a_0)^\top$, then $a_q-C_s=(e_1^\top P^n-\pi^\top)x_0$. Total-variation duality gives the displayed bound, and choosing $x_{0,j}=1$ on the positive part of $(P^n)_{1j}-\pi_j$ shows that $\beta_n$ is the exact operator constant for the selected coordinate.

The worst-row Dobrushin coefficient $\delta_n=\frac12\max_{i,k}\sum_j|(P^n)_{ij}-(P^n)_{kj}|$ satisfies $\beta_n\le\delta_n$. For a reusable geometric bound, submultiplicativity gives $\delta_n\le\delta_M^{\lfloor n/M\rfloor}$ whenever $\delta_M<1$, and $\delta_M\le1-\bar L\min_{i,j}(P^M)_{ij}$.

For both certificate recurrences, $\pi=(2/3,1/3)$, $\delta_n=2^{-n}$, and $\beta_n=2^{-n}/3$. The selected-row coefficient bounds the true recurrence error by $6.801\times10^{-21}$ at aperiodic power 60, and by $1.746\times10^{-11}$ and $1.274\times10^{-11}$ at periodic powers 60 and 61.

A finite empirical phase-law bound

Put $\nu_T=T^{-1}\sum_{K=0}^{T-1}\delta_{c_K^{(m)}}$ and $\nu_\infty=g^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\delta_{C_s}$. Write $T=gQ+r$ with $0\le r<g$, let $n_s(T)$ count the integers $K<T$ congruent to $s$ modulo $g$, and set $D=\max_sC_s-\min_sC_s$.

For $O_s=\max_{t<\bar L}a_t-\min_{t<\bar L}a_t$ and $B_s(T)=\sum_{q=0}^{\min\{n_s(T),\bar L-1\}-1}|a_q-C_s|$,

$$\boxed{W_1(\nu_T,\nu_\infty)\le \frac1T\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\!\left[B_s(T)+O_s \sum_{n=0}^{n_s(T)-\bar L}\beta_n\right] +\frac{D\,r(g-r)}{gT}.}$$

A sum with upper index below zero is empty. Couple each $c_{s+gq}^{(m)}$ to $C_s$ and use the selected-row bound; the last term moves the empirical residue frequencies to the uniform phase weights, whose total-variation distance is exactly $r(g-r)/(gT)$.

Because the primitive companion chain makes $\sum_n\beta_n$ finite, the displayed estimate is $O(T^{-1})$. It is computable from the same boundary values and matrix powers as the pointwise theorem, without a spectral-root search.

At horizon $T=61$, the stage-eighteen exact $W_1$ errors are $0.000256397938$ and $0.001496458896$ for the aperiodic and period-two fixtures. The finite bounds are $0.000256397938$ and $0.001649428961$; after adding both staircase-enclosure errors, the corresponding true-staircase bounds are $0.000286915516$ and $0.001771499273$.

The second-order empirical phase law

The $O(T^{-1})$ rate has its own exact phase classification. Write $a_{s,q}=c_{s+gq}^{(m)}$, let $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{KR}}$ denote the Kantorovich–Rubinstein norm of a zero-mass signed measure, and define the convergent KR series

$$\eta_s=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\left(\delta_{a_{s,q}}-\delta_{C_s}\right),\qquad \xi_r=\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\left(\mathbf1_{\{s<r\}}-\frac rg\right)\delta_{C_s}.$$

The series converges because its $q$th summand has KR norm $|a_{s,q}-C_s|$ and the finite-power theorem makes these distances summable. For $r=0,\ldots,g-1$, put

$$\boxed{L_r=\left\|\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\eta_s+\xi_r\right\|_{\mathrm{KR}},\qquad T\,W_1(\nu_T,\nu_\infty)\longrightarrow L_r\quad(T\to\infty,\ T\equiv r\!\!\pmod g).}$$

Indeed, if $T=gQ+r$, then the number $n_s(T)$ of observations in residue $s$ is $Q+\mathbf1_{\{s<r\}}$, and exactly

$$T(\nu_T-\nu_\infty)=\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\sum_{q<n_s(T)} \left(\delta_{a_{s,q}}-\delta_{C_s}\right)+\xi_r.$$

Taking KR norms and passing to the summable limit proves the theorem. Thus the complete cluster set of $T\,W_1(\nu_T,\nu_\infty)$ is $\{L_0,\ldots,L_{g-1}\}$, and the scaled empirical error converges exactly when these constants agree.

The convergence is itself computable. If $N_s=n_s(T)-\bar L+1\ge0$ and $\delta_M<1$, then

$$\left|T\,W_1(\nu_T,\nu_\infty)-L_r\right|\le \sum_{s=0}^{g-1}O_s\sum_{n=N_s}^{\infty}\beta_n \le\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\frac{O_s}{1-\delta_M} \sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\beta_{N_s+j}.$$

These finite-stage constants can be transferred to the limiting staircase. If every boundary coefficient and phase limit changes by at most $\varepsilon$, convexity of the recurrence gives the same bound at every later power.

Put $S_N=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\beta_n$ and define, using the stage approximation's boundary oscillation $O_s$,

$$R_s(\varepsilon)=2\varepsilon(\bar L-1)+ \inf_{N\ge0}\left\{2\varepsilon N+(2O_s+2\varepsilon)S_N\right\}.$$

An elementary dipole $\delta_a-\delta_C$ changes in KR norm by at most $\min\{2\varepsilon,|a-C|+|\widetilde a-\widetilde C|\}$. Splitting the series at $N$ and applying the selected-row bound therefore gives

$$|L_r-\widetilde L_r|\le \sum_{s=0}^{g-1}R_s(\varepsilon) +\frac{2r(g-r)}g\varepsilon.$$

At stage eighteen, $\varepsilon=0.00001525878906$ for the aperiodic fixture and $0.00006103515625$ for the period-two fixture. The actual staircase constants lie in $[0.015304511273,0.015976037130]$, and in $[0.062080448845,0.066949191702]$ for even $T$ versus $[0.088788586133,0.093779399302]$ for odd $T$.

The periodic intervals are disjoint by at least $0.021839394432$. Thus the limiting singular staircase has a quantitatively certified second-order phase oscillation, not merely distinct finite-stage constants.

The phase distribution of the rescaled error

The second-order phases themselves have an empirical law. Put $Z_T=T\,W_1(\nu_T,\nu_\infty)$, $\rho_M=M^{-1}\sum_{T=1}^{M}\delta_{Z_T}$, and $\rho_\infty=g^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^{g-1}\delta_{L_r}$.

Let $d_T=|Z_T-L_{T\bmod g}|$ and $\Delta_L=\max_rL_r-\min_rL_r$. The computable tail estimate above makes $\Gamma=\sum_{T=1}^{\infty}d_T$ finite, and a termwise coupling followed by the residue-count correction gives, for $M=gQ+r$,

$$\boxed{W_1(\rho_M,\rho_\infty)\le \frac{\Gamma}{M}+\frac{\Delta_L\,r(g-r)}{gM}.}$$

Hence $\rho_M\Rightarrow\rho_\infty$ with an explicit $O(M^{-1})$ Wasserstein rate. The first term couples each $Z_T$ to its own residue limit; the second moves the slightly unbalanced finite residue counts to uniform weights.

To see that $\Gamma$ is computable, use $d_T\le\sum_sO_sS_{n_s(T)-\bar L+1}$ once every residue count reaches $\bar L-1$. Each tail $S_N=\sum_{n\ge N}\beta_n$ occurs at most $g$ times, while Dobrushin contraction makes $\sum_N S_N=\sum_n(n+1)\beta_n$ finite with a geometric bound.

The exact certificate checks 118 finite-horizon distribution inequalities. At $M=61$, the stage-eighteen Wasserstein distances are $0.000256397938$ and $0.002188590244$ for the aperiodic and period-two fixtures, while the corresponding limiting-staircase bounds are $0.001538205788$ and $0.008876497830$.

The second layer of rescaled phases

The $O(M^{-1})$ distributional rate also has exact residue limits. Define the convergent signed-measure series and the finite residue imbalance

$$\Theta=\sum_{T=1}^{\infty}\left(\delta_{Z_T}-\delta_{L_{T\bmod g}}\right),\qquad \zeta_q=\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}\left(\mathbf1_{\{1\le s\le q\}}-\frac qg\right)\delta_{L_s}.$$

The series converges in KR norm because its $T$th dipole has norm $d_T$ and $\sum_Td_T<\infty$. Put $H_q=\|\Theta+\zeta_q\|_{\mathrm{KR}}$ for $q=0,\ldots,g-1$.

$$\boxed{M\,W_1(\rho_M,\rho_\infty)\longrightarrow H_q \quad(M\to\infty,\ M\equiv q\!\!\pmod g).}$$

Indeed, the residues represented among $T=1,\ldots,M$ are counted $Q+1$ times for $1\le s\le q$ and $Q$ times otherwise when $M=gQ+q$. Therefore the exact signed-measure identity is

$$M(\rho_M-\rho_\infty)= \sum_{T=1}^{M}\left(\delta_{Z_T}-\delta_{L_{T\bmod g}}\right)+\zeta_q.$$

Taking KR norms and comparing the finite dipole sum with $\Theta$ gives the computable remainder

$$\left|M\,W_1(\rho_M,\rho_\infty)-H_q\right| \le\sum_{T>M}d_T.$$

Consequently the complete cluster set is $\{H_0,\ldots,H_{g-1}\}$, and the twice-rescaled error converges exactly when these constants agree. Once all lower indices are nonnegative, summing the selected-row tails gives

$$\sum_{T>M}d_T\le g\sum_{s=0}^{g-1}O_s \sum_{N\ge n_s(M+1)-\bar L+1}S_N,\qquad S_N=\sum_{n\ge N}\beta_n.$$

Indeed, for each fixed residue $s$, every $S_N$ occurs at most $g$ times as $T$ varies. The same block-Dobrushin contraction used above bounds the final double tail geometrically.

An all-order phase hierarchy

The preceding argument is an abstract closure principle, not a one-off calculation. Suppose a real sequence $x_n$ and phase vector $a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{g-1})$ satisfy

$$|x_n-a_{n\bmod g}|\le C\vartheta^n,\qquad 0<\vartheta<1.$$

Define its empirical-rescaling transform by

$$(\mathcal R_a x)_M= M\,W_1\!\left(\frac1M\sum_{n=1}^{M}\delta_{x_n}, \frac1g\sum_{r=0}^{g-1}\delta_{a_r}\right).$$

Let $b_q$ be the KR norm obtained from the dipole series and residue imbalance above, with $x_n,a_r$ in place of $Z_T,L_r$. The exact identity and the geometric tail give

$$\boxed{\left|(\mathcal R_a x)_M-b_{M\bmod g}\right| \le\frac{C\vartheta^{M+1}}{1-\vartheta}.}$$

Thus empirical rescaling preserves exponential convergence to a finite residue-phase vector. Starting from $x^{(0)}_T=Z_T$ and $a^{(0)}_r=L_r$, define recursively $x^{(j+1)}=\mathcal R_{a^{(j)}}x^{(j)}$ and let $a^{(j+1)}$ be its phase vector.

Induction gives, for every fixed depth $j\ge0$,

$$|x^{(j)}_n-a^{(j)}_{n\bmod g}| \le C\left(\frac{\vartheta}{1-\vartheta}\right)^j\vartheta^n.$$

The selected-row block contraction supplies the required exponential bound for $Z_T-L_{T\bmod g}$. Hence every finite iterated empirical-rescaling layer has a complete residue-indexed cluster set and a computable exponential remainder; the theorem does not assert that the phase values remain distinct.

The exact moment budget

Exponential convergence is sufficient for all depths but is not needed at any fixed depth. Write $d^{(0)}_n=|x^{(0)}_n-a^{(0)}_{n\bmod g}|$ and define the tail operator $(\mathsf Td)_M=\sum_{n>M}d_n$.

If the phase vector at layer $j+1$ exists, the same signed-measure identity gives

$$d^{(j+1)}_M\le(\mathsf Td^{(j)})_M.$$

Consequently, for $j\ge1$,

$$\boxed{d^{(j)}_M\le(\mathsf T^jd^{(0)})_M =\sum_{n>M}\binom{n-M-1}{j-1}d^{(0)}_n.}$$

The hockey-stick identity gives $\sum_{M\ge1}(\mathsf T^jd^{(0)})_M =\sum_{n\ge1}\binom{n-1}{j}d^{(0)}_n$. Therefore, if $\sum_{n\ge1}\binom{n-1}{J}d^{(0)}_n<\infty$ for an integer $J\ge0$, the hierarchy and finite residue-phase vectors exist through depth $J+1$.

This moment budget is sharp for the abstract theorem. For $g=1$ and $x^{(0)}_n=a+d_n$ with $d_n\ge0$, every displayed inequality is an equality: the layer-$j$ residual is exactly $\mathsf T^jd$, so the phase at depth $J+1$ is finite exactly when $\sum_n\binom{n-1}{J}d_n<\infty$.

Thus a polynomially decaying residual can support only finitely many layers even though an exponentially decaying renewal residual supports every fixed layer. This is a depth-by-depth statement and supplies no limit uniform in depth.

A summable sequence whose hierarchy stops

The obstruction has an exact rational family. For an integer $r\ge1$, use the rising factorial $(n)_k=n(n+1)\cdots(n+k-1)$ and set

$$d_n=\frac1{(n)_{r+1}}.$$

The elementary telescoping identity

$$\frac1{(n)_k}-\frac1{(n+1)_k}=\frac{k}{(n)_{k+1}}$$

gives, for $0\le j\le r$, with the empty product interpreted as one,

$$\boxed{(\mathsf T^jd)_M= \frac1{r(r-1)\cdots(r-j+1)\,(M+j)_{r+1-j}}.}$$

In the one-phase construction above, $d\in\ell^1$, so the first phase exists. The phases remain finite through depth $r$, but the depth-$r$ residual is

$$(\mathsf T^rd)_M=\frac1{r!\,(M+r)},$$

whose sum diverges. Hence the phase at depth $r+1$ does not exist: summable initial phase error alone cannot support arbitrary finite iteration.

The family also shows exactly how polynomial decay is spent. Since $d_n\sim n^{-(r+1)}$, each layer loses one power, and the final admissible residual is the harmonic boundary case.

The depth generating function

In the sharp one-phase construction, the phase values themselves are an exact binomial transform. Put $\alpha_j=a^{(j+1)}$ for the phase reached after $j+1$ rescalings. Whenever that phase is finite,

$$\boxed{\alpha_j=\sum_{n\ge1}\binom{n-1}{j}d_n,\qquad j\ge0.}$$

Indeed, the depth-$j$ residual is $\mathsf T^jd$, and summing it over the horizon index gives the hockey-stick identity already used in the moment budget. Thus the full finite-depth phase record is exactly the sequence of binomial moments of the initial residual.

Write $D(w)=\sum_{n\ge1}d_nw^{n-1}$. If $D(R)<\infty$ for some $R>1$, Tonelli's theorem and the binomial formula give the analytic identity

$$\boxed{A(z):=\sum_{j\ge0}\alpha_jz^j=D(1+z),\qquad |z|<R-1.}$$

The right side analytically continues $A$ to $|1+z|<R$, which contains $z=-1$. Consequently the depth phases determine the residual inside this exponential-moment class:

$$d_n=\frac{A^{(n-1)}(-1)}{(n-1)!}.$$

This is an exact recovery statement for the abstract one-phase hierarchy, not a claim that finitely many empirical layers identify an arbitrary staircase. Without an exponential moment, every fixed phase may still exist while the depth series has zero radius to the right of the origin; for example, a residual proportional to $e^{-\sqrt n}$ has every polynomial moment but no positive exponential moment.

The geometric family makes depth growth explicit. For $d_n=q^n$, $0<q<1$,

$$ (\mathsf T^jd)_M=\frac{q^{M+j}}{(1-q)^j},\qquad \alpha_j=\left(\frac q{1-q}\right)^{j+1},\qquad A(z)=\frac q{1-q-qz}. $$

Every finite layer exists, but its phase magnitudes decay, remain constant, or grow according as $q<1/2$, $q=1/2$, or $q>1/2$. This separates existence at each fixed depth from any bound uniform in depth.

Finite-depth inversion

The analytic recovery formula has a quantitative finite-depth version. Suppose $D(R)le B$ for some $R>2$, put $r=R-1$, and reconstruct the $n$th residual from phases through depth $Jge n-1$ by

$$\widehat d_{n,J}=\sum_{j=n-1}^{J}(-1)^{j-n+1}\binom{j}{n-1}\alpha_j.$$

Since $A(r)=D(R)=\sum_j\alpha_jr^j$, the omitted Taylor terms give

$$\boxed{|d_n-\widehat d_{n,J}|le B\sup_{j>J}\binom{j}{n-1}r^{-j}.}$$

The bound is computable and tends to zero because $r>1$. Once $J+2\ge r(n-1)/(r-1)$, the displayed supremum is its first term, so the error is at most $B\binom{J+1}{n-1}r^{-(J+1)}$.

The strict radius threshold cannot be removed for this Taylor reconstruction. In the geometric family, with $a=q/(1-q)$, the depth-$J$ estimate of the first residual is exactly

$$\widehat d_{1,J}=q\{1-(-a)^{J+1}\},\qquad |d_1-\widehat d_{1,J}|=q a^{J+1}.$$

It converges for $q<1/2$, oscillates without convergence at $q=1/2$, and diverges for $q>1/2$. Exact analytic continuation still identifies the residual when available; this result distinguishes that infinite-data statement from stable recovery by raw finite Taylor truncation.

Scope and certificate

This is an exact statement for a fixed multiplier $m$ and commensurable base-power horizontal scales. It does not describe arbitrary cell counts, nonlattice contraction ratios, or all singular-continuous quantiles.

The exact rational certificate checks 90 finite-stage recurrences, 120 selected-coordinate finite-power inequalities, 122 finite empirical-phase inequalities, 118 exact scaled signed-measure identities, 118 rescaled phase-distribution inequalities, 122 exact signed-KR identities at each of the next two empirical-rescaling layers, 36 sharp finite-support tail identities, 48 exact critical rational-tail identities, 359 exact depth-transform identities, and 104 exact finite-depth inversion identities. Direct grids reach 768 cells.

The cell-error functional obeys $|E_N(f)-E_N(h)|\le2\|f-h\|_\infty$, while the staircase iterates satisfy $\|C-C_n\|_\infty\le w_*^n$ for $w_*=\max_rw_r$. Since each limit coefficient is a convex combination of its boundary values, its stage-$n$ approximation has rigorous error at most $2mb^{L-1}w_*^n$.

At stage eighteen this gives the actual aperiodic limit enclosure $[0.209564408470,0.209594926049]$. The periodic even and odd limits lie respectively in $[0.129714973555,0.129837043869]$ and $[0.201915666289,0.202037736602]$, proving that its true cluster set has exactly two points.

Erdős, Feller and Pollard (1949) proves the classical arithmetic renewal coefficient for positive power series. Hausdorff (1921) provides the classical moment-sequence context for the binomial transform; no novelty is claimed for that transform.

Kantorovich and Rubinstein (1958) supplies the signed-measure transport norm, while Dobrushin (1956) introduces the stochastic-kernel overlap coefficient used in the computable tails.

This remains a marginal conditional-mean approximation statement. It is not feature-conditional conformal coverage, unrestricted optimal quantization, or a maturity-integrated rank claim.
