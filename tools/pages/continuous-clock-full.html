Infinite-mean random clocks can identify generators

An arbitrary known positive mixture of exponential intervals identifies a finite irreducible generator, even without a finite mean waiting time.

Let $Q$ be an irreducible row generator on $n\ge2$ fully labeled states. Independently of the chain, draw iid rates $\Lambda_k$ from a known probability law $\nu$ on $(0,\infty)$, then independent intervals $\tau_k\mid\Lambda_k\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\Lambda_k)$. Keep every sampled label, including repeats, but discard the rates and all realized timing information, including total elapsed time.

For a fixed sample count the labels are a Markov chain with kernel

$$R=\int_{(0,\infty)}\lambda(\lambda I-Q)^{-1}\,\nu(d\lambda),\qquad F(z)=\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+z}\,\nu(d\lambda).$$

There is no lower bound on the support of $\nu$ and no moment assumption on $\Lambda$ or $\tau$. The integral defining $R$ exists because each integrand is a stochastic matrix of infinity norm one. The population kernel determines $Q$ uniquely, without reversibility, diagonalizability or a speed cap.

Univalence away from the stationary mode

On $\mathcal H=\{z:\operatorname{Re}z>0\}$ the transform is holomorphic, by locally uniform dominated differentiation. For $z\ne w$ in this half-plane,

$$F(z)-F(w)=-(z-w)\int\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+z)(\lambda+w)}\,\nu(d\lambda).$$

When $z,w$ are both in the upper half-plane the integrand has strictly negative imaginary part, so the integral cannot vanish; the lower half-plane is the conjugate case. On the positive real axis the integral is strictly positive. Also $\operatorname{Im}F(z)$ has the opposite sign to $\operatorname{Im}z$, excluding collisions across these cases.

Thus $F$ is one-to-one on $\mathcal H$ with holomorphic inverse $G$ on $F(\mathcal H)$. The same argument at $w=z$ gives $F'(z)\ne0$, so nontrivial Jordan information is retained. For $\operatorname{Re}z>0$ one also has $|F(z)|<1$.

Why the zero eigenvalue must be separated

The transform may fail to be analytic at zero, so applying a holomorphic inverse blindly to the whole matrix would not be justified. Instead, $R$ is strictly positive, shares $Q$'s unique stationary row $\pi$, and determines the projector $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$. On the known invariant subspace $E=\ker\pi$, the operator $A_E=(-Q)|_E$ has spectrum in $\mathcal H$.

Restricting the resolvent integral to this subspace gives $R_E=F(A_E)$ by holomorphic functional calculus. The integral and all derivatives are locally dominated away from zero, irrespective of the mixing law's behavior near zero or infinity. Hence

$$\boxed{Q\mathbf1=0,\qquad Q|_E=-G(R_E).}$$

The decomposition $\mathbb R^n=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\oplus E$ is recovered from $R$ itself, proving uniqueness of every entry of $Q$. No derivative of $F$ at zero is needed because the stationary eigenvalue of an irreducible generator is simple.

A clock with infinite mean

In fixed time units take $\Lambda\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$. For $t>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}z>0$, direct integration gives

$$\Pr(\tau>t)=\frac{1-e^{-t}}t,\qquad f_\tau(t)=\frac{1-(1+t)e^{-t}}{t^2},\qquad F(z)=1-z\log(1+1/z).$$

The logarithm is principal on $\mathcal H$. The survival tail makes $\mathbb E\tau=\infty$, while $F(0)=1$ by continuity and $(F(x)-1)/x=-\log(1+1/x)\to-\infty$ as $x\downarrow0$. This is exactly the missing-analyticity boundary addressed by the stationary-mode split.

For a numerical kernel formula, put $A=-Q$, $B=A+\Pi$ and use the principal matrix logarithm:

$$R=I-B\operatorname{Log}(I+B^{-1})+(\log2)\Pi.$$

The shift replaces the zero eigenvalue by one, leaves the centered modes unchanged, and the last term restores the stationary eigenvalue to one. For opposite four-state unit-rate cycles $Q_+=C-I$ and $Q_-=C^\top-I$, the exact direction gap is

$$\|R(Q_+)-R(Q_-)\|_\infty =\log(5/2)-2\arctan(1/3)=0.272789623081.$$

The certificate checks 33 matrix-log kernels against rate integrals and spectral inversions, three independent waiting-density integrals, and three diagonalization-free defective Jordan inversions. Numerical inversion is a demonstration on these examples, not a universally certified inversion algorithm.

Scope and classical context

This is an ordinary iid renewal clock started at time zero, not a stationary renewal point process of positive intensity. Each finite sample count is reached in finite time almost surely, but its expected calendar time may be infinite. No calendar-time rate of estimation, finite-sample confidence guarantee or uniformly stable inverse is claimed.

Indeed, for $Q_m=m(\Pi-I)$ the kernel is $\Pi+F(m)(I-\Pi)$, and dominated convergence gives $F(m)\to0$. Kernels at $m$ and $2m$ therefore approach one another while their generator distance diverges. Global population identification does not imply uniform inverse continuity.

The rate law and its physical scale must be known and redrawn independently each interval. A single persistent hidden rate, state-dependent sampling, unknown clock parameters, noisy regime labels or calendar-time stopping is a different model.

Random-arrival identification is classical; Duffie and Glynn (2004), Proposition 4, use resolvent identification for a known arrival-intensity model. Here the specialization is an arbitrary independent positive rate mixture, including an infinite-mean interval law and a nonanalytic stationary spectral point. No priority claim for random-time identification, resolvent transforms or functional calculus is made.
