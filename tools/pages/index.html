Homogenization

Averaging a fast hidden variable, and computing what the average leaves out.

Many models carry a slow quantity of interest and a fast one that nobody observes: a regime that switches, a volatility that mean-reverts quickly, a hidden state that mixes. The first step is always the same. Replace the fast variable by its average over its stationary law. Homogenization treats that averaged model as the leading term and computes the next one, the corrector, as a series in the ratio of the two time scales.

This site starts with a problem from the second half of my 2001 Stanford thesis. The hazard rate, or short rate, is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process whose mean level and volatility switch between two regimes at rate $\lambda$. The quantity wanted is the survival probability, which is also a zero-coupon bond price. When the regimes switch infinitely fast the answer is Vasicek's formula with the regime averages plugged in. The corrector adds terms in $\varepsilon = 1/\lambda$.

The averaged model forgets the regime. The corrector remembers it at order ε.

The corrected formula

Let $dx_t = \kappa(\theta_{y_t} - x_t)\,dt + \sigma_{y_t}\,dW_t$, where $y_t \in \{1,2\}$ switches at rate $\lambda$ in each direction, and let $u_y(t,x) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{-\int_0^t x_r\,dr} \,\big|\, x_0 = x,\ y_0 = y\big]$. Write $B(r) = (1-e^{-\kappa r})/\kappa$, the regime averages and half-differences $\bar\theta, \tilde\theta = (\theta_1 \pm \theta_2)/2$ and $\bar s, \tilde s = (\sigma_1^2 \pm \sigma_2^2)/2$, and

$$\bar g(r) = -\kappa\bar\theta B(r) + \tfrac12 \bar s B(r)^2, \qquad \tilde g(r) = -\kappa\tilde\theta B(r) + \tfrac12 \tilde s B(r)^2 .$$

With $\varepsilon = 1/\lambda$ and the upper sign for regime 1,

$$u_y(t,x) = \exp\!\Big(-B(t)x + \int_0^t \bar g + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_0^t \tilde g^{\,2} - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\tilde g(t)^2\Big)\Big(1 \pm \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\tilde g(t) \mp \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\tilde g'(t)\Big) + O(\varepsilon^3).$$

The first two terms in the exponent are Vasicek's bond price with the averaged parameters. The $\varepsilon$ term inside the exponent is the same for both regimes and grows with maturity. The bracket is the part that depends on the starting regime, and it vanishes as $\lambda \to \infty$.

The model also has an exact solution, which is what makes the expansion checkable. Because $\kappa$ does not switch, $u_y(t,x) = a_y(t)\,e^{-B(t)x}$, where $a$ solves a two-dimensional linear ODE driven by $\bar g \pm \tilde g$ and the switching generator. This reduction is known: Elliott and Mamon (2002) solve the case of a switching mean level, and Elliott and Siu (2009) give Markov-modulated exponential-affine bond prices in general.

Against the exact solution, the error after zero, one and two correction orders falls like $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon^2$ and $\varepsilon^3$. The convergence demo shows the three slopes, and the survival demo shows the curves. The published fast-switching expansions treat option prices and transition densities, and a search of the literature did not find the corrector for this bond price or survival probability.

The formula holds away from an initial layer of width about $1/(2\lambda)$, in which the regime has not yet had time to switch. Inside the layer the second-order bracket carries an extra transient of size $\varepsilon^2 e^{-2\lambda t}$.

A correction to the 2020 code

The first-order terms in the homogenize package, released in 2020 with an article on the same model, are wrong. Its regime-dependent term has the wrong sign on $\tilde\theta$, is squared, and lacks a factor of one half, and its regime-independent term does not match $\tfrac12\int_0^t \tilde g^2$. With those terms the error stays of order $\varepsilon$, so the correction adds nothing. The formula above replaces them. The certificate script is verify_expansion.py.

The wider program

  • Fast mean-reverting stochastic volatility. The same expansion for interest rate derivatives when volatility, rather than the regime, is the fast variable: Cotton, Fouque, Papanicolaou and Sircar (2004), listed on the papers page.
  • Conformal prediction as the averaged limit. Pooling residuals across states is the same averaging step, taken without a corrector: Homogenization without the corrector.
  • Forecast calibration. An online two-point scale mixture replaces an unresolved mixture of regimes by an effective width: homogenization in skaters.
  • Choice among racing processes. Softmax as the homogenized choice law of fast hidden kinetics, with its first correction: kinetics papers.
