Three regimes preserve the share-mean sign; four can reverse it

A positive inverse-determinant kernel removes the possibility of accidental maturity cancellation in three states.

Let an irreducible finite chain $Y$, independent of Brownian motions $B,W$, have row generator $mQ$, $m>0$, and stationary initial law $\pi$. Use a common fixed $v_0\ge0$, $X_0=0$, positive switched coefficients $a_i,\kappa_i,\xi_i$, and $|\rho_i|\le1$:

$$dv=(a_Y-\kappa_Yv)dt+\xi_Y\sqrt v\,dB,\qquad dX=-v\,dt/2+\sqrt v(\rho_Y\,dB+\sqrt{1-\rho_Y^2}\,dW).$$

Compare $Q$ with $Q^\leftarrow=D_\pi^{-1}Q^TD_\pi$ using the same initialization. Assume $S=e^X$ is a true martingale and the two share-weighted means below are finite. Put $k_i=\kappa_i-\rho_i\xi_i$, $A=mQ-D_k$, and define forward minus reverse values by

$$D_m(T)=\mathbb E_Q[e^{X_T}X_T]-\mathbb E_{Q^\leftarrow}[e^{X_T}X_T],\qquad \mathcal B=\langle k,Qa\rangle_\pi-\langle a,Qk\rangle_\pi.$$

With three states, either $\mathcal B=0$ and $D_m(T)=0$ for every $m,T>0$, or $D_m(T)$ has the strict sign of $\mathcal B$ for every $m,T>0$. A nonzero gap cannot reverse sign or vanish accidentally at an isolated maturity or speed.

Four states are the smallest possible exception: the positive CIR example below has a rigorously negative gap at $T=0.6$ and a positive one at $T=0.8$, at the same speed.

For a nonreversible three-state chain and nonconstant $k$, equality at even one positive speed and maturity is therefore equivalent to $a\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,k\}$. This strengthens the all-maturity mean criterion, not a claim that all call prices or all return moments agree.

The scalar mean and its resolvent

Under the share measure with density $e^{X_T}$, variance drift is $a_Y-k_Yv$ and log-price drift is $+v/2$; regime jump intensities stay $mQ$. The backward conditional variance mean has the form $b_i(t)v+d_i(t)$, with $b'=Ab$, $b(0)=\mathbf1$, $d'=mQd+D_ab$, $d(0)=0$. Stationarity and weighted adjointness under reversal give the exact identity

$$D_m(T)=\frac12\int_0^T(T-u)F(u)du,\qquad F(u)=\langle a,e^{uA}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,e^{uA}a\rangle_\pi.$$

The fixed $v_0$ contribution is $v_0\langle\mathbf1,e^{tA}\mathbf1\rangle_\pi$ in each variance mean and cancels. This uses only first moments and the usual change of numéraire, not one common affine transform for switched vol-of-vol.

For the linear functional $L(M)=\langle a,M\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,Ma\rangle_\pi$, stationarity gives $L(I)=L(A)=0$ and $L(A^2)=m\mathcal B$. In dimension three, $\operatorname{adj}(zI-A)=A^2+(z-\operatorname{tr}A)A+\{z^2-z\operatorname{tr}A+c_2\}I$, where $c_2$ is the sum of principal order-two minors. Thus, for real $z$ sufficiently large,

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-zu}F(u)du=\frac{m\mathcal B}{\det(zI-A)}.$$

Why the inverse characteristic kernel is strictly positive

Choose $c=\max\{0,\max_i(m(-q_{ii})+k_i)\}$, so $P=A+cI$ is entrywise nonnegative, and let $R=\|P\|_\infty=c-\min_i k_i$. The ordinary determinant identity

$$\frac1{\det(I-xP)}=\exp\left(\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(P^r)}r x^r\right) =\sum_{j\ge0}h_jx^j$$

has $h_0=1$ and $h_j\ge0$, since every trace is nonnegative. Also $h_j\le\binom{j+2}{2}R^j$: factor the determinant over the three eigenvalues, each of modulus at most $R$, and bound its geometric-series coefficients. Consequently the everywhere convergent series

$$g_A(u)=e^{-cu}\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{h_j u^{j+2}}{(j+2)!}$$

has Laplace transform $1/\det(zI-A)$ for $z>R-c$, by termwise integration. It is strictly positive for $u>0$, and satisfies

$$\frac{u^2}2e^{-cu}\le g_A(u)\le\frac{u^2}2e^{-(\min_i k_i)u}.$$

Uniqueness of Laplace transforms now gives $F(u)=m\mathcal B g_A(u)$, proving the strict sign law. No diagonalization, real-spectrum assumption or exclusion of defective matrices is needed; even negative share-measure reversion coefficients are allowed when the stated means exist.

The reciprocal-determinant generating function is classical, as in the commuting specialization of Garoufalidis, Lê and Zeilberger (2006), equations (1)–(2). The trace-series proof is included here; no novelty is claimed for that identity or for positive-kernel Laplace inversion.

Writing $I_b(T)=\int_0^T(T-u)u^2e^{-bu}du$, the argument also supplies the explicit bounds

$$\frac{m|\mathcal B|}4I_c(T)\le |D_m(T)|\le\frac{m|\mathcal B|}4I_{\min_i k_i}(T).$$

These are finite-parameter inequalities, not a truncated maturity-uniform expansion. Finally $\mathcal B=J\det\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1&1\\k_1&k_2&k_3\\a_1&a_2&a_3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, where $J=\pi_1q_{12}-\pi_2q_{21}$. Nonreversibility gives $J\ne0$, proving the stated affine equality criterion when $k$ is nonconstant.

A four-state, strict-Feller sign reversal

Use the directed unit four-cycle $q_{i,i+1}=1$, $q_{ii}=-1$ with indices modulo four, uniform $\pi$, $m=1$, and

$$\kappa=2,\quad\rho=-3/5,\quad\xi=(1/5,2/5,1,3/2),\quad a=(2,1,5,5),\quad v_0=1/25,\quad k=(53/25,56/25,13/5,29/10).$$

Every regime has $2a_i>\xi_i^2$, with minimum margin $46/25$. Common positive variance loadings $b=1/2$ at return moment $r=2$, and $b=1/10$ at $r=-1/10$, satisfy $\xi_i^2b^2/2+(\rho\xi_i r-\kappa)b+r(r-1)/2<0$ in every state, ensuring true-stock-martingale and moment-strip conditions by localization.

Here $L(A^2)=-3/200$ and $L(A^3)-(\operatorname{tr}A)L(A^2)=3/40$. The exact adjugate calculation gives

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-zT}D_1(T)dT =\frac{-3z/200+3/40}{2z^2\det(zI-A)}.$$

The small-maturity coefficient is $L(A^2)/48<0$ while the long-maturity slope is $(3/40)/(2\det(-A))=9375/35231888>0$. All $k_i$ are positive, so $A$ is stable. More directly, exact rational arithmetic certifies

$$D_1(3/5)\in[-0.000001665616,-0.000001665615],\qquad D_1(4/5)\in[0.000002658062,0.000002658063].$$

Thus at least one zero lies in $(3/5,4/5)$; uniqueness is not claimed. Two states are reversible and three obey the strict sign law, so four states are minimal for this maturity sign reversal under the stated initialization.

What the sign boundary does and does not say about calls

If the MGFs exist on an open interval containing $[0,1]$, moment-tail bounds and payoff integration give $\int_{\mathbb R}(C_Q(x)-C_{Q^\leftarrow}(x))dx=D_m(T)$, with $x$ denoting log strike. When the original $\kappa_i$ are common, as in the example, also $\int e^{-x}(C_Q(x)-C_{Q^\leftarrow}(x))dx=0$.

The four-state zero therefore restores the unweighted zero-area constraint at a maturity even though it fails at nearby maturities. If the two full call curves differ there, both areas force at least two crossings. Neither the area sign nor its maturity reversal counts pointwise call crossings or proves a strikewise sign law.

The certificate checks $90$ independent forward-moment/kernel comparisons, resolvent identities, nonnegative series and both kernel-derived bounds, at portable tolerance $3\times10^{-9}$. The four-state sign intervals use rational degree-$160$ Taylor actions in dimension $9$ and induced-norm remainder bounds; their summed tails are below $10^{-100}$, not floating tolerances.

This complements the finite mean-cancellation criterion and leaves sharp full call-crossing counts in issue #204 open. Joint-invariant-start variance profiles concern a different observable and initialization; no conflict with their possible maturity sign changes is implied.
