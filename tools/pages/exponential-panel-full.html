Exact ranks from exponential-age calibration

Randomize the whole panel, not only the last gap: one CDF contrast permits exact marginal rank coverage without a mixing-rate estimate.

Let $X_t$, $t\in\mathbb R$, be a bounded, jointly measurable, strictly stationary real signal, with $\mathbb E X_0=0$. Conditional on the full signal and the sampling times, score emissions are independent with fixed continuous CDFs

$$F_{X_t}(s)=\bar F(s)+X_tH(s).$$

Each realized signal value must give a genuine CDF; $\bar F$ is the stationary pooled CDF. No Markov property, ergodicity, mixing or path continuity is assumed. A stationary finite-state chain is one example, with $X_t=h(Y_t)$ and $\pi h=0$.

The one-contrast condition is automatic for any stationary binary regime process, with arbitrary continuous CDFs $F_1,F_2$: take $X_t=\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=1\}}-\pi_1$, $\bar F=\pi_1F_1+\pi_2F_2$ and $H=F_1-F_2$. It is not automatic for three or more regime-specific CDFs.

The sampling rule and exact rank theorem

For any $N\ge1$ and $\beta>0$, independently draw chronological gaps $D_j\sim\operatorname{Exp}(j\beta)$, $1\le j\le N$, where the argument is a rate. Observe $N$ calibration scores at $0,D_1,\ldots,D_1+\cdots+D_{N-1}$ and the test score at $D_1+\cdots+D_N$. For their $k$th calibration order statistic $Q_k$,

$$\boxed{\Pr(S_{\rm test}\le Q_k)=\frac{k}{N+1},\qquad 1\le k\le N.}$$

The probability averages over the stationary signal, scores and randomized schedule. No transition model or mixing rate is needed to choose the rule. The panel span is unbounded, with expectation $H_N/\beta$, where $H_N=\sum_{j=1}^N1/j$.

For $N=9$, $\beta=1$, the expected span is $7129/2520=2.828968253968$ and rank-nine marginal coverage is $9/10$.

Proof by a self-calibrated scalar filter

By stationarity, place the test time at zero and draw labeled calibration ages $A_1,\ldots,A_N$ iid $\operatorname{Exp}(\beta)$, independently of the full process. Exponential order statistics have independent spacings with rates $N\beta,(N-1)\beta,\ldots,\beta$ going backward from the test. Reversing the spacing list gives the chronological rule above; sorting calibration labels does not change $Q_k$.

Define the bounded stationary scalar filter

$$U_t=\beta\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta a}X_{t-a}\,da,\qquad \dot U_t=\beta(X_t-U_t)\quad\hbox{almost everywhere}.$$

The filter has absolutely continuous paths even when $X$ does not. For every continuously differentiable $\varphi$ and $T>0$, the chain rule, boundedness and Fubini give

$$0=\mathbb E[\varphi(U_T)-\varphi(U_0)] =\beta\int_0^T\mathbb E[\varphi'(U_t)(X_t-U_t)]\,dt =\beta T\mathbb E[\varphi'(U_0)(X_0-U_0)].$$

The final equality uses joint stationarity of $(X,U)$, not differentiation at a prescribed exceptional time. Every continuous function on the compact range is a derivative of such a $\varphi$, so

$$\mathbb E[X_0\mid U_0]=U_0.$$

Conditional on the entire past signal including its current value, independently sampled calibration ages and emission noise make the labeled calibration scores iid with CDF $\bar F+U_0H$. Their joint conditional law depends on that past only through $U_0$, while the test CDF given $U_0$ is also $\bar F+U_0H$ by the displayed identity. Consequently the age-labeled calibration scores and test score are iid conditional on $U_0$.

These conditional CDFs are continuous mixtures of the emission laws, so there are no ties and the test rank is uniform. This proves the marginal formula without any decorrelation assumption or stationary filter density. The chronologically ordered score vector itself need not be exchangeable: the iid statement uses the unsorted age labels.

Why the assumptions matter

The result is not coverage conditional on the realized sampling times, calibration scores or current regime. For the two-state generator $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-3&3\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, emissions uniform on $[0,1]$ and $[2,3]$, $N=k=2$ and $\beta=1$, current-state conditional coverages are $14/15$ and $26/45$. Their stationary average is $2/3$.

The one-contrast assumption also has a genuine boundary. With

$$Q=\frac16\begin{pmatrix}-5&1&4\\1&-5&4\\4&4&-8\end{pmatrix},\qquad \pi=\tfrac13(1,1,1),\qquad S\mid Y=i\sim\operatorname{Unif}[2i,2i+1],\quad i=0,1,2,$$

take $N=k=2$, $\beta=1/2$. Exact rational enumeration gives coverage $1621/2430=2/3+1/2430$, not $2/3$. The three CDFs do not lie on one affine line.

The protocol changes every gap and has a random total span; it is not a fixed-span optimality result or a universal final-gap adjustment. Nonstationary starts, overlapping path scores, score maps updated from this panel and general multi-contrast emission laws are outside the theorem.

A nonmixing, non-Markov witness

Let $Y_t=\mathbf1_{\{(t+\Theta)\bmod P<A\}}$, with $P=A+B$, $A,B>0$ and independent $\Theta\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,P)$. This binary process is stationary, has deterministic holding lengths and is not Markov in its binary state. It is not mixing: $\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_{mP})=p(1-p)$ for every integer $m$, where $p=A/P$.

For $W=\beta\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta a}Y_{-a}\,da$, put $a=e^{-\beta A}$ and $b=e^{-\beta B}$. The filter ranges between $w_-=b(1-a)/(1-ab)$ and $w_+=(1-a)/(1-ab)$, with

$$f_W(w)=\frac1{\beta P\,w(1-w)},\quad w_-<w<w_+,\qquad \Pr(Y_0=1\mid W=w)=w.$$

The rising and falling branches have phase densities $1/[\beta P(1-w)]$ and $1/(\beta Pw)$, respectively, proving both formulas. Thus arbitrary continuous binary emissions have exact randomized marginal ranks despite dependence that never decays. This does not turn stationarity alone into a guarantee for arbitrary score processes: conditional emission independence and one CDF contrast remain essential.

A finite-history variant with an explicit error budget

If each labeled age is instead drawn independently from $\operatorname{Exp}(\beta)$ conditioned to be at most $L$, then the rule stays within a history of length $L$ and

$$\left|\Pr(S_{\rm test}\le Q_k)-\frac{k}{N+1}\right| \le 1-(1-e^{-\beta L})^N\le Ne^{-\beta L}.$$

Couple the truncated age vector with the untruncated one; they agree unless at least one of the $N$ exponential ages exceeds $L$. The same signal and score draws then couple the rank events, proving the bound without a mixing assumption. At $N=9$, $\beta=1$, $L=10$, the bound is $4.085251741918\times10^{-4}$.

Certificate and relation to other guarantees

The certificate performs 120 exact polynomial rank-mass checks through $N=5$ on two-, three- and four-state chains, including nonreversible and defective examples. An independent count recursion and Gaussian quadrature check 171 rank masses through $N=9$ with unequal Gaussian emission laws.

Both counterexamples above use rational arithmetic. Six exact two-gap resolvent identities separately verify $2R_1=R_2+R_1R_2$, where $R_j=j\beta(j\beta I-Q)^{-1}$. The all-$N$ theorem follows from the filter argument, not from a finite mesh.

The periodic witness adds 63 exact separated-uniform rank identities and 57 unequal-Gaussian rank comparisons, through $N=9$. An independent phase integral checks the latent calibration-count/current-state weights.

The full path-conformal note gives the classical filtered-telegraph conditional-mean identity as a special case. General mixing-based concentration and decoupling guarantees remain separate; see Oliveira, Orenstein, Ramos and Romano (2024).
