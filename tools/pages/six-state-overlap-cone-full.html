Six- through eight-state Green–Kubo obstructions

Unrestricted tree-coefficient barriers, with sharper bounds for sparse six-state mixtures and reversible attachments.

Research note, 23 October 2026. Weighted-circulation log-concavity closes the unrestricted eight-state case and makes the nine-state construction minimal.

Result

Every connected balanced stationary flow on six states obeys the unrestricted product bound

$$\sigma_1\sigma_2\le1.$$

Equivalently, its two positive matrix phases satisfy $\arctan\sigma_1+\arctan\sigma_2\le\pi/2$. The target has both frequencies $\kappa=\cot(\pi/5)$, hence product $\kappa^2=1+2/\sqrt5>1$, so no six-state flow realizes it.

A sharper theorem holds when the flow is the sum of at most three genuinely directed simple cycles and an arbitrary nonnegative reversible stationary flow. Only the final support, not the directed base, is assumed connected.

Let $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge0$ be its two intrinsic energy-normalized skew frequencies. Then

$$\sigma_2\le\sqrt{\frac35}.$$

For two cycles the bound is attained at equal weights by, for example,

$$c_A=(0,5,2,3,4,1),\qquad c_B=(0,3,4,5,2,1),$$

whose union is neither a cactus nor translation invariant. Within this cone equality holds exactly when $a=b>0$, and then both frequencies equal $\sqrt{3/5}$.

Up to state relabeling and simultaneous reversal of all directed cycles, the equal-weight theorem has a unique equality-support orbit, represented by

$$c_A=(0,1,2,3),\qquad c_B=(0,1,4,5),\qquad c_C=(2,3,4,5).$$

Every support in this orbit permits arbitrary positive weights. Equality within each such weight cone occurs exactly when the three weights agree; its normalized pencil is then $t(t^2+3/5)^2$. A one-cycle irreducible flow must itself be Hamiltonian and also satisfies the bound.

The three-directed-cycle attachment class obeys the same sharp bound without counting or restricting its reversible edges. In particular, reversible two-cycles do not consume the three genuinely directed-cycle slots. The one-directed-cycle subclass obeys the stronger bound $\sigma_2\le1/\sqrt3<\sqrt{3/5}$.

The three-triangle class is rigid rather than merely bounded: every connected positive mixture has $\sigma_1=\sigma_2=1/\sqrt3$, independently of its three weights.

More generally, on any finite state space, a nonnegative mixture of directed cycles of lengths at most $L$, plus arbitrary reversible stationary flow, has every intrinsic skew frequency at most $\cot(\pi/L)$. The constant is sharp, and the triangle theorem is the case $L=3$.

If the sharp value $\cot(\pi/L)$ occurs in $r$ positive modes, then at least $1+r(L-1)$ states are necessary. Thus a flow decomposable into cycles of lengths at most five needs at least nine states to carry two target modes, and the shared-hub pair of five-cycles attains nine.

More quantitatively, two five-cycles sharing at least two states, with arbitrary positive weights and reversible attachments, satisfy the sharp gap $\sigma_2\le1$.

Consequently no six-, seven- or eight-state four-feature compression realizes $I_4+\cot(\pi/5)\operatorname{diag}(J,J)$, where $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The unrestricted minimum is exactly nine, attained by two directed five-cycles sharing one state.

Stationary-flow coordinates

Any prescribed positive stationary law $\pi$ gives a generator through $q_{ij}=W_{ij}/\pi_i$ for $i\ne j$. Thus varying holding times or $\pi$ changes the generator but not the stationary-flow Laplacian

$$C=\operatorname{diag}(W{\bf1})-W.$$

Delete one row and column and write the grounded form as $\bar C=H+D$, with $H=(\bar C+\bar C^\top)/2$ and $D=(\bar C-\bar C^\top)/2$. Irreducibility makes $H$ positive definite.

The eigenvalues of $H^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2}$ are $\{\pm i\sigma_1,\pm i\sigma_2,0\}$. Hence

$$\frac{\det(tH-D)}{\det H}=t(t^2+\sigma_1^2)(t^2+\sigma_2^2).$$

Unrestricted six-state phase-product obstruction

For $0\le x\le1$, set $C_x=xC+(1-x)C^\top$. Balance makes $C_x$ another Laplacian, and transposition gives the same grounded cofactor at $x$ and $1-x$.

The directed matrix-tree theorem expands that cofactor uniquely as

$$P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^5 B_kx^k(1-x)^{5-k},\qquad B_k\ge0,\qquad B_k=B_{5-k}.$$

Here $B_k$ sums rooted tree terms in which exactly $k$ edges use their forward flow and the other $5-k$ use their reverse flow. All root cofactors, and therefore all $B_k$, agree because every $C_x$ is balanced.

After summing over all roots, there is a weight-preserving injection from the objects counted by $B_0$ to those counted by $B_1$. Start with an out-arborescence rooted at $r$, choose one child $s$ of $r$, and reroot the same tree at $s$.

The edge $r\to s$ is then the unique edge pointing toward the new root, while every other edge points away from it. Choosing the least-labeled child makes the map injective, since the image tree, new root and exceptional edge recover $r$.

Thus $B_1\ge B_0$. On writing $z=2x-1$, the same determinant is

$$P(x)=\det(H+zD)=\det H\{1+(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)z^2+\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^2z^4\}.$$

Exact conversion of the symmetric Bernstein basis gives

$$\det H\,(1-\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^2)=\frac{B_1-B_0}{4}\ge0.$$

This proves the product bound for every connected balanced six-state flow, with no cycle-count or support restriction. A directed six-cycle has normalized polynomial $1+(10/3)z^2+z^4$ and attains equality in the product bound.

The dependency-free certificate checks the injection on all $6^4=1{,}296$ labeled trees and all $7{,}776$ rootings, verifies the exact basis identity, and recovers the directed-cycle equality polynomial.

The cofactor expansion is the weighted directed matrix-tree theorem of De Leenheer (2020). The phase terminology follows the semi-sectorial framework of Wang, Mao, Chen and Qiu (2023); the coefficient injection and resulting product obstruction are proved directly above.

Unrestricted seven-state tree barrier

On seven states the grounded skew operator has three positive frequencies $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\sigma_3\ge0$. Put $s_j=\sigma_j^2$ and

$$e_1=s_1+s_2+s_3,\qquad e_2=s_1s_2+s_1s_3+s_2s_3,\qquad e_3=s_1s_2s_3.$$

The same balanced interpolation and rerooting injection now give a symmetric degree-six tree expansion

$$P(x)=\sum_{k=0}^6B_kx^k(1-x)^{6-k},\qquad B_k=B_{6-k},\qquad B_1\ge B_0.$$

With $z=2x-1$, its spectral form is

$$P(x)=\det H\{1+e_1z^2+e_2z^4+e_3z^6\}.$$

Exact conversion from the symmetric Bernstein basis gives the new unrestricted barrier

$$\det H\,(5+e_1-3e_2-7e_3)=B_1-B_0\ge0.$$

A directed seven-cycle has normalized polynomial $1+5z^2+3z^4+z^6/7$ and attains equality, so the coefficient inequality itself is sharp.

Now suppose a four-dimensional feature compression had both target frequencies $\kappa=\cot(\pi/5)$. Hermitian compression interlacing forces the two largest full frequencies to obey $\sigma_1,\sigma_2\ge\kappa$.

Write $r=\kappa^2=1+2/\sqrt5$ and $(x,y,z)=(s_1,s_2,s_3)$. The barrier polynomial

$$\Phi(x,y,z)=5+x+y+z-3xy-3xz-3yz-7xyz$$

is strictly decreasing in each variable when $x,y\ge r>1$ and $z\ge0$. Its largest possible value on that region is therefore

$$\Phi(r,r,0)=5+2r-3r^2=\frac85-\frac8{\sqrt5}<0,$$

contradicting the tree barrier. Hence seven states are impossible without any support, cycle-count or translation-invariance assumption, and the unrestricted bracket improves to

$$8\le N_{\min}\le9.$$

The dependency-free certificate checks the rerooting injection on all $7^5=16{,}807$ labeled trees and all $117{,}649$ rootings, verifies the exact basis identity, and recovers the seven-cycle equality polynomial. Interlacing is applied only to this fixed-flow, fixed-feature compression; it makes no claim about rank after integrating maturity-dependent loadings.

Unrestricted eight-state log-concavity obstruction

On eight states the grounded skew operator has three positive frequencies $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\sigma_3\ge0$. With $s_j=\sigma_j^2$ and $e_1,e_2,e_3$ the elementary symmetric functions of the $s_j$, the balanced tree polynomial has degree seven:

$$P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^7B_ku^k(1-u)^{7-k}=\det H\{1+e_1(2u-1)^2+e_2(2u-1)^4+e_3(2u-1)^6\}.$$

Palindromicity gives $B_k=B_{7-k}$. Exact Bernstein conversion gives

$$\frac1{\det H}\begin{pmatrix}B_0\\B_1\\B_2\\B_3\end{pmatrix}= \begin{pmatrix}1+e_1+e_2+e_3\\7+3e_1-e_2-5e_3\\21+e_1-3e_2+9e_3\\35-5e_1+3e_2-5e_3\end{pmatrix}.$$

The positive arcs of the stationary flow form a connected directed support carrying positive circulation weights. After parallel arcs with the same orientation are merged, let $\beta=m_{\mathrm{red}}-7$; then $1\le\beta\le49$.

A theorem of Gao and Yuan (2026) applies exactly to this weighted spanning-tree polynomial. Its coefficients are positive, palindromic and log-concave, and at each internal index

$$Q_i^{(\beta)}=\beta B_i^2-B_i(B_{i-1}+B_{i+1})-(\beta-2)B_{i-1}B_{i+1}\ge0.$$

Since $\beta\le49$ and log-concavity gives $B_i^2\ge B_{i-1}B_{i+1}$, it follows that $Q_i^{(49)}\ge0$. This upper-rank replacement is deliberately weaker than the cited theorem and therefore applies uniformly to every eight-state support.

Now write $(x,y,z)=(s_1,s_2,s_3)$. If $y\ge7/4$, palindromic log-concavity first gives $B_1\ge B_0$, hence

$$3+x+y-xy-z(x+y+3xy-1)\ge0.$$

Because $x\ge y\ge7/4$, this forces the compact rational bounds

$$\frac74\le y\le3,\qquad y\le x\le\frac{19}{3},\qquad0\le z\le\frac3{10}.$$

The first two bounds follow from $xy\le3+x+y$. For the last, $z\le R(x,y)=(3+x+y-xy)/(x+y+3xy-1)$, and the two derivative numerators of $R$ are $-4(y+1)^2$ and $-4(x+1)^2$; hence $R(x,y)\le R(7/4,7/4)=55/187<3/10$.

On this box, exact rational interval arithmetic proves that every point with $x\ge y$ and $B_1\ge B_0$ has either $Q_1^{(49)}<0$ or $Q_2^{(49)}<0$. The certificate uses 5,576 terminal boxes, reaches depth 42, and verifies the Bernstein identities before the cover.

Thus every connected balanced eight-state flow obeys the strict bound

$$\sigma_2^2<\frac74.$$

This rational cutoff is nearly sharp for the coefficient relaxation, rather than an arbitrary loose box. At the exact point $x=y=1749/1000$, $z=33241/250000$, the four independent $B_i$ are positive and strictly increasing and every internal $Q_i^{(49)}$ inequality is strict; thus these universal coefficient inequalities alone permit $\sigma_2^2=1.749$, only $1/1000$ below $7/4$.

The relaxation witness is not claimed to come from a balanced graph. Improving the universal graph bound below $1.749$ would therefore require additional realizability information, not just the Gao–Yuan coefficient inequalities used here.

Graph realizability nevertheless does not preserve the complete Fourier ladder of one directed cycle. Give the directed eight-cycle $(0\,5\,6\,1\,2\,3\,7\,4)$ weight one and the directed six-cycle $(0\,5\,7\,6\,1\,4)$ weight $2/5$. $$\frac{\det(H+uD)}{\det H} =1+\frac{14057}{4541}u^2+\frac{10791}{4541}u^4+\frac{1187}{4541}u^6.$$

Its squared intrinsic frequencies are the three roots of

$$q(s)=4541s^3-14057s^2+10791s-1187.$$

$$\frac{51}{50}<\sigma_2^2<\frac{103}{100}.$$

A single directed eight-cycle has $\sigma_2^2=\cot^2(\pi/4)=1$, so the overlapping shorter cycle raises the second mode. Thus the single-cycle bounds $\sigma_j\leq\cot(j\pi/8)$ do not extend mode by mode to arbitrary balanced flows, even to a mixture of two cycles. The example is a graph-realizable lower bound, not a claim that the unrestricted $7/4$ upper cutoff is sharp.

Exact pencil calculation

Direct expansion of the five-dimensional determinant gives

$$\frac{\det(tH-D)}{\det H}=t\{t^4+S(a,b)t^2+P(a,b)\},$$ $$S(a,b)=\frac{2(5a^4+12a^3b+11a^2b^2+12ab^3+5b^4)}{3(a^2+3ab+b^2)^2},$$ $$P(a,b)=\left(\frac{a^2+ab+b^2}{a^2+3ab+b^2}\right)^2.$$

Put $x=ab/(a+b)^2\in[0,1/4]$. Then

$$S(x)=\frac{2(5-8x-3x^2)}{3(1+x)^2},\qquad P(x)=\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^2.$$

The squared frequencies are the roots of $f_x(z)=z^2-S(x)z+P(x)$. At the proposed sharp value,

$$f_x(3/5)=\frac{16(4x-1)}{25(1+x)}\le0.$$

Since $f_x$ is monic with two nonnegative real roots, $3/5$ lies between them. Therefore $\sigma_2^2\le3/5$.

Equality requires $x=1/4$, equivalently $a=b>0$. Substitution gives $t(t^2+3/5)^2$, proving both equality and multiplicity without a floating search.

Arbitrary weights on two cycles

There are 409 simple directed cycles on six labeled states, counted up to cyclic rotation. Relabel the first cycle canonically according to its length, from two through six; retaining connected unions leaves 1,372 relative pairs and 133 distinct exact coefficient triples.

For each pencil write $r=b/a=s/(1-s)$ with $0\le s\le1$, and let $f(z)=z^2-Sz+P$ be its squared-frequency polynomial. Exact rational arithmetic covers $[0,1]$ by at most six dyadic intervals, on each of which Bernstein coefficients certify either

$$S\le\frac65\qquad\text{or}\qquad f(3/5)\le0.$$

The first inequality gives $\sigma_2^2\le S/2\le3/5$. In the second case $3/5$ lies between the two nonnegative roots, so again $\sigma_2^2\le3/5$.

Equal weights on three cycles

Arbitrary weights on the extremal support orbit

Give the displayed cycles weights $a,b,c>0$, and put

Adding a fourth cycle at the sharp triple

Let $W_0$ be the equal-weight flow on the displayed extremal triple, let $C$ be any simple directed cycle on the six states, and put $W_\varepsilon=W_0+\varepsilon C$. Write $z_-(\varepsilon)=\sigma_2(\varepsilon)^2$ for the smaller squared intrinsic frequency, so $z_-(0)=3/5$.

The entire nonnegative ray, not just its tangent, obeys the sharp ceiling:

$$z_-(\varepsilon)\le\frac35\qquad(\varepsilon\ge0).$$

Indeed, write the exact pencil as $a_5t^5+a_3t^3+a_1t$ and set

$$A=5a_3-6a_5,\qquad B=9a_5-15a_3+25a_1.$$

The leading coefficient $a_5$ is positive. If $A\le0$, the sum of the two squared frequencies is at most $6/5$, so the smaller is at most $3/5$. If $B\le0$, their monic quadratic is nonpositive at $3/5$, which places $3/5$ between its roots.

Exact interpolation in $\varepsilon$ reduces all 409 choices of $C$ to 50 polynomial pairs $(A,B)$. Sturm isolation gives at most two positive zeros of $AB$, hence at most three open sign cells, and an exact rational sample in every cell verifies $A\le0$ or $B\le0$. At a cell boundary one barrier vanishes, so the conclusion also holds there.

Exact enumeration of all 409 cycle directions gives the one-sided tangent bound

$$\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0} \frac{z_-(\varepsilon)-3/5}{\varepsilon}\le-\frac4{25}.$$

For the proof, expand $S=a_3/a_5$ and $P=a_1/a_5$ through second order in $\varepsilon$. At the double root, the linear term of $\Delta=S^2-4P$ vanishes exactly, and

The ray theorem keeps the three base weights equal and varies only the coefficient of $C$; it does not cover arbitrary independent perturbations of all four weights or a general four-cycle cone.

Two-parameter imbalance slices

The ray theorem extends when two base weights move oppositely. For any permutation $(i,j,k)$ of the three displayed base cycles, any simple cycle $C$, and

$$W_{u,v}=(1+u)C_i+(1-u)C_j+C_k+vC, \qquad -1\le u\le1,\quad v\ge0,$$

the same sharp bound $\sigma_2(W_{u,v})^2\le3/5$ holds. Up to common scaling, these are precisely the three slices on which one base weight is the arithmetic mean of the other two.

This remains a slice theorem: it imposes one linear relation on the three base weights. It does not establish the bound throughout any unrestricted four-weight cone.

All four-cycle cones with exact rank collapse

There is an exact finite classification behind the first rank-collapse example. For a grounded $5\times5$ skew form, rank at most two is equivalent to the vanishing of its five principal $4\times4$ Pfaffians.

Each Pfaffian is quadratic in the cycle weights, and its pure terms vanish for an individual four-cycle. Thus a family has rank at most two for every weighting if and only if every pair of generators has zero polarized Pfaffians.

There is also a direct combinatorial form of this test. Put $x_{ij}=e_i-e_j$; then every directed four-cycle satisfies the exact exterior-product identity

$$2D_{(i,j,k,l)}=x_{ik}\wedge x_{lj}.$$

Ignoring orientation, a cycle is therefore a two-edge matching: its two opposite vertex pairs. Two such generators are compatible exactly when their matchings share an edge or their union is an alternating four-cycle, since incidence vectors indexed by a forest are linearly independent.

On the 90 labeled directed four-cycles, this compatibility relation has 1,125 edges and is 25-regular. Its four-cliques are exactly the four-component rank-collapse supports: there are 7,830 in total, of which 6,705 are connected on all six states. State relabeling and simultaneous reversal reduce them to 21 support orbits, 16 connected.

The 45 maximal compatible families have three explicit architectures, each with 15 labeled instances:

  • choose an opposite pair $\{p,q\}$ and take all 12 cycles $(p,r,q,s)$ with ordered distinct $r,s$ among the other four states;
  • choose a perfect matching of the six states and, for each two matched pairs, take both alternating orientations, giving six cycles;
  • choose four states and take all six directed four-cycles on that face, a family disconnected on six states before any reversible attachments.

The list is exhaustive without matrix calculation. If a clique of two-edge matchings has a common edge it lies in the first family; otherwise a pair sharing an edge forces the three two-edge subsets of one perfect matching, while a pair sharing no edge forces the three perfect matchings of one four-state set. Maximality then includes both orientations of every matching.

The rank bound is equally direct. In the opposite-pair family every weighted sum factors as

$$2D=x_{pq}\wedge\sum_{r\ne s}w_{rs}x_{sr}.$$

For a perfect-matching family, all forms lie in $\bigwedge^2U$ where $U$ is spanned by its three pair-difference vectors. For a four-state face, take $U$ to be the three-dimensional zero-sum space on that face; in either case every skew form on $U$ has rank at most two.

Every rank-collapse support is contained in one of these maximal families. Their full cones, not merely their four-generator subcones, therefore have skew rank at most two for arbitrary nonnegative weights. For continuity, the previously isolated representative is

$$\{(0123),(0124),(0325),(0524)\}$$

with positive weights $(a,b,c,d)$. In the grounded coordinates $0,\ldots,4$, its integer skew form factors exactly as

$$2D=uv^\top-vu^\top,\qquad u=(1,0,-1,0,0)^\top,\qquad v=(0,-a-b,0,a-c,b+d)^\top.$$

Since $a+b>0$, the vectors are independent and $D$ has rank two. The union is connected, so whitening by $H^{-1/2}$ preserves rank and leaves exactly one positive intrinsic frequency:

$$\sigma_2=0.$$

This representative has an orbit of 360 labeled supports and is one of the 16 connected classes. Arbitrary reversible attachments preserve $D$ and therefore preserve the rank conclusion whenever the final flow is connected; they can also connect a four-state face family without changing its skew rank. This is a fixed-flow Green–Kubo rank statement; it does not constrain rank obtained after integrating different maturity-dependent loadings.

Equal weights on four distinct four-cycles

A separate exact theorem covers arbitrary supports when all four components are distinct directed four-cycles and have equal weight. Every connected such mixture obeys the stronger bound

$$\sigma_2^2\le\frac13.$$

There are 90 labeled directed four-cycles and $\binom{90}{4}=2{,}555{,}190$ unordered supports. Exactly 2,390,985 are connected; the other 164,205 have zero leading grounded determinant, do not define an irreducible six-state flow and are excluded from this theorem.

For the equal-weight pencil $a_5t^5+a_3t^3+a_1t$, put

$$A_{1/3}=3a_3-2a_5,\qquad B_{1/3}=a_5-3a_3+9a_1.$$

If $A_{1/3}\le0$, the sum of the squared frequencies is at most $2/3$. If $B_{1/3}\le0$, their monic quadratic is nonpositive at $1/3$, so $1/3$ lies between its roots.

Exact integer determinants certify the first alternative on 2,144,115 connected supports and the second, with the first unavailable, on the remaining 246,870. Thus no floating spectral comparison enters the proof.

Equality occurs for 1,980 labeled supports in four orbits under state relabeling and simultaneous reversal. Below, $(0123)$ abbreviates the directed cycle $(0,1,2,3)$; orbit representatives are

$$\begin{aligned} &\{(0123),(0124),(0351),(0354)\},\\ &\{(0123),(0124),(0354),(2354)\},\\ &\{(0123),(0132),(2345),(2453)\},\\ &\{(0123),(0134),(0142),(2345)\}. \end{aligned}$$

Their orbit sizes are respectively $720,360,180,720$, and their coefficient triples $(a_5,a_3,a_1)$ are $(1104,800,144)$, $(1152,1152,256)$, $(864,576,96)$ and $(1008,696,120)$. In every case one squared frequency is $1/3$; the other is respectively $9/23$, $2/3$, $1/3$ and $5/14$.

This is an equal-weight theorem, not an arbitrary-weight four-support theorem. Indeed an unequal fourth weight can tend to zero on the sharp three-cycle face, where the lower squared frequency tends to $3/5$ rather than $1/3$.

An exact alternating-weight instability

The first equality orbit already loses the $1/3$ ceiling under an arbitrarily small imbalance that keeps all four weights positive. On the representative

$$\{(0123),(0124),(0351),(0354)\},$$

give the cycles alternating weights $(x,y,x,y)$ with $x,y>0$. Exact determinant factorization makes the two squared frequencies

$$z_1=\frac{y}{2x+y},\qquad z_2=\frac{(2x+y)^2}{8x^2+12xy+3y^2}.$$

Thus $y>x$ implies both $z_1,z_2>1/3$. For example, weights $(5,6,5,6)$ give the exact frequencies $3/8$ and $64/167$, so the lower one is $3/8>1/3$.

This slice also has a closed-form optimum. With $r=y/x$, $z_1$ is strictly increasing, $z_2$ is strictly decreasing, and

$$\operatorname{sign}(z_1-z_2) =\operatorname{sign}(r^2+2r-4).$$

They cross at $r=\sqrt5-1$, where both equal $(3-\sqrt5)/2$. Consequently

$$\max_{x,y>0}\min(z_1,z_2)=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.$$

The conclusion is confined to this one-dimensional projective slice of one equality orbit. It disproves persistence of the equal-weight $1/3$ ceiling, but it neither finds a violation of the general $3/5$ ceiling nor proves that ceiling for arbitrary four-four-cycle supports.

A full-cone crossing test on the second orbit

The next equality orbit admits an exact classification for every positive weighting, not just a slice. Order its representative as

$$C_0=(0123),\quad C_1=(0124),\quad C_2=(0354),\quad C_3=(2354),$$

and give these cycles weights $w_0,w_1,w_2,w_3>0$. Put

$$s=w_0+w_1,\quad t=w_2+w_3,\qquad p=w_0w_1,\quad q=w_2w_3.$$

For the pencil $a_5z^2-a_3z+a_1$, exact expansion gives

$$3a_3-2a_5=8stE>0,\qquad a_5-3a_3+9a_1=-32st(p-q)(s-t),$$

where $E=2ps+2qt+3s^2t+3st^2-5pt-5sq$ expands with only strictly positive monomial coefficients. The first identity says that the squared-frequency sum is strictly greater than $2/3$.

The second barrier equals $9a_5(1/3-z_1)(1/3-z_2)$. Since the two roots cannot both lie below $1/3$, the smaller squared frequency satisfies the complete trichotomy

$$\begin{aligned} z_->1/3&\iff (p-q)(s-t)<0,\\ z_-=1/3&\iff (p-q)(s-t)&=0,\\ z_-<1/3&\iff (p-q)(s-t)>0. \end{aligned}$$

Thus the $1/3$ equality set is the union of the equal-sum surface $s=t$ and the equal-product surface $p=q$. As an exact interior witness, weights $(9,1,4,4)$ have squared frequencies

$$\frac{771-3\sqrt{9489}}{1414},\qquad \frac{771+3\sqrt{9489}}{1414},$$

and the lower value is strictly above $1/3$. This classifies the $1/3$ crossing on one full equality-support cone; it still does not establish the separate $3/5$ ceiling for arbitrary four-cycle supports.

A pinned root on the third full cone

The third equality orbit is rigid in its lower mode for every positive weighting. Order its representative as

$$C_0=(0123),\quad C_1=(0132),\quad C_2=(2345),\quad C_3=(2453),$$

The first identity makes $1/3$ a squared-frequency root throughout the cone. If the other root is $z_+$, the second identity and $a_5>0$ give

A full-cone crossing theorem on the fourth orbit

The last equality orbit admits an exact $1/3$ classification throughout its positive weight cone. Order its representative as

$$C_0=(0123),\quad C_1=(0134),\quad C_2=(0142),\quad C_3=(2345),$$

give the cycles weights $(a,b,c,d)>0$, and put $S=a+b+c$, $e_2=ab+ac+bc$ and $e_3=abc$. For the root barrier $B=a_5-3a_3+9a_1$, exact factorization gives

$$B=-32d\{U+dS(a-c)(b-c)\},\qquad U=S^2e_2-e_2^2-6Se_3,$$ $$2U=\sum_{\rm cyc}(a-b)^2\{2c(a+b)+c^2\}.$$

Thus $U\ge0$, with equality exactly when $a=b=c$. If $c$ does not lie strictly between $a$ and $b$, and the first three weights are not all equal, then $B<0$ and the lower squared frequency is strictly below $1/3$.

If $c$ lies strictly between $a$ and $b$, define the positive threshold

$$d_*=\frac{U}{S(a-c)(c-b)}.$$

An exact positive-coefficient expansion proves that the sum barrier $3a_3-2a_5$ is positive at $d_*$; its remaining quadratic factor is strictly increasing for $d>0$. Consequently the complete crossing law is

$$\sigma_2^2 \begin{cases} <1/3,&d<d_*,\\ =1/3,&d=d_*,\\ >1/3,&d>d_*. \end{cases}$$

The exceptional line $a=b=c=x$ has $B=0$ for every $d$. Its two squared frequencies are

$$\frac13,\qquad \frac{(x+3d)(3x+2d)}{(5x+2d)(5x+3d)}.$$

The second root increases through $1/3$ at $d/x=(\sqrt{13}-1)/3$. Hence the lower squared frequency is below $1/3$ before that point and equals $1/3$ from the threshold onward; the off-line weights $(3,1,2,10)$ lie in the strict-above region.

This classifies the $1/3$ crossing on the final equality-support cone. It does not establish the separate $3/5$ ceiling for arbitrary four-cycle supports.

Bounded cycle lengths

The triangle result extends sharply to cycles of any bounded length. For one directed cycle of length $\ell$ and weight $w$, the nonconstant Fourier mode with angle $\theta_k=2\pi k/\ell$ has symmetric and skew eigenvalues $w(1-\cos\theta_k)$ and $-iw\sin\theta_k$.

Its intrinsic skew frequencies are therefore

$$\left|\frac{\sin\theta_k}{1-\cos\theta_k}\right| =\left|\cot\frac{\pi k}{\ell}\right|, \qquad k=1,\ldots,\ell-1.$$

The largest is $c_\ell=\cot(\pi/\ell)$. Equivalently, after embedding the cycle in any finite state space, its two forms obey

$$|x^\top D_\ell y| \le c_\ell \sqrt{x^\top H_\ell x} \sqrt{y^\top H_\ell y}.$$

Suppose a balanced flow admits a nonnegative decomposition into directed cycles with lengths $\ell_\alpha\le L$. Summing the component inequalities, then applying Cauchy–Schwarz, gives

$$|x^\top Dy| \le\cot(\pi/L) \sqrt{x^\top H_0x} \sqrt{y^\top H_0y}, \qquad H_0=\sum_\alpha H_{\ell_\alpha}.$$

Adding arbitrary reversible stationary flow replaces $H_0$ by $H=H_0+R\succeq H_0$ and leaves $D$ unchanged. If the final support is connected, substitution of $x=H^{-1/2}u$ and $y=H^{-1/2}v$ proves the sharp strong-sector bound

$$\left\lVert H^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2}\right\rVert_2 \le\cot\frac{\pi}{L}.$$

A single directed $L$-cycle attains the constant. This is a finite-dimensional strong sector condition in the terminology introduced by Varadhan (1995); for $L=3$ it recovers the exact $1/\sqrt3$ triangle bound above.

In particular, any number of directed cycles of lengths at most four, with arbitrary reversible attachments, has every intrinsic frequency at most one and cannot realize the target $\cot(\pi/5)>1$. At $L=5$ the bound equals the target frequency, so this norm argument alone does not exclude two repeated maximal modes.

After separating the reversible remainder, all remaining stationary flow must be represented by the bounded-length cycles. The hypothesis cannot be inferred from the final undirected support alone.

Multiplicity at the sharp sector

The equality case supplies a stronger state-count obstruction. Fix an integer $q\ge3$, put $c_q=\cot(\pi/q)$, and suppose the directed remainder is decomposed into cycles of lengths at most $q$.

On the full complexified state space, let

$$E=\ker(c_qH-iD).$$

Connectivity leaves constants in $E$, while every additional complex dimension is a positive intrinsic mode equal to $c_q$. Thus if that maximal frequency has multiplicity $r$, then $\dim_{\mathbb C}E=r+1$.

For each cycle component, $c_qH_\ell-iD_\ell$ is positive semidefinite, as is $c_qR$ for the reversible remainder. Their sum is $c_qH-iD$, so a vector lies in $E$ only if it lies in every component kernel.

When $\ell<q$, the component inequality is strict off constants, and every vector in $E$ is constant on that cycle. On a directed $q$-cycle, the kernel consists exactly of

$$f_j=a+b\zeta^j,\qquad j=0,\ldots,q-1,$$

where $\zeta$ is the oriented first $q$th root of unity. Two distinct vertex values determine $a$ and $b$ because the powers of $\zeta$ are distinct.

Identify vertices on which every vector in $E$ agrees. Reversible edges and shorter cycles collapse under this identification. A surviving $q$-cycle either also collapses or becomes a $q$-vertex hyperedge carrying the displayed affine Fourier form; the surviving hypergraph is connected.

Order its hyperedges so that every new one meets the preceding union. The first has at most two complex degrees of freedom. A new hyperedge meeting the old union in one vertex introduces at most one more degree of freedom and costs $q-1$ new vertices; if it meets in at least two vertices, its two affine parameters are already fixed and it introduces none.

If $k$ hyperedges meet the preceding union in exactly one vertex, then

$$\dim_{\mathbb C}E\le2+k, \qquad N\ge q+k(q-1).$$

Eliminating $k$ proves the sharp multiplicity bound

$$N\ge1+r(q-1).$$

A bouquet of $r$ directed $q$-cycles sharing one hub attains equality. For $q=5$ and $r=2$, this gives the exact minimum $N=9$ within the entire bounded-five-cycle decomposition class, including overlapping noncactus supports.

This does not settle the unrestricted six-, seven- or eight-state cases, because their stationary-flow decompositions may require cycles longer than five. It is a fixed-parameter spectral multiplicity statement, not a rank inference after integrating maturity-dependent loadings.

A sharp two-five-cycle gap below nine states

The qualitative state obstruction has a quantitative two-cycle refinement. Let the genuinely directed remainder be a positive mixture of two directed five-cycles that share at least two vertices, so their union has at most eight states. Then, even after adding arbitrary nonnegative reversible stationary flow,

$$\sigma_2\le1<\cot\frac\pi5.$$

To prove the bound, scale the cycle weights to $1$ and $r>0$ and put $B(r)=iD-H$ on a grounded space. The positive inertia of $B(r)$ counts the signed generalized eigenvalues of $(iD,H)$ above one. Thus $\sigma_2>1$ would require at least two positive eigenvalues of $B(r)$.

Fix the first cycle as $(0,1,2,3,4)$. There are 624 relative placements of the second cycle with overlap two through five, partitioned as $240,240,120,24$. Exact Faddeev–LeVerrier expansion reduces their characteristic polynomials $\det(xI-2B(r))$ to 70 types.

For each type, the square-free product of its nonzero coefficient polynomials has at most six positive roots. Exact Sturm isolation therefore leaves at most seven open weight-ratio cells.

On one rational point in every cell, the coefficient sequence has at most one sign variation. Descartes' rule gives at most one positive eigenvalue throughout that cell; continuity covers the isolated boundaries. Adding reversible flow replaces $B$ by $B-R\preceq B$, so its positive inertia cannot increase.

The ceiling is attained. At equal weights the two cycles

$$ (0,1,2,3,4),\qquad(0,5,7,6,4) $$

share the directed edge $4\to0$ and have normalized pencil

$$\frac{\det(tH-iD)}{\det H} =t(t^2-1)\left(t^4-2t^2+\frac15\right).$$

The positive frequencies are therefore $\cot(\pi/5)$, $1$, and $\cot(2\pi/5)$, proving sharpness. This is again a fixed-parameter statement: it controls the intrinsic rank at one generator and does not turn rank accumulated from different maturity loadings into a regime count.

The exact overlap size gives sharper ceilings, still with arbitrary reversible attachments. If the two cycles share exactly three states, then $\sigma_2\le1/\sqrt2$; if they share exactly four, then $\sigma_2\le1/\sqrt3$. Both constants are attained at equal weights.

Substituting $y=z+1/2$ or $y=z+1/3$ turns a violation into two positive $z$-roots. Exact expansion gives 16 types for the 240 overlap-three placements and 13 types for the 120 overlap-four placements; their coefficient breakpoints number at most two and three respectively, and the same Sturm–Descartes argument leaves at most one positive root.

Sharp representatives have normalized pencils

$$t^2(t^2-1)(t^2-1/2),\qquad t(t^2-1/2)(t^2-1/3),$$

for overlaps three and four respectively. The all-five-state stratum has a different algebraic sharp constant.

When both cycles use the same five states, the 24 relative placements reduce to eight exact squared-frequency types. Six satisfy the stronger rational bound $\sigma_2^2\le1/7$; the remaining two are interchanged by replacing the weight ratio $r$ with $1/r$.

For the representative pair $(0,1,2,3,4)$ and $(0,2,4,1,3)$, put

$$A=r^4+r^2+2r+1,\qquad F=r^4-r^3-2r^2-2r-1,\qquad d=r^2+3r+1.$$

The squared frequencies are exactly

$$y_\pm(r)=\frac{A(r)\pm(2/\sqrt5)|F(r)|}{d(r)^2}.$$

Write $D=d^2$, $U=A'D-AD'$ and $W=F'D-FD'$. The certificate factors

$$U=2d(3r^4+r^3-3r^2-4r-2),\qquad W=d(7r^4+5r^3+3r^2+6r+4)>0,$$

and verifies the exact identity

$$5U^2-4W^2=-16(r^2+r-1)(r^2+3r+1)^5,$$

together with $W>0$, shows that $y_-$ decreases on $(0,(\sqrt5-1)/2)$, increases until the unique positive root $\rho>2$ of $F$, and decreases thereafter.

$$\sigma_2\le\sqrt\gamma=0.473753\ldots,\qquad \gamma=\frac{\rho^4+\rho^2+2\rho+1}{(\rho^2+3\rho+1)^2}.$$

Equivalently, $\gamma$ is the smaller positive root of $125\gamma^4-100\gamma^3+6\gamma^2-20\gamma+5=0$. The inverse type attains the same ceiling at $r=1/\rho$, and arbitrary reversible attachments cannot increase it.

Reversible attachments cannot amplify skew

This extension is not limited by the number of reversible two-cycles. Let $H_0,D$ be the grounded symmetric and skew forms of any balanced directed component, and add an arbitrary reversible stationary flow with grounded form $R\succeq0$. Then $D$ is unchanged and $H=H_0+R$.

Assume $H$ is positive definite and $\ker H_0\subseteq\ker D$, as holds for a directed cycle on any subset of the states.

Using the Moore–Penrose inverse on the range of $H_0$, put

$$K_0=H_0^{\dagger/2}DH_0^{\dagger/2},\qquad C=H^{-1/2}H_0^{1/2}.$$

The kernel condition gives $D=H_0^{1/2}K_0H_0^{1/2}$, while $H_0\preceq H$ gives $CC^\top\preceq I$. Therefore

$$H^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2}=CK_0C^\top,\qquad s_j(CK_0C^\top)\le\lVert C\rVert^2s_j(K_0)\le s_j(K_0).$$

Thus every intrinsic skew frequency contracts under reversible attachments. A directed cycle of length $\ell$ has positive Fourier frequencies

$$\cot\frac{\pi}{\ell},\ \cot\frac{2\pi}{\ell},\ldots, \cot\frac{\lfloor(\ell-1)/2\rfloor\pi}{\ell}.$$

No connectivity assumption on the directed component is needed. If its active support is connected and has six states, the exact classification above gives $s_2(K_0)\le\sqrt{3/5}$.

Why feature choice cannot evade the bound

Let $U$ collect any four independent Poisson potentials and normalize their symmetric energy. The resulting feature skew matrix is the compression

$$X^\top(iH^{-1/2}DH^{-1/2})X,\qquad X^\top X=I_4.$$

Hermitian compression interlacing, in the form proved by Fan and Pall (1957), bounds its second positive eigenvalue by $\sigma_2$. This is a necessary condition for a fixed-parameter Green–Kubo block; it is not a rank claim after integrating different maturity loadings.

The target has symmetric part $I_4$ and two positive skew frequencies equal to $\cot(\pi/5)$. The strict comparison above therefore excludes every generator in any stated sparse-cycle class, for every positive stationary law and every four-feature embedding.
