Exponential-mixture clocks identify finite generators

Every known finite mixture is globally identifying, even with discarded timestamps and unobserved clock-component labels.

Let $Y$ be a fully labeled finite-state chain with irreducible row generator $Q$. Independently of the chain, draw iid components $J_k$ with known probabilities $p_j>0$, then independently draw $\tau_k\mid J_k=j\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda_j)$, with known rates $\lambda_j>0$. Keep every sampled state label, including repeats, but discard both the clock-component labels and all realized timing information, including total elapsed time.

At a fixed sample count the labels are a Markov chain with population kernel

$$R=\sum_{j=1}^d p_j\lambda_j(\lambda_j I-Q)^{-1}=F(-Q),\qquad F(z)=\sum_{j=1}^d\frac{p_j\lambda_j}{\lambda_j+z},\qquad\sum_j p_j=1.$$

The theorem is exact: this $R$ identifies every entry of $Q$, without a speed cap, reversibility or diagonalizability. A mixture of exponential intervals is not a sum of exponential stages, and this statement does not cover arbitrary renewal clocks.

Why the scalar transform has no aliases

Put $\lambda_* =\min_j\lambda_j$ and $D=\{z:\operatorname{Re}z>-\lambda_*/2\}$. For distinct $z,w\in D$,

$$F(z)-F(w)=-(z-w)\sum_j\frac{p_j\lambda_j}{(\lambda_j+z)(\lambda_j+w)}.$$

If $z,w$ are both in the upper half-plane, every summand on the right after removing $-(z-w)$ has strictly negative imaginary part, since the two denominator arguments lie in $(0,\pi/2)$. The lower half-plane is the conjugate case; for two real arguments the sum is strictly positive.

Finally $\operatorname{Im}F(z)$ has the opposite sign to $\operatorname{Im}z$, so arguments in different half-planes, or one real and one nonreal, cannot collide. Thus $F$ is one-to-one on $D$, and its holomorphic inverse $G$ exists on $F(D)$. The same divided-difference argument at $z=w$ gives $F'(z)\ne0$, so Jordan information is not lost.

The spectrum of $A=-Q$ lies in $\operatorname{Re}z\ge0\subset D$. Holomorphic functional calculus therefore gives

$$\boxed{Q=-G(R).}$$

This is a uniqueness theorem for the stated population model, not a method for accepting arbitrary noisy matrices as valid sampled kernels. Scalar injectivity alone would not suffice without analyticity and the nonvanishing derivative needed for Jordan blocks.

Two rates: an explicit principal-square-root inverse

Take $0 $$D_R=cR^{-1}-(a+b)I,\qquad \Delta_R=D_R^2+4ab(R^{-1}-I).$$ $$\boxed{Q=-\tfrac12\{D_R+\Delta_R^{1/2}\},}$$

Here the square root is the principal matrix square root, not an arbitrary stochastic root. The matrix $R$ is invertible because $\operatorname{Re}F(z)>0$ for $\operatorname{Re}z\ge0$.

For the branch proof, $R=(abI+cA)\{A^2+(a+b)A+abI\}^{-1}$ with $A=-Q$. The quadratic identity yields $\Delta_R=(2A-D_R)^2$, and direct division gives

$$2A-D_R=A+\frac{ab}{c}I+ \frac{ab(c-a)(b-c)}{c}(abI+cA)^{-1}.$$

For every eigenvalue $z$ of $A$, the corresponding scalar expression has strictly positive real part: $\operatorname{Re}z\ge0$, $ab/c>0$, and the last coefficient is nonnegative. Consequently $2A-D_R$ is the principal square root of its square, proving the formula even for defective generators. This branch argument is essential; merely solving the quadratic does not select the correct generator.

A cycle-direction witness and the limits of the result

For opposite four-state unit-rate cycles $Q_+=C-I$ and $Q_-=C^\top-I$, choose rates $(a,b)=(1,4)$ and $p=1/3$. Exact rational arithmetic gives

$$\|Q_+-Q_-\|_\infty=2,\qquad \|R(Q_+)-R(Q_-)\|_\infty=\frac{22}{65}=0.3384615385.$$

Thus the component labels need not be retained to detect cycle direction under this known mixture clock. The mixture is redrawn each interval; a single persistent unobserved clock rate is a different model.

Independent sampling preserves the stationary law, $\pi R=\pi$. Unknown clock parameters, state-dependent or adaptive sampling, hidden/noisy regime labels, and calendar-time stopping are outside scope.

Global identification is not uniform stability: for $Q_m=m(\Pi-I)$, $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$, the sampled kernel is $\Pi+F(m)(I-\Pi)$. Since $F(m)\to0$, kernels at $m$ and $2m$ approach each other while their generator distance diverges. No finite-sample, anytime-confidence or uniform sample-complexity theorem follows from the inverse alone.

Certificate and classical background

The certificate checks the rational branch identities symbolically, 42 exact divided-difference checks, 192 matrix inversions on random, cyclic and defective generators, and 12 independent waiting-density quadratures. Its four-cycle kernel gap is an exact rational identity, not a floating-point equality claim.

Random-arrival identification and estimation are classical; see Duffie and Glynn (2004). Principal and nonprimary matrix roots are discussed by Higham and Lin (2011). The divided-difference and branch proofs specialize these standard transform and matrix-function ideas, without a priority claim.
