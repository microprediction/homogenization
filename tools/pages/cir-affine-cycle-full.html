Affine loading need not give monotone variance rates

One complex-conjugate mode pair produces two turning points, even while stationary CIR variance strictly decreases.

Exact affine-loading formulas without reversibility

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, $m>0$, where $Q$ is any finite irreducible generator with stationary law $\pi$. Consider the joint stationary process

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta_{Y_t}-v_t)\,dt+\sqrt{a_{Y_t}v_t}\,dB_t, \qquad V_T=\int_0^T v_t\,dt.$$

Here $\kappa>0$ is common, $B$ is independent of $Y$, and $a_i=b_0+b\theta_i>0$, $\theta_i>0$, $2\kappa\theta_i>a_i$ in every regime. Use the joint invariant law at each speed, rather than one common deterministic starting state.

Write $f=\theta-\bar\theta\mathbf1$, $\bar\theta=\pi\theta$, $\bar a=\pi a$, and let $H=(-Q)^{-1}$ on centered functions. Set $g(m)=\langle f,(\kappa I-mQ)^{-1}f\rangle_\pi$ and $K=\langle f,Hf\rangle_\pi$.

$$\operatorname{Var}(v)=\frac{\bar a\bar\theta}{2\kappa} +(\kappa+b/2)g(m),$$ $$\Gamma(m):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Var}(V_T)}T =\Gamma_\infty+\frac{2K}{m}+\frac b\kappa g(m), \qquad\Gamma_\infty=\frac{\bar a\bar\theta}{\kappa^2}.$$

To prove these identities, stationarity of $vu(Y)$ for centered $u$ gives $\operatorname{Cov}(v,u_Y)=\kappa\langle f,(\kappa I-mQ)^{-1}u\rangle_\pi$. Applying the generator to $v^2$ gives $\operatorname{Var}(v)=\operatorname{Cov}(v,\theta_Y)+\mathbb E[a_Yv]/(2\kappa)$, proving the first formula.

The Poisson solution $\phi(v,i)=v/\kappa+(Hf)_i/m$ satisfies $-\mathcal L\phi=v-\bar\theta$. The covariance identity $\Gamma=2\operatorname{Cov}(v,\phi)$ and $\kappa(\kappa I-mQ)^{-1}H=H-m(\kappa I-mQ)^{-1}$ on centered functions prove the second formula.

Finite second moments follow from the common positive mean reversion and bounded regime coefficients. Affine conditional means decay exponentially (with possible polynomial factors), so the covariance integral exists. No diagonalizability or detailed balance is used.

A strict-Feller six-cycle

Index regimes modulo six and take $Q_{j,j+1}=1$, $Q_{jj}=-1$, all other rates zero; then $\pi$ is uniform and the chain is irreducible but nonreversible. Choose $\kappa=1$ and

$$h=(2,1,-1,-2,-1,1),\qquad \theta=(40\mathbf1+h)/1000, \qquad a=\frac1{25}\mathbf1-\frac95(\theta-\tfrac1{25}\mathbf1).$$

The affine slope is $b=-9/5>-2\kappa$. Both regime coefficients are positive, and the minimum Feller margin is $81/2500=0.0324$.

The integer identities $(Q^2+Q+I)h=0$, $h^Th=12$, $h^TQh=-6$ give $S=\|f\|_\pi^2=1/500000$ and $K=S/2$. Indeed, $(I-mQ)^{-1}f=((1+m)I+mQ)f/(1+m+m^2)$, whence

$$g(m)=S\frac{1+m/2}{1+m+m^2},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(v)=\frac1{1250}+\frac{g(m)}{10},\qquad \Gamma(m)=\frac1{625}+S\frac{m^2-8m+10}{10m(1+m+m^2)}.$$

Since $g'(m)=-S(m^2+4m+1)/(2(1+m+m^2)^2)<0$, stationary variance strictly decreases at every speed. But the rate derivative has a different sign law:

$$\Gamma'(m)=\frac{S P(m)}{10m^2(1+m+m^2)^2},\qquad P(m)=-m^4+16m^3-21m^2-20m-10.$$

The descending coefficients have two sign changes, hence at most two positive roots counted with multiplicity. Exact signs $P(2)=-22$, $P(3)=92$, $P(15)=-1660$ locate two distinct roots; therefore both are simple and there are no others.

Thus $\Gamma$ first decreases, then increases, then decreases. Its local minimum is at $m\approx2.3045224033$, rate $0.0015999685170$; its local maximum is at $m\approx14.4473055464$, rate $0.0016000063696$.

Moreover, $\Gamma(3)=0.0015999743590<\Gamma_\infty=0.0016$. More exactly, the rate lies below its averaged limit precisely when $4-\sqrt6<m<4+\sqrt6$.

The effect persists on an open loading interval

Keep $Q,\kappa,\theta$ fixed, but set $a=\tfrac1{25}\mathbf1-2c f$. Positivity and the strict Feller test hold throughout $169/198<c<1$, and stationary variance strictly decreases there.

Up to its positive denominator, $\Gamma'$ now has numerator $(c-1)m^4+(4c-2)m^3+(c-3)m^2-2m-1$. It has two coefficient sign changes, is negative at zero and at infinity, and at $m=3$ equals $198c-169>0$; hence it has exactly two simple positive roots throughout this interval.

Scope and verification

For the reversible comparison, write $S_\lambda=\|E_\lambda f\|_\pi^2$ for the orthogonal spectral weights. Then $g=\sum_\lambda S_\lambda/(\kappa+m\lambda)$ and $K=\sum_\lambda S_\lambda/\lambda$. In the derivative of $\Gamma$, each positive mixed contribution is dominated by the $2K/m$ contribution when $-2\kappa\le b<0$, since $(\kappa+m\lambda)^2>m^2\lambda^2$; for $b\ge0$ both derivatives are nonpositive.

For reversible chains, affine slopes $b\ge-2\kappa$ make the long-run variance rate decrease; the example shows that reversibility cannot be dropped from that implication. It does not contradict the separate constant-vol-of-vol case $b=0$, where $\Gamma=\Gamma_\infty+2K/m$.

These are exact stationary one-time variance and long-run variance-rate statements, not a classification of finite-maturity $\operatorname{Var}(V_T)$, log-return cumulants, or options. No minimal state count or complete affine-loading classification is claimed.

Finite polynomial moment calculations are classical; see Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012), Theorem 2.7. No priority claim is made for the moment, Poisson, or invariant-subspace methods.
