One occupation third cumulant resolves a three-state Gram

With known stationary law and complete fixed-state Green–Kubo data, one additional scalar identifies any irreducible three-state generator up to time reversal. Occupation statistics alone cannot remove that last ambiguity.

Data and exact reconstruction

Fix a three-state irreducible row generator $Q$, its known positive stationary row law $\pi$, state labels and time unit. Put $D_\pi=\operatorname{diag}\pi$, $\Pi=\mathbf1\pi$, $P=I-\Pi$, and

$$H=(\Pi-Q)^{-1}-\Pi=-Q^\#,\qquad H^*=D_\pi^{-1}H^TD_\pi,\qquad G=(H+H^*)/2,\qquad K=(H-H^*)/2.$$

The observed full symmetric Green–Kubo form is $\mathcal G(f,h)=\langle f,Gh\rangle_\pi$ on all centered state functions. A known complete fixed-feature basis determines the state operator $G$, with $G\mathbf1=0$ and $\pi G=0$; twice this form is the long-run occupation covariance rate.

Choose any one state $i$ and additionally observe the exact long-run third cumulant rate

$$\tau_i=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\kappa_3\left(\int_0^T (\mathbf1_{\{Y_t=i\}}-\pi_i)\,dt\right).$$

Every centered weighted-skew operator in three states has the form $K=aD_\pi^{-1}C$, where

$$C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad K^2=-\eta P,\qquad\eta=\frac{a^2}{\pi_1\pi_2\pi_3}\ge0.$$
$$\tau_i=6\pi_i\left[G_{ii}^2-\pi_i(G^2)_{ii}+\pi_i(1-\pi_i)\eta\right],$$ $$\eta=\frac{\tau_i/(6\pi_i)-G_{ii}^2+\pi_i(G^2)_{ii}}{\pi_i(1-\pi_i)},\qquad Q_\pm=\Pi-\left(\Pi+G\pm\sqrt{\pi_1\pi_2\pi_3\eta}\,D_\pi^{-1}C\right)^{-1}.$$

For exact data from an irreducible generator, these are precisely $Q$ and $Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^TD_\pi$. They coincide when $\eta=0$; otherwise there are exactly two candidates. No reversibility or diagonalizability assumption is needed.

Proof from the third Poisson coefficient

For any centered real $f$, the eigenvalue $\lambda(s)$ of $Q+s\operatorname{diag}f$ through zero is analytic near zero, and the long-run third cumulant rate is $\lambda'''(0)$. Normalize the right eigenvector by $\pi r(s)=1$; successive coefficients give

$$\lambda(s)=s^2\langle f,Hf\rangle_\pi +s^3\langle f,H(fHf)\rangle_\pi+O(s^4).$$

Here $H$ annihilates constants, so the eigenvector coefficients are $Hf$ and $H(fHf)$. The spectral remainder is local for each fixed $Q,f$; the inversion uses the exact limiting cumulant, not a finite-$T$ approximation.

For $f=e_i-\pi_i\mathbf1$, $Hf=He_i$ and $fHf=H_{ii}e_i-\pi_iHe_i$. Since $\pi H=0$, this proves the general finite-chain identity

$$\tau_i=6\pi_i\left[H_{ii}^2-\pi_i(H^2)_{ii}\right].$$

Weighted skewness makes the diagonal of $K$ and of $GK+KG$ zero. Hence $H_{ii}=G_{ii}$ and $(H^2)_{ii}=(G^2)_{ii}+(K^2)_{ii}$.

In three states $D_\pi K$ is a skew matrix with zero row sums, so it is a scalar multiple of $C$. Direct multiplication gives $K^2=-\eta P$, yielding the boxed scalar formula. Only the sign of $a$ remains, and $H\leftrightarrow H^*$ gives exactly stationary time reversal.

A same-Gram family separated by the third rate

For uniform $\pi$, let $P=I-\mathbf1\pi$ and $Q_s=(sC-3P)/(3+s^2)$, $|s|\le1$. These are irreducible generators with $H_s=P+sC/3$, $G=P$ and $\eta=s^2/3$, but

$$\tau_i=\frac49\left(1+\frac{s^2}{3}\right).$$

Thus $s=1/4$ and $s=1/2$ share the complete Gram, yet have respective third rates $49/108=0.4537037037$ and $13/27=0.4814814815$. One occupation third rate recovers $|s|$, not its orientation.

Why no occupation cumulant can distinguish reversal

Start in $\pi$ and let $D$ be any real diagonal potential. The finite-state Feynman–Kac transform of the full occupation vector is $M_Q(T,D)=\pi e^{T(Q+D)}\mathbf1$.

$$Q^*+D=D_\pi^{-1}(Q+D)^TD_\pi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M_{Q^*}(T,D)=M_Q(T,D).$$

Thus the entire joint occupation vector has the same law under $Q$ and $Q^*$ at every finite horizon. It is bounded, so equality of these transforms identifies its law and every joint occupation cumulant. Ordered observations or time-weighted scores are different data and are not covered by this blindness statement.

One prescribed occupation is not enough in four states

For uniform $\pi=(1,1,1,1)/4$, put $P=I-\Pi$, $C_4=\operatorname{diag}(0,C)$ and

$$Q_a=\Pi-(I+aC_4)^{-1},\qquad H_a=P+aC_4,\qquad a=0,1/10.$$

Both have full symmetric Gram operator $G=P$ and $\tau_1=9/16$. The nonzero-$a$ generator has all off-diagonal rates at least $59/412>0$ and is nonreversible, whereas $Q_0$ is reversible, so they are neither reversals nor relabelings.

In fact, every statistic of the first state's occupation agrees: its binary indicator has exit rate $3/4$ and entry rate $1/4$ for both chains, independently of the other regime. Thus even its entire occupation law does not repair the ambiguity; the three-state theorem does not extend to four with this data.

Verification, literature and scope

The certificate checks 39 rational three-state generators, including nonuniform stationary laws, a reversible case, a one-way cycle and a defective generator. All 117 indicator reconstructions and cubic characteristic-polynomial identities are exact; it also checks the four-state obstruction rationally.

Seventy-two independent spectral-coefficient calculations and 64 finite-horizon transform comparisons provide floating-point checks, not interval enclosures. The analytic cumulant/Poisson framework is classical; see Kontoyiannis and Meyn (2003).

The result requires known state representation, stationary law, time unit and complete fixed-feature Gram. It is not a theorem about partial observations, noisy empirical estimates, regime counting, or a matrix formed by integrating different maturity loadings. No statistical estimation-rate or finite-maturity cumulant-error bound is inferred from this exact inversion.
