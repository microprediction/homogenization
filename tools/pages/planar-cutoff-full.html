The sharp planar hard-cutoff sector

An exact two-dimensional threshold, a spherical-Bessel root, and a stationary Gaussian counterexample.

For a real two-dimensional matrix with positive definite symmetric part, the optimal strong-sector bound guaranteeing a positive semidefinite integrated semigroup at every horizon is $\kappa_*=3.1314582802\ldots$. Above it a stationary two-coordinate Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process can have a negative hard correlation cutoff, although its Bartlett covariance remains positive.

The sharp uniform statement

$$S=\operatorname{sym}L\succ0,\qquad \kappa(L)=\|S^{-1/2}\operatorname{skew}(L)S^{-1/2}\|_2,\qquad R_T(L)=\int_0^T e^{-tL}dt.$$

Let $\theta_*$ be the unique root of $\tan\theta=\theta$ in $(\pi,3\pi/2)$, and put

$$C=\sqrt{1+\theta_*^2},\qquad w=W_0(C/e),\qquad \boxed{\kappa_*={C\over\sqrt{w(w+2)}}}.$$

For every such real $2\times2$ matrix, $\kappa(L)\le\kappa_*$ implies $\operatorname{sym}R_T(L)\succeq0$ for every $T>0$. If $\kappa(L)<\kappa_*$ the symmetric part is positive definite. For every $k>\kappa_*$ there exists an $L$ with $\kappa(L)=k$ and a horizon with a negative eigenvalue of $\operatorname{sym}R_T(L)$.

This is a sharp guarantee over the whole class, not an iff test based only on the sector of an individual matrix. In particular every real-spectrum matrix in this class has positive definite hard cutoffs, even when its sector exceeds $\kappa_*$.

$$4.493409457909<\theta_*<4.493409457910,\qquad .777917140533<w<.777917140535,$$ $$3.1314582802<\kappa_*<3.1314582803.$$

Reduction to the first local minimum

An orthogonal change of basis, reflection and positive time rescaling reduce the matrix to

$$L=I+\begin{pmatrix}-d&c\\-c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\le d<1,\quad c\ge0,\quad \kappa={c\over\sqrt{1-d^2}}.$$

The square of the displayed traceless matrix is $(d^2-c^2)I$. Consequently the symmetric integrated semigroup is diagonal, with entries

$$H_\pm(T)=\int_0^T e^{-t}\left(\cos(bt)\pm{d\over b}\sin(bt)\right)dt, \qquad b=\sqrt{c^2-d^2},$$

in the complex-spectrum case $c>d$. In the real-spectrum case $h=\sqrt{d^2-c^2}<1$, the entries are instead $H_\pm(T)=\int_0^T e^{-t}[\cosh(ht)\pm d\sinh(ht)/h]dt$.

The real-spectrum plus integrand is positive; the minus integrand can change sign only once, from positive to negative. Its integral either increases throughout or decreases after its maximum to the positive limit $(1-d)/(1-h^2)$. At $h=0$ the integrands are $e^{-t}(1\pm dt)$, so the same argument covers Jordan blocks.

For complex spectrum set $\phi=\arctan(d/b)\in[0,\pi/2)$. The first local minima occur at $T_\pm=(3\pi/2\pm\phi)/b$, and direct integration gives

$$H_-(T_-)={1-d-ce^{-T_-}\over1+b^2},\qquad H_+(T_+)={1+d-ce^{-T_+}\over1+b^2}.$$

Later minima are larger, since their negative exponential terms are smaller. The first minus numerator is no larger than the first plus numerator, and both functions start at zero with positive derivative. Thus all cutoffs are positive semidefinite exactly when $ce^{-T_-}\le1-d$.

The optimizing angle is independent of the sector

At fixed $\kappa>0$ write $c=\kappa/\sqrt{1+\kappa^2\sin^2\phi}$, $d=c\sin\phi$, $b=c\cos\phi$. The logarithm of the preceding sign ratio is

$$F_\kappa(\phi)=\log\kappa+\operatorname{arsinh}(\kappa\sin\phi) -{(3\pi/2-\phi)\sqrt{1+\kappa^2\sin^2\phi}\over\kappa\cos\phi}.$$ $$F_\kappa'(\phi)= {(1+\kappa^2)[\cos\phi-(3\pi/2-\phi)\sin\phi] \over\kappa\cos^2\phi\sqrt{1+\kappa^2\sin^2\phi}}.$$

The numerator's bracket decreases strictly from positive to negative: its derivative is $-(3\pi/2-\phi)\cos\phi<0$. Its unique zero satisfies $\cot\phi_*=3\pi/2-\phi_*=\theta_*$, giving $\tan\theta_*=\theta_*$. This maximizes $F_\kappa$ for every sector, not merely near the critical one.

Since $\sin\phi_*=1/C$, put $u=\sqrt{1+C^2/\kappa^2}>1$. At the optimum the sign test simplifies to

$$F_\kappa(\phi_*)=\log{C\over u-1}-u\le0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (u-1)e^u\ge C \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa\le{C\over\sqrt{w(w+2)}}.$$

The middle expression is strictly increasing in $u$, which decreases as $\kappa$ increases. This proves sufficiency and sharpness, including the nonnegative boundary case.

Exact equality and an above-threshold witness

$$d_*={1\over1+w},\qquad c_*={C\over1+w},\qquad L_*=\begin{pmatrix}1-d_*&c_*\\-c_*&1+d_*\end{pmatrix},\qquad T_*=1+w.$$

Here $b_*T_*=\theta_*$ and $C=we^{1+w}$, so $1-d_*-c_*e^{-T_*}=0$ exactly. The symmetric cutoff is positive semidefinite and singular; numerically $d_*=.5624559082$, $c_*=2.5891751330$, $T_*=1.7779171405$.

A simpler rational example lies strictly above the threshold:

$$L=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&3\\-3&3/2\end{pmatrix},\quad S=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,3/2),\quad \kappa(L)^2=12,\quad T=3/2.$$

For the second coordinate, $b=\sqrt{35}/2$ and $C_f(t)=e^{-t}[\cos(bt)-\sin(bt)/\sqrt{35}]$. Exact rational matrix actions certify

$$R_{3/2,f}\in[-.014475211893,-.014475211892],\qquad K_f=\int_0^\infty C_f(t)dt=2/39.$$

A genuine stationary Gaussian driver, not a finite chain

For any $L$ with $S\succ0$, the diffusion $dZ_t=-LZ_tdt+(2S)^{1/2}dW_t$, $Z_0\sim N(0,I)$ independent of $W$, is stationary. Indeed the covariance equation is $\dot\Sigma=-L\Sigma-\Sigma L^\top+2S$, solved by $\Sigma=I$, and stability follows from $S\succ0$.

For a linear coordinate $f(Z)=v^\top Z$, its stationary scalar correlation is $C_f(t)=v^\top e^{-tL}v$. The rational witness therefore has noise covariance $\operatorname{diag}(1,3)$ and is an actual population counterexample, not an empirical estimate or just a matrix with no stochastic realization.

$$B_{T,f}=\int_0^T(1-t/T)C_f(t)dt,\qquad \operatorname{Var}\left(\int_0^T f(Z_t)dt\right)=2T B_{T,f}.$$ $$B_{3/2,f}\in[.123303663116,.123303663117],\qquad \operatorname{Var}\left(\int_0^{3/2} Z_{t,2}dt\right) \in[.369910989348,.369910989349].$$

Thus the negative rectangular cutoff coexists with positive integrated variance. In one Gaussian coordinate a stable OU correlation is a positive exponential, so two coordinates are minimal within this linear Gaussian class.

Certificates, literature and scope

Rational Taylor bounds for sine, cosine and exponential enclose $\theta_*$ and $w$; squared inequalities then enclose $\kappa_*$ without numerical square-root rounding. A degree-160 rational action encloses the witness's single and double integrals, with cutoff error below $3.4\times10^{-148}$ and all three reported output errors below $6.8\times10^{-148}$.

There are also 280 rotated/scaled complex-spectrum semigroup comparisons, 36 real-spectrum/Jordan checks, an equality-case check and independent time quadrature. These floating consistency checks use tolerance $3\times10^{-9}$; the analytic minimization and exact rational inequalities prove the theorem and signs.

The root $\theta_*$ is the first positive zero of the spherical Bessel function $j_1(\theta)=\sin\theta/\theta^2-\cos\theta/\theta$. The special-function identities are classical: NIST DLMF, 10.49.3 and 4.13.1. For the Markovian Poisson/Green–Kubo background see Pavliotis (2010).

This sharpens the sufficient planar matrix lemma in the three-state note, not its finite-chain state count. A two-coordinate diffusion has infinitely many possible states; the witness is not a four-to-eleven-state CTMC. The unrestricted finite-chain bracket in issue 233 remains $4\le n_*\le12$.

The theorem requires a closed two-dimensional semigroup. It cannot be applied to two arbitrary features of a larger chain without proving closure, nor used to count regimes from maturity-integrated rank or to infer conditional conformal coverage.
