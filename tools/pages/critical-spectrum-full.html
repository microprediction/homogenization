The critical spectral endpoint

Cumulative spectral mass does not control resonant phases at index two.

Let $X$ be centered and stationary, with finite one-sided spectral measure $\mu$ and $\mu(\{0\})=0$. Write $F(x)=\mu((0,x])$ and

$$V(R)=\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\int_0^R X_tdt\right) =2\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{1-\cos(R\omega)}{\omega^2}\mu(d\omega).$$

The implication $F(x)\sim bx^2L(1/x)\Rightarrow V(R)\sim4bH(R)$, where $H(R)=\int_1^R L(s)ds/s\to\infty$, is false without further assumptions. This note proves the obstruction and a sufficient local-density repair.

An exact atomic counterexample

For integers $k\ge0$, define

$$d_k=\max\{1,\lceil\tfrac14\log_2(k+1)\rceil\},\quad N_k=k+d_k,\quad M_k=2^{d_k},\quad h_k=2^{-N_k}.$$

In the annulus $(2^{-k-1},2^{-k}]$, place atoms

$$\omega_{k,l}=lh_k,\qquad w_{k,l}=(2l-1)h_k^2, \qquad l=M_k/2+1,\ldots,M_k.$$

These are right-endpoint increments of the square function. The block mass is $(3/4)4^{-k}$, and all blocks together have mass one.

For $2^{-k-1}<x\le2^{-k}$, the complete lower blocks have mass $4^{-(k+1)}$, hence

$$F(x)=h_k^2\lfloor x/h_k\rfloor^2,\qquad (1-2/M_k)^2\le\frac{F(x)}{x^2}\le1.$$

Since $M_k\to\infty$, this proves $F(x)\sim x^2$. The measure has no atom at zero and gives a valid centered stationary real Gaussian process by Bochner's representation; Gaussianity does not remove spectral resonance. No ergodicity is claimed for this atomic Gaussian process.

The integers $N_k$ increase. At the horizons $R_j=2\pi\,2^{N_j}$, every atom in blocks $k\le j$ has $R_j\omega_{k,l}\in2\pi\mathbb Z$ and contributes exactly zero to $V(R_j)$.

The remaining mass is $4^{-(j+1)}$. Therefore $2(1-\cos u)\le u^2$ gives

$$0\le V(R_j)\le R_j^2\,4^{-(j+1)}=4\pi^2\,2^{2(d_j-1)}.$$

Take $j_n=16^n-1$ for $n\ge1$, so $d_{j_n}=n$. Then

$$0\le\frac{V(R_{j_n})}{4\log R_{j_n}} \le\frac{\pi^2 4^n}{4\{\log(2\pi)+(16^n+n-1)\log2\}} \longrightarrow0.$$

This contradicts the asserted limit one with $b=L=1$. Equivalently, at $T=1$ and $\varepsilon_n=1/R_{j_n}$, the ratio of $\operatorname{Var}A_{\varepsilon_n}(1)$ to $4\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)$ tends to zero.

A sufficient density theorem

Suppose instead that $\mu$ is absolutely continuous in a neighborhood of zero, with

$$f(\omega)\sim2b\omega L(1/\omega),\qquad b>0,\qquad H(R)=\int_1^R L(s)\frac{ds}{s}\longrightarrow\infty,$$

where $L$ is positive and slowly varying. The spectrum away from zero may be any finite measure.

$$V(R)\sim4bH(R),\qquad \operatorname{Var}A_\varepsilon(T_\varepsilon) \sim4b\varepsilon^2H(T_\varepsilon/\varepsilon) \quad(T_\varepsilon/\varepsilon\to\infty).$$

Here is the oscillatory estimate missing from a cumulative-mass argument. Set $I(R)=V(R)/2$ and fix a small $\eta>0$ inside the density neighborhood.

The part below $R^{-1}$ is $O(L(R))$ by the quadratic cosine bound and Karamata integration; the part above $\eta$ is $O(1)$ by finiteness of $\mu$. The nonoscillatory middle integral is

$$\int_{R^{-1}}^\eta\frac{f(\omega)}{\omega^2}d\omega\sim2bH(R).$$

For the middle cosine integral, replacing $f(\omega)/\omega^2$ by $2bL(1/\omega)/\omega$ costs at most $o_\eta(1)H(R)$ in absolute value. On dyadic bands $[x,2x]$ with $x=2^r/R$, uniform slow variation similarly permits freezing $L(1/\omega)$ at $L(1/x)$, at total cost $o_\eta(1)H(R)$.

Integration by parts gives $|\int_x^{2x}\cos(R\omega)d\omega/\omega|\le C/(Rx)$, including the final partial band. For any fixed $0<\delta<1$, Potter bounds therefore give

$$\sum_{r\ge0:\,2^r/R<\eta}2^{-r}L(R/2^r) \le C_\delta L(R)\sum_{r\ge0}2^{-(1-\delta)r}=O(L(R)).$$

Thus the cosine integral is $O(L(R))+o_\eta(1)H(R)$. Since $L(R)=o(H(R))$, taking $R\to\infty$ and then $\eta\downarrow0$ proves $I(R)\sim2bH(R)$ and the displayed theorem.

Cumulative regular variation alone cannot justify this bandwise freezing of spectral mass. The atomic construction puts the entire mass on zeros of the oscillatory kernel at selected horizons.

Scope and certificate

The density-free theorem for $0<\alpha<2$ is unchanged. The primary discrete-time result of Deligiannidis and Utev (2013), Theorem 1.1, assumes a variance index strictly between zero and two and does not include this zero-index endpoint.

The Gaussian-dust and no-càdlàg-limit arguments in the full note require directly that $V$ diverges and is slowly varying. The density theorem supplies this hypothesis; the cumulative law alone does not.

The certificate checks exact integer block masses, cumulative-mass bounds, and resonant phases before evaluating only the remaining blocks. At $n=4$ and $n=8$, the variance ratios are respectively $9.561889\times10^{-5}$ and $2.436520\times10^{-9}$.

It truncates after block $K=j_n+n+30$, whose omitted variance is bounded by $4\pi^2 2^{-62}=8.560517\times10^{-18}$, separately from floating-point roundoff. The infinite-sequence contradiction is proved by the analytic upper bound, not extrapolated from the finite numerical samples.
