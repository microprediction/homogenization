A gamma-Poisson replacement with a finite-rate bound

Let $N\mid\Lambda\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda)$, where $\Lambda\geq0$ has positive mean $\mu$, variance $v>0$ and a finite fourth moment. Put $m_j=\mathbb E(\Lambda-\mu)^j$, and let $G$ be gamma with shape $r=\mu^2/v$ and scale $\theta=v/\mu$.

Define $N_*\mid G\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(G)$. Integrating the gamma density gives the negative-binomial generating function

$$\mathbb Ez^{N_*}=(1+\theta(1-z))^{-r},\qquad \mathbb EN_*=\mu,\quad \operatorname{Var}N_*=\mu+v.$$

It is a genuine probability law, unlike a generic finite signed corrector. The third and fourth mixing cumulants need not match those of $\Lambda$; matching two moments alone is not an accuracy guarantee.

A general fourth-moment comparison theorem

Use $d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)=\tfrac12\sum_{k\geq0}|P\{k\}-Q\{k\}|$. The gamma centered moments are $\mathbb E(G-\mu)^3=2v^2/\mu$ and $\mathbb E(G-\mu)^4=3v^2+6v^3/\mu^2$.

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(N),\mathcal L(N_*))\leq \min\left\{1,\frac23\left|m_3-\frac{2v^2}\mu\right| +\frac13\left(m_4+3v^2+\frac{6v^3}{\mu^2}\right)\right\}.$$

To prove it, let $p(x)$ denote the entire probability vector of a Poisson variable with mean $x$. If $S$ shifts this vector one place to the right, then $p^{(j)}(x)=(S-I)^jp(x)$, so

$$\|p^{(j)}(x)\|_1\leq2^j\quad(x\geq0).$$

Taylor-expand in the Banach space $\ell^1$ through degree three at $\mu$. Every point between $\mu$ and either nonnegative mixing variable lies in the domain of this derivative bound, so the remainder norm is at most $2^4|x-\mu|^4/4!$.

Taking expectations cancels the constant, linear and quadratic terms because the mean and variance match. The cubic difference has norm at most $2^3|m_3-2v^2/\mu|/3!$; the two remainder norms sum. Dividing by two gives the displayed total-variation bound.

Mixed-Poisson expansions by Poisson laws and finite signed measures are classical; see Roos (2003). The loose gamma-Poisson comparison above is proved directly here, with no sharpness or priority claim.

A stationary telegraph intensity supplies the required orders

Let $Z_t\in\{-1,1\}$ start symmetrically and flip at rate $\lambda$ in each direction, and let $\Lambda_T=\bar\ell T+\delta\int_0^T Z_t\,dt$, with $\bar\ell>0$ and $\bar\ell\pm\delta\geq0$. Then $\mu=\bar\ell T$, $m_3=0$ by symmetry, and

$$v=\delta^2\left\{\frac T\lambda-\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda T}}{2\lambda^2}\right\} \leq\frac{\delta^2T}{\lambda}.$$

For ordered times $t_1<t_2<t_3<t_4$, conditional expectation and $Z_t^2=1$ give

$$\mathbb E(Z_{t_1}Z_{t_2}Z_{t_3}Z_{t_4}) =e^{-2\lambda(t_2-t_1)}e^{-2\lambda(t_4-t_3)}.$$

Fixing $t_1<t_3$, each of the two intervening gap integrals is at most $1/(2\lambda)$. The $(t_1,t_3)$ domain has area $T^2/2$ and there are $4!$ orderings, yielding

$$m_4\leq\frac{3\delta^4T^2}{\lambda^2}.$$

Substituting these inequalities into the general theorem proves, at every positive speed and horizon,

$$d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(N_T),\mathcal L(N_*))\leq \min\left\{1,\frac{\delta^4}{\lambda^2} \left(2T^2+\frac{4T}{3\bar\ell}\right) +\frac{2\delta^6T}{\bar\ell^2\lambda^3}\right\}.$$

If $\delta=0$, $v=0$ and the exact law is Poisson, so no gamma parameterization is needed. With fixed $T$ and intensities, the bound is second order in inverse speed; it grows with $T$ and does not assert a joint long-maturity approximation.

The finite-horizon variance and centered fourth moment, not just their Green–Kubo leading terms, enter the general bound. This keeps the approximation scope separate from maturity-uniform cumulant statements.

The explicit telegraph bound assumes stationary symmetric initialization. The general moment theorem still applies to other nonnegative mixing laws, but their third and fourth centered moments must be controlled separately to conclude a second-order rate.

This is a one-horizon marginal count approximation, not an exact gamma intensity distribution, independent increments for the full arrival process, or hidden-chain identification. The finite-rate bound is deliberately conservative.
