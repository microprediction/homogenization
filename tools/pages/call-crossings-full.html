Moment identities and call crossings under cycle reversal

Every distinct zero of the log-strike call transform forces a strict strike crossing, and the bound is sharp for every finite node set. The two endpoints give the exact moment-only bound for zero-leverage switched CIR models.

The call transform

Normalize the initial stock and discount factor to one. Let $X$ have either of two risk-neutral laws $P_+$ and $P_-$, assume $\mathbb E_\pm e^X=1$, and suppose their moment generating functions are finite on an open interval containing $[0,1]$.

Write

$$G(k)=\mathbb E_+(e^X-e^k)_+-\mathbb E_-(e^X-e^k)_+,\qquad \Delta M(s)=\mathbb E_+e^{sX}-\mathbb E_-e^{sX}.$$

Two integrations by parts in log strike give the bilateral transform identity

$$H(s):=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{(s-1)k}G(k)\,dk =\frac{\Delta M(s)}{s(s-1)},\qquad 0<s<1.$$

Indeed, in the distributional sense $G''-G'=e^k(P_+-P_-)$, and the moment strip makes the boundary terms vanish. Since both laws have unit mass and unit stock mean, continuation to the endpoints yields

$$H(0)=-\Delta\mathbb E[X],\qquad H(1)=\Delta\mathbb E[e^XX]=\Delta\mathbb E^{S}[X].$$

The last equality is the stock-numeraire change of measure. Its measure-theoretic foundation is the change-of-numeraire theorem of Geman, El Karoui and Rochet (1995).

Every transform zero forces a crossing

More generally, let $s_1,\ldots,s_r$ be distinct points of $[0,1]$. If $H(s_i)=0$ for every $i$ and $G$ is not identically zero, then $G$ has at least $r$ strict sign changes. This statement includes endpoint values by continuous extension; an interior condition is simply the fractional-moment identity $\Delta\mathbb E[e^{s_iX}]=0$.

To prove it, suppose that $G$ has only $q<r$ sign-change locations $x_1<\cdots<x_q$, with zero intervals removed. The $q+1$ functions $e^{(s_i-1)k}$ form a strict Chebyshev system: their Wronskian is a nonzero exponential times the Vandermonde determinant of the distinct exponents $s_i-1$. Therefore a nonzero linear combination $p(k)$ can be chosen to vanish at the $x_j$.

This exponential total-positivity mechanism is classical; see Schoenberg (1947).

The Chebyshev zero bound says that these are all its zeros and that they are simple. After changing its overall sign, $pG\geq0$ everywhere and $pG>0$ somewhere. But

$$\int_{\mathbb R}p(k)G(k)\,dk=\sum_{i=1}^{q+1}c_iH(s_i)=0,$$

a contradiction. The argument is fixed-maturity and uses distinct transform nodes; it does not turn zeros across maturities into strike crossings.

One endpoint moment forces one crossing; two force two

A crossing means a strict sign change of the continuous function $G$, after zero intervals are removed. If $\Delta\mathbb E[X]=0$ and $G$ is not identically zero, then

$$\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-k}G(k)\,dk=0,$$

so $G$ must have both signs and therefore at least one crossing.

If also $\Delta\mathbb E^S[X]=0$, then $\int G(k)\,dk=0$ as well. One crossing is impossible: if its location is $a$ and $G$ is nonnegative to the left and nonpositive to the right, then

$$\int_{\mathbb R}(e^{-k}-e^{-a})G(k)\,dk>0,$$

contradicting the two zero integrals. Reversing the signs gives the opposite strict inequality, so every nonzero call gap has at least two crossings.

This is a two-function Chebyshev argument for the positive weights $1$ and $e^{-k}$. It counts strike crossings at one fixed maturity, not zeros of a scalar observable as maturity varies.

Application to simultaneous CIR switching

Let a stationary finite-state chain $Z$ have row generator $mQ$ and invariant law $\pi$, and compare it with its stationary reversal. Consider

$$dX_t=-\tfrac12V_t\,dt+\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t^S,\qquad dV_t=(a_{Z_t}-\kappa V_t)\,dt+\xi_{Z_t}\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t^V,$$

with $d\langle W^S,W^V\rangle_t=\rho\,dt$, common $\kappa$, the same deterministic $V_0$ in both orientations, and true stock martingales. The ordinary variance mean solves

$$\overline v'(t)=\langle a,\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\kappa\overline v(t),$$

so reversal preserves $\mathbb E[X_T]=-\tfrac12\int_0^T\overline v(t)\,dt$ for every $\rho$.

At zero leverage, the stock-numeraire change flips the drift of $X$ but leaves the variance dynamics unchanged. Hence reversal also preserves $\mathbb E^S[X_T]=\tfrac12\int_0^T\overline v(t)\,dt$.

$$\boxed{\rho=0:\quad G_T\not\equiv0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{at least two strict call-price crossings in strike}.}$$

Thus simultaneous switching of immigration $a$ and vol-of-vol $\xi$ destroys some stronger identities but not this two-crossing lower bound. The statement is marginal over the stationary regime start and applies at each fixed maturity for which the displayed moment strip holds.

What leverage changes

Under the stock measure the variance killing vector becomes $c=\kappa\mathbf1-\rho\xi$. Put $A=mQ-D_c$ and

$$L(M)=\langle a,M\mathbf1\rangle_\pi-\langle\mathbf1,Ma\rangle_\pi.$$

A direct first-moment calculation gives the forward-minus-reverse stock-log mean

$$D_S(T):=\Delta\mathbb E^S[X_T] =\frac12\int_0^T(T-u)L(e^{uA})\,du.$$

The contribution of $V_0$ cancels because $mQ^{\leftarrow}-D_c$ is similar to $A^\top$ through $D_\pi$. Immigration supplies the displayed antisymmetric functional.

Consequently the same two-moment theorem applies at a leveraged maturity $T$ whenever $D_S(T)=0$; this is exactly the additional endpoint identity used by its proof. If $r$ is the degree of the minimal polynomial of $A$, cancellation at every maturity is equivalent to the finite conditions

$$L(A^j)=0,\qquad j=0,\ldots,r-1.$$

Without that identity only the ordinary-log moment is universally matched, so the moment argument guarantees one crossing. This does not say that a particular leveraged CIR pair has only one crossing; it identifies the precise point where a second universal sign change can no longer be deduced.

Sharpness for every finite transform-node set

The lower bound cannot be improved from transform zeros alone. Fix any distinct $s_1,\ldots,s_r\in[0,1]$, choose $b>1$, put $a_i=b^{s_i}$, and define

$$P(z)=(z-1)(z-b)\prod_{i=1}^r(z-a_i)=\sum_{j=0}^{r+2}c_jz^j.$$

All roots are positive, so the nonzero coefficients $c_j$ alternate in sign. Normalize their positive and negative parts into two laws on the geometric stock grid $b^j$, then divide both grids by their common mean.

The identities $P(1)=P(b)=0$ give unit mass and the common mean. Moreover,

$$\Delta M(s)=\frac{\mu^{-s}}{Z}P(b^s).$$

An interior $s_i$ is therefore a zero of $H$. If $s_i$ is an endpoint, its factor duplicates the baseline factor $z-1$ or $z-b$, so division by $s(s-1)$ still leaves a zero. Thus the prescribed nodes are exactly the zeros of $H$ on $[0,1]$.

It remains to count the crossings rather than merely invoke the lower bound. Write

$$S(z)=\frac{P(z)}{(z-1)(z-b)}=\sum_{j=0}^rd_jz^j,$$

and let $\Gamma(K)=G(\log K)$ be the call gap in ordinary strike. At the interior grid points $K_i=b^i/\mu$, a coefficient-tail telescoping gives the exact identity

$$Z\mu\,\Gamma(K_i)=b^i(b-1)d_{i-1},\qquad i=1,\ldots,r+1.$$

The $d_j$ alternate because $S$ also has only positive roots. Hence the call gap alternates sign at these $r+1$ grid points and, being affine between adjacent support points, has exactly one strict zero in each of the $r$ intervening intervals. It has no further strict crossing in the two edge intervals, where one endpoint is the identically zero outer call gap.

This proves sharpness for every $r$ and every prescribed node set, but the constructed laws are finite martingale laws, not switched-CIR models.

Small exact examples

For three crossings, let $P_+$ put masses $(16/25,53/150,1/150)$ at stock values $(1/8,2,32)$, and let $P_-$ put masses $(16/75,53/75,2/25)$ at $(1/32,1/2,8)$.

The laws have unit means, equal ordinary and stock-weighted log means, and equal square-root moments, so $H$ vanishes at $s=0,1/2,1$. Their call difference changes sign exactly at strikes $11/64$, $1$ and $64/11$.

For two crossings, let $P_+$ put masses $(2/9,13/18,1/18)$ at stock values $(1/4,1,4)$, and let $P_-$ put masses $(2/3,1/3)$ at $(1/2,2)$.

Both laws have mean one, equal log means, and equal stock-weighted log means. Their call difference changes sign exactly at strikes $5/8$ and $8/5$.

For one crossing, let $P_+$ put masses $(5/6,1/6)$ at $(2/3,8/3)$, and let $P_-$ put masses $(1/3,2/3)$ at $(1/3,4/3)$. These laws have mean one and equal log means but unequal stock-weighted log means; their call difference changes sign exactly at $8/9$.

The coefficient vectors are the positive and negative parts of $(z-1)^2(z-2)(z-4)^2$, $(z-1)^2(z-2)^2$ and $(z-1)^2(z-2)$. Double roots encode the endpoint log moments; the simple root at $z=2$ in the three-crossing fixture encodes the square-root moment.
