Singular directed blocks and the cost of approximation

A directed Green–Kubo block needs more than a nonnegative symmetric part: its skew part must annihilate every symmetric null direction. The resulting cone is not closed, but a fixed state budget leaves a positive approximation floor; a two-feature example has a sharp inverse-square state-count law.

The exact singular boundary

Allow any finite irreducible row generator $Q$, its stationary law $\pi$, and arbitrary centered real features $F$ with $d$ columns. Write $K=F^\top D_\pi(-Q)^{-1}_0F=G+A$, where $G=G^\top$ and $A^\top=-A$.

$$K\text{ is realizable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\succeq0\quad\text{and}\quad\ker G\subseteq\ker A.$$

The number of states is unrestricted here, but finite for each realization. The zero block permits the trivial one-state chain; every nonzero block has at least $\operatorname{rank}G+1$ states.

For necessity put $L=-Q$, $F=LU$ and $C=D_\pi L$. Then $K=U^\top C^\top U$, and the balanced-flow Dirichlet identity gives

$$x^\top Gx=\tfrac12\sum_{v\ne w}\pi_vq_{vw}\big((Ux)_v-(Ux)_w\big)^2.$$

If this vanishes, irreducibility makes $Ux$ constant, hence $Fx=0$ and both $Kx=0$ and $K^\top x=0$. Thus $Ax=0$; equivalently the main fixed-feature kernel theorem supplies the necessity.

For sufficiency restrict to $\operatorname{range}G$, where $G$ is positive definite and $A$ is supported by the kernel condition. Whitening and a real orthogonal canonical form reduce the problem to blocks $I_2+\kappa_jJ$ and scalar ones, where $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$.

Here is a finite construction, included to make the reduction self-contained. For each skew block choose $n\ge3$ with $|\kappa|\le\cot(\pi/n)$, put $\theta=2\pi/n$, and use a cycle with stationary forward/reverse flows $a,b$ satisfying

$$a+b=\frac{2}{n(1-\cos\theta)},\qquad a-b=\frac{2\kappa}{n\sin\theta}.$$

Attach these cycles at a common hub, adding one leaf with unit flow in both directions for each scalar block. On each cycle use potential columns $(\cos(k\theta)-1,\sin(k\theta))$, zero elsewhere, and leaf indicators for scalar blocks.

The potentials vanish at the hub, so different blocks have zero pairings in $U^\top C^\top U$; the displayed flows make each cycle block exactly $I_2+\kappa J$. Divide flows by any positive stationary law to obtain $Q$, undo the whitening/congruence, extend by zero on $\ker G$, and take $F=-QU$.

The realizable set $\mathcal R_d$ is a convex cone: the kernel of a sum of nonnegative symmetric parts is the intersection of their kernels. Its closure is all real matrices with nonnegative symmetric part, since $K+\delta I$ is realizable for every $\delta>0$ whenever $\operatorname{sym}K\succeq0$.

For $d\ge2$ the cone is not closed. With $a\ne0$, the block

$$K_a=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\-a&0\end{pmatrix}$$

is impossible for every finite irreducible chain: its symmetric null vector $e_2$ is not a null vector of its skew part. Nevertheless $K_a+\delta I\to K_a$ through realizable blocks.

A fixed state budget prevents arbitrary approximation

Fix $N\ge3$, $c_N=\cot(\pi/N)$, and let $\mathcal R_{2,\le N}$ contain two-feature blocks realizable using at most $N$ states. This set is exactly the closed convex cone

$$\mathcal R_{2,\le N}=\{G+A:c_NG+iA\succeq0,\ c_NG-iA\succeq0\}.$$

To see necessity, decompose stationary flows into directed simple cycles. The cycle Fourier multipliers $1-e^{2\pi ik/n}$ bound the absolute imaginary quadratic form by $\cot(\pi/n)$ times its real part; sum the bounds and apply them to complex Poisson potentials.

For sufficiency, when $G\succ0$ the two inequalities say $|A_{12}|\le c_N\sqrt{\det G}$. The first cosine/sine pair on a bidirectional $N$-cycle realizes every normalized skew ratio in $[-c_N,c_N]$ by varying forward/reverse flow bias, then a real congruence gives $K$.

At a singular boundary the inequalities force $A\ker G=0$; in two dimensions this means $A=0$, realized with at most two states. The zero block is included.

Use the spectral operator norm in the displayed feature coordinates, and define $\rho_N(a)=\inf_{K\in\mathcal R_{2,\le N}}\|K-K_a\|_2$. Put

$$\delta_N(a)=\frac{\sqrt{1+4a^2/c_N^2}-1}{2}.$$
$$\frac{c_N}{c_N+1}\delta_N(a)\le\rho_N(a)\le\delta_N(a), \qquad\lim_{N\to\infty}N^2\rho_N(a)=\pi^2a^2.$$

For the upper bound use $K=K_a+\delta_N I$; its sector inequality is saturated, so an oriented $N$-cycle realizes it exactly. For the lower bound, $c_N\operatorname{sym}K_a+i\operatorname{skew}K_a$ has smallest eigenvalue $-c_N\delta_N$.

A perturbation $E$ of operator norm $\eta$ changes this Hermitian matrix by at most $(c_N+1)\eta$, so positivity requires the stated lower bound. Since $c_N\sim N/\pi$, the squeeze proves the sharp leading constant, not just the exponent.

The upper construction need not be the closest block at finite $N$. The conclusion concerns arbitrary generators and freely changing embeddings, not just one-way-cycle approximation.

An exact nearest four-state block

For $a=14/15$ and $N=4$, an exact primal/dual certificate gives

$$K_{\rm best}=\begin{pmatrix}4/3&2/3\\-2/3&1/3\end{pmatrix}, \qquad\rho_4(14/15)=\frac{\sqrt{41}}{15}=0.426874949162\ldots.$$

Take the one-way four-cycle $Q=\tfrac23(P-I)$, with $P_{k,k+1}=1$ cyclically and uniform $\pi$. Potentials $(2,0,-2,0)^\top$ and $(0,1,0,-1)^\top$, followed by $F=-QU$, produce $K_{\rm best}$ exactly in rational arithmetic.

For global optimality, put $z=(1,2i)^\top/\sqrt5$ and $M=\tfrac15\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}$. Every feasible four-state block obeys

$$\ell(K)=z^*(G+iA)z=\operatorname{tr}(M^\top K)\ge0, \quad\ell(K_a)=-41/75,\quad\|M\|_*=\sqrt{41}/5.$$

Here $\|M\|_*$ is the sum of singular values. Trace duality forces $\|K-K_a\|_2\ge\sqrt{41}/15$ for every feasible block, while $(K_{\rm best}-K_a)^\top(K_{\rm best}-K_a)=(41/225)I_2$ attains the bound.

Scope, literature and checks

These are exact population coefficient constraints in fixed feature units; operator-norm distances change under arbitrary feature rescaling. A small fit residual alone cannot distinguish a realizable block from this singular impossible limit unless the state budget is controlled.

No inference about finite-time path laws, finite-sample confidence or maturity-integrated rank follows. The four-feature minimum-state problem in issue 222 is unchanged.

The classical Poisson representation is Pavliotis (2010), Proposition 2.1, equations (2.3)–(2.4). Stationary cycle decomposition is Landim and Xu (2016), Lemma 4.1; the sharp sector context is Ohga, Ito and Kolchinsky (2023), equation (3).

The certificate checks singular realizations, the state-budget bounds, the exact rational primal/dual benchmark and an independent correlation quadrature. Floating consistency checks and restricted-family numerical projections are not interval enclosures or substitutes for the global analytic dual proof; no priority claim is made for the classical ingredients.
