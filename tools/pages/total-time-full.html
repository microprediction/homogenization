One elapsed-time total restores identification

Individual timestamps and the clock law can remain unknown: a block's exact elapsed time can remove generator aliasing.

The observation protocol

Let $Y$ have a finite, time-homogeneous row generator $Q$ on a complete known labeled state space. Let $\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_m$ be iid, positive and finite almost surely, independent of the entire chain, for a fixed integer $m\ge1$. Write $T=\sum_{k=1}^m\tau_k$ and retain only the two endpoint labels and the exact total $(Y_0,Y_T,T)$.

The initial law $\nu$ has full support and is independent of the clock. The clock law $\mu$ need not be known or have finite mean; irreducibility, reversibility and diagonalizability are not assumed. Identification here means uniqueness from the population law of this record, not recovery from one realized block.

Call the clock grid-free if there is no $h>0$ with $\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset h\mathbb Z$. This is a grid through zero, not a ban on all translated lattices: the two-point clock $\{1,1+\sqrt2\}$ is grid-free in this sense. Any clock with a nonzero continuous component is grid-free.

The exact criterion

A grid-free clock makes $Q$ identifiable from the retained record for every finite generator. Conversely, a clock supported on a common grid admits distinct, strictly positive three-state generators with exactly the same record law. The converse also extends to every fixed dimension at least three; it is a global obstruction, not ambiguity at every generator.

Independence of $T$ and $Y$ gives the key conditional kernel, for $\mu^{*m}$-almost every $t$:

$$\Pr(Y_T=j\mid Y_0=i,T=t)=(e^{tQ})_{ij}.$$

Thus no individual interval needs to be reconstructed, and no deconvolution of the clock law is required. Full support of $\nu$ exposes every row. Continuity extends equality of two candidate kernels almost everywhere to the whole support of $\mu^{*m}$.

For two candidate matrices $Q,G$, the set $H=\{t\in\mathbb R:e^{tQ}=e^{tG}\}$ is a closed additive subgroup of $\mathbb R$. Indeed matrix exponentials are invertible, and equality at $s,t$ gives equality at $s-t$ by taking products with inverses. If $Q\ne G$, then $H\ne\mathbb R$ by differentiation at zero, so $H$ is either $\{0\}$ or $h\mathbb Z$.

The support of a positive total is not contained in $\{0\}$. If $\operatorname{supp}\mu^{*m}\subset h\mathbb Z$, then for every $u\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$ the repeated sum $mu$ lies in that support, forcing $\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset(h/m)\mathbb Z$. The reverse implication, that a grid-supported interval has a grid-supported total, is immediate.

Therefore a grid-free clock forces $H=\mathbb R$, hence $Q=G$. This argument uses full labeled matrices, not just unordered spectra, and handles Jordan blocks without selecting a logarithm branch.

A pair of support times with irrational ratio is sufficient but not necessary. For example, a clock assigning positive mass to every $1/k!$ is grid-free although all pairwise ratios are rational, since its positive support times approach zero.

Why the grid exception is real

For any $h>0$, put $P=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3$, $E=I-P$ and

$$J=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q_0=-aE,\quad Q_1=-aE+bJ,\quad a=\frac{4\pi}h,\quad b=\frac{2\pi}h.$$

Both generators have strictly positive off-diagonal entries and the same uniform stationary law. Since $J^2=-E$ and $PJ=JP=0$,

$$e^{tQ_1}=P+e^{-at}\{\cos(bt)E+\sin(bt)J\},\qquad e^{khQ_0}=e^{khQ_1}=P+e^{-akh}E\quad(k\ge1).$$

If every interval lies in $h\mathbb Z$, even the full label path together with every individual timestamp has the same law under these two generators. Consequently retaining its elapsed-time total cannot distinguish them.

The same fixed centered feature $f=(1,-1,0)^\top$ has different physical-time Green–Kubo strengths:

$$\Gamma_0=\frac h{3\pi},\qquad \Gamma_1=\frac{4h}{15\pi}=\frac45\Gamma_0.$$

These are strength differences, not different fixed-feature ranks or claims about maturity-integrated rank. In any larger dimension embed $J$ in its first three coordinates, set $E_n=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/n$, and choose $a>n|b|/\sqrt3$. The two generators $-aE_n$ and $-aE_n+bJ$ remain strictly positive and have identical kernels on the grid.

Near-grid clocks: uniqueness is not stable estimation

Keep the same three-state pair and independently choose each interval from $\{h,h+\epsilon\}$ with equal probabilities, where $\epsilon/h$ is irrational and positive. This clock is grid-free, so the population law identifies the generator. Nevertheless its $m$-transition label-path/total record laws $\mathcal L_0,\mathcal L_1$ satisfy

$$\|\mathcal L_0-\mathcal L_1\|_{\rm TV} \le\min\left\{1,\frac{m\epsilon e^{-ah}}4\|Q_1-Q_0\|_\infty\right\},\qquad \|A\|_\infty=\max_i\sum_j|A_{ij}|.$$

To prove this, couple the two clocks identically. The kernels at $h$ coincide, while the semigroup property and Duhamel's identity give $\|e^{\epsilon Q_1}-e^{\epsilon Q_0}\|_\infty\le\epsilon\|Q_1-Q_0\|_\infty$. Multiplication by the common kernel $P+e^{-ah}E$ contributes exactly $e^{-ah}$ because $P(e^{\epsilon Q_1}-e^{\epsilon Q_0})=0$.

The jitter branch has probability one half, and a row's total variation is one half its $\ell^1$ distance. Sequential coupling gives the displayed bound for the stronger record retaining every timestamp, hence also for its coarsening to the total. For fixed $m$, its right side tends to zero while the generator and Green–Kubo gaps stay fixed.

No uniform inverse-continuity guarantee over grid-free clock designs follows from identification. Timestamp rounding, state-dependent sampling and elapsed-time stopping are different experiments not covered by the theorem. A single confined path need not expose all rows of a reducible chain.

Relation to previous work and checks

Non-uniform sampling and removal of aliasing are classical; Ding, Qiu and Chen (2009), Section 5, treat multirate reconstruction, including a criterion based on intersections of eigenvalue sets. Duffie and Glynn (2004), Section 4, distinguish observed and unobserved sampling times for Markov-process identification. Here the point is the coarser block-total protocol, its exact common-grid criterion and its near-grid bound, not novelty of semigroup or multirate identification.

In particular, any smooth independently redrawn clock becomes identifying under this protocol, even when discarding all times would completely erase its generator. No finite-sample accuracy or calendar-time efficiency is implied by conditioning on an exact continuous total.

The certificate checks the exact grid-alias and strength identities, endpoint/total laws by enumerating hidden interval assignments and intermediate labels, a defective chain and an absorbing chain. It also checks total-time Laplace transforms of a smooth erasing clock by independent integration, and near-grid path-law bounds, with portable tolerances and final literal PASS.
