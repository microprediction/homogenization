Count bins decouple one order faster

A joint finite-grid theorem, with stationary initialization and explicit constants.

Let a finite irreducible stationary chain with generator $mQ$ drive nonnegative arrival intensities $\ell_i$. For successive bins of lengths $T_j$, let $X_j$ count arrivals and $p_j=\mathcal L_\pi(N_{T_j})$ be its exact marginal law.

$$d_{\rm TV}\left(\mathcal L(X_1,\ldots,X_r),\bigotimes_jp_j\right) \leq\min\left\{1,\frac{2(r-1)CC_*F^2}{m^2\gamma\gamma_*}\right\}.$$

Here $F=\|\ell-(\pi\ell)\mathbf1\|_\infty$, and $(C,\gamma)$ and $(C_*,\gamma_*)$ bound exponential mixing of the forward and reversed chains. The proof multiplies an $O(m^{-1})$ boundary-state memory defect on each side of a stationary cut.

The bound holds at all speeds and uniformly over bin lengths, with zero intensities and defective generators allowed. Replacing each marginal by an approximation adds at most the sum of its TV errors, giving a second-order joint-grid approximation whenever the marginal errors are second order.

For two unit bins, a unit-rate symmetric base chain, intensities $(3,1)$ and speed 80, the independence error is $0.0000057234$, below the bound $0.0000781250$. The full note gives the proof and 84 certificate cases.

Stationary start and a bounded number of bins matter: the bound grows with $r$. No full-path approximation, refinement consistency or arbitrary-prior theorem is claimed.
