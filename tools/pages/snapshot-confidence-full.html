Anytime two-state confidence from snapshots

Labeled snapshots of a fixed two-state continuous-time chain give simultaneous confidence bounds for its generator and stationary law, without a supplied mixing rate or jump times. The bounds can become informative on observed records, but no uniform calendar-time accuracy is asserted. A simple computable gate keeps the inverse away from its singular boundary.

Observation model and exact inverse

Let $Q=\begin{pmatrix}-q_{12}&q_{12}\\q_{21}&-q_{21}\end{pmatrix}$ with fixed finite $q_{12},q_{21}>0$. Observe $X_k=Y_{kh}$, $k\ge0$, with known labels and fixed known spacing $h>0$. The initial state is arbitrary, and the chain need not start in stationarity.

$$P=e^{hQ}=\begin{pmatrix}1-a&a\\b&1-b\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b>0,\quad s:=a+b<1.$$

Conversely every such $(a,b)$ has the unique two-state generator and stationary row law

$$f_h(s)=\frac{-\log(1-s)}{hs},\qquad q_{12}=a f_h(s),\quad q_{21}=b f_h(s),\quad \pi=\left(\frac b{s},\frac a{s}\right).$$

Indeed the nonconstant eigenvalue of $P$ is $1-s=e^{-h(q_{12}+q_{21})}$ and its off-diagonal ratio is $a/b=q_{12}/q_{21}$. This inverse is valid for every fixed $h$, but becomes ill-conditioned as $s\uparrow1$. It is not a general-state snapshot-identification theorem.

A confidence sequence for each transition row

For $i\in\{1,2\}$ and $K$ observed transitions, put $V_i(K)=\sum_{k<K}\mathbf1\{X_k=i\}$ and $C_i(K)=\sum_{k<K}\mathbf1\{X_k=i,X_{k+1}\ne i\}$. Write $u_1=a,u_2=b$ and fix $0<\delta<1$ before seeing the data.

$$M_{i,K}(u)=\frac{\mathrm B(C_i+1,V_i-C_i+1)}{u^{C_i}(1-u)^{V_i-C_i}},\qquad \mathcal C_i(K)=\{u\in[0,1]:M_{i,K}(u)\le2/\delta\}=[l_i,r_i].$$

Here $\mathrm B$ is the beta function; use continuous endpoint limits and $0^0=1$. For an unvisited row $V_i=0$, the interval is $[0,1]$. For $V_i>0$, the log mixture is convex with its minimum at $C_i/V_i$, so its sublevel set is one interval.

For $C_i=0$ its upper endpoint is $1-\exp[-\{\log(2/\delta)+\log(V_i+1)\}/V_i]$ and its lower endpoint is zero. For $C_i=V_i$ reflect this interval about $1/2$; otherwise solve the two scalar log-mixture equations. These are frequentist confidence sets, not posterior credible intervals.

Proof. For fixed $v\in(0,1)$, multiply by $(v/u_i)$ on an observed departure from row $i$, by $(1-v)/(1-u_i)$ on a self-transition in that row, and by one otherwise. Given $\mathcal F_k=\sigma(X_0,\ldots,X_k)$, the next multiplier has conditional mean one, since row selection is already known. The product is a nonnegative martingale in the full snapshot filtration.

Integrate over a fixed uniform $v\in[0,1]$ to obtain exactly $M_{i,K}(u_i)$, initially one. Ville's inequality gives $\Pr(\exists K:M_{i,K}(u_i)>2/\delta)\le\delta/2$. A union bound over the two rows proves, conditional on each initial state,

$$\boxed{\Pr_{Q,x}\{a\in\mathcal C_1(K),\ b\in\mathcal C_2(K)\text{ for every }K\ge0\}\ge1-\delta.}$$

The alternative transition rows need not themselves be CTMC-embeddable; their valid one-step probabilities suffice for the martingale.

No row independence or conditional-binomial assertion is used. In fact, with $X_0=1$ and $K=2$, conditioning on $V_1=1$ forces $C_1=1$, although $a<1$. Random visit totals must not be treated as fixed independent sample sizes.

A finite, explicit generator certificate

At any $K$, abbreviate $[l_a,r_a]=\mathcal C_1(K)$ and $[l_b,r_b]=\mathcal C_2(K)$. Use the sufficient reporting gate $l_a,l_b>0$ and $r_a+r_b<1$; otherwise abstain from this finite generator certificate. On the same simultaneous event, every passing time obeys

$$\begin{aligned} q_{12}&\in[L_{12},U_{12}]=[l_a f_h(l_a+l_b),\ r_a f_h(r_a+r_b)],\\ q_{21}&\in[L_{21},U_{21}]=[l_b f_h(l_a+l_b),\ r_b f_h(r_a+r_b)],\\ \pi_1&\in[L_p,U_p]=\left[\frac{l_b}{r_a+l_b},\frac{r_b}{l_a+r_b}\right],\qquad\pi_2=1-\pi_1. \end{aligned}$$

These coordinate extrema are exact over the reporting rectangle, though the separate extrema need not be attained together. To prove monotonicity, write $f_h(s)=h^{-1}\int_0^1(1-st)^{-1}dt$, which is positive and increasing. Thus both rates increase in both arguments, while $b/(a+b)$ increases in $b$ and decreases in $a$.

For any record-dependent row generator $\bar Q$ and stationary-law estimate $(\bar p,1-\bar p)$, define $d_{ij}=\max(|\bar q_{ij}-L_{ij}|,|\bar q_{ij}-U_{ij}|)$ and $d_p=\max(|\bar p-L_p|,|\bar p-U_p|)$. Then $\|\bar Q-Q\|_F\le\sqrt{2(d_{12}^2+d_{21}^2)}$ and $\|(\bar p,1-\bar p)-\pi\|_2\le\sqrt2d_p$ at all passing times on that event.

The point estimates need not be stationary-consistent with one another for these separate bounds. Data-dependent stopping or repeated reports preserve the simultaneous guarantee; coverage conditional on the event of reporting is not claimed. An unvisited row cannot pass, and a box reaching $a+b=1$ does not give this finite upper-rate bound.

Pointwise eventual usefulness, not a uniform time guarantee

For every fixed generator in the stated class, $V_i(K)\to\infty$ and $C_i(K)/V_i(K)\to u_i$ almost surely by finite-chain ergodicity. With $\widehat u=C_i/V_i$, the exact identity is $\log M_{i,K}(u)=\log M_{i,K}(\widehat u)+V_i\operatorname{kl}(\widehat u,u)$. Stirling's formula gives $\log M_{i,K}(\widehat u)=-\tfrac12\log V_i+O(1)$ along these paths.

The Bernoulli divergence is locally quadratic at each fixed $u_i\in(0,1)$, so the interval endpoints differ from $\widehat u$ by $O(\sqrt{\log V_i/V_i})$. Consequently the reporting gate eventually passes almost surely and the rate and stationary-law interval widths tend to zero. The constants depend on the fixed model and $h$; neither a uniform calendar-time rate nor an optimal iterated-logarithm width is asserted.

Numerical record and relation to the literature

A feasible record with $h=1$, $V=(600000,400000)$, $C=(120000,120000)$ and $\delta=.05$ gives $q_{12}\in[.272670087,.281924947]$, $q_{21}\in[.409388974,.422489528]$ and $\pi_1\in[.594577497,.605397323]$ (display-rounded endpoints). The inverse of $(\widehat a,\widehat b)=(.2,.3)$ has generator Frobenius radius $.011432265$.

The certificate checks 168 exact rational martingale means and 84 finite-path first-crossing laws over horizons 1–14; the largest crossing probability is $.025832473$ at error budget $.2$. It also checks 36 independent matrix-exponential inversions, scalar endpoint equations and 441 box points. These finite checks audit the formulas; the proof, not a simulation, supplies infinite-horizon coverage.

Floating root evaluations are not outward-rounded arithmetic certificates; computational roundoff is not included in the statistical error budget.

The two-state embedding and its singular boundary are classical: see Bladt and Sørensen (2005), Section 2.1. The mixture method is classical confidence-sequence machinery; see Howard et al. (2021), Section 3.2 and Proposition 7. Fully empirical inference for stationary laws and gaps from one reversible discrete chain is developed by Hsu et al. (2019), Section 4.

The contribution here is the explicit time-uniform two-state CTMC rate box and reporting gate, with their observation assumptions and failure modes spelled out. This does not cover hidden or noisy labels, adaptive sampling spacings, a changing generator, or replacement of continuous occupation windows by grid sums. It is model-based confidence conditional on the initial state, not feature-conditional conformal coverage.
