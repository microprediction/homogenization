Initial-regime memory in CIR integrated variance

A known starting regime creates a first-order CIR-noise boundary term; retaining it restores a maturity-uniform second-order error bound.

Let $Y$ have generator $mQ$, where $Q$ is a fixed finite irreducible row generator with stationary row $\pi$. Take $Y_0\sim\nu$, independent Brownian motion $B$, a deterministic $v_0>0$, and

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta(Y_t)-v_t)dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t,\qquad V_T=\int_0^T v_t\,dt.$$

Keep $\kappa>0$ and $\xi\ge0$ fixed and assume $2\kappa\min_i\theta_i\ge\xi^2$, so the CIR variance stays positive. Put $f=\theta-(\pi\theta)\mathbf1$, $F=\|f\|_\infty$, $H=\int_0^\infty(e^{tQ}-\mathbf1\pi)dt=-Q^\#$, $h=Hf$ and $K=\pi(f\odot h)$. Assume a justified envelope $\|e^{tQ}-\mathbf1\pi\|_\infty\le Ce^{-\gamma t}$, with $C\ge1$, $\gamma>0$.

$$B_\kappa(t)=\frac{1-e^{-\kappa t}}\kappa,\qquad I_2(T)=\int_0^T B_\kappa(t)^2dt,$$ $$J_\kappa(T)=\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-u)^2e^{-\kappa u}du =\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa T}-2\kappa T e^{-\kappa T}}{\kappa^3}.$$

Let $\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T)$ refer to the CIR model with level $\pi\theta$ and the same deterministic $v_0$. Explicitly, it is $\xi^2\{(\pi\theta)I_2(T)+(v_0-\pi\theta)J_\kappa(T)\}$.

An all-prior, all-maturity bound

$$\operatorname{Var}_\nu(V_T)=\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T) +\frac{2\kappa^2K}{m}I_2(T) +\frac{\xi^2\kappa}{m}J_\kappa(T)\,\nu h+R_\nu(T,m),$$ $$\sup_{\nu}\sup_{T\ge0}|R_\nu(T,m)| \le\frac{(4C+3C^2)F^2+2\xi^2CF/\kappa}{m^2\gamma^2},\qquad m>0.$$

The first correction is the stationary Green–Kubo term. The new initial-memory term vanishes when $\nu=\pi$, but generally has order $m^{-1}$ for a specified start. Since $J_\kappa(T)\to\kappa^{-3}$, it approaches the finite offset $\xi^2\nu h/(\kappa^2m)$ at the long end, not a change in the variance slope.

For an independent-price Brownian return $M_T=\sqrt{V_T}Z$, with independent standard normal $Z$, $\kappa_4(M_T)=3\operatorname{Var}_\nu(V_T)$ exactly. Multiply every correction and the remainder bound by three to obtain its fourth-cumulant statement. This is not the fourth cumulant of the drifted log return, nor a leverage theorem.

Proof: the noise mean and the occupation covariance are different effects

Conditioning on the full regime path makes the CIR stochastic integral mean zero. Hence the regime integral and stochastic integral in the integrated CIR equation are uncorrelated, giving

$$\operatorname{Var}_\nu(V_T)=\xi^2\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-s)^2\mathbb E_\nu v_s\,ds +\kappa^2\operatorname{Var}_\nu\!\left(\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-s)f(Y_s)\,ds\right).$$

Write $a_\nu(u)=\nu e^{muQ}f$. The difference in variance means from the averaged model is $\mathbb E_\nu v_s-\mathbb E_{\rm av}v_s=\kappa\int_0^s e^{-\kappa(s-u)}a_\nu(u)du$. Interchanging these deterministic integrals identifies the additional noise variance exactly as

$$N_\nu(T)=\xi^2\kappa\int_0^T a_\nu(u)J_\kappa(T-u)du.$$

Extend $J_\kappa$ by zero to negative arguments. Its integral representation gives $\|J'_\kappa\|_\infty\le2/\kappa^2$, and $\int_0^\infty a_\nu(u)du=\nu h/m$. Therefore the global Lipschitz bound and $|a_\nu(u)|\le CF e^{-m\gamma u}$ give

$$\left|N_\nu(T)-\frac{\xi^2\kappa}{m}J_\kappa(T)\nu h\right| \le\frac{2\xi^2CF}{\kappa m^2\gamma^2}.$$

For the remaining occupation term, at $0\le s\le t$ the difference between nonstationary and stationary covariances is

$$D_\nu(s,t)=(\nu e^{msQ}-\pi) [f\odot(e^{m(t-s)Q}f)]-a_\nu(s)a_\nu(t),$$ $$|D_\nu(s,t)|\le C^2F^2\{e^{-m\gamma t}+e^{-m\gamma(s+t)}\}.$$

Since $0\le\kappa B_\kappa\le1$, integration over the time triangle gives an all-maturity occupation-variance difference at most $3C^2F^2/(m^2\gamma^2)$. The stationary weighted-kernel bound contributes at most $4CF^2/(m^2\gamma^2)$ when replacing the stationary occupation variance by $2\kappa^2KI_2(T)/m$. Adding these two bounds and the noise-memory error proves the theorem, without reversibility or diagonalizability.

A sharp first-order witness

Take the symmetric chain flipping at rate $m$, with $\theta_\pm=\bar\theta\pm\delta$. The regime-integral variances for pure $+$ and pure $-$ starts coincide by path reflection, whereas their noise terms have opposite signs. Thus, exactly,

$$\operatorname{Var}_+(V_T)-\operatorname{Var}_-(V_T) =2\xi^2\kappa\delta\int_0^T e^{-2mu}J_\kappa(T-u)du,$$ $$m\{\operatorname{Var}_+(V_T)-\operatorname{Var}_-(V_T)\} \longrightarrow\xi^2\kappa\delta J_\kappa(T).$$

For $T>0$, $\xi>0$ and $\delta\ne0$, this limit is nonzero. A stationary-start approximation used unchanged for both specified starts therefore cannot have an $O(m^{-2})$ error even at that fixed positive maturity. If $\xi=0$, this first-order noise-memory term disappears and the occupation boundary effect remains second order.

Random initial variance: stationary regime marginals do not suffice

Now allow a joint initial law with $v_0>0$ almost surely and $\mathbb E v_0^2<\infty$. Conditional on $Y_0$, the future chain is independent of $v_0$; Brownian motion is independent of the initial pair and the chain. Put $\mu_0=\mathbb E v_0$ and $r_i=\mathbb E[(v_0-\mu_0)\mathbf1_{\{Y_0=i\}}]$, so $r\mathbf1=0$.

The averaged CIR now uses the same marginal law of $v_0$, independently of its Brownian motion, giving

$$\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T)=B_\kappa(T)^2\operatorname{Var}(v_0) +\xi^2\{(\pi\theta)I_2(T)+(\mu_0-\pi\theta)J_\kappa(T)\}.$$

The extended approximation and its finite-rate bound are

$$\operatorname{Var}(V_T)=\operatorname{Var}_{\rm av}(V_T)+\frac{2\kappa^2K}{m}I_2(T) +\frac{\xi^2\kappa}{m}J_\kappa(T)\nu h+\frac{2\kappa}{m}B_\kappa(T)^2rh+R(T,m),$$ $$\sup_{T\ge0}|R(T,m)|\le \frac{(4C+3C^2)F^2+2\xi^2CF/\kappa+2CF\|r\|_1}{m^2\gamma^2}.$$

Conditioning additionally on $v_0$ preserves the zero conditional mean of the stochastic integral. The deterministic-start decomposition acquires $B_\kappa(T)^2\operatorname{Var}(v_0)$ and the cross term

$$L(T,m)=2\kappa B_\kappa(T)\int_0^T B_\kappa(T-u)r e^{muQ}f\,du.$$

Extend $B_\kappa$ by zero to negative times; it is globally one-Lipschitz, $\kappa B_\kappa\le1$, and $r\mathbf1=0$. Replacing $B_\kappa(T-u)$ by $B_\kappa(T)$ inside the integral over $[0,\infty)$ therefore gives

$$\left|L(T,m)-\frac{2\kappa}{m}B_\kappa(T)^2rh\right| \le 2CF\|r\|_1\int_0^\infty u e^{-m\gamma u}du =\frac{2CF\|r\|_1}{m^2\gamma^2}.$$

The earlier two error bounds are unchanged, proving the extension with only a second initial moment and no exponential-moment assumption. The bound is uniform over maturities and joint initial laws with bounded $\|r\|_1$, not with an unrestricted correlation norm.

For symmetric switching, let $Y_0=\pm1$ equiprobably, $\theta_\pm=\bar\theta\pm\delta$, and $v_0=\mu_0+aY_0$, where $\mu_0>|a|$. Compare an independent initial variance with the same two-point marginal law; the exact variance difference is $L(T,m)$, with $r e^{muQ}f=a\delta e^{-2mu}$. Thus $m\{\operatorname{Var}_{\rm corr}(V_T)-\operatorname{Var}_{\rm indep}(V_T)\}\to\kappa a\delta B_\kappa(T)^2$, nonzero when $a\delta\ne0$ and $T>0$.

Here both regime marginals are stationary, yet only the independent start has $r=0$. This is not joint stationarity of $(v,Y)$, nor a comparison of speed-dependent invariant initial laws. The fourth-cumulant identity remains a finite-moment statement, without requiring a finite cumulant-generating function.

Certificate and scope

The certificate checks 756 prior/speed/maturity cases on four chains, including directed cycles and a defective generator. It also checks 16 independent noise decompositions and 42 independent stiff-ODE comparisons at long horizons. Moments of $V_T-(\pi\theta)T$ avoid subtracting two quadratically growing moments; the finite polynomial-moment method follows Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012), not a new moment-computation principle.

With $\theta=(0.08,0.02)$, $\kappa=2$, $\xi=0.2$, $v_0=0.04$ and $T=1.5$, the profile is $J_\kappa(T)=0.0873498547$. The predicted scaled pure-start variance difference is $0.0002096397$; at $m=128$ the exact value is $0.0002091593$. The corrected positive-start approximation has error $-2.6761231\times10^{-8}$ there.

Another 1512 bound cases use state-correlated point masses or conditional shape-three Pareto laws, which have finite second moments but no positive moment-generating function. An additional 21 independent stiff-ODE comparisons check random-start moments at long horizons. In the stationary-regime witness with $\mu_0=0.05$, $a=0.02$ and the same model parameters, the predicted scaled correlation gap is $0.0002708714$; at $m=128$ it is $0.0002707596$.

The theorem fixes $Q,\theta,\kappa,\xi$ and a valid semigroup envelope; its constant is uniform in the regime prior and maturity, not in a closing spectral gap. The random-start extension requires the stated conditional independence and a finite second moment; only the mean level switches, with no price-level, log-return, leverage or simultaneous parameter-switching approximation. The finite numerical mesh audits the implementation; the integral estimates supply the continuous uniform bound.
