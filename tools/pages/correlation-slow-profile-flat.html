Slow-profile covariance

Green–Kubo geometry moves from leading order to the logarithmic correction.

At regular-variation index zero, the leading covariance rate no longer sees the chain or gauge. Their geometry returns one order later, where it also proves that the squared mean is negligible.

The theorem

Use the finite-chain and tangent-gauge setup of the preceding notes. Let $h$ be eventually positive, increasing and twice continuously differentiable, regularly varying with index $\gamma>1$, and suppose

$$\frac{x h'(x)}{h(x)}\to\gamma,\qquad \frac{x h''(x)}{h'(x)}\to\gamma-1.$$

Near zero define

$$\Psi_{h,r}(w)=\exp\{-h(\log(1/r(w)))\},\qquad \Psi_{h,r}(0)=0,$$

with any fixed continuous bounded extension away from the local region. Put $n=mT$, $L_n=\tfrac12\log n$ and $d_n=h'(L_n)$. For every fixed $p>0$, uniformly over arbitrary initial priors,

$$-\log\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T})^p =p h(L_n)-\frac p2d_n \{\log[p\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)d_n]-1\}+o(d_n).$$

Consequently,

$$-\log\operatorname{Var}(\Psi_{h,r}(W_{m,T})) =2h(L_n)-d_n\{\log[2\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)d_n]-1\}+o(d_n),$$ $$-\log\frac{(\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r})^2}{\mathbb E\Psi_{h,r}^2} =d_n\log2+o(d_n).$$

The leading variance law is therefore $-\log\operatorname{Var}\sim2h(\tfrac12\log n)$, independent of $\Gamma$ and $\rho$. This remains a fixed-feature, fixed-maturity scalar statement.

Why geometry moves down one order

Scale the occupation average as $W_{m,T}=d_n^{1/2}y/n^{1/2}$. Its spectral moderate-deviation speed is $d_n$, while smooth regular variation gives, uniformly on compact sets away from zero,

$$h(\log(1/r(W_{m,T}))) =h(L_n)-d_n\{\tfrac12\log d_n+\log\rho(y)\}+o(d_n).$$

After removing the common $p h(L_n)$ term, the remaining contraction is

$$\inf_{y\in\operatorname{Ran}\Gamma}\left\{\frac12y^\top\Gamma^+y-p\log\rho(y)\right\} =\frac p2\{1-\log[p\Lambda_\rho(\Gamma)]\}.$$

The range restriction is essential when the fixed-feature Green–Kubo matrix is singular. Substitution gives the moment expansion; the difference between twice the first-moment cost and the second-moment cost is $d_n\log2+o(d_n)\to\infty$, which resolves the leading-order tie and yields the variance.

Power-log specialization and literature

For $h(t)=t^\gamma$, the leading law is $2^{1-\gamma}[\log(mT)]^\gamma$. This recovers the scalar telegraph constant while adding arbitrary finite chains, vector features, asymmetric gauges and the geometry-sensitive second-order term.

Karamata (1933) supplies regular variation, de Bruijn (1959) the classical slow-profile Laplace setting, Varadhan (1966) the Laplace principle, and Gärtner (1977) the spectral Markov large-deviation framework.

No fixed-parameter covariance-rank or maturity-integrated identification claim is made.
