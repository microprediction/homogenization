Faster switching can raise finite-maturity variance

The variance of integrated CIR variance can increase with switching speed even when one-time stationary variance and the long-run integrated-variance rate both decrease. A fixed positive, strict-Feller model gives a rational-interval-certified increase of more than six percent.

A fixed model, two stationary starts

Let $Y$ be the directed cycle on $j=0,\ldots,31$, with rate $m$ from $j$ to $j+1$ modulo $32$. Its invariant law is uniform. Independently of the chain, let $B$ be Brownian motion and set

$$dv_t=\kappa(\theta_{Y_t}-v_t)\,dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dB_t,\qquad \theta_j=.04+.01\cos(2\pi j/32),\qquad\kappa=100,\qquad\xi^2=.001.$$

Use the joint invariant law of $(v,Y)$ at each speed, not a common deterministic $v_0$. The mean level is $\bar\theta=.04$, its variance is $S=1/20000$, and the smallest Feller margin is $2\kappa\min_j\theta_j-\xi^2=5999/1000>0$.

Write $V_T=\int_0^T v_t\,dt$ and $\Gamma_m=\lim_{T\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}(V_T)/T$. At the same fixed maturity $T=1$, increasing speed from $36$ to $40$ gives:

$m$$\operatorname{Var}(v)$ $\operatorname{Var}(V_1)$$\Gamma_m$
364.961609842070e-52.495547874206e-61.392888888889e-6
404.952284284554e-52.655274666807e-61.254000000000e-6

The table is rounded; the strict increase is established by these enclosing rational brackets:

$$2.49554\times10^{-6}<\operatorname{Var}_{m=36}(V_1)<2.49556\times10^{-6},$$ $$2.65526\times10^{-6}<\operatorname{Var}_{m=40}(V_1)<2.65529\times10^{-6}.$$

Thus the increase exceeds $1.5970\times10^{-7}$ and six percent. The same interval calculation certifies the reverse order of both other columns.

Exact covariance and finite-maturity formula

The level contrast is one real Fourier mode of the cycle, so, with $z=m(1-e^{-2\pi i/32})$ and $b=\xi^2\bar\theta/(2\kappa)$,

$$\operatorname{Cov}(\theta_{Y_0},\theta_{Y_t})=S\operatorname{Re}e^{-zt},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(v_0,\theta_{Y_t}) =\kappa S\operatorname{Re}\frac{e^{-zt}}{\kappa+z}.$$

The latter follows by applying the stationary product generator to $v$ times the complex Fourier mode. Conditional means then give $C_v'=-\kappa C_v+\kappa\operatorname{Cov}(v_0,\theta_{Y_t})$, with $C_v(0)=b+\kappa S\operatorname{Re}(\kappa+z)^{-1}$.

Solving yields the exact real covariance

$$C_v(t)=b e^{-\kappa t}+\kappa S\operatorname{Re} \frac{\kappa e^{-zt}-z e^{-\kappa t}}{\kappa^2-z^2}.$$

For $F_a(T)=2(aT-1+e^{-aT})/a^2$, direct integration against $2(T-t)$ gives

$$\operatorname{Var}(V_T)=bF_\kappa(T)+\kappa S\operatorname{Re} \frac{\kappa F_z(T)-zF_\kappa(T)}{\kappa^2-z^2}.$$

Here $z$ has nonzero imaginary part, so there is no resonant denominator. The one-time variance is $b+\kappa S\operatorname{Re}(\kappa+z)^{-1}$, while integration to infinity gives

$$\Gamma_m=\frac{\xi^2\bar\theta}{\kappa^2}+ 2S\operatorname{Re}\frac1z =\frac1{250000000}+\frac1{20000m}.$$

Writing $z=m(a+ic)$, with $a>0$, shows the one-time regime variance is $\kappa S(\kappa+ma)/D_m$, where $D_m=(\kappa+ma)^2+m^2c^2$. Its first derivative is strictly negative because

$$\frac{d}{dm}\frac{\kappa+ma}{D_m} =-\frac{a\kappa^2+2\kappa m(a^2+c^2)+am^2(a^2+c^2)}{D_m^2}<0.$$

The rate also strictly decreases, but neither fact orders the finite-window covariance integral. Damped oscillations shift relative to the window as speed changes.

What is rigorously certified

The certificate uses only rational interval endpoints for the inequality proof. It constructs $\cos(\pi/16)$ and $\sin(\pi/16)$ by half-angle square roots, enclosed using integer square roots, and rounds every arithmetic operation outward on a $10^{-30}$ rational lattice.

For exponentials it encloses the degree-$40$ Taylor polynomial at $x/128$, with remainder at most $3/41!$ for $|x/128|\le1$, then raises the positive interval to power $128$. Sine and cosine use their degree-$120$ Taylor polynomials, with remainder at most $8^{121}/121!$ on $[-8,8]$. These exact bounds, not floating-point agreement, prove the separated brackets above.

An independent degree-two, regime-resolved polynomial semigroup checks both values numerically. Such finite moment semigroups are classical: see Cuchiero, Keller-Ressel and Teichmann (2012), Theorem 2.7. No priority claim is made for Fourier diagonalization, polynomial moments, or interval arithmetic.

This is a counterexample to an unrestricted finite-maturity speed order, not to a fast-switching expansion, a maturity-uniform error bound, or a reversible-chain theorem. All coefficients and the maturity stay fixed; only speed and its corresponding joint invariant start change. No minimal number of states or complete turning-point classification is claimed.
