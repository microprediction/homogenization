An explicit moving-generator initial-layer bound

A centered residual gains a power of the fast scale by a Poisson integration by parts, including at time zero.

Back to solvability · Papers · certificate.

Setup and composite

On a fixed $[0,T]$, let $Q(t)$ be a $C^2$ family of irreducible finite row generators and let $g(t)$ be a real $C^2$ vector, both fixed independently of $\varepsilon$. We approximate the column-vector ODE $u'=\{Q(t)/\varepsilon+D(t)\}u$, $u(0)=\mathbf1$, with $D=\operatorname{diag}g$, for every $\varepsilon>0$.

All coefficients use this ODE's time variable; a calendar-time pricing protocol must first be converted to its appropriate backward-duration convention. No periodicity, reversibility, diagonalizability or vanishing initial forcing is assumed.

Write $\pi Q=0$, $\pi\mathbf1=1$, $P=\mathbf1\pi$, $R=Q^\#$, $\bar g=\pi g$ and $f=g-\bar g\mathbf1$. Define the outer coefficients

$$a(t)=\exp\!\int_0^t\bar g(s)\,ds,\quad b(t)=\int_0^t\{-\pi D_fRf-\pi'Rf\}(s)\,ds,\quad v_0=a\mathbf1,\quad v_1=a(b\mathbf1-Rf).$$

The two terms in $b'$ are respectively the dynamic Green–Kubo contraction and the geometric term from the moving stationary row. Set $w=R(0)f(0)$ and restore the frozen initial layer:

$$u_{\rm app}(t)=v_0(t)+\varepsilon v_1(t) +\varepsilon e^{tQ(0)/\varepsilon}w.$$

This matches $u_{\rm app}(0)=\mathbf1$ exactly. Without the layer the initial error is precisely $\varepsilon\|w\|_\infty$, so a uniform second-order claim would fail whenever $w\ne0$.

A nonasymptotic constant

Use the vector maximum norm and its induced matrix maximum absolute row-sum norm throughout. Let $H=\sup_{[0,T]}\|g\|_\infty$, $L_Q=\sup_{[0,T]}\|Q'\|_\infty$, and supply any valid frozen-chain envelope

$$\|e^{sQ(0)}-P(0)\|_\infty\le C_0e^{-\gamma_0s},\qquad s\ge0.$$

A spectral gap alone is not the prefactor $C_0$; the supplied envelope must hold in the stated norm. Define $r=v_1'-Dv_1$, $z=Rr$, and take rigorously valid bounds $Z\ge\sup\|z\|_\infty$, $Z_1\ge\sup\|z'\|_\infty$ on the whole interval.

These quantities use only the supplied protocol and outer coefficients, not the unknown exact solution $u_\varepsilon$.

$$\boxed{\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|u(t)-u_{\rm app}(t)\|_\infty \le\varepsilon^2e^{HT}\left[2Z+T(Z_1+HZ) +C_0\|w\|_\infty\left(\frac{L_Q}{\gamma_0^2}+\frac{H}{\gamma_0}\right)\right].}$$

The $C^2$ assumptions make $z$ continuously differentiable and these constants finite. This is an absolute vector-error bound on a fixed interval, not a relative-price, log-price, infinite-maturity or growing-horizon statement.

Why a centered residual is enough

The outer identities are $v_0'-Dv_0=Qv_1$ and $\pi r=0$. To check the second one, differentiate the moving gauge $\pi Rf=0$: the $\pi'$ contribution is exactly what the geometric term in $b'$ cancels.

Let $U_\varepsilon(t,s)$ be the exact evolution operator for $Q/\varepsilon+D$. Cooperative comparison gives $\|U_\varepsilon(t,s)\|_\infty\le e^{H(t-s)}$, independently of the switching speed.

Since $Qz=r$, differentiating $U_\varepsilon(t,s)z(s)$ yields the exact identity

$$\varepsilon\int_0^tU_\varepsilon(t,s)r(s)\,ds =\varepsilon^2\left\{U_\varepsilon(t,0)z(0)-z(t) +\int_0^tU_\varepsilon(t,s)(z'-Dz)(s)\,ds\right\}.$$

Thus the outer residual contributes at most $\varepsilon^2e^{HT}\{2Z+T(Z_1+HZ)\}$, despite its pointwise order being only $\varepsilon$. This cancellation uses a Poisson equation, not a nonautonomous exponential-mixing assertion.

The initial-layer residual is $\{Q(0)-Q(t)-\varepsilon D(t)\}e^{tQ(0)/\varepsilon}w$. Because $\pi(0)w=0$, its norm is at most $C_0\|w\|e^{-\gamma_0t/\varepsilon}(L_Qt+\varepsilon H)$.

Its Duhamel integral is bounded using $\int_0^\infty t e^{-\gamma_0t/\varepsilon}dt=\varepsilon^2/\gamma_0^2$ and $\int_0^\infty e^{-\gamma_0t/\varepsilon}dt=\varepsilon/\gamma_0$. Adding the two contributions, with zero initial mismatch, proves the boxed bound for every positive $\varepsilon$.

A rationally bounded moving protocol

Take $T=1$, $p(t)=1/4+t/4$, $\pi=(p,1-p)$, $Q(t)=2(\mathbf1\pi(t)-I)$ and $g=(1,0)^\top$. Here $R=-(I-P)/2$, $f=(1-p,-p)^\top$, and

$$a=e^{t/4+t^2/8},\qquad b=\frac{7t}{32}+\frac{t^2}{32}-\frac{t^3}{96},\qquad b'=\frac{p(1-p)}2+\frac18.$$

Put $h=b+1/2-p$; then $h'=-(1-t)^2/32$, $23/96\le h\le1/4$, and the centered residual is $r=-ahf$, so $z=ahf/2$. The following are analytic interval bounds, not maxima inferred from a sampled trajectory:

$$H=L_Q=1,\quad C_0=3/2,\quad\gamma_0=2,\quad\|w\|_\infty=3/8, \qquad Z=9/64,\quad Z_1=69/512.$$

Indeed $a\le e^{3/8}<3/2$, $p\le1/2$, $\|f\|\le3/4$, and $\|z'\|\le(3/4)\{(1/8+1/32)(3/4)+(1/4)(1/4)\}=69/512$. The bracket in the theorem is $501/512$; using $e<3$ gives

$$\sup_{0\le t\le1}\|u(t)-u_{\rm app}(t)\|_\infty \le\frac{1503}{512}\varepsilon^2<3\varepsilon^2.$$

The certificate verifies the polynomial identities and exponential majorants with exact rational arithmetic, and compares independent high-accuracy ODE solves on ordinary and fast-time meshes. Numerical trajectory checks illustrate the bound; they are not the proof of its continuum supremum.

At $\varepsilon=1/512$ the sampled error is approximately $6.18350\times10^{-7}$, with last-doubling order $2.001724$. A nonreversible moving three-state control separately checks centering and gives order $1.999996$; its sampled rate is not a certified supremum constant.

Relation to the existing results

Matched expansions for nonstationary finite chains are classical; see Khasminskii, Yin and Zhang (1996) and Yin and Zhang (1998). This note supplies a direct computable finite-time residual constant for the geometric corrector, not a new claim of priority for the second-order matched-expansion rate.

The long-end note instead retains exact Perron or Floquet growth and treats normalized/log-price behavior over long maturities. The present formula requires neither a periodic protocol nor a perturbed spectral projector, but its constant can grow with $T$ and cannot replace those long-end results.
