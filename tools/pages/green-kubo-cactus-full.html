Repeated skew modes on a cactus

An exact state count for cycle networks joined at articulation vertices.

A cactus is a connected undirected simple graph whose distinct simple cycles share at most one vertex. Here it is the support graph of an irreducible row generator: an edge exists when either transition rate is positive. Rates may be one-way, and holding times and real feature values are unrestricted.

The exact count

For centered features $F$ and stationary law $\pi$, let $K=F^TD_\pi(-Q)^\#F=G+A$, with $G\succ0$ and $A^T=-A$. Suppose the target has $2r$ features and all $r$ positive frequencies of $iG^{-1/2}AG^{-1/2}$ equal the same $\kappa>0$.

$$q=\min\{n\ge3:\ \kappa\le\cot(\pi/n)\},\qquad \boxed{N_{\rm cactus}(K)=1+r(q-1).}$$

The minimum is over all cactus-supported generators and all real centered feature embeddings. It is attained by $r$ biased $q$-cycles sharing one hub, with any prescribed positive stationary law on these states. No claim is made that this is the minimum among all graphs.

For $K_*=I_4+\cot(\pi/5)\operatorname{diag}(J,J)$, $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$, the cactus minimum is exactly nine. Thus any six-, seven- or eight-state realization sought in issue #222 must have noncactus support; the unrestricted gap remains open.

Why the articulation decomposition is exact

Write $w_{ij}=\pi_iq_{ij}$ and $C=\operatorname{diag}(w\mathbf1)-w$. Every graph block is a bridge or a simple cycle; stationary flow restricts to a balanced flow on each block. To see this, peel off leaf blocks: balance at their nonarticulation vertices forces zero net flow at the articulation too, and induction removes the block.

Choose a root and represent a potential modulo constants by its values within each block relative to that block's parent articulation. These $N-1=\sum_b(|V_b|-1)$ coordinates are independent; reconstruct the full potential by adding the offsets along the block tree. Each block's balanced bilinear form is unchanged by offsets, so $U^TC^TU$ is a direct sum of its grounded block forms.

Consequently the energy-normalized Hermitian skew spectrum is the union of the block spectra, including zeros from bridges. This is a congruence decomposition, not a claim that $Q$ has block-diagonal dynamics or that its ordinary eigenvalues combine this way.

Higher-mode bound for a cycle with arbitrary edge traffic

On a cycle of length $\ell$, balance makes the net current $j=w_{k,k+1}-w_{k+1,k}$ constant. If $j=0$, the skew form vanishes. Otherwise orient the cycle so $j>0$ and subtract the reverse flow on every edge: the remaining flow is exactly the constant one-way current $j$.

In grounded coordinates write the symmetric and skew parts of $C^T$ as $H,D$. If $H_0,D_0$ are those of the unit-current one-way cycle, then $H=jH_0+R$, $D=jD_0$, where $R\succeq0$ is the symmetric edge-conductance form. Fourier diagonalization on the quotient by constants gives the one-way signed frequencies $\cot(k\pi/\ell)$, $1\le k\le\ell-1$.

For every $t>0$, $iD-tH=j(iD_0-tH_0)-tR\preceq j(iD_0-tH_0)$. Hermitian eigenvalue monotonicity therefore bounds its number of nonnegative eigenvalues by that of the one-way expression. Sylvester inertia under whitening identifies these counts with the numbers of intrinsic frequencies at least $t$.

In particular, if this block supplies $k\ge1$ frequencies at least $\kappa$, then $\kappa\le\cot(k\pi/\ell)$, including equality. Arbitrary reversible traffic cannot increase this capacity; bridges supply none.

Packing the required modes

Any observed normalized skew form is a real isometric compression of the full intrinsic skew form. Indeed, for $F=-QU$, write $M=U^TC^TU$, $G=\operatorname{sym}M$ and whiten the ambient symmetric energy and $G$. Classical Hermitian interlacing, as in Fan and Pall (1957), Theorem 1, therefore requires at least $r$ intrinsic frequencies at least $\kappa$.

Let $k_b$ be the number supplied by cycle block $b$, and set $\alpha=\pi/\operatorname{arccot}\kappa$, using $\operatorname{arccot}\kappa\in(0,\pi/2)$. By definition $q-1<\alpha\le q$; the cycle bound implies $\ell_b\ge k_b\alpha>k_b(q-1)$ whenever $k_b>0$. Since lengths are integers,

$$N-1=\sum_b(|V_b|-1)\ge\sum_{b:k_b>0}(\ell_b-1) \ge(q-1)\sum_b k_b\ge r(q-1).$$

The reasoning uses all large intrinsic modes, not merely the largest sector ratio. It excludes every cactus of insufficient size, not just the tested examples.

Matching construction

On each of $r$ cycles put $\theta=2\pi/q$ and choose constant forward/reverse stationary flows

$$a+b=\frac{2}{q(1-\cos\theta)},\qquad a-b=\frac{2\kappa}{q\sin\theta}.$$

These are nonnegative precisely when $\kappa\le\cot(\pi/q)$; at the endpoint the cycle is one-way but still irreducible. Divide every flow leaving state $i$ by the prescribed $\pi_i$ to obtain rates, and choose the diagonal to make row sums zero.

Use potentials $(\cos(k\theta)-1,\sin(k\theta))$ on each cycle, zero off that cycle and at the common hub. For $F_0=-QU$ the block form is exactly $\operatorname{diag}(I_2+\kappa J,\ldots,I_2+\kappa J)$. A real congruence maps this to every target with the stated intrinsic frequencies and positive-definite symmetric part.

The stationary cycle-flow construction is classical; see Landim and Xu (2016), Lemma 4.1 and Remark 4.2. The point here is the matching cactus lower bound, which permits nonuniform edge traffic and arbitrary arrangements of articulation vertices.

Certificate and scope

The certificate checks arbitrary cycle-tree attachments, both current orientations, edge-dependent reverse flows, block-spectral unions, higher-mode bounds, exact rational grounded identities and matching repeated-mode realizations. An integer capacity calculation independently checks the small state-budget exclusions; a correlation quadrature checks the Green–Kubo convention.

Floating checks are numerical consistency tests, not rigorous interval enclosures. This is one fixed-parameter population block, not a regime count from maturity-integrated rank, a finite-sample identification guarantee, or equality of path laws.
