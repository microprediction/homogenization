Fast mixing certifies one optimum below log utility

For relative risk aversion below one, a constant-fraction objective need not be concave. Nevertheless, every finite irreducible regime model has an explicit sufficient switching speed above which its global optimum is unique, for every initial prior.

Localize before bounding curvature

Fix $T>0$, $0<\gamma<1$, $p=1-\gamma$, constant interest rate $r$, excess returns $b_i$, and variances $s_i\ge s_{\min}>0$. A finite chain $Y$ with generator $mQ$ and arbitrary prior $\nu$ is independent of the Brownian stock noise.

The investor chooses one deterministic fraction $w\in\mathbb R$ for the whole horizon, not a filtered or adaptive strategy. Its exact certainty-equivalent rate is

$$h_i(w)=r+b_iw-\tfrac12\gamma s_iw^2,\qquad J_m(w)=\frac1{pT}\log\mathbb E_\nu e^{p\int_0^T h_{Y_t}(w)dt}.$$

Let $\mathbb E_w$ denote the path tilt proportional to the exponential in this formula and put $q_i(w)=b_i-\gamma ws_i$, $R_w=\int_0^Tq_{Y_t}(w)dt$, and $V=\int_0^Ts_{Y_t}dt$.

$$J_m'(w)=\frac{\mathbb E_wR_w}{T},\qquad J_m''(w)=-\frac\gamma T\mathbb E_wV+\frac pT\operatorname{Var}_w(R_w).$$

These identities follow by differentiating the finite path exponential; bounded coefficients justify differentiation locally in $w$. The positive variance term is the obstruction below log utility.

$$L=\min_i\frac{b_i}{\gamma s_i},\qquad U=\max_i\frac{b_i}{\gamma s_i},\qquad I=[L,U].$$

For $w<L$ every $q_i(w)>0$, and for $w>U$ every $q_i(w)<0$. Thus every maximizer lies in $I$, at every speed and prior; existence follows from $J_m(w)\le r+\max_i|b_i||w|-\gamma s_{\min}w^2/2\to-\infty$.

If $L=U$, that common regimewise fraction is uniquely optimal at every speed, without a mixing bound. Otherwise only curvature on the compact interval $I$ is needed, not concavity over the entire real line.

A computable sufficient speed

Let $\pi$ be the stationary row law, $P=\mathbf1\pi$, and supply a valid infinity-norm mixing estimate

$$\|e^{tQ}-P\|_\infty\le C_0e^{-\lambda t},\qquad t\ge0,\qquad C_0,\lambda>0.$$

For any finite irreducible $Q$ such constants exist, with no reversibility or diagonalization assumption. Define

$$F_* =\max_{w\in I}\|q(w)-(\pi q(w))\mathbf1\|_\infty,\qquad D_* =\max_{w\in I}\{\max_i h_i(w)-\min_i h_i(w)\},$$ $$G(a,T)=\frac1T\int_0^T(T-t)e^{-at}dt =\frac1a-\frac{1-e^{-aT}}{Ta^2},\qquad A_*=2pC_0F_*^2e^{pTD_*}.$$

The maximum defining $F_*$ is attained at an endpoint. For $D_*$, maximize each quadratic difference $h_i-h_j$ over the endpoints and its interior critical point, if present.

$$J_m''(w)\le-\kappa_m\quad(w\in I),\qquad \kappa_m=\gamma s_{\min}-A_*G(m\lambda,T).$$ $$\kappa_m>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{exactly one global maximizer};\qquad m>m_*:=\frac{A_*}{\lambda\gamma s_{\min}}\ \Longrightarrow\ \kappa_m>0.$$

The second implication uses $G(a,T)<1/a$. This is a sufficient speed, not a sharp phase boundary; the first test can succeed at smaller speeds.

Why the bound holds for every prior

Fix $w\in I$, write $f=q(w)-(\pi q(w))\mathbf1$, and $X=\int_0^Tf(Y_t)dt$. Conditional transition probabilities give

$$|\mathbb E_\nu f(Y_s)f(Y_t)| \le C_0\|f\|_\infty^2e^{-m\lambda(t-s)}\quad(s\le t),\qquad \mathbb E_\nu X^2\le2C_0TF_*^2G(m\lambda,T).$$

This uses stationary centering, not a stationary start: $\mathbb E_\nu X$ need not vanish. The bound follows by integrating the two-time product over the ordered triangle, and remains valid for pure starting states.

The path likelihood ratio of the tilted law to the original law is at most $e^{pTD_*}$. Since variance is at most the mean squared distance from any fixed constant,

$$\operatorname{Var}_w(R_w)\le\mathbb E_w(R_w-T\pi q(w))^2 \le e^{pTD_*}\mathbb E_\nu X^2.$$

Insert this inequality and $V\ge Ts_{\min}$ into the exact curvature identity. Strong concavity on $I$, together with the derivative signs outside $I$, proves global uniqueness.

A posteriori optimizer certificates

Whenever $\kappa_m>0$, every proposed $w\in I$ and the unique optimum $w_*$ satisfy

$$|w-w_*|\le\frac{|J_m'(w)|}{\kappa_m},\qquad 0\le J_m(w_*)-J_m(w)\le\frac{J_m'(w)^2}{2\kappa_m}.$$

Integrating the curvature bound proves the distance inequality; maximizing its quadratic upper model proves the value inequality. This is the standard strong-convexity residual argument with signs reversed, as in Boyd and Vandenberghe (2004), §9.1.2, equation (9.9).

First clip a proposed fraction to $I$ if necessary, which cannot decrease the objective. A certified derivative error $\eta$ is accommodated by replacing $|J_m'|$ with $|\widehat J_m'|+\eta$; an ordinary matrix-exponential check is not such an error certificate.

A rational nonreversible, switched-variance example

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix}-3&2&1\\1&-2&1\\1/2&3/2&-2\end{pmatrix},\quad \pi=(5/24,11/24,1/3),\quad b=(3/25,-2/25,1/20),\quad s=(1/25,9/100,1/16),\quad\gamma=1/2,\quad T=1.$$

Every off-diagonal rate is at least $1/2$, so a common refresh to the uniform law at rate $3/2$ gives $C_0=2$, $\lambda=3/2$ by coupling. Subtracting that refresh generator leaves a valid generator; after the shared refresh, the two copies can evolve together.

$$I=[-16/9,6],\qquad F_*=33/160,\qquad D_*=33/20,\qquad m_*=(363/128)e^{33/40}<8349/1280<8.$$

Exact rational Taylor bounds prove $e^{33/40}<23/10$. Consequently every $m\ge8$ and every prior have one global optimum, with the rigorous uniform curvature modulus $\kappa\ge1891/512000$.

The averaged fraction is $24/169$. At $m=8$, the stationary-prior numerical optimum is $0.1428792777$, its distance from that fraction is $0.0008674433$, and the derivative-residual distance bound is $0.0082177770$.

Checks and scope

The certificate checks the rational constants, pure and stationary priors, unequal variances, nonreversible and defective generators, and independently differentiated versus tilted-moment matrix systems. Numerical root and residual evaluations are not interval enclosures; the analytic theorem does not rely on a sampled curvature maximum.

The regime-market background is related to Bäuerle and Rieder (2004), whose Theorem 3 optimizes regime-adapted fractions, a different control class. Here the uniqueness threshold is a direct covariance/change-of-measure bound for one constant fraction, without a priority claim for its elementary ingredients.

The speed bound is conservative and depends on the fixed horizon through $e^{pTD_*}$. It is not horizon-uniform, does not imply global concavity outside $I$, and does not optimize adaptive trading or establish an actual multi-optimum transition at $m_*$.
